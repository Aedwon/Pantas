# Pattern checklist: the five classes Codex round 5 found (session 3, manager ruling 1, Nov 26)

Fix by pattern, not by finding. Apply all five to EVERY `:::check`, `:::try`, worked chain, `:::pair` note, `:::why`, Speed Tricks or shortcut line, and `:::checklist` line in each lesson you touch, not only the lines a finding names. Source: `codex-grammar-r5.md` (round 5 at `44d38877f`: 42 Blockers, 52 Majors).

## P1. The answer is named above the question

A Quick Check or try sits under a heading, label, list or lead-in that names the category it asks for (complex, compound, finite, possibility, sentence type, *ang* form, aspect name). Or the only option that appears in the list just printed is the right one.
- Bad: under "### Complex Sentences", a check asks "Is this sentence simple or complex?"
- Bad: the lesson prints the *ang* list (*ako, ikaw, siya, kami, tayo, kayo, sila*), then offers *sila* against *kanila*: only *sila* is in the list.
- Fix: neutral headings (rule 5b); options that both come from what was taught, so the reader must use the new sentence to decide.

## P2. A chain or Check ends short, or repeats the step above

A worked chain, a try's answer or a `**Check:**` ends at a label, a letter, a choice number or a fragment, not the finished sentence. Or the Check only restates the step just above it.
- Bad: "So the answer is B." / "Verb: *runs*." as the last line.
- Fix: end at the finished sentence with the change visible (for example the corrected sentence in full). A Check confirms the result a different way (reread the whole sentence, test the swap, count the subjects), in one line.

## P3. A try accepts more than one answer

An open rewrite try ("Rewrite this sentence so it is clear") that several correct sentences satisfy, while the answer shows only one.
- Fix: give a fixed slot ("Fill the blank: ...") or complete options to choose from, with exactly one defensible answer. If a rewrite stays open, the answer says what any correct version must do and gives one model, and the Check tests that property.

## P4. A rule stated as universal

A rule, pair note, shortcut or checklist line says "always", "never", "every" or states a pattern with no scope, when it has real exceptions. Known cases: a comma always follows an opening clause; *will* always shows future; the verb agrees with the nearest noun; *ang* marks every subject; a clause always needs a visible subject; every *-um-* contemplated form repeats a consonant and vowel; tense or agreement is the only finite test.
- Fix: one short scope word or phrase ("usually", "in this kind of sentence", "here", "in this lesson"). Do not add exception lists. Check against Cambridge, British Council, Purdue OWL, Merriam-Webster (English) or KWF, Ortograpiyang Pambansa 2013, Schachter and Otanes (Filipino).

## P5. A rule fixed in one place but still wrong in its restatements

A lesson states a rule in the core, then again in a pair note, a Why, Speed Tricks, a shortcut and the checklist. A fix that changes one line and leaves the others reopens the finding.
- Fix: when you change a rule, search the whole lesson for every restatement of it (grep its key words) and make them agree in the same pass.

## Report line per lesson

For each lesson: P1 to P5 each "ok" or what you changed (one line each change).
