# Figure briefs: coordinate geometry

Lesson: `content/lessons/mathematics/geometry/coordinate-geometry.md`. Folder: `content/illustrations/mathematics/geometry/coordinate-geometry/`.

Note for the artist: the lesson also has older FIGURE markers; they are not part of this pass. Four ILLUSTRATION markers only. In every figure here the coordinate plane has an x-axis and a y-axis with arrowheads, small unlabeled tick marks at equal spacing, and the axis letters "x" and "y". No tick numbers anywhere. Draw all figures to one equal scale on both axes, so circles look round.

## 01-circle-center-radius-equation.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/geometry/coordinate-geometry/01-circle-center-radius-equation.svg`
- Marker: circle with center C(3, -2) and radius 5 on a coordinate plane, a point P(x, y) on the circle, the radius segment from C to P labeled r = 5, and the equation (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25
- Purpose: In Equation of a Circle, show that every point P on a circle is the same distance r from the center C, and tie that to the standard-form equation.
- Layout: One coordinate plane filling the 360-wide frame, with the equation as a line of text at the top. Scale about 20 units per grid step. The axes cross near the upper left part of the plane so the whole circle fits: the center C sits 3 steps right of and 2 steps below the crossing, and the circle has a radius of 5 steps (so it reaches 2 steps left of the y-axis, 8 steps right of it, 3 steps above the x-axis and 7 steps below it). Draw the circle as a thin outline with a light fill. Mark C with a dot and the label "C(3, −2)" placed inside the circle, below the dot. Mark a point P on the circle in the upper right, with a dot and the label "P(x, y)" outside the circle. Draw the radius as a line from C to P, with the label "r = 5" beside its middle. Put the equation at the top.
- Labels (verbatim): "(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25"; "C(3, −2)"; "P(x, y)"; "r = 5"; "x"; "y"
- Focus (accent): The radius segment from C to P with its label "r = 5".
- Must not show: Any other point or coordinate, tick numbers, a right triangle under the radius, the expanded form of the equation, the words "distance formula", or the squaring step.
- Suggested height: 400

## 02-parallel-line-through-point.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/geometry/coordinate-geometry/02-parallel-line-through-point.svg`
- Marker: coordinate plane with line 2x + 3y = 6 (y-intercept 2, slope -2/3) and a parallel dashed line through (3, -1) with y-intercept 1, both labeled with slope -2/3
- Purpose: In Example #5, after the solution, show the given line and the parallel line through (3, −1), with the same slope and their y-intercepts.
- Layout: One coordinate plane in the 360-wide frame, about 20 units per grid step, axes crossing in the middle left of the plane. Draw the line 2x + 3y = 6 as a solid line falling from upper left to lower right, crossing the y-axis at 2 steps up. Draw a second line, dashed, falling at exactly the same angle, crossing the y-axis at 1 step up and passing through a marked dot at the point 3 steps right of and 1 step below the axes crossing. Mark both y-intercepts with dots labeled "2" and "1" beside the y-axis. Label the dot on the dashed line "(3, −1)". Label the solid line "slope −2/3" near its upper left end, and the dashed line "slope −2/3" near its upper left end, each placed away from the other line. Put the label "2x + 3y = 6" on the solid line as well, on a separate line of text above the slope label. Extend both lines across the plane with no arrowheads. Matching small arrow marks on both lines are allowed.
- Labels (verbatim): "2x + 3y = 6"; "slope −2/3" (twice); "2"; "1"; "(3, −1)"; "x"; "y"
- Focus (accent): The dashed line and the dot at (3, −1).
- Must not show: The equation of the dashed line, the rise and run, any solving step, or tick numbers.
- Suggested height: 320

## 03-circle-through-point.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/geometry/coordinate-geometry/03-circle-through-point.svg`
- Marker: coordinate plane with center C(2, -1), point P(5, 3) on the circle, a right triangle with legs 3 and 4 and hypotenuse r = 5, and the circle drawn through P
- Purpose: In Example #7, after the solution, show the center C, the point P on the circle, and the right triangle whose hypotenuse is the radius.
- Layout: One coordinate plane in the 360-wide frame, about 22 units per grid step, axes crossing about 4 steps below the top of the plane and 4 steps right of its left edge, so that the whole circle fits (the circle reaches 3 steps left of the y-axis, 7 steps right, 4 steps above the x-axis and 6 steps below). Draw the circle as a thin outline with a light fill, centered 2 steps right of and 1 step below the axes crossing, passing through P, which is 5 steps right of and 3 steps above the axes crossing. Mark the center with a dot labeled "C(2, −1)". Mark P with a dot labeled "P(5, 3)". Draw the radius from C to P. Draw a right triangle with that radius as the hypotenuse: a horizontal leg from C to the point directly below P, and a vertical leg up to P, with a small square right-angle mark at the corner. Label the horizontal leg "3", the vertical leg "4" and the hypotenuse "r = 5". Keep the whole circle inside the frame with a 16-unit margin.
- Labels (verbatim): "C(2, −1)"; "P(5, 3)"; "3"; "4"; "r = 5"; "x"; "y"
- Focus (accent): The hypotenuse labeled "r = 5".
- Must not show: The equation of the circle, the words "distance formula", the coordinates of the triangle's third corner, tick numbers, or any solving step.
- Suggested height: 360
