## 01-three-parabolas-y-ax.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/algebra/quadratic-equations/01-three-parabolas-y-ax.svg`
- Marker: three parabolas y = ax^2 + bx + c on the same kind of axes, labeled D > 0 (crosses the x-axis at two points), D = 0 (touches the x-axis at one point), D < 0 (stays above the x-axis and never touches it)
- Purpose: In the discriminant section, show how the sign of D = b² − 4ac decides how a parabola meets the x-axis, and so how many real roots there are.
- Layout: Three rows stacked top to bottom, each row about 328 wide. Every row has a small xy graph on the left (about 150 wide, same size and same kind of axes in all three, no tick numbers) and its text on the right. Every parabola opens upward. Row 1: the parabola crosses the x-axis at two points, each marked with a dot. Row 2: the parabola's lowest point sits on the x-axis, marked with one dot. Row 3: the parabola sits wholly above the x-axis and never touches it, no dots. Right-hand text in each row: the condition on the first line (large), the plain meaning below it. Use a thin divider between rows. A short title above the rows: "y = ax² + bx + c".
- Labels (verbatim): "y = ax² + bx + c"; "D > 0" with "two different real roots"; "D = 0" with "one repeated real root"; "D < 0" with "no real roots"
- Focus (accent): The points where each parabola meets the x-axis (the two dots, the one dot), and the gap between the curve and the axis in row 3.
- Must not show: No numeric values for a, b or c, no specific equation, no roots written as numbers, no formulas for the roots, no complex numbers, no downward-opening parabolas.
- Suggested height: 500

## 02-parabola-y-2x-5x.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/algebra/quadratic-equations/02-parabola-y-2x-5x.svg`
- Marker: parabola y = 2x^2 - 5x - 3 opening up, crossing the x-axis at x = -1/2 and x = 3, with the part of the curve on or below the x-axis between those two points marked
- Purpose: In Example #9, show the parabola of the quadratic and the stretch of curve that lies on or below the x-axis between its two x-axis crossings, so the student sees what the inequality asks about.
- Layout: One xy graph centered in the 360-wide frame, about 300 wide, with x and y axes and no tick numbers. Draw the upward-opening parabola y = 2x² − 5x − 3 crossing the x-axis at two points, the left one close to the y-axis on its left side, the right one farther to the right. Mark both crossings with closed dots on the x-axis and label them below the axis. Draw the part of the curve from one dot to the other (on or below the axis) in the accent color, thicker, with a light accent fill between that arc and the x-axis. Draw the rest of the curve in ink. Put the equation as a title above the graph. No words on the marked arc.
- Labels (verbatim): "y = 2x² − 5x − 3"; "−1/2" under the left dot; "3" under the right dot; "x"; "y"
- Focus (accent): The arc of the curve between the two dots.
- Must not show: This sits before the worked solution, so do not write the solution interval "−1/2 ≤ x ≤ 3", the factored form, "(2x + 1)(x − 3)", the words "below zero", "include the roots", or any check at x = 0. Do not label the y-intercept, the lowest point or any other point. Do not draw an interval bar on the axis. (The marker itself asks for the two x-axis crossings and the marked arc; those are the only solution-related items allowed.)
- Suggested height: 300
