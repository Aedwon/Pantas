# Figure briefs: parallel and perpendicular lines

Lesson: `content/lessons/mathematics/geometry/parallel-perpendicular-lines.md`. Folder: `content/illustrations/mathematics/geometry/parallel-perpendicular-lines/`.

Note for the artist: the angle labels are placed by hand, so place each label inside its own angle, next to a small arc drawn at the crossing. Every angle label must sit clearly inside the angle it names, with at least 6 units of space between it and any line. Draw each line as a thin ink line across the full frame width with no arrowheads, except where a brief asks for parallel arrow marks (a small chevron drawn on the line, one on each parallel line).

## 01-transversal-one-angle-65.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/geometry/parallel-perpendicular-lines/01-transversal-one-angle-65.svg`
- Marker: parallel lines l (top) and m (bottom) cut by a slanted transversal; the interior angle at the top crossing, below l and right of the transversal, is labeled 65°; question marks mark its vertical angle, its linear pair neighbor, its corresponding angle at the bottom crossing, and its same-side interior angle
- Purpose: In Example #1, show the one known angle and the four angles the student must find.
- Layout: Two horizontal parallel lines, l on top and m below, about 140 units apart, each with its name at the left end ("l", "m"), with matching parallel arrow marks near the right end. A transversal runs from the upper left to the lower right at about 65° to the horizontal, so the labeled angle is the acute one. Name no point. At the top crossing: the angle below l and right of the transversal gets a small arc and the label "65°" in the accent color. The angle above l and left of the transversal (its vertical angle) gets a small arc and "?". The angle below l and left of the transversal (the linear pair neighbor) gets a small arc and "?". At the bottom crossing: the angle below m and right of the transversal (the corresponding angle) gets a small arc and "?". The angle above m and right of the transversal (the same-side interior angle) gets a small arc and "?". Show no other angle marks. Make the "?" labels secondary ink and the "65°" accent.
- Labels (verbatim): "l"; "m"; "65°"; "?" (four times)
- Focus (accent): The 65° angle.
- Must not show: The values 115° or any other angle value, the words vertical, linear pair, corresponding or same-side interior, equal marks or "180°", or any arrow or note linking a question mark to the 65° angle.
- Suggested height: 280

## 07-zigzag-between-parallels.svg

Draw the angles to roughly their stated size: AP leaves A at about 30° below l, and BP leaves B at about 40° above m. Put a dot and a letter at A, B, C, D and P, with each letter set away from the lines. At A, mark angle CAP with a small arc and the label "30°". At B, mark angle DBP with a small arc and the label "40°". At P, mark the angle between PA and PB with a small arc and the label "?" in the accent color.
- Labels (verbatim): "l"; "m"; "A"; "B"; "C"; "D"; "P"; "30°"; "40°"; "?"
