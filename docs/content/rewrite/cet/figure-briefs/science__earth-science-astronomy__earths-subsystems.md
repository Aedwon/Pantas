## 01-four-sphere-cycle.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/science/earth-science-astronomy/earths-subsystems/01-four-sphere-cycle.svg`
- Marker: four-sphere cycle diagram with the Sun at the top, arrows labeled "evaporation" from ocean (hydrosphere) to air (atmosphere), "rain" from air to land, "weathering" from rain to rock (lithosphere), "photosynthesis" from air to a tree (biosphere), and "volcanic gases" from a volcano to air
- Purpose: In How the Spheres Interact, show how matter moves between the four spheres: water, rock, air, and living things are linked by five named processes, with the Sun as the energy source.
- Layout: One scene in the 360-wide frame. The Sun (circle) sits at the top center. Below it, a wide band of air across the full width, labeled "air" with "atmosphere" beside it. Under the air, on the left, an ocean (wavy top, filled shape) labeled "ocean" with "hydrosphere"; on the right, land: a ground block holding a rock, a tree and a volcano, labeled "land" with "lithosphere" at the rock and "biosphere" at the tree. Place the volcano at the far right, the tree in the middle of the land, and the rock on the left part of the land near the shore. Arrows, each with its label next to it: "evaporation" rising from the ocean to the air; "rain" falling from the air to the land; "weathering" a short arrow from the rain's landing spot to the rock; "photosynthesis" from the air down to the tree; "volcanic gases" rising from the volcano to the air. Keep arrows from crossing each other. Give each sphere its own tint: structure tints for the four spheres, no warm colors.
- Labels (verbatim): "Sun"; "air"; "atmosphere"; "ocean"; "hydrosphere"; "land"; "rock"; "lithosphere"; "tree"; "biosphere"; "volcano"; "evaporation"; "rain"; "weathering"; "photosynthesis"; "volcanic gases"
- Focus (accent): The five labeled arrows, drawn as the one cycle of exchange between the spheres.
- Must not show: Do not add other processes (no condensation, runoff, respiration, or arrows from the tree back to the air), percentages, or extra spheres such as cryosphere.
