## 01-energy-profile-catalyst.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/science/chemistry/thermodynamics-and-kinetics/01-energy-profile-catalyst.svg`
- Marker: energy profile with energy in kJ on the vertical axis and reaction progress on the horizontal axis: reactants at 50 kJ, peak at 120 kJ labeled transition state, products at 20 kJ; arrows for Ea forward = 70 kJ and delta H = -30 kJ; a dashed lower peak at 90 kJ labeled with catalyst
- Purpose: In Activation Energy and Collision Theory, show an energy profile of an exothermic reaction: the barrier the reactants must climb (activation energy), the net energy change, and a lower path when a catalyst is used.
- Layout: One graph filling the 360-wide frame, with a 16-unit margin. Vertical axis titled "Energy (kJ)" with tick numbers 20, 50, 90 and 120 only. Horizontal axis titled "reaction progress" with no ticks. Use one linear scale for energy, for example 2 units per kJ: 20 at y = 290, 50 at y = 230, 90 at y = 150, 120 at y = 90. Draw the main solid curve: a flat reactant level at 50 on the left, a smooth rise to a single peak at 120 in the middle, then a fall to a flat product level at 20 on the right. Label the left level "reactants", the right level "products", and the peak "transition state". Draw a second, dashed, lower curve from the same reactant level to the same product level with its peak at 90, labeled "with catalyst". Draw a vertical double-headed arrow from the reactant level (50) up to the peak level (120), placed at the left of the peak, labeled "Eₐ = 70 kJ" (E italic, subscript a, a few units lower). Draw a vertical arrow at the right, pointing down from the 50 level to the 20 level, with thin horizontal guide lines from each plateau to the arrow, labeled "ΔH = −30 kJ". Keep all labels clear of the curves.
- Labels (verbatim): "Energy (kJ)"; "reaction progress"; "20"; "50"; "90"; "120"; "reactants"; "products"; "transition state"; "Eₐ = 70 kJ"; "ΔH = −30 kJ"; "with catalyst"
- Focus (accent): The Eₐ = 70 kJ arrow and the peak it reaches.
- Must not show: Do not show the reverse activation energy, any activation energy for the catalyzed path (no 40 kJ, no 100 kJ), the drop of 30 kJ in the barrier, or any other arrow or number beyond the ones listed. Do not label the 90 kJ peak as a transition state.
- Suggested height: 340

## 02-rate-against-time-equilibrium.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/science/chemistry/thermodynamics-and-kinetics/02-rate-against-time-equilibrium.svg`
- Marker: graph of rate against time: the forward rate curve falls and the reverse rate curve rises from zero until they meet and level off at the same height, with the meeting point labeled equilibrium
- Purpose: In Chemical Equilibrium, show that the forward and reverse rates become equal and stay equal, which is dynamic equilibrium.
- Layout: One graph, about 300 wide, centered in the 360-wide frame. Vertical axis titled "rate" and horizontal axis titled "time", with no tick numbers. Forward curve: starts high at the left on the vertical axis and falls smoothly. Reverse curve: starts at zero (on the horizontal axis at the origin) and rises smoothly. The two curves meet at one point about two thirds of the way across, then run together as one flat horizontal line to the right edge of the graph at that same height. Mark the meeting point with a dot and a dashed vertical line down to the time axis. Label the dot "equilibrium". Label the forward curve "forward rate" near its start and the reverse curve "reverse rate" near its start, each on its own side so the labels do not cross the curves.
- Labels (verbatim): "rate"; "time"; "forward rate"; "reverse rate"; "equilibrium"
- Focus (accent): The meeting point labeled "equilibrium" and the flat shared line after it.
- Must not show: Do not show any numbers, units, concentrations, or a K value. Do not show the rates stopping at zero, since both reactions keep going.
- Suggested height: 280
