## 01-coordinate-plane-x-and-y.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/algebra/systems-of-equations/01-coordinate-plane-x-and-y.svg`
- Marker: coordinate plane, x and y from -2 to 6; line x + y = 5 through (0, 5) and (5, 0); line y = 2x - 1 through (0, -1), (1, 1) and (2, 3); the two lines cross at the labeled point (2, 3)
- Purpose: In Example #4, show the graphing method: two lines drawn on one plane, and their crossing point is the solution of the system.
- Layout: One square coordinate plane centered in the 360-wide frame, about 300 wide, with x and y both running from −2 to 6. Light grid lines at every whole number; tick numbers on both axes at −2, 0, 2, 4, 6 only (so the labels stay at 14 units). Draw the line x + y = 5 through (0, 5) and (5, 0) in ink, and the line y = 2x − 1 through (0, −1), (1, 1) and (2, 3) in secondary ink with a different dash-free weight; keep the lines inside the plane (cut them at the plane edge). Mark (0, 5), (5, 0), (0, −1), (1, 1) and (2, 3) with dots. Label each line by its equation near its upper end, inside the plane. The crossing (2, 3) is the accent dot, larger, with its label.
- Labels (verbatim): "x + y = 5"; "y = 2x − 1"; "(0, 5)"; "(5, 0)"; "(0, −1)"; "(1, 1)"; "(2, 3)"; axis names "x" and "y"; tick numbers "−2", "0", "2", "4", "6"
- Focus (accent): The crossing point (2, 3).
- Must not show: Do not write "solution" next to the point, the check equations, or any extra points or lines.
- Suggested height: 340

## 02-coordinate-plane-two-parallel.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/algebra/systems-of-equations/02-coordinate-plane-two-parallel.svg`
- Marker: coordinate plane with two parallel lines y = -2x + 5 and y = -2x + 9, y-intercepts labeled 5 and 9, no crossing
- Purpose: In Example #5(a), show that two lines with the same slope and different y-intercepts never cross, so the system has no solution.
- Layout: One coordinate plane centered in the 360-wide frame, about 300 wide, with x and y axes and a light grid. The marker gives no axis range; use x from −2 to 6 and y from −2 to 10 (the plane may be taller than wide), and cut the lines at the plane edge. No tick numbers except the two y-intercepts. Draw both lines sloping down to the right, clearly parallel and never meeting inside the plane: y = −2x + 5 and y = −2x + 9, the upper one starting at 9 on the y-axis and the lower at 5. Mark each y-intercept with a dot on the y-axis and write its number beside the dot, to the left of the y-axis. Label each line by its equation near its right end. Put the word "parallel" between the lines, in secondary ink.
- Labels (verbatim): "y = −2x + 5"; "y = −2x + 9"; "5"; "9"; "parallel"; axis names "x" and "y"
- Focus (accent): The two y-intercepts 5 and 9, and the constant gap between the lines.
- Must not show: Do not show a crossing point, an extended meeting of the lines, the elimination steps, "0 = −4", or any other points.
- Suggested height: 380

## 03-coordinate-plane-with-parabola.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/algebra/systems-of-equations/03-coordinate-plane-with-parabola.svg`
- Marker: coordinate plane with parabola y = x^2 - 4 with lowest point (0, -4); horizontal line y = -4 touching it at (0, -4); horizontal line y = -5 below it, not touching
- Purpose: In Example #8, show a line that touches a parabola at its lowest point and a line that passes below it and never meets it.
- Layout: One coordinate plane centered in the 360-wide frame, about 300 wide, with x and y axes and a light grid. The marker gives no range; use x from −3 to 3 and y from −6 to 4. The vertical scale must be large enough that the lines y = −4 and y = −5 are at least 28 units apart on screen. No tick numbers. Draw the upward-opening parabola y = x² − 4 with its lowest point on the y-axis, below the x-axis, in ink; cut it at the top of the plane. Draw the horizontal line y = −4 across the full width in the accent color, touching the parabola at its lowest point, with a dot there and the point labeled. Draw the horizontal line y = −5 across the full width in secondary ink, clearly below the parabola. Label each line by its equation at its right end, inside the plane, the lower label below its line. Put the parabola's equation near its upper right branch.
- Labels (verbatim): "y = x² − 4"; "y = −4"; "y = −5"; "(0, −4)"; axis names "x" and "y"
- Focus (accent): The touching point (0, −4) where the line y = −4 meets the parabola.
- Must not show: Do not write "no solution", "one solution", the algebra "x² = 0" or "x² = −1", or any extra points.
- Suggested height: 380
