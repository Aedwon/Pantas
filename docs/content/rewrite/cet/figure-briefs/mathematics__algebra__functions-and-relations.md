## 01-two-small-xy-graphs-side.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/algebra/functions-and-relations/01-two-small-xy-graphs-side.svg`
- Marker: two small xy graphs side by side; left, y = x^2 for all real x with the horizontal line y = 4 crossing at (-2, 4) and (2, 4), labeled "not one-to-one"; right, y = x^3 with the horizontal line y = 4 crossing once, labeled "one-to-one"
- Purpose: In Inverse Functions, show the horizontal line test: a horizontal line that meets a graph twice means the function is not one-to-one, and a line that meets it once is consistent with one-to-one.
- Layout: Two small xy graphs side by side in the 360-wide frame, each about 150 wide, with a 16-unit margin and a gap between them. Each graph has x and y axes with no tick numbers. Left graph: the full parabola y = x² opening upward, with a horizontal line at y = 4 crossing it at two points marked with dots and labeled (−2, 4) and (2, 4). Right graph: the curve y = x³ rising through the origin from lower left to upper right, with a horizontal line at y = 4 crossing it at one point marked with a dot (no coordinate label). Put the equation above each graph and the caption below each graph. Label each horizontal line y = 4 at its right end, inside the graph box.
- Labels (verbatim): "y = x²"; "y = x³"; "y = 4"; "(−2, 4)"; "(2, 4)"; "not one-to-one"; "one-to-one"
- Focus (accent): The two crossing points on the left graph, with the y = 4 line through them.
- Must not show: Do not label the single crossing on the right graph with a coordinate, add other tick numbers or points, or add any inverse function.
- Suggested height: 260
