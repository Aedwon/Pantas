## 01-two-coastal-panels-labeled-day.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/science/earth-science-astronomy/weather-and-climate/01-two-coastal-panels-labeled-day.svg`
- Marker: Two coastal panels labeled Day and Night; Day shows warm rising air over land and a surface arrow from sea to land labeled Sea breeze; Night shows rising air over the warmer sea and a surface arrow from land to sea labeled Land breeze.
- Purpose: In Sea Breezes and Land Breezes, show how faster heating and cooling of land reverses the local surface wind between day and night.
- Layout: Use a 360-wide figure with two stacked coastal panels: Day above Night. In both, place sea on the left and land on the right, with the same shoreline position. In Day, draw an upward air arrow over land and a near-surface arrow from sea to land labeled Sea breeze. In Night, draw an upward air arrow over the relatively warmer sea and a near-surface arrow from land to sea labeled Land breeze. Place sea and land labels beside their surfaces in each panel. Use a small sun and moon to reinforce the panel headings.
- Labels (verbatim): "Day"; "Night"; "sea"; "land"; "Sea breeze"; "Land breeze"
- Focus (warm accent): The upward arrow showing warm air rising over land in the Day panel.
- Must not show: Do not reverse the surface arrows or name a breeze for its destination. Do not add upper-air return currents or imply these breezes override stronger weather-system winds.
- Suggested height: 360

## 02-two-simplified-asia-ocean-maps.svg

- Purpose: In Philippine Monsoons, show how seasonal heating changes pressure over Asian land and reverses the direction of surface airflow.
- Layout: Use a 360-wide figure with two stacked simplified maps, Summer above Winter. Keep north upward and repeat the same Asian coastline in both, with Asian land toward the upper left and nearby ocean toward the lower right. Place Asian land inside each landmass. Under it, place Warmer, lower pressure in Summer and Cooler, higher pressure in Winter. Draw two broad arrows from ocean toward land in Summer and reverse them from land toward ocean in Winter. Put the caption below both maps. Keep the maps schematic and omit borders and city details.
- Labels (verbatim): "Summer"; "Winter"; "Asian land"; "Warmer, lower pressure"; "Cooler, higher pressure"; "Seasonal heating changes / pressure and reverses winds."
- Focus (warm accent): The Warmer, lower pressure label in the Summer map.
- Must not show: Do not add Habagat or Amihan names, exact dates, regional rainfall answers, or specific compass directions to these simplified heating-and-pressure maps. Do not depict the arrows as precise monsoon tracks.

## 03-tropical-cyclone-over-a-warm.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/science/earth-science-astronomy/weather-and-climate/03-tropical-cyclone-over-a-warm.svg`
- Marker: Tropical cyclone over a warm ocean with arrows labeled Water vapor rises, Air spirals toward low pressure, and Moist air rises; a cloud label reads Condensation releases heat; a top-view inset shows counterclockwise winds around a Low-pressure center north of the equator.
- Purpose: In Tropical Cyclones, connect the warm ocean's moisture supply, inward surface airflow, rising moist air, and heat released by condensation with counterclockwise cyclone rotation north of the equator.
- Layout: Use a 360-wide figure with a large side-view scene above and a compact top-view inset below. In the main scene, place ocean across the bottom and a storm cloud above its center. Draw a short upward arrow from the ocean labeled Water vapor rises. Draw curved surface arrows approaching the storm center labeled Air spirals toward low pressure. Continue with a central upward arrow entering the cloud labeled Moist air rises. Attach Condensation releases heat to the cloud. In the inset, show inward-curving counterclockwise arrows around a point labeled Low-pressure center; place north of the equator beneath the inset to establish its location.
- Labels (verbatim): "Water vapor rises"; "Air spirals toward / low pressure"; "Moist air rises"; "Condensation releases heat"; "Low-pressure center"; "north of the equator"
- Focus (warm accent): The cloud region labeled Condensation releases heat.
- Must not show: Do not show clockwise rotation, a cyclone centered on the equator, or heat being released at the ocean surface by condensation. Do not imply warm water alone guarantees cyclone formation. Omit wind categories, numerical wind speeds, and a landfall weakening sequence.
- Suggested height: 460
