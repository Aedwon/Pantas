## 02-horizontal-bar-graph-titled-help.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/reading-comprehension/comprehension-skills/inferencing-and-conclusions/02-horizontal-bar-graph-titled-help.svg`
- Marker: horizontal bar graph titled "Help Desk Visitors"; horizontal axis "Visitors" from 0 to 30 in steps of 5; Monday bar 20, Tuesday bar 30, Wednesday bar 15; vertical reference line at 25 labeled "Extra window opens above 25"
- Purpose: In Example #7, students should connect the written threshold with the graph to determine which morning satisfies the condition for opening an extra window.
- Layout: Use a 360-unit-wide canvas. Center the title at the top, followed by the scale note. Reserve space above the plot for the two-line threshold label. Place Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday in three rows, top to bottom. Put day labels left of the plot, which runs from x=100 for zero to x=340 for 30. Draw adjoining equal squares: four for Monday, six for Tuesday, three for Wednesday, all starting at zero. Add a vertical reference line at x=300, corresponding to 25, across all three rows. Place its label above it, with a short leader connecting the label to the line. Below the bars, show ticks every 5 visitors and the axis label.
- Labels (verbatim): "Help Desk Visitors"; "Each square represents / 5 visitors."; "Monday"; "Tuesday"; "Wednesday"; "0"; "5"; "10"; "15"; "20"; "25"; "30"; "Visitors"; "Extra window opens / above 25"
- Focus (warm accent): The vertical reference line at 25, in the warm accent color.
- Must not show: Do not highlight or name the qualifying day, shade the qualifying region, add window icons beside any day, or imply that exactly 25 visitors meets the condition. Do not suggest that the graph alone establishes a staffing decision.
- Suggested height: 330
