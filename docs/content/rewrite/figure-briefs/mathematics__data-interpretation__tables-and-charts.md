- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/data-interpretation/tables-and-charts/01-table-titled-quarterly-office-expenses.svg`

## 03-bar-chart-titled-factory-output.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/mathematics/data-interpretation/tables-and-charts/03-bar-chart-titled-factory-output.svg`
- Marker: bar chart titled "Factory Output" with x-axis labels Factory A and Factory B, y-axis labeled "Units produced" from 0 to 500, labeled every 100 with smaller tick marks every 20; Factory A bar reaches 440 and Factory B bar reaches 320
- Purpose: In Example #3, students should use the smaller tick spacing to read both factory outputs before finding their difference.
- Layout: Use a 360-unit-wide figure with the title centered at the top. Place a vertical y-axis on the left, its label running vertically outside the tick numbers, and a horizontal baseline below. Use a linear scale from 0 to 500, numbering every 100 and adding four smaller ticks between each numbered pair. Place two equal-width bars above Factory A and Factory B; their tops align exactly with 440 and 320. Keep bars neutral and omit value labels and comparison arrows.
- Labels (verbatim): "Factory Output"; "Units produced"; "0"; "100"; "200"; "300"; "400"; "500"; "Factory A"; "Factory B"
- Focus (warm accent): The y-axis tick marks, emphasizing the smaller scale divisions.
- Must not show: Do not label the difference as 120 or add subtraction, a difference bracket, or numeric bar-top labels that bypass reading the scale.
- Suggested height: 360
