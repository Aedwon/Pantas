## 01-original-3d-joint-with-long.svg

- File: `content/illustrations/reasoning/spatial-abstract-reasoning/mental-rotation-3d-visualization/01-original-3d-joint-with-long.svg`
- Marker: original 3D joint with long, medium, and short arms meeting at one point; Candidate A shows the same three-arm order after a turn; Candidate B shows the mirrored three-arm order; label long, medium, and short in every view
- Purpose: Help students compare the three arms around a joint to distinguish a possible rotation from a mirror image in Example #1.
- Layout: Use a 360-unit-wide figure with three vertically stacked views, headed “original”, “Candidate A”, and “Candidate B”. Center each joint beneath its heading. Draw three solid arms meeting at one point, using consistent thickness, shading, and clearly unequal lengths in every view. Use an isometric view of three mutually perpendicular arms: in the original, long points upper-left, medium upper-right, and short downward. For Candidate A, turn the entire original view 120° clockwise: long points upper-right, medium downward, and short upper-left. For Candidate B, reflect the original horizontally: long points upper-right, medium upper-left, and short downward. Preserve each arm’s length across views. Place “long”, “medium”, and “short” beside the corresponding arm in every view, with short leader lines if needed. Keep all views equally sized and visually separated; use no connecting arrows.
- Labels (verbatim): "original"; "Candidate A"; "Candidate B"; "long"; "medium"; "short"
- Focus (warm accent): The central joint in the original view.
- Must not show: No correct-answer text, checkmarks, crosses, rotation arrows, mirror symbols, or captions identifying which candidate is possible. Do not change arm lengths or connections between views.
- Suggested height: 480
