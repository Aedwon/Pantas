## 3. Required topics and skills

Curriculum entries are preparation candidates, not organizer requirements. The ordinary K to 12 guides provide expected background for standard graduates, not proof of each applicant's actual learning. The organizer accepts multiple applicant pathways and past graduates, and explicitly accepts strengthened-SHS pilot applicants. Therefore neither STEM specialization nor one current curriculum can represent every taker. [1, 3; official]

### Part I: Creative Reasoning

**Confirmed, 2026**

- Mechanical: understand mechanical/electrical relationships and forces. [2, PDF p. 6; official]
- Spatial: visualize, rotate, and disassemble objects. [2, PDF p. 7; official]
- Verbal: interpret information and reason logically. [2, PDF p. 9; official]

**Supported, curriculum**

- Mechanical reasoning: interpret motion graphs, explain forces, work, energy transfer, and conservation; distinguish charging and electrical behavior. These are science foundations, not a separate school mechanical-reasoning course. [5, PDF pp. 7-8, 120, 125-126; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Spatial foundations: reason about plane and solid figures, measurements, polygons, triangle relations, circles, and coordinates. This does not establish specific folding or matrix tasks in the current examination. [4, PDF p. 8; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Verbal relations: analyze word relationships and complete analogies; use context and critical reading to interpret information. [6, PDF pp. 163-164; 8, PDF pp. 1-2; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Logical arguments: identify propositions, determine truth values, evaluate syllogisms, and recognize fallacies. Ordinary Grade 11 General Mathematics supports these foundations; strengthened General Mathematics also includes logical operations and conditionals. [7, PDF pp. 3-4; 18, PDF p. 8; official curriculum, cohort conditional]
- Strengthened-SHS mechanical foundations: compare translational and rotational motion, explain simple/compound machines, and apply fluid principles in hydraulic systems. The accessible guide is cohort-specific, not evidence that every applicant completed it. [19, PDF pp. 2-3; official curriculum, cohort conditional]

**Supported, weak**

None retained as a requirement. Reviewer-only additions cannot extend the confirmed organizer scope.

**Unknown**

- Chemistry: distinguish mixtures/substances and particle models; explain atomic rearrangements, reaction conditions, and pressure-volume-temperature relationships in gases. [5, PDF pp. 7-8, 196; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Physics: interpret distance/speed representations; apply laws of motion, projectile motion, work, energy, and momentum; reason about heat transfer and electricity. [5, PDF pp. 7-8, 120, 125-126, 168-169; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Earth Science: explain earthquakes, faults, volcanoes, plate boundaries, weather/climate, resource use, geothermal energy, and hazards. [5, PDF pp. 7-8, 121-122, 176-177; official curriculum, inferred preparation]

### Part III: Mathematics

**Confirmed, 2026**

- Algebra, Geometry, Trigonometry, Statistics, and Calculus: interpret quantitative relationships and representations. [2, PDF p. 13; official]

**Supported, curriculum**

- Number/measurement foundations: use sets, real numbers, fractions, ratio/proportion, percent, integers, and unit conversions in problems. [4, PDF p. 8, p. 210; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Algebra: manipulate expressions and rational expressions; solve linear equations/inequalities and systems; work with quadratics, variation, radicals, sequences, and polynomial functions. [4, PDF p. 8, pp. 220, 232, 240, 247-250; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Geometry: reason about polygons, congruence, triangle inequalities/similarity, parallelograms, circles, and coordinate geometry. [4, PDF p. 8, p. 251; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Trigonometry: use basic triangle trigonometry. Advanced circular functions and identities need separate strand-specific support. [4, PDF p. 8; 13, competency listing; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- Statistics/probability: interpret data, central tendency/variability, simple-event probability, permutations/combinations, and quartiles/deciles/percentiles. [4, PDF p. 8, pp. 252-253; official curriculum, inferred preparation]
- SHS functions: evaluate/compose functions, solve rational/exponential/logarithmic equations and inequalities, interpret graphs/domain/range, and use inverse functions. [7, PDF pp. 1-2; official curriculum, ordinary-cohort preparation]
- Financial applications and logic: solve interest/annuity/loan problems and evaluate logical arguments. These are curriculum-supported candidates, not a published DOST topic list. [7, PDF pp. 3-4; official curriculum, ordinary-cohort preparation]
- SHS statistics: use random-variable distributions, normal probabilities, sampling/estimation, and hypothesis testing. Correlation/regression is explicitly an enrichment section; neither its exam inclusion nor prior completion is confirmed. [9, PDF pp. 1-4, 6; official curriculum, completion conditional]
- STEM Pre-Calculus: graph conics, solve nonlinear systems, use series/induction, work with circular functions/identities and inverse trigonometry, and convert polar/rectangular coordinates. [13, competency listing; official curriculum, STEM-specific]
- STEM Basic Calculus: study limits/continuity, derivatives, and integration; approximate areas with Riemann sums and compute definite integrals/areas. The calculus domain is organizer-confirmed for 2026; these particular competencies are curriculum-supported only. [12, competency listing; official curriculum, STEM-specific]

## 5. Version and cycle notes

- **Coverage evidence:** the latest retrievable primer in this bounded search is labeled 2026. A verified 2027 continuation/change statement was not found. Its sample formats do not establish current topic proportions or exhaustive requirements. [21; unknown current continuity]
