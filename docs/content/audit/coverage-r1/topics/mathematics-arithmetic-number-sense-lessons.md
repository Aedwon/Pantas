## Findings and proposals

Three splits improve focus without declaring the extensions unnecessary:

- Retail percent versus time-value interest: business-math, lines 14-38 and 52-149. Keep common percent teaching once; separate interest period/rate setup and later cash-flow methods. Cost: move two examples and definitions, then author genuinely missing annuity/loan methods if separately approved. Conditional financial rowsdcat-L057, acet-L079, ustet-L092, pupcet-L099, dost-sei-L132, sm-foundation-L094 are not supplied fully by simple/compound interest. A single-principal future value is taught; present value, annuities, payments and securities are different facets.
- Real-number sets versus complex arithmetic: real-imaginary-numbers, lines 12-34 and 70-117. Real classification supports basic preparation; imaginary-unit powers, complex products and conjugate division form a distinct algebra goal. Cost: redistribute three examples and clarify conjugate meaning. Basic-number rows such asacet-L062, ustet-L083, dost-sei-L126 do not establish required complex arithmetic.
- Sequence rules/terms versus series sums: sequences-and-series, lines 12-52 and 56-128. Retain common pattern definitions once, then build sums from term methods. Cost: move sum examples and develop finite-geometric-sum and convergence applications. CSC cse-professional-L109/cse-subprofessional-L075 name number sequences, not every series method. Other curriculum/conditional rows retain their broader scopes.

Local absence remains separate from missing corpus content. Real-set classification does not teach ordering or absolute value. Inventory leads to algebra/functions and word-problem sources must be reconciled by their owners. Statistical percentile rows, polynomial/function rows and figural-pattern rows are broader than fraction conversion, factorization or numeric sequence teaching. Retail discounts occur in both business-math and fractions-decimals-percentages; teach their changing-base method once and preserve distinct applications. Divisibility tests and coprime explanations should precede composite-digit applications. Cross-multiplication should precede its first proportional use. Complex conjugate meaning should precede division. These are intralesson/shared-core recommendations, not self-links or new content edits.

## Concise lesson summaries

- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-business-math**: Model price changes and calculate simple/compound interest with compatible rate and time periods. Structure split-candidate. Retail percent changes reuse a basic percent foundation; compounding/period alignment is a separate time-value task. Broader financial preparation is not supplied by these five examples. Proposal: Keep discount/markup applications with percentage preparation; create a focused interest/time-value extension that can later develop annuities/loans as required. Teach shared percent conversion once. Cost: Move two interest examples and period/rate definitions; adding annuities/loans is new authoring, not accomplished by a split. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/business-math.md:14-38,52-149.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-divisibility-rules**: Test integer divisibility, constrain missing digits, and combine tests only when factors are coprime. Structure reorder. The basic core defines divisibility but the methods reside in mnemonics; coprimality is used in a worked combined test before its explanation. Proposal: Move digit tests and the coprime condition before applications. Keep them in one divisibility lesson with a short necessary factor foundation. Cost: Local reordering and a coprime explanation, no extra standalone lesson. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/divisibility-rules.md:10-154,48-138.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-fractions-decimals-percentages**: Convert rational-number representations and choose the correct base for percentage calculations. Structure reorder. Representation conversion is coherent, but percent-base equations and reciprocal fraction division appear only when examples already use them. Basic rational operations are not developed. Proposal: Keep representation conversion together; teach fraction operations and percent/base meaning before compound applications. Share successive-discount teaching with retail percent applications once. Cost: Develop rational-operation foundations and reorder applications; moving duplicate discounts reduces repeated teaching, without a new lesson per representation. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/fractions-decimals-percentages.md:10-149,28-149.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-order-of-operations**: Evaluate grouped numerical and substituted algebraic expressions with equal-priority operations handled left to right. Structure retain. The hierarchy, grouping and substituted-expression applications form one consistent evaluation goal. Signed arithmetic is used but not taught as a general method. Proposal: Retain the core; place sign-rule preparation before the negative-input example rather than split grouping types. Cost: Add or connect a short signed-arithmetic foundation; no restructuring of the four coherent application types. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/order-of-operations.md:10-144,36-144.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-prime-factorization-gcf-lcm**: Use prime powers to choose GCF or LCM for common groups, repeating events and remainder conditions. Structure retain. Factorization, greatest common groups and least repeating intervals are one coherent foundation. The monomial extension assumes exponent/algebra knowledge beyond integer grouping. Proposal: Retain integer methods together; present monomial GCF/LCM as a clearly bounded algebra extension. The factorization example already shows prime-power compression, so a deeper exponent lesson is optional for that illustrated method. Cost: Move or label one existing example and connect its foundation; no new lesson solely for one extension. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/prime-factorization-gcf-lcm.md:10-141,30-127.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-properties-of-real-numbers**: Recognize equivalent expressions and use identities, inverses and distribution to simplify calculations. Structure retain. All named properties support equivalent-expression reasoning. Algebraic collection is assumed in one distribution example and needs a bounded foundation. Proposal: Retain property teaching once; explain like-term grouping in the symbolic example more clearly. The local worked distribution/collection sequence already teaches the procedure, so no strict algebra dependency is added. Cost: Small foundation connection or local example clarification; no separate property lesson for each rule. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/properties-of-real-numbers.md:10-144,63-161.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-ratio-and-proportion**: Choose direct, inverse or partitive models and solve proportional problems while separating completed and remaining work. Structure retain. The three proportional relationships are distinct but belong together so students choose a model from invariant ratio/product or part totals. Mid-change work is an application, not a new topic. Proposal: Retain; bring cross-multiplication before first use and connect separately taught rate/variation extensions rather than claim them from generic proportion. Cost: Small method reordering and bounded links; no split supported solely by five scenario types. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/ratio-and-proportion.md:10-128,28-124.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-real-imaginary-numbers**: Classify real-number sets and use the imaginary unit to multiply/divide complex numbers. Structure split-candidate. Real-set classification is a prerequisite for many basic operations; complex products/division introduce a distinct algebra task and conjugate method. Three of four examples develop the latter. Proposal: Separate real-number sets from imaginary/complex arithmetic; explain conjugate meaning before its first use. The worked complex product/division already shows expansion, so no redundant property prerequisite is added. Cost: Relocate complex core and three examples; develop the conjugate/expansion foundations. Splitting does not establish that any exam excludes complex arithmetic. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/real-imaginary-numbers.md:12-34,70-117.
- **lesson-arithmetic-number-sense-sequences-and-series**: Recognize common sequence rules, find terms, and distinguish finite or convergent infinite sums from individual terms. Structure split-candidate. Recognizing rules/finding terms differs from calculating sums and deciding convergence. CSE names number sequences, while sums/infinite convergence have separate preparation scope. Proposal: Separate sequence rules/terms from series sums; share arithmetic/geometric pattern definitions once, then build sums on existing term methods. Keep harmonic/Fibonacci as bounded sequence families rather than individual lessons. Cost: Redistribute core and examples into two focused units, add a finite-geometric-sum application and convergence explanation. Unknown exam depth does not prove series is unnecessary. Evidence content/lessons/mathematics/arithmetic-number-sense/sequences-and-series.md:12-52,56-128.
