## Findings from the chat read

- **B01** (Blocker; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "eight evenly spaced holes."
  - Problem: The Example #4 figure label says the eight reflected holes are evenly spaced. Symmetry from exact half-folds does not guarantee equal spacing; the spacing depends on the punch location within the folded packet.
- **L02** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "The layers have all been cut, but the copies **coincide or connect at the center** rather than becoming four separated holes."
  - Problem: The word "coincide" is unnecessarily difficult for Grade 5 teaching prose and is not explained in simple language at this point. A common word such as "overlap" would be easier to read.
- **L17** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "**Undo folds in reverse order.** Reflect the cut across the last fold first, then the previous fold, until the sheet is fully opened."
  - Problem: Reasoning lessons are required to use the reduced Core Concept → Examples → Traps to Watch For layout and drop Speed Tricks & Shortcuts. This material appears inside a Speed Tricks & Shortcuts section, so the subject-specific section fit is not followed.
- **L18** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "A rectangular sheet is folded in half three times so the punched location contains all eight layers. A small circular punch is made through the interior of the folded packet, away from all fold lines. How many separate holes appear?"
  - Problem: The examples cover cuts through all available layers and cuts on fold lines, but none works the distinct layer-location case where a cut crosses only some layers, such as near an irregular packet boundary. That case is explicitly taught as something students must check and is part of the learning goal's layer conditions.
- **L19** (Major if a step is skipped, otherwise Minor; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "Reflecting the cut across either fold maps it onto itself at the intersection. The layers have all been cut, but the copies **coincide or connect at the center** rather than becoming four separated holes."
  - Problem: Example #3 gives its worked solution as a prose paragraph instead of the required numbered solution steps.
- **L19** (Major if a step is skipped, otherwise Minor; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "so unfolding produces **eight separate symmetric holes**."
  - Problem: Example #4 ends with prose around a display equation instead of presenting the worked solution as a numbered list of steps.
- **L25** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "reflect the cut across each fold line."
  - Problem: Reflection is the central operation students must perform, but the lesson never explains what reflecting across a fold line means geometrically, such as placing the copy on the opposite side at the same distance from the fold.
- **L26** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "Reflect the cut across the last fold first, then the previous fold, until the sheet is fully opened."
  - Problem: This tells students the order in which to reflect cuts but not how the reflection changes a cut's position and orientation. The core method is therefore named procedurally without fully explaining how it works.
