# Choosing an Appropriate Title

Lesson: `lesson-comprehension-skills-choosing-a-title`. Lesson version: `63c30ecffce8`. Standards: `710d7062b594`.

## Findings from the chat read

- **L02** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "Think of choosing a title as finding the "just right" fit: not too narrow (missing parts of the passage), not too broad (could apply to many different passages), but matching the passage's actual main idea precisely."
  - Problem: This teaching sentence is long and layered, with two parenthetical explanations and the abstract word "precisely," so it is not very simple Grade 5 English.
- **L03** (Minor; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "Explain why "Solar Power: The Energy of the Future" would be a poor title for this passage."
  - Problem: The exam verb "Explain" is used as a command, but the lesson never gives the required one-line explanation of what that verb asks the student to do.
- **L05** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "A title focusing on just one part of a multi-part passage is too narrow, since it misrepresents the passage's actual full scope."
  - Problem: The worked solution uses abstract vocabulary such as "misrepresents" and "actual full scope" instead of the simple language required for solutions.
- **L15** (Minor; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "A passage discusses how bees pollinate flowers"
  - Problem: All four worked-example questions begin with "A passage," so their sentence openings are repetitive instead of varied.
- **L18** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "A passage discusses how bees pollinate flowers, how this process helps plants reproduce, and how declining bee populations threaten global food supplies."
  - Problem: Every worked example gives the passage's content as an explicit summary. None makes the student identify the central point from an actual passage before choosing a title, so the lesson misses an exam-relevant variation and does not reach authentic target difficulty.
- **L26** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "Find the main idea first, then shrink it into a short phrase."
  - Problem: The lesson tells students to find the main idea first but never teaches how to identify that main idea from a passage. With no prerequisite lesson supplied, a necessary part of the method is only named, not explained.
- **L27** (Major; one read, paste run)
  - Quote: "If the passage discusses multiple distinct points, ideas, or examples, the title must be broad enough to cover all of them, not just the one mentioned first or most memorably."
  - Problem: This shortcut is stated too absolutely. A good title should follow the passage's main emphasis and central point; it does not need to literally cover every supporting example or detail, so the needed limitation is missing.
