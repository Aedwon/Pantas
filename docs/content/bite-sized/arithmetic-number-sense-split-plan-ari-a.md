- Teaching-word estimate: 388.
- Key points:
  1. Convert a terminating decimal to a fraction.
  2. Convert a percentage to a decimal or fraction.
- Original examples: #1, #2.
- Original traps: #3.
- Core Concept material: Fractions, decimals, and percentages can show the same quantity in different forms. Fractions and Conversions A **fraction** shows division. Its **numerator** is the top number. Its **denominator** is the bottom number, and it cannot be $0$. When a fraction describes part of a whole, the denominator gives the number of equal parts in the whole. The numerator counts the parts taken. A **decimal** uses place value based on $10$. The first places after the decimal point are tenths, hundredths, and thousandths. A **percentage** means "per hundred" or "out of $100$." The symbol $\%$ means percent. To turn a fraction into a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator. Some answers end. Others have digits that repeat forever. A **terminating decimal** ends after a fixed number of decimal places. To turn it into a fraction, write its digits without the decimal point over the matching place value. Use $10$ for $1$ decimal place, $100$ for $2$, or $1000$ for $3$. For more places, continue adding zeros to the denominator. This method applies to terminating decimals, not to decimals that repeat forever. "Simplify" means write an equal fraction whose numerator and denominator share no factor greater than $1$. Divide both by the same nonzero common factor until no further reduction is possible. To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by $100$. To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by $100$ and attach the percent sign. To turn a percentage into a fraction, write the percent number over $100$ and simplify. $$p\%=\dfrac{p}{100}$$.

- File: `percentage-base-and-fraction-division.md`.

- Core Concept material: Percentage Amount, Rate, and Base The **base** is the whole or reference amount from which a percentage is taken. The **part** is the amount found by taking that percentage. The **rate** tells how much of the base to take. Use the decimal form of the rate in these equations: $$\text{Part}=\text{Rate}\times\text{Base}$$ $$\text{Base}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Rate}}$$ $$\text{Rate}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Base}}$$ Finding the base by division needs a nonzero rate. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. Multiplying both numbers in the division by that reciprocal makes the divisor $1$ and keeps the answer unchanged. $$\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}$$ Here, $b$ and $d$ must not be $0$. The second form may be easier because $25\%$ is one-fourth. $$16\div4=4$$ ### Find $10\%$ First To find $15\%$ mentally, find $10\%$, then half of that amount for $5\%$. Add the two parts. For $80$, $10\%$ is $8$ and $5\%$ is $4$. So $15\%$ of $80$ is $12$.

- Original examples: #4, #5.
- Original traps: none.
- Core Concept material: An **arithmetic sequence** has the same difference between neighboring terms. This value is the **common difference** $d$. Subtract a term from the term after it to find $d$. The symbol $a_1$ means the first term. The symbol $a_n$ means the term in position $n$. When finding a sum, $n$ is the number of terms being added. In an arithmetic sequence, moving from the first term to position $n$ takes $n-1$ equal steps. This gives: $$a_n=a_1+(n-1)d$$ If the first term, last term, and nonzero common difference are known, count the steps and add $1$ to include the first term: $$n=\dfrac{a_n-a_1}{d}+1$$ Use this only when the last term belongs to the sequence. The result must be a positive whole number..
- Other sections: Speed Tricks & Shortcuts: ### Number of Terms in an Arithmetic Sequence Subtract the first term from the last term. Divide by the nonzero common difference, then add $1$ to count the first term. Check that the result is a positive whole number.

- Core Concept material: Signed Integers Inside Expressions A **signed integer** is a whole number with a positive or negative sign. The **absolute value** of a number is its distance from $0$, so $|-6|=6$. When adding integers with the same sign, add their absolute values and keep the sign. When the signs differ, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger one and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. If the absolute values are equal, the sum is $0$.
