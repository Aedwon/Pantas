### Part 1: English Word Jobs

- File: `english-word-jobs.md`.
- Id: `lesson-grammar-and-structure-english-word-jobs`.
- One idea: Identify English word classes from their sentence jobs.
- Teaching-word estimate: 387. Ordering: 3.1667.
- Key points:
  1. Identify nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs by their jobs.
  2. Classify a word again when its job changes.
  3. Check the word’s job instead of relying on its ending.
- Core Concept: A **part of speech**, or **word class**, groups words by their job in a sentence. Traditional school grammar teaches eight English classes. / A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea: *Maria, city, book, freedom*. A **pronoun** can take the place of a noun or name: *she, they, it, who*. In “Maria left because she was tired,” *she* refers to Maria. / A **verb** shows an action, event, or state: *run, happen, is, become*. An **adjective** describes a noun or pronoun: *red* in *red apple*. / An **adverb** adds information about a verb, adjective, another adverb, or a whole sentence. In *ran quickly*, *quickly* tells how someone ran. In *very tall*, *very* tells how tall. / Some grammar books group words differently. Use the system named in the question. Within that system, check the word's job in its sentence. / The same word can do different jobs. In “I need a drink,” *drink* names a thing, so it is a noun. In “I will drink the water,” it shows an action, so it is a verb.
- Original examples: 1, 2.
- Original traps: 1, 2.
- Speed Tricks & Shortcuts: Ask what the word does in that sentence. A word's meaning alone does not decide its class..

### Part 2: Joining Words: Phrases and Clauses

- File: `joining-words-phrases-and-clauses.md`.
- Id: `lesson-grammar-and-structure-joining-words-phrases-and-clauses`.
- One idea: Use the words after a joining word to identify its class.
- Teaching-word estimate: 342. Ordering: 3.3333.
- Key points:
  1. Identify prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections by their jobs.
  2. Distinguish a phrase from a subordinate clause after a joining word.
- Core Concept: A **preposition** begins a group of words that shows a relation, such as place, time, or direction: *under the desk, before dinner, toward home*. A **conjunction** joins words or groups of words: *and, but, because*. An **interjection** expresses a reaction: *Wow!* or *Ouch!* / ### A Phrase or a Clause After a Joining Word / The **subject** tells who or what a clause is about. In “it rained,” *it* is the subject and *rained* is the verb. A **clause** has a subject and a verb, though a command can leave *you* understood. / A **phrase** is a group of words that works as one part of a sentence. “Before dinner” is a phrase showing time. It has no subject paired with a verb. / A **subordinate clause** needs another clause to complete its meaning. “Before it rained” leaves us waiting to learn what happened. A **subordinating conjunction** introduces such a clause, as *before* does here. / Check what follows *before*. A noun such as *dinner* gives “before dinner,” a prepositional phrase. A subject and verb such as *it rained* give “before it rained,” where *before* is a subordinating conjunction in traditional school grammar.
- Original examples: 3, 4.
- Original traps: none.
- Speed Tricks & Shortcuts: Check what follows *before*. In the patterns taught here, a noun gives a prepositional phrase. A subject and verb give a clause introduced by a conjunction..

### Part 3: Pangalan, Kilos, at Pandiwari

- File: `pangalan-kilos-at-pandiwari.md`.
- Id: `lesson-grammar-and-structure-pangalan-kilos-at-pandiwari`.
- One idea: Kilalanin ang mga salitang tumutukoy sa tao, bagay, at kilos.
- Teaching-word estimate: 409. Ordering: 3.5.
- Key points:
  1. Kilalanin ang pantukoy, pangngalan, at panghalip sa pangungusap.
  2. Kilalanin ang pandiwa ayon sa kilos, pangyayari, o kalagayan.
  3. Suriin ang pandiwari ayon sa pag-uuring itinakda sa tanong.
- Core Concept: ### Sa Filipino: Mga Bahagi ng Pananalita / Hindi laging magkatugma ang mga pangkat ng salita sa Filipino at Ingles. Sundin ang paraan ng pag-uuri na binanggit sa tanong. Pagkatapos, suriin ang tungkulin ng salita sa pangungusap. / Ang **pantukoy** (marker/article) ay tumutukoy sa kasunod na pangngalan o pangalan. Hanapin ang salitang gaya ng *ang* sa *ang guro* o *si* sa *si Liza*. Hindi ito ang pangalan mismo. / Ang **pangngalan** (noun) ay ngalan ng tao, bagay, hayop, lugar, o ideya. Itanong kung ano o sino ang pinapangalanan. Sa *Masipag ang guro*, ang *guro* ay tawag sa tao. / Ang **panghalip** (pronoun) ay humahalili sa pangngalan o pangalan. Hanapin kung ano o sino ang tinutukoy nito. Sa *Dumating si Liza. Umupo siya*, ang *siya* ay pumapalit kay Liza. / Ang **pandiwa** (verb) ay nagsasaad ng kilos, pangyayari, o kalagayan. Sa *Sumulat si Ben*, itanong kung ano ang ginawa ni Ben. Ang *sumulat* ang pandiwa. / Ang **pandiwari** (participle) ay katawagang ginagamit sa ilang tradisyunal na balarila. Ito ay anyong mula sa pandiwa na maaaring gamitin na parang pang-uri o pangngalan. Sa *Tinulungan nila ang nasugatan*, ang *nasugatan* ay tumutukoy sa taong nasugatan. / Upang suriin ang ganitong gamit, hanapin ang kilos na pinagmulan. Pagkatapos, tingnan kung naglalarawan o tumutukoy na sa tao o bagay ang anyo. Maaaring iba ang tawag dito sa ibang sistema, kaya sundin ang pag-uuring itinakda sa tanong.

### Part 1: Clear Pronoun Reference and Singular They

- File: `clear-pronoun-reference-and-singular-they.md`.
- Id: `lesson-grammar-and-structure-clear-pronoun-reference-and-singular-they`.
- One idea: Choose a pronoun with a clear intended reference.
- Teaching-word estimate: 405. Ordering: 5.2.
- Key points:
  1. Match an English pronoun to the person or thing meant.
  2. Use singular they and its forms for one person.
  3. Keep the verb forms after everyone and singular they correct.
  4. Repeat a name when a pronoun has two possible antecedents.
- Core Concept: A **pronoun** stands for a person or thing, as *she, it,* and *they* do. An **antecedent** is the word or phrase the pronoun refers to. In "Maria forgot her umbrella," *Maria* is the antecedent of *her*. / Choose a pronoun that fits the person or thing meant. A singular form refers to one. A plural form refers to more than one: "The girls lost their books." / ### Singular They / **Singular they** refers to one person when gender is unknown or does not matter. It is also used for a person who uses *they* as their pronoun. Its forms include *they, them, their,* and *theirs*. / "Everyone must bring their lunch" is accepted modern English. *Everyone* still takes a singular verb: "Everyone is ready." With *they*, use "they are," even when it refers to one person. / *His or her* is also possible in some sentences. It is not always a better choice than *their*. Follow any special rule stated in the task. / ### Clear Reference / "When Mrs. Santos spoke to Anna, she seemed upset" leaves two possible meanings. *She* could mean either person. Repeat the intended name when needed.
- Original examples: 1, 4.
- Original traps: 1, 3.
- Usage Notes: Find the antecedent before choosing an English pronoun. Check its number, its meaning, and whether the reader can tell who is meant. / Singular *they* does not change the verb after *everyone*. Write "Everyone has said they are ready.".

- File: `pronouns-for-groups-and-joined-antecedents.md`.
- Id: `lesson-grammar-and-structure-pronouns-for-groups-and-joined-antecedents`.
- One idea: Choose clear pronouns for groups and joined antecedents.
- Teaching-word estimate: 346. Ordering: 5.4.
- Key points:
  1. Distinguish a group acting as one unit from its individual members.
  2. Use plural reference for antecedents joined by and.
  3. Rewrite mixed or/nor references when the pronoun makes the meaning unclear.
- Core Concept: ### Groups and Joined Antecedents / A **collective noun** names a group, such as *company* or *team*. Use *it* or *its* when the group acts as one unit: "The company announced its policy." / Some uses, especially in British English, treat a group as its members and use a plural pronoun. To make that meaning clear, name the members: "The team members packed their bags." / Antecedents joined by *and* are normally plural: "Ana and Ben brought their snacks." / With *or* or *nor*, the sentence may mix a single person and a group. A pronoun can then leave the meaning unclear. When editing, name the people meant or remove the pronoun if it adds nothing. / For example, "Neither the players nor the coach brought his or their required equipment" is awkward. "Neither the players nor the coach brought the required equipment" keeps the main meaning clear.

Alamin muna kung sino ang tinutukoy at kung ano ang papel niya. / Ang anyong **ang** ay pamalit sa taong itinatampok sa pangungusap. Sa "Si Ana ang nagsulat," maaaring ipalit ang "Siya ang nagsulat."   1. Identify independent and dependent clauses from their complete thoughts.
  2. Recognize a simple sentence even with a compound subject or verb.
  3. Recognize independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction or semicolon.
- Core Concept: A **clause** is a group of words with a subject and a verb. The **subject** tells who or what the clause is about. The **verb** tells what the subject does or is. / An **independent clause** gives a complete thought and can stand as a sentence. "The dog barked" is an independent clause. / A **dependent clause** has a subject and a verb but cannot stand as a complete sentence. "Because the dog barked" leaves the reader waiting for the result. / A **simple sentence** has one independent clause and no dependent clause. "The dog barked." It can have a compound subject or verb: "The dog and the cat played and ran." / A compound subject joins subjects, as in "the dog and the cat." A compound verb joins verbs that share a subject, as in "played and ran." Neither feature alone adds another clause. / A **compound sentence** has at least two independent clauses and no dependent clause. A **coordinating conjunction** joins equal parts, including independent clauses. These conjunctions include *and, but, or, so, for, nor,* and *yet*. / "The dog barked, and the cat ran" is compound. A semicolon can also join independent clauses: "The dog barked; the cat ran." / "Classify" means name the sentence type.

Piliin ang *at* para sa dagdag na diwa, *ngunit* para sa pagsalungat, at *o* para sa pagpipilian. Upang bumuo ng tambalan, tiyaking makapag-iisa ang bawat sugnay na pagdurugtungin.
