## Core Concept

### Reading Clock Time

A **clock time** tells when something happens. A **duration** tells how long it lasts. There are $60$ minutes in an hour and $24$ hours in a day.

In **$12$-hour time**, the hour runs from $1$ through $12$. Use am for times from midnight up to noon. Use pm for times from noon up to midnight.

Midnight is $12{:}00$ am. Noon is $12{:}00$ pm.

The hour after $12{:}59$ am is $1{:}00$ am. The hour after $12{:}59$ pm is $1{:}00$ pm.

In **$24$-hour time**, the hours run from $00$ through $23$. Midnight at the start of a day is $00{:}00$.

Noon is $12{:}00$. Do not add am or pm to this form.

For an am time, keep the hour unless it is $12$. Change that hour to $00$.

For a pm time, add $12$ to the hour unless it is already $12$. Leave the minutes unchanged.

To change back, hours $00$ through $11$ are am. Change $00$ to $12$.

Hour $12$ is pm. For hours $13$ through $23$, subtract $12$ and use pm.

### Finding Elapsed Time

**Elapsed time** means the time that passes from a start to an end.

$$\text{elapsed time}=\text{end time}-\text{start time}$$

Before subtracting, put the times in the same form. Include the day if the end is after midnight.

You can also count forward. Stop when you reach the ending time. If the end is before the next exact hour, subtract the starting minutes from the ending minutes.

For example, find the time from $8{:}10$ am to $8{:}20$ am on the same day. Subtract $20-10=10$ minutes. The elapsed time is $10$ minutes.

Otherwise, count the minutes to the next exact hour. Then count whole hours without passing the ending time.

If minutes remain, count from the last exact hour to the ending time. Add these durations.

When subtracting hours and minutes, you may need to **borrow**. This means changing $1$ hour into $60$ minutes.

Reduce the hour by $1$ and add $60$ to the minutes. The total time stays the same.

For an end on the next day, you can add $24$ to its $24$-hour clock hour before subtracting. That larger hour is for the calculation. Change back to a clock time when naming a time of day.

### Adding a Duration

$$\text{end time}=\text{start time}+\text{duration}$$

Add hours to hours and minutes to minutes. If the minutes reach $60$, change each complete group of $60$ minutes into $1$ hour.

If the hour reaches $24$, subtract $24$ and move to the next day. Repeat for a duration that passes more days.

### Using a Time-Zone Offset

A **time-zone offset** is a stated difference between the clocks in two places at the same moment. If the destination is ahead, its clock shows a later time. Add the given offset.

If it is behind, its clock shows an earlier time. Subtract the offset.

$$\text{later clock}=\text{earlier clock}+\text{offset}$$

$$\text{earlier clock}=\text{later clock}-\text{offset}$$

Use the offset given in the question. Check which place is ahead before choosing an operation.

Moving forward past midnight gives the next day. Moving backward past midnight gives the previous day.

## Examples

### #1: Reading a Museum Schedule

A museum opens at $12{:}25$ pm and closes at $6{:}40$ pm. Write both times in $24$-hour form.

A delivery is scheduled for $00{:}15$ the next day. Write that time in $12$-hour form.

**Solution:**

1. The opening hour is $12$ pm. Keep that hour unchanged. The opening time is $12{:}25$.

2. The closing hour is after noon but is not $12$. Add $12$ to the hour.

$$6+12=18$$

The closing time is $18{:}40$.

3. The delivery hour is $00$. Change it to $12$ and use am. The delivery is at $12{:}15$ am the next day.

4. Check the labels. The opening is shortly after noon.

   The delivery is shortly after midnight. Both use $12$ in $12$-hour time, but their am and pm labels differ.

### #2: Elapsed Time Across Noon

A school garden team starts work at $8{:}46$ am and finishes at $1{:}18$ pm on the same day. How long does the team work?

**Solution:**

1. Count from $8{:}46$ am to the next exact hour, $9{:}00$ am.

$$60-46=14\text{ minutes}$$

2. Count whole hours from $9{:}00$ am to $1{:}00$ pm. From $9{:}00$ am to noon is $3$ hours. From noon to $1{:}00$ pm is another $1$ hour.

$$3+1=4\text{ hours}$$

3. Count from $1{:}00$ pm to $1{:}18$ pm. That is $18$ minutes. Combine the minute parts.

$$14+18=32\text{ minutes}$$

The elapsed time is $4$ hours $32$ minutes.

4. Check by subtraction. Change $1{:}18$ pm to $13{:}18$.

   The start is $08{:}46$. Since $18$ minutes is less than $46$ minutes, borrow $1$ hour from $13$ hours.

$$13\text{ h }18\text{ min}=12\text{ h }78\text{ min}$$

5. Subtract the minutes and then the hours.

$$78-46=32\text{ minutes}$$

$$12-8=4\text{ hours}$$

Both methods give $4$ hours $32$ minutes. Crossing noon did not begin a new day.

### #3: Elapsed Time Across Midnight

A bus leaves at $10{:}50$ pm on Friday and arrives at $1{:}35$ am on Saturday. How long is the trip?

**Solution:**

1. Count from $10{:}50$ pm to $11{:}00$ pm.

$$60-50=10\text{ minutes}$$

2. Count whole hours from $11{:}00$ pm to $1{:}00$ am. Reaching midnight takes $1$ hour. Reaching $1{:}00$ am takes another $1$ hour.

$$1+1=2\text{ hours}$$

3. Count the last $35$ minutes. Add the minute parts.

$$10+35=45\text{ minutes}$$

The trip lasts $2$ hours $45$ minutes.

4. Check with $24$-hour time. The start is $22{:}50$.

   The end is $01{:}35$ on the next day. Add $24$ to the ending hour for the subtraction.

$$1+24=25$$

5. Use $25$ hours $35$ minutes as the end. Borrow $1$ hour.

$$25\text{ h }35\text{ min}=24\text{ h }95\text{ min}$$

$$95-50=45\text{ minutes}$$

$$24-22=2\text{ hours}$$

This confirms $2$ hours $45$ minutes. The calculation hour $25$ does not belong on the arrival clock. The arrival clock still reads $01{:}35$.

### #4: Adding a Duration to a Start Time

A science club starts recording the night sky at $9{:}45$ pm on Saturday. The recording lasts $3$ hours $35$ minutes. When does it end?

**Solution:**

1. Change the start to $24$-hour time.

$$9+12=21$$

The start is $21{:}45$ on Saturday.

2. Add the minutes.

$$45+35=80\text{ minutes}$$

$$80\text{ minutes}=1\text{ h }20\text{ min}$$

3. Add the given hours and the extra hour from the minutes.

$$21+3+1=25$$

The result for the calculation is $25$ hours $20$ minutes.

4. Remove $24$ hours for one complete day.

$$25-24=1$$

The recording ends at $01{:}20$, or $1{:}20$ am on Sunday.

5. Check by counting forward. From $9{:}45$ pm to midnight is $2$ hours $15$ minutes.

   From midnight to $1{:}20$ am is $1$ hour $20$ minutes. Together, these make the given $3$ hours $35$ minutes.

### #5: A Place Behind the Given Time

At the same moment, the clock in Town B is $5$ hours $45$ minutes behind the clock in Town A. It is $3{:}20$ am on Tuesday in Town A. What time and day is it in Town B?

**Solution:**

1. Town B is behind Town A. Subtract $5$ hours $45$ minutes from $03{:}20$.

2. Subtracting the offset crosses midnight. Add $24$ hours to the starting value for the calculation.

$$3+24=27$$

We will place the result on the previous day because the actual starting time was early Tuesday.

3. The starting minutes are less than $45$. Borrow $1$ hour.

$$27\text{ h }20\text{ min}=26\text{ h }80\text{ min}$$

4. Subtract minutes and hours.

$$80-45=35\text{ minutes}$$

$$26-5=21\text{ hours}$$

Town B's clock reads $21{:}35$, or $9{:}35$ pm on Monday.

5. Check forward from Town B. From $9{:}35$ pm Monday to midnight is $2$ hours $25$ minutes.

   From midnight to $3{:}20$ am Tuesday is $3$ hours $20$ minutes. Their sum is the given offset of $5$ hours $45$ minutes.

### #6: A Place Ahead of the Given Time

Town D is $4$ hours $50$ minutes ahead of Town C. It is $8{:}35$ pm on Wednesday in Town C. What time and day is it in Town D?

**Solution:**

1. Town D is ahead. Add the offset to Town C's time. Change $8{:}35$ pm to $20{:}35$.

2. Add the minutes and regroup.

$$35+50=85\text{ minutes}$$

$$85\text{ minutes}=1\text{ h }25\text{ min}$$

3. Add the hours, including the extra hour.

$$20+4+1=25$$

4. The hour has passed midnight. Subtract $24$ and move forward one day.

$$25-24=1$$

Town D's time is $01{:}25$, or $1{:}25$ am on Thursday.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### Choose Counting or Subtraction

Count forward when the next exact hour is close. Subtract when both times are already in $24$-hour form. For either method, write down the day if midnight lies between the times.

### Keep Hours and Minutes Separate

Clock minutes use groups of $60$. Do not treat a colon like a decimal point.

For a total number of minutes, divide by $60$. The whole-number quotient gives hours. The remainder gives minutes.

### Check by Reversing the Work

Add an elapsed time back to the start. It should reach the end.

For a time-zone subtraction, add the offset back to your answer. It should reach the given time and day.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Mixing Up Noon and Midnight

> $12{:}00$ pm is noon. $12{:}00$ am is midnight.

Adding $12$ to the hour of a noon time would give a wrong conversion. Keep the hour $12$ for noon. Use $00$ for the midnight hour in $24$-hour time.

### #2: Borrowing the Wrong Amount

> Borrow $60$ minutes from an hour.

Borrowing $100$ minutes treats time like decimal numbers. In Example #2, borrowing changes $18$ minutes to $78$ minutes. It also reduces the hour by $1$.

### #3: Reversing the Offset

> Name the place whose clock you need before adding or subtracting.

If the requested place is behind, subtract. If it is ahead, add. The same stated difference can require either operation when the question switches which place's time is given.

### #4: Dropping the Day

> Crossing midnight changes the day.

A time without its day can leave a time-zone answer incomplete. In Example #5, Town B is still on Monday when Town A has reached Tuesday. Track the day with the hours and minutes.
