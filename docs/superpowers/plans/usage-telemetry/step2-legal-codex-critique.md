1. **Must.** Exact sentence: “For everyone, on by default, the web app's existing speed and error reports also carry anonymous screen counts.”

   On these facts, the authenticated session makes counts identifiable at receipt. Hashing the uid and not logging it do not erase that processing. Stored counts are anonymous only if remaining data cannot identify users. Replacement: “For everyone, on by default, existing speed and error reports also carry screen counts. Pantas processes personal information when receiving these reports through your sign-in session.” [RA 10173, Sections 3(g), 3(j)](https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act/).

2. **Must.** Exact sentence: “The speed and error reports do not rest on consent: they are on for everyone, carry no account ID, and the notice presents them as separate from optional usage sharing.”

   Defaults and labels establish no lawful basis. Document purpose, necessity, alternatives, and balancing in the privacy impact assessment, including children's interests and default-on risks. Check for sensitive education data, which legitimate interest cannot cover. Replacement, if justified, in both notices: “Pantas relies on legitimate interest to diagnose problems and understand screen use. These reports are separate from optional usage sharing.” [NPC Circular 2023-07, Sections 3–8](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-07_Guidelines-on-Legitimate-Interest_13-December-2023.pdf).

3. **Should.** Exact sentence: “Screen counts do not include your account ID or the order in which you moved between screens.”

   First/last markers reveal partial order; the count description is otherwise accurate. Replacement: “Screen counts contain no account ID and show no sequence beyond which screens were first and last.” This avoids an absolute promise. [IRR, Section 18(a)](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/).

4. **Should.** Exact sentence: “Nothing else in the notices changes: the 30-day retention, "on by default", "separate from optional usage sharing", and the Settings switch sentence already cover the counts.”

   Coverage requires verification. Replacement: “Existing retention and Settings wording covers counts only if deletion and collection controls work as described.” Avoid adding promises about aggregation, anonymity, or deletion timing. [IRR, Sections 18–19](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/).

5. **Must.** Exact sentence: “The counts carry no account ID and do not rely on consent, so a new confirmation (C) is not needed and would harm learners; telling guardians once (B) meets the transparency duty with little cost.”

   Usage measurement adds a substantive purpose. A version date alone neither supplies notice nor triggers consent. Replacement: “Choose B before collection, plus a clear in-app update for everyone, including teens, explaining counts and the Settings opt-out. No fresh confirmation is required if legitimate interest is valid. For previously consent-based processing, assess compatibility and reasonable expectations; obtain specific guardian consent for incompatible or unexpected processing. Preserve original consent records.” Guardian email is prudent protection, not a universal requirement for every notice revision. [RA 10173, Section 16(b)](https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act/); [Circular 2023-04, Sections 4–6](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf); [Child transparency guidance, Section 3](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024-COMPENDIUM_26-May-2025.pdf).
