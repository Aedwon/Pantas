What this preview cannot show faithfully

  • It is HTML, not Flutter. Geist is the app's own font, but line breaks, cut-off text and card heights can differ by a word or a line in the app. Every line count and height below is measured in HTML.
  • No motion. Item 4 Option A changes the list shape when a topic is picked. How that moves is not drawn.
  • Sidebar, tab bar and reader top bar come from the code and boards, with simplified icons. Hover, focus and press states are not drawn.
  • Frames hide scroll bars (item 13). Scroll with the wheel or trackpad.
  • Lesson text: the opening section comes from your screenshot. The Examples and Traps text comes from the lesson file and the app may word it more simply. Lesson minutes and order in the topic list are not drawn.
  • The phone is 390 wide, text area only. No browser bars, safe areas or phone top bar.
  • The paper and dark toggle changes only the device frames. Colors come from the app's color files.

What the evidence says about line length

  • Dyson and Haselgrove (2001), International Journal of Human-Computer Studies 54(4): 55 characters per line gave the best comprehension of the lengths they tested on screen. Short lines read slower. I read the abstract summary only, not the full paper.
  • Shaikh (2005), Usability News 7(2): 20 college students read at 35, 55, 75 and 95 characters per line. Reading was fastest at 95. People showed no clear preference. This measured speed, not understanding. I read a summary only.
  • WCAG 2.1, success criterion 1.4.8 (web accessibility rules, the strictest level): text width of no more than 80 characters.

Today's 680 px column is already about 80 characters. The studies I can name stop near 100. For lines longer than that, the evidence is uncertain: I cannot name a study that supports them. None of these studies tested exam-prep lessons.
