## For Aedwon to approve

### What this audit should answer

Ensure full coverage of all mentioned exams: verify that Pantas teaches and provides practice for every required subject and topic, and identify everything missing before full coverage can be claimed. Check where coverage is too thin, misplaced, or combined in a way that makes exam preparation less focused.

Start with what each exam requires. Compare that with the app's subjects, topics, and lesson names first. Only then check the lessons and questions. The previous question audit supplies findings to investigate later; it does not define what the app should teach.

### Exams included

- UPCAT, DCAT, ACET, USTET, and PUPCET.
- DOST undergraduate scholarship examination and SM Foundation scholarship examination.
- CSE Professional and CSE Subprofessional, assessed separately.

For other CETs, identify the common content this corpus supports. Claim full coverage of an additional exam only when it is named and checked separately.

Check language requirements and differences between exam versions where they affect content. Shared subjects do not mean the exams require the same topics or depth. If an exam listed here is not yet offered by the app, assess the corpus against its requirements and report that separately.

### The order of the audit

1. **Establish exam requirements.** Find reliable, current sources for each exam's subjects and topics. Prefer the exam organizer's information. Separate confirmed requirements from reasonable supporting evidence and things that remain unknown. An unpublished topic list cannot become a claim of complete coverage. In the same step, record how each exam's items look (number of choices, item types, wording style, negative questions, and the language of each part) in the exam format profiles, with each entry's source and confidence. Also record each exam's mix of subtests by proportion and its topic weights, but only where a published or verified source states them; otherwise mark them unknown. Mock exam timing and item count are not required.
2. **Check the corpus outline.** Read the existing corpus tab in the Master SSOT Google Doc and compare it with the chosen app corpus. Keep that tab unchanged as a reference. Create a separate audited corpus tab showing every subject, topic, and lesson. Compare the exam requirements with this complete outline and add provisional exam tags to lessons only. Produce a first coverage finding for every exam before reading lessons for depth or investigating individual questions. Identify apparent gaps, unclear names, and content whose relevance needs checking. A matching name is only a lead, not proof that the topic is taught.
3. **Check what the lessons teach.** Read all lessons to confirm the apparent coverage. Check whether required ideas, basic knowledge needed beforehand, and examples are present. Identify topics that are named but barely explained, lessons that combine too much, and missing teaching hidden inside broad lesson names.
4. **Check the practice against the teaching.** Check the topics and skills tested across all question sets. Investigate the previous audit's unresolved teaching and placement findings. For each mismatch, determine whether teaching is missing, another lesson already teaches it, earlier knowledge is reasonably needed, the question is misplaced, or the question's relevance is unsupported. Missing teaching alone is not a reason to remove a question.
5. **Recommend a clearer shared structure.** Propose additions, expansions, moves, and focused lesson splits. State which exams need each part and what remains shared. Teach a shared idea once, with exam-specific additions where needed. Recommend a split only when it improves focus or learning; do not split every concept into a separate lesson.

- A comparison showing each required topic, the lessons that teach it, and the available practice.
- A prioritized list of gaps and question mismatches, with evidence and recommended actions.
- A proposed subject, topic, and lesson outline showing useful splits and shared content.
- A separate audited corpus tab in the Master SSOT Google Doc, showing the complete app corpus and lesson-level exam tags while preserving the original corpus tab.
- A finding on whether the mock exam's subtest mix and topic weights match each exam's published or verified proportions, where those exist, with recommendations only.
- Filled exam format profiles for every named exam, each entry with its source and confidence, or marked unknown where no evidence exists.
- Additional quality improvements supported by what the audit finds, with their costs and priorities.

Teaching and practice quality are judged against the content standards revised on October 4, 2026, including the content checklist and the Filipino-language rules. Older standards are not used.

Keep topic relevance separate from teaching quality and practice quality. A topic may be relevant and taught, but still lack useful practice. Likewise, content may exist in the corpus but be unavailable to students preparing for the relevant exam. Report both cases.

### How coverage will be judged

Judge each required skill separately. A matching topic name or a question count does not establish coverage.

- Teaching explains the idea and how to use it.
- Worked examples show the steps where solving is involved.
- Necessary prior knowledge is taught somewhere in the corpus and clearly connected to the skill.
- Practice tests the skill, including application where the exam requires it.
- Students preparing for that exam can access the relevant teaching and practice.

Use the exam's documented requirements and the actual content to judge the required depth. Report missing teaching, incomplete teaching, missing or incomplete practice, and content unavailable for the relevant exam separately. Where the required depth is unknown, state that uncertainty rather than claim full coverage.

Do not set arbitrary minimum numbers of examples or questions. Explain coverage findings with evidence from the requirements, lessons, and practice. These findings do not prove student mastery or replace a full answer-correctness review.

### Boundaries and approval

This is a coverage and alignment audit. It does not repeat a full review of every answer key or rewrite every lesson. Record accuracy concerns encountered, including unresolved concerns from the previous audit, without claiming a complete correctness review.

The audit produces findings and recommendations. Adding, changing, splitting, moving, or removing app content requires separate approval. No app changes, content publication, or release work are included.

Filling the exam format profiles is also part of the audit's documentation output; the filled profiles are merged to main, where the standards live, after review. Creating and updating the separate audited SSOT tab is part of the audit's documentation output. It does not authorize editing the original corpus tab or changing app content. No Google Doc changes are made during this spec-and-plan stage.

The proposed scope is balanced: complete coverage checking, followed by lesson scope and practice alignment. The earlier 2–4 hour audit estimate is provisional; the implementation plan will refine effort after the scope is approved. No new ongoing audit service is proposed.

This draft is complete when it is saved and available for your review. After you approve it, write the implementation plan based on this spec. This request does not authorize starting the audit. Stop after the plan unless you authorize execution.

### What counts as a finished audit

Every named exam has a source-backed coverage conclusion and a filled format profile. Every subject, topic, lesson, and question set in the chosen corpus has been accounted for. Every required topic has a teaching and practice finding, or an explicit uncertainty. The separate audited SSOT tab shows the complete corpus with supported or explicitly uncertain lesson-level exam tags, distinguishes existing content from proposals, and leaves the original tab unchanged. The prior audit's relevant unresolved findings have an evidence-backed recommendation or a clearly stated remaining question. Recommendations identify the affected exams and preserve shared content without duplication.
