# Pantas Landing Page IA Decision Log

## 31 August 2026 - Narrative backbone

Three landing-page narrative approaches were presented.

### Option A - Coaching journey

The page follows the learner's experience: direction first, then the Pantas study loop, then evidence of adaptation, deeper product capabilities, trust, pricing, and the final CTA.

Cost: this requires more deliberate section choreography and product-artifact storytelling than a conventional feature page, but it best matches Pantas's positioning and the approved Quiet Editorial Product Design direction.

### Option B - Product showcase

The page follows a conventional app-marketing structure with hero, feature-by-feature sections, pricing, and CTA.

Cost: it is simpler to design and implement, but risks becoming a repeated feature catalog and weakening the scene-based editorial direction.

### Option C - Exam-preparation problem first

The page begins with study overload, uncertainty, and weak feedback, then introduces Pantas as the solution.

Cost: it can create a strong marketing argument, but gives more emotional weight to exam anxiety and risks making preparation feel stressful in order to sell the solution.

### Decision

Option A, the coaching journey, is the approved backbone for the landing page.

User comment, verbatim:

> "okay let's go with A"

### Consequence

The next design round should turn the coaching journey into the full page-level content architecture. Features should appear when they become relevant to the learner's story. The page should not contain a generic catch-all "Features" section unless later content work proves one is necessary.

## 1 September 2026 - Hero promise territory

The first hero round considered three message territories:

- Direction-first: "Know what to study next."
- Coaching-first: "Exam prep that learns with you."
- Outcome/process-first: "Learn. Practice. Improve where it matters."

The user then proposed a more specific promise centered on eliminating the work of creating a study roadmap and finding materials. Five concise headline directions were presented:

1. "Stop planning your studying. Start studying."
2. "Never wonder what to study next."
3. "Your studying, already figured out."
4. "Skip the study planning."
5. "Less time figuring out how to study. More time studying."

Cost of option 1: strong and memorable, but frames the value mainly around planning and could understate Pantas's ongoing guidance.

Cost of option 2: direct, broad enough to cover roadmap and material selection, and closely matches the product's core promise. It needs supporting copy to explain how Pantas fulfills it.

Cost of option 3: distinctive and compact, but more abstract and potentially overclaims if read as Pantas doing all studying work for the learner.

Cost of option 4: concise and concrete, but narrower than the full product because it emphasizes planning only.

Cost of option 5: communicates the time-saving benefit clearly, but is longer and less suitable as a large editorial hero line.

### Decision

Option 2 is the approved hero direction, with "again" added at the end:

> **Never wonder what to study next again.**

User comment, verbatim:

> "i think i like 2 best. just add an \"again\" at the end"

### Consequence

The hero should use this line as the current approved headline direction. Supporting copy should explain that Pantas turns the learner's progress into a roadmap and surfaces the relevant lessons, practice, and review. Exact supporting copy, CTA copy, and visual composition remain open for the next design round.
