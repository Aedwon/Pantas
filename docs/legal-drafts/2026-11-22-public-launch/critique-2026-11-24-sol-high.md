## Must-fix

1. **Teen Notice, “When you turn 18.”** “If you are 18 or older and the update succeeds, our servers record that you are 18 or older, your guardian's controls end and the guardian page links stop working.”

   “Record that you are 18 or older” reads as a finding about the student’s age. The server acts on the student’s statement. The timestamp can show when Pantas ended guardian controls and began treating later purchases as the student’s own. Actual age and legal capacity remain unverified. [Data Privacy Act, Sections 11(c) and 16(b)](https://privacy.gov.ph/DATA-PRIVACY-ACT/) · [RA 6809, age of majority](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1989/ra_6809_1989.html)

   **Replace the paragraph with** “When you turn 18, open Settings, then Account, and choose ‘I’m 18 now.’ Enter your current age and choose Continue. If you say you are 18 or older and the update succeeds, we mark your account as an adult account. We rely on what you tell us. Your guardian’s controls end, and their page links stop working. If a guardian confirmed your account, we keep the date and time you made that statement as guardian evidence.”

2. **Teen Notice, legal basis.** “We keep a separate, limited record of their answer, including a decline, as needed for legal claims about permission (Section 13(f)).”

   The sentence still describes the answer record accurately. It is too narrow for the expanded list. **Claim Section 13(f) for the sensitive data in both additions, tied to specific claims.** The single timestamp is proportionate evidence of when Pantas stopped applying a confirmed guardian’s permission. The sharing changes show when the guardian allowed sharing or took back permission, which matters if someone claims Pantas shared data without permission. A current setting cannot establish that history. The NPC permits preparation before a case is filed. Section 13(a) covers the under-18 label for the purposes the guardian approved from confirmation onward. Separate permission covers optional sharing itself. Section 12(f) can cover ordinary personal data in the evidence if Pantas meets the legitimate-interest test. [Data Privacy Act, Sections 12 and 13](https://privacy.gov.ph/DATA-PRIVACY-ACT/) · [NPC Advisory 2024-02, Sections 3–5](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NPC-Advisory-No.-2024-02-Personal-Data-Processing-Based-on-Section-13-f.pdf)

   **Replace the quoted sentence with** “We keep limited guardian evidence as needed for legal claims about account permission, purchases, or optional usage sharing. This includes when a confirmed account switched to adult status based on what you told us. We rely on Section 13(f) for sensitive data in this evidence.”

## Should-fix

3. **All three evidence lists.** The Teen Notice deletion paragraph says “the guardian's answers, permission changes and withdrawal.” The Privacy Policy repeats that phrase under “limited guardian evidence for an under-18 account.” The Teen Notice retention bullet says “any permission change (such as turning optional usage sharing on or off).”

   All three lists contain the same two additions, and “said” properly signals self-report. Only the retention bullet explains which permission changes count. Naming the sharing choice also distinguishes it from withdrawal of permission to use Pantas. In the Privacy Policy, “for an under-18 account” can sound like the account must still be in that group when deleted. [NPC Advisory 2024-03, child-oriented transparency](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Advisory-2024.12.17-Guidelines-on-Child-Oriented-Transparency-w-SGD.pdf) · [Data Privacy Act, Section 16(b)](https://privacy.gov.ph/DATA-PRIVACY-ACT/)

   **Use this wording in both Teen Notice lists** “We keep limited guardian evidence. It includes your guardian’s answers, when they gave or took back permission for optional usage sharing, and any withdrawal of permission to use Pantas. If a guardian confirmed your account, we also keep the date and time you said you were 18 or older.”

   **Use the same wording in the Privacy Policy**, with “the student” and “their account.” Add “See ‘How long we keep data’ in the Notice for teens for the guardian evidence schedule.” Keep the existing checkout details in all three lists.

   The existing period sentences fit the additions. After account deletion, the ten-year schedule for confirmed evidence runs from the newest record. Either addition can supply that date. The four-year schedule still applies to evidence containing only declines. Article 1144 runs from when the claim arises, so it does not establish that every row needs the full scheduled period. [Civil Code, Articles 1144 and 1391](https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html) · [Data Privacy Act, Section 11(e)–(f)](https://privacy.gov.ph/DATA-PRIVACY-ACT/)

## Nit

4. **Teen Notice, “Guardian evidence.”** “For an account a guardian confirmed, we also keep the date you said you are 18 or older.” Use “the date and time you said you were 18 or older,” as above. That describes the stored timestamp and reads naturally when the student returns to the notice later. [Data Privacy Act, Section 16(b)](https://privacy.gov.ph/DATA-PRIVACY-ACT/)

**Verdict.** Section 13(f) is defensible for the single timestamp on a guardian-confirmed account. The biggest risk is justifying ten years from the newest record for every older entry, especially optional-sharing changes.
