### DPO: may the notice name the officer by title and contact only?

Answer: yes. The notice must give the DPO's contact details. It does not have to give the DPO's name.

- **NPC Advisory No. 2017-01, Designation of Data Protection Officers.** The contact details must be published in at least the website, the privacy notice, the privacy policy and the privacy manual. They "should include": title or designation, postal address, a dedicated telephone number, and a dedicated email address. It then says: "The name or names of the DPO or COP need not be published. However, it should be made available upon request by a data subject or the NPC." Read from a mirror of the Advisory (legaldex.com, "Designation of Data Protection Officers"). The NPC's own PDF returned HTTP 403.
- **RA 10173, Section 21(b).** The controller "shall designate an individual or individuals who are accountable" for compliance, and "the identity of the individual(s) so designated shall be made known to any data subject upon request." Read in full at lawphil.net. This is why the notice offers the name on request.
- **IRR, Section 26(a).** The controller must designate a DPO or compliance officer. Section 26(c) lists the DPO's name and contact details among the controller's internal processing records, not the public notice. Read in the Official Gazette copy of the IRR (officialgazette.gov.ph, 2016). Section 34(a)(2)(g) asks a notice for the identity and contact details of the controller or its representative, not of the DPO.
- **NPC Circular 2022-04 (registration).** Registration, which is not public text, takes the DPO's name and contact details and "a unique and official email address specific to the position of DPO" (a summary by Alburo Alburo and Associates; Circular text not readable). dpo@pantas.study fits. Registration is voluntary below the thresholds (250 staff, or sensitive data of 1,000 or more people, or likely risk to data subjects). An entity that does not register must file a sworn declaration. This is the private declaration he already has in hand.
- **NPC Circular 2023-04 (consent).** Only secondary mentions were readable. It does not bear on whether the name is published.
- **Not read:** the NPC's own PDFs of the Advisory, the IRR and both Circulars (privacy.gov.ph returns 403 to automated reads).
- **Open point for the Codex round.** The Advisory says "a dedicated telephone number". The notices use the business phone, which is also the support number. If Codex says a separate line is needed, the notice can drop the phone and keep the email and postal address.
