## Major

**The age-check assurance is too broad.** [teen-notice.md, Why we may use your data](../../../docs/legal-drafts/2026-11-22-public-launch/teen-notice.md:152) says, “The check stays on the device and no record of it reaches our servers.” That is accurate for a **new sign-up that the age check blocks**. Legacy requests sent the age group and sometimes the notice version. A successful “I'm 18 now” update also sends an adult-status result. The local check carries **low** residual legal risk. Pantas determines how its app uses the age, and use in memory can count as processing if the person is identifiable. The stored `18` is the threshold, but the presence of the block conveys that someone entered a lower age. Server receipt is immaterial to that legal test. I found no Philippine ruling resolving this exact design. If the gate processes identifiable data, Circular 2022-04 §§5(A) and 26–27 also raise a registration and notification question. The exemption declaration alone does not resolve it.

**Replace the teen age-check bullet with:** “For a new sign-up, your device checks the age you enter. If you enter an age under 18, setup stops and the device saves only the block. The app does not send your age or the block to our servers. We do not rely on a teen’s agreement for this check.” Use the same scope in “Why we ask your age.” In the Privacy Policy, replace “For an account that a parent or guardian already confirmed, the Notice for teens gives the basis.” with “The Notice for teens explains how we use data from earlier guardian requests and confirmed accounts.” Adult consent under §13(a) is defensible if the check is necessary, the notice is clear before processing, and the affirmative choice is evidenced. The Continue label alone does not establish those facts. ## Verdicts

1. **Name.** Keep two public uses, in the Terms and Privacy Policy. Use direct links for the teen notice and seller disclosure, and retain the BIR-registered name on invoices.
2. **DPO.** Publish the title and dedicated contacts, use a dedicated number, and give the name on request. The proprietor can serve as DPO without stating the dual role in the notices.
3. **Option C.** Fix the cutoff wording. Existing open requests can still be answered. Preserve account closure for false age and the buyer’s legal remedies.
4. **On-device check.** **Low residual risk.** It can be processing by Pantas if the age is identifiable. The no-server sentence is accurate only when scoped to the new blocked flow.

**Biggest legal risk:** Pantas still holds and uses an account-linked under-18 flag while a legacy request is open, before guardian consent. I found no clear Section 13 basis for that interim use. The seven-day limit reduces exposure, and any valid guardian consent applies prospectively. Wording cannot cure the remaining risk under the fixed ruling.
