# Pantas public-launch legal critique

The strongest position is to keep the no-change-of-mind rule, limit voluntary refunds to clearly defined cases, and preserve the remedies Philippine law gives buyers. I would fix the teen age basis, merchant disclosures, payment-record workflow, and several factual claims before publishing.

I reviewed `legal/public-launch-r1` at `bfa090c83`, the baseline texts, the release checklist and decisions, and the relevant source. I retrieved the `main` policy through GitHub and read the local `origin/main` policy at `47ac60058`. The findings distinguish branch behavior from promises that depend on the launch build, provider settings, or business records. Live merchant settings, mail delivery, registrations, and the off-app ledger remain unverified.

## Must-fix

### 1. The teen age check has a weak basis before guardian confirmation

> “Choosing Continue means you agree to this check.”
>
> “For a teen, Continue shows that you read this notice.”
>
> “We run the check because Pantas must apply its minimum age and its parent or guardian rule, and because we need to be able to establish and defend legal claims about whether a person could agree to the Terms (Data Privacy Act, Section 13(f)).”
>
> “We rely on this basis before your guardian answers, including if they decline or do not answer.”
>
> “Your parent or guardian's permission, once given, is a further basis (Section 13(a)).”

**Problem.** Section 13(f) is arguable for **specific, necessary evidence** about capacity, guardian authority, or an actual or anticipated unauthorized purchase. A case need never be filed. The case for ordinary onboarding is weaker. The typed age is used to route the user and remains briefly in memory for Back navigation. It supplies no durable evidence. The server flag survives even after a decline or expired request because checkout uses it operationally. That continuing use is the hardest to justify as necessary for legal claims. I found no Philippine ruling deciding this exact design. [DPA §§3, 11–13](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), [NPC Advisory 2024-02 §§2–5, 7](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024-COMPENDIUM_26-May-2025.pdf).

An account-linked under-18 group very likely remains sensitive information about age. Section 12(f) can cover genuinely ordinary data, such as the adult contact details needed to send the request, after a purpose, necessity, and balancing assessment. It cannot carry the age flag or an identifiable record that itself reveals minority. Section 13(b) is stronger only if a qualifying law or regulation authorizes the particular processing. The Civil Code capacity rules explain Pantas’s purpose. I found no provision in them authorizing this persistent flag. The NPC’s child guidance supports proportionate age assurance and expressly calls for a lawful basis. [NPC Circular 2023-07 §§3–5](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-07_Guidelines-on-Legitimate-Interest_13-December-2023.pdf), [NPC Advisory 2024-03 §2(B)(1) and FAQ 6, 13–15](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024-COMPENDIUM_26-May-2025.pdf), [Civil Code Arts. 1327, 1390–1391](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html).

Opinion 2017-049 supports avoiding reliance on a teen’s own consent. It concerned a teacher searching a student’s phone and gives no age-screen safe harbor. Guardian consent under Section 13(a) works prospectively, for specified uses the authorized adult actually approves. A later confirmation cannot authorize the earlier write. The Continue click also proves an action, with no proof the user read the notice. [NPC Opinion 2017-049, p. 3](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/NPC_AdvisoryOpinionNo._2017-049.pdf), [NPC Circular 2023-04](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf).

**On-device argument.** The best argument is that a transient entry, inaccessible to Pantas and genuinely unlinked to any identifiable person, falls outside personal-data processing by Pantas. Here, Pantas designs the check, controls its use, and stores a result on the device. Section 3 covers use and storage without requiring upload. I would state what stays local and leave the legal characterization out of the notice. The account-linked server record requires its own basis in any event. [DPA §§3(g), (h), (j), (l), 4](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), [age step](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/lib/features/onboarding/presentation/onboarding_age_gate_step.dart#L209), [guardian request write](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/guardianConsent.ts#L240).

**Replacement for the current flow.** Use this in the teen notice and align the age screen with its first paragraph. The second paragraph states the strongest honest position for the current design. Its operational use of the flag remains legally uncertain.

> “Your device uses the age you enter to check whether you can use Pantas and whether a parent or legal guardian must confirm. Pantas keeps the number in memory during setup and does not send it to our servers. Your device saves your age group and the notice version, or a block if you enter an age below 13. Continue runs the check.”
>
> “If you send a guardian request, we record that your account is in the under-18 group before anyone answers. We use this record to route the request and apply the adult purchase requirement. If the request expires or is declined, we remove the request details during cleanup and keep the under-18 group until the account is updated or deleted. We rely on Section 13(f) where sensitive information is necessary to establish or defend a claim about capacity or authorization. We use Section 12(f) for the ordinary contact information needed to send and protect the request. If your parent or legal guardian gives permission, we use it for the uses they approve from then on under Section 13(a).”

**Stronger basis.** Obtain the authorized guardian’s specific permission **before** an identifiable child’s age is processed. Keep any invitation before that point genuinely age-neutral, with no account marker that reveals minority. Require an authenticated adult purchaser even when age is unknown. Keep evidence of completed authorization or purchase only as necessary for the defined claim, with a justified end date. This requires a flow change. Copy alone cannot supply the missing basis for routine pre-confirmation use.

### 2. Guardian confirmation helps, but the purchase box overstates what Pantas can prove

> “At checkout, the buyer must tick "I am the parent or guardian and accept the Terms."”
>
> “Checkout does not work without it.”
>
> “By ticking, the buyer confirms they are the parent or guardian, that they accept these Terms as the buyer, and that they are allowed to use the payment method chosen.”
>
> “If we learn that someone who is not a parent or guardian ticked the box, we may cancel the purchase, refund it and end the access.”

The first two assertions also appear in substance in **Teen Notice 164**. The web server checks the current Terms version on a submitted declaration **only when it already has the minor flag**. It does not establish who ticked the box, require confirmed guardian status at that check, or prove authority over the payment method. The native paywall has a client-side box for known minors. The web evidence record does not cover native purchases. The existing box also says nothing about authority over the payment method. The age recheck uses self-declaration, so the record is evidence of a declaration with limited weight. [Checkout enforcement](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/paymongoCheckout.ts#L38), [paywall box](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/lib/features/settings/presentation/pages/pro_page.dart#L835), [adult recheck](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/guardianConsent.ts#L493).

A parent or legal guardian can act within their authority for the child. The strongest payment contract has the authorized adult buy **in their own name** for the teen. Account permission and assent to each purchase should remain distinct. A contract made by a minor alone is generally voidable and binding until annulled or ratified. Under Article 1391, the four-year period for incapacity runs from when guardianship ceases, ordinarily at 18. Articles 1398–1399 generally require restitution on annulment but limit what the incapacitated party must return to what actually benefited them. Article 1397 also limits a capable party’s ability to invoke the other party’s incapacity. A verified cancel-and-refund option is sound. Treating the checkbox as conclusive or the minor as owing the whole price is risky. [Civil Code Arts. 1327, 1390–1399](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html), [Family Code Art. 220(6)](https://www.lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2021/sep2021/gr_209278_2021.html), [RA 6809 §§1, 3](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1989/ra_6809_1989.html), [RA 8792 §§8, 16, 18](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2000/ra_8792_2000.html).

**Replacement.** “If the student is under 18, a parent or legal guardian with authority to act for them must buy in their own name and use a payment method they are authorized to use. When our web records identify the account as under 18, we require a declaration and record the date, time and Terms version. We may ask for proof if authority is disputed. If we find that a purchase lacked authorization, we may cancel the access and return the payment to the verified payer, subject to applicable law and any payment reversal already made.” Apply the same scope in Teen Notice 45 and 164. Add the payment-method declaration to the checkbox itself if Pantas wants evidence of that specific statement.

### 3. Publish a real seller address and issue Pantas invoices

> “Pantas is run by [registered business name, as on the DTI certificate; the live texts use "Pantas Online Tutorial Services"], a sole proprietorship owned by Aerol Dwayne Daños Balayon.”
>
> “Address: [address policy: the live texts show "Poblacion, Norala, South Cotabato, Philippines"; confirm whether to keep this or add a full postal address]”
>
> “Phone: [business phone]”
>
> “The price is the amount in Philippine pesos shown on the Pro screen and at checkout when you pay.”
>
> “PayMongo may send you a receipt.”

Pantas is an **e-retailer** when it sells through its own app or site. Put the verified proprietor and trade name, the physical place-of-business address, phone, and valid email on the homepage. A Terms page alone is insufficient. “Poblacion, Norala, South Cotabato” identifies a barangay and municipality. It ordinarily fails to identify the actual place of business. I found no rule requiring a street number where none exists. Use the full locatable address on the business registration, with a sitio, road, building, or equivalent identifier as applicable. The statute calls for proof to the Bureau. It does not require a public government ID or a separate TIN line in the Terms. BIR proof-of-registration display is a separate obligation. [RA 11967 §§3, 23(a), (f), (h)–(i)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902), [JAO 24-03 §34](https://ecommerce.dti.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Joint-Administrative-Order-No.-24-03.pdf), [BIR RR 7-2024 §§5(H), 6](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-%202024.pdf), [BIR RMC 38-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2038-2026%20Digest.pdf).

Display the plan, coverage, duration or sitting, total price in pesos including applicable taxes and seller charges, payment terms, and refund and complaint policies **before** handing the buyer to PayMongo. Put the material terms beside the purchase action and make the full policy readily accessible there. The observed checkout request supplies a price, item name, payment methods, and transaction metadata. It does not establish that PayMongo shows Pantas’s policies or issues a Pantas tax invoice. ITA §23(h) and IRR §34(i) cover all sales. The general NIRC invoicing threshold does not excuse the separate ITA duty. A micro-taxpayer exemption from mandatory structured electronic invoicing, if applicable, still leaves the ordinary invoice duty. [RA 7394 Arts. 50, 81, 83](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3302), [JAO 22-01 §8.3](https://www.aseanconsumer.org/file/post_image/JAO%20NO.%2022-01.Guidelines%20on%20Online%20Businesses.pdf), [RA 11976, amended NIRC §237](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html), [BIR RR 26-2025](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2026-2025%20Digest.pdf), [checkout request](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/paymongoClient.ts#L28).

**Replacement.** “Aerol Dwayne Daños Balayon operates Pantas as [verified registered trade name]. Our place of business is [full registered business address]. For purchases and complaints, contact support@pantas.study or [business phone]. The checkout price is the total amount Pantas charges in Philippine pesos, including applicable taxes and seller charges. Pantas issues a sales invoice for each web purchase.” Use the invoice sentence once the workflow exists. Display the applicable BIR registration proof and verify the business’s tax classification before making a VAT statement. Separately, DTI’s signed DAO 25-12 and its public FAQ give conflicting signals on whether Trustmark registration is mandatory. I would treat the signed order as the stronger authority pending confirmation of any later controlling issuance. [DTI DAO 25-12](https://trustmark.dti.gov.ph/img/%5BCTC%5D%2BDAO%2BNo.%2B25-12.pdf), [DTI Trustmark FAQ](https://trustmark.dti.gov.ph/faqs).

### 4. Keep the voluntary refund limits. Put statutory remedies first.

> “Ask within 30 days of the payment.”
>
> “We do not give partial refunds for unused time.”
>
> “A period that has started, or a Season Pass whose exam sitting has passed, is outside this policy, except as the list above says.”
>
> “We do not refund app store payments: ask the store.”
>
> “A refund that the law requires for a defective service does not shorten your access.”

I found no general cooling-off or change-of-mind right under the ITA or Consumer Act for conforming prepaid study access bought through ordinary online checkout. The ITA’s return-of-original-goods provision does not create a right to return consumed digital study time. The existing legal savings sentence helps. Put it before the restrictions so buyers can see immediately that defective, misdescribed, or undelivered service is different. Article 102 gives the consumer a choice of remedies for service imperfections, including reperformance, reimbursement with monetary updating and possible damages, or a proportionate price reduction. The draft’s “fix it or refund” language should not suggest that Pantas always chooses. Article 169 generally gives Consumer Act claims two years under its own accrual rules, including discovery for hidden defects. The 30 days can govern the voluntary policy. [RA 7394 Arts. 50, 52, 102, 106, 167, 169](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3302), [RA 11967 §§20, 23, 25](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902), [DTI refunds guidance](https://fairtrade.dti.gov.ph/services/complaints-handling/).

The last quoted sentence gives away access even after a full refund. The code also contradicts it. A full refund revokes the affected grant. A partial refund shortens an unexpired grant proportionately. The handler does not distinguish statutory refunds from goodwill refunds. A statutory price reduction may require continued service in a particular case, so that case needs an operational way to honor the remedy. A store’s billing channel also does not erase Pantas’s duties for its own service. [Refund implementation](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/paymongoGrants.ts#L39).

**Replacement.** “Philippine law gives you remedies if the service is defective, differs from what we promised, or is not delivered. Those rights apply even after access starts or ends and are separate from our 30-day voluntary refund policy. For access delivered as promised, we do not refund a change of mind, a change of exam, an exam result, or unused time. Ask for a voluntary refund within 30 days of payment. A full cancellation refund ends the access from that payment. For a partial refund, access may change as allowed by law and the terms of the remedy. For store billing, use the store’s refund process. Contact us about Pantas service problems or any remedy you have against us.”

### 5. QR Ph and failed-payment language can mislead buyers

> “PayMongo cannot refund these.”
>
> “We refund by bank transfer or e-wallet transfer.”
>
> “If PayMongo shows a payment as failed, you were not charged.”
>
> “This is usually at once.”

PayMongo’s current documentation lists QR Ph refunds through its dashboard and API. The Pantas runbook records a test-mode refusal and a manual **bank transfer** process. I could not verify eligibility on Pantas’s live merchant account or a separate e-wallet payout procedure. Manual transfer is a defensible fallback when the original route is unavailable and the verified payer agrees. Return the full amount due, avoid duplicate payouts, and check that the recipient is the payer or an authorized recipient. The webhook grants access only after a verified paid event. It provides no basis for a general guarantee about bank debits, pending holds, or typical activation time. [PayMongo refund documentation](https://docs.paymongo.com/docs/payment-acceptance-refunds), [Pantas refund runbook](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/docs/OPERATIONS.md#L156), [payment verification](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/paymongoWebhook.ts#L64), [RA 7394 Arts. 50, 102](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3302).

**Replacement.** “We use the original payment route when it is available. If we cannot return a QR Ph payment that way, we will arrange a bank transfer with the verified payer. We ask only for the details needed to verify, send, and document the refund. Pro starts after we verify that payment succeeded. If checkout reports failure but your bank or wallet shows a debit or hold, contact us before paying again.”

### 6. M10 is viable only if the off-app records actually exist

> “Deleting your account removes its study records and its payment records in our app.”
>
> “Tax books, receipts and refund records: five years, counted as tax law requires from the filing deadline of the related tax return.”
>
> “The business keeps these outside the app.”
>
> “They are kept even if you delete your account, because the law requires them.”

The five years begin **the day after the filing deadline**, or **on the actual filing date if the return was filed late**, for the taxable year in which the last entry was made in the books. Material records for a pending tax protest or tax-credit or refund claim must remain until final resolution, even beyond five years. The rule covers books, subsidiary books, invoices, receipts, vouchers, returns, and other source documents supporting the entries. It does not require the operational Firestore database to be the archive. If Firestore is the only source of a required record, preserve that record in the proper format before deletion. A dashboard screenshot or a ledger total alone may leave the underlying source documents missing. [RA 11976, amended NIRC §235](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html), [BIR RR 7-2024 §4(A)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-%202024.pdf).

The code erases the ordinary account-linked grants, payment index, orders, checkouts, and follow-ups. It does **not** erase all payment-related records. It keeps guardian purchase evidence where present, including the product and reference. Payment audits, event-deduplication records, some refund and dispute records keyed to payment ID, and detached native pass records can also survive. The deletion creates a permanent record keyed to the former account ID and retires the public ID. The README’s “keeps only” list is incomplete. The code performs no export into a tax archive. The manual refund runbook starts from a payment index that deletion removes, so a late request needs reconciliation from other records. [Deletion implementation](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/deleteUserData.ts#L35), [refund and dispute records](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/paymongoGrants.ts#L242), [native pass records](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/functions/src/revenuecatPasses.ts#L173), [M10 draft](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/docs/legal-drafts/2026-11-22-public-launch/README.md#L34).

Deleting redundant app copies is consistent with DPA minimization. Losing the only required source record is a tax problem. Losing evidence needed for refunds or disputes also creates consumer risk. Keep the minimum records needed for tax compliance under Section 12(c). Use a qualifying Section 13 basis where sensitive information is involved, and reserve legal holds for records necessary to a defined claim. The public text should describe the archive only after the business has a working process for invoices, sales, settlements, and refund evidence. [DPA §§11–13, 16(e)](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html).

**Replacement.** “When account deletion finishes, we remove the app’s account-linked study records and payment history. We retain limited records needed to prevent a deleted account from reopening, record security events, verify past transactions or guardian authorization, and meet legal duties. We keep the business’s required accounting records separately for five years, starting the day after the applicable filing deadline, or on the actual filing date if the return was late, for the tax year of the last entry in the books. We keep relevant records longer when the law requires it.”

### 7. Electronic acceptance is valid. Continued use is weak evidence for material changes.

> “By creating an account or using Pantas, you agree to these Terms, the Privacy Policy and, if you are under 18, the Notice for teens.”
>
> “We tell you in the app before a material change takes effect, at least 14 days ahead unless the law or security requires sooner.”
>
> “If you keep using Pantas after the change takes effect, you accept it, except where the law requires your new permission.”

Electronic acceptance works under RA 8792. The law still requires actual assent, capacity, attribution, and reliable evidence. The setup gate looks for an acceptance timestamp. Changing the document version alone does not require an existing adult to accept again. The setup wording has its own version, and the repo has no general 14-day publication and reacceptance mechanism. The notices also cannot serve as bundled consent to everything disclosed. A material unilateral change to an existing contract risks Article 1308. Continued use after prominent notice is more defensible for minor prospective changes, especially to Free access. It is weak for new liability, venue, payment, or other significant terms. Fourteen days is a contractual commitment, with no general 14-day requirement found for these Terms. [RA 8792 §§7–9, 12, 16, 18](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2000/ra_8792_2000.html), [Rules on Electronic Evidence, Rules 4–5](https://lawphil.net/courts/supreme/am/am_01-7-01_sc_2001.html), [Civil Code Arts. 1306, 1308, 1319](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html), [Supreme Court on unilateral changes under Art. 1308](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2018/jun2018/gr_192934_2018.html), [onboarding gate](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/lib/features/onboarding/application/onboarding_flow.dart#L153).

**Replacement.** “You accept these Terms by confirming them electronically. The Privacy Policy and Notice for teens explain how we handle data. We ask separately for permission where required. We give advance notice of material changes. We ask you, or the adult who contracts for a teen, to accept material changes before they apply to your agreement. A new purchase uses the Terms shown before payment. The terms of an existing paid period continue to apply to that purchase.” Keep the 14-day promise only if Pantas intends to operate it.

### 8. Preserve the statutory complaint process without adding a second appeal

> “We aim to reply within [5] business days.”
>
> “Tell us at support@pantas.study and we will review the decision again.”
>
> “If you have a problem, please email support@pantas.study first.”
>
> “You may always complain to the Department of Trade and Industry, the National Privacy Commission or any other government office.”

For covered ITA complaints, internal redress is a statutory first step and is deemed exhausted after **seven calendar days** if unresolved. Five business days can run longer. A second review should be discretionary, with no reset of the statutory clock. Requiring account-email access, a particular subject line, or an invoice in every case could frustrate redress after deletion or where the adult paid for a teen. Preserve independent NPC procedures and bank dispute rights. [RA 11967 §§23(i), 24](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902), [JAO 24-03 §§34(j), 38](https://ecommerce.dti.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Joint-Administrative-Order-No.-24-03.pdf), [BSP Memorandum M-2024-030](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances/2024/M-2024-030.pdf).

**Replacement.** “Send purchase complaints to support@pantas.study. Include enough information for us to identify the payment. We may ask for more information to verify your claim. If a complaint covered by the Internet Transactions Act remains unresolved after seven calendar days from filing with us, our internal process is deemed exhausted. You may then use the applicable external remedies. You may also exercise any separate rights you have with your payment provider or the National Privacy Commission.”

### 9. Screen counts are missing from both notices

> “To find slow screens and errors, the web app sends short technical reports.”

The following field list omits screen names, counts, and first and last screen flags. The README says the flag was off. The launch log records multiple production builds with `SCREEN_COUNTS=true`, including the November 20 build. The branch supports those fields and waits until a later visit after the device shows the notice. Optional usage sharing is a separate stream. This omission matters even though the report body has no account ID field, since the request uses the sign-in session. [Production build record](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/docs/superpowers/decisions/2026-10-19-pwa-launch-log.md#L4052), [approved disclosure](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/docs/superpowers/plans/usage-telemetry/step2-legal-draft.md), [DPA §§11, 12(f), 16](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html).

**Replacement.** “We use these reports to find slow screens and errors and see which screens people use. After the app shows you a notice on this device, reports from later visits may include which screens you opened, how often, and which you opened first or last. We use these details to improve the app. You can stop future reports from this device in Settings.” Add the purpose to the legitimate-interest section and use the approved implementation-specific wording if the launch flag stays on.

### 10. The privacy notices leave material gaps in consent, recipients, registration, and change notices

- **Core study consent.** Privacy 117 says, “Study records: your specific permission (Section 13(a)), given at setup when you accept the Terms and Privacy Policy.” The setup control combines the Terms, policy, and study-data language. The wording does mention study records, which helps. A separately identified authorization is stronger for sensitive education data. A general policy acknowledgment cannot authorize additional purposes. For adults, the age click can support Section 13(a) if it is informed, specific, recorded, and confined to the necessary check. The mandatory button and the notice-version marker alone do not settle that question. **Use after aligning the controls:** “We ask for your permission to use your answers, results and study records to provide study features. For a teen, a parent or legal guardian gives that permission. Optional usage sharing has a separate choice.” [DPA §§3(b), 12–13](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), [NPC Circular 2023-04](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf), [setup control](https://github.com/Aedwon/Pantas/blob/bfa090c83/lib/features/onboarding/presentation/onboarding_consent_step.dart#L175).
- **Purposes and bases.** “Account and payment records: we need them to provide the service you asked for (Section 12(b)).” This works for ordinary data necessary to contract with **that data subject**. It needs qualification where the adult is the buyer and the teen is a separate data subject. “Messages to us: we need them to answer you.” This gives a purpose but no non-consent basis. The Teen Notice omits the equivalent line. Neither notice adequately maps public sharing, off-app refund transfers, or pre-confirmation guardian data to distinct purposes and bases. **Use:** “We use the buyer’s payment details to complete the purchase and resolve payment problems. We use ordinary account and contact data as needed to provide the requested service, answer requests, and prevent fraud. Where we rely on legitimate interests, we use only the data needed for those purposes. If you choose to publish a share card where that feature is available, we use your separate permission to publish it.” Give the guardian a short notice in the first email about where Pantas got their details, how it uses them, and how to object. Identify the specific Section 13 basis wherever a record reveals age or education. [DPA §§11–13, 16](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), [DPA IRR §34(a)](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/), [JAO 22-01 §10](https://www.aseanconsumer.org/file/post_image/JAO%20NO.%2022-01.Guidelines%20on%20Online%20Businesses.pdf).
- **Provider roles.** “These providers act for us under their own terms.” This overgeneralizes. Google, Cloudflare, Resend, and RevenueCat can provide services on Pantas’s instructions. Roles depend on the activity and actual agreements. PayMongo, banks, and app stores also process data for their own payment, security, and legal purposes. The checkout sends PayMongo the account ID, product, and order reference. ## Should-fix

### Terms enforceability and concessions

1. **Liability cap.** “For other claims arising from one event or related events, our total money liability is limited to the greater of ₱1,000 or the amount you paid Pantas for the affected account in the 12 months before the first event.” “This applies whether the claim is in contract, negligence or otherwise.” Keep the existing carve-outs. Limit the cap to residual, good-faith contract damages that may lawfully be capped. Articles 1170–1172 leave liability for fraud and negligence actionable and make advance waiver of future fraud void. Article 2201 allows foreseeable loss in good-faith breach and a broader measure for fraud or bad faith. The Consumer Act bars reducing statutory indemnity, and the ITA places primary liability on the seller. The ₱1,000 floor is voluntary. I found no authority approving this amount or formula for an app. A cap limits recovery. Articles 2226–2227 principally concern agreed damages payable on breach, which is closer to the forfeiture below. **Use:** “For good-faith breach of contract, these limits apply only to damages that Philippine law allows us to limit. We exclude indirect losses to that extent. For related events, the total cap is the greater of ₱1,000 or the amount paid to Pantas for the affected service during the preceding 12 months. All exceptions in this section apply.” [Civil Code Arts. 1170–1173, 1306, 1409, 2201, 2226–2227](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html), [RA 7394 Arts. 52, 106, 167](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3302), [RA 11967 §25](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902).
2. **Norala venue.** “For other civil disputes, the parties agree to bring them only in the proper courts serving Norala, South Cotabato.” “Only” does the work of making venue exclusive. A written, pre-suit agreement can do that. The Supreme Court enforced an adhesion clause in *Tecson* and rejected an oppressive one in *Sweet Lines*. A distant forum for a small consumer claim invites scrutiny even if the claim is pleaded under ordinary contract law. Clarify that the exception covers civil claims arising from the consumer purchase or use of Pantas. **Use:** “Consumer claims may be filed in any venue the applicable law and court rules allow. For non-consumer civil disputes, where the rules permit exclusive venue, the parties agree to the courts with jurisdiction serving Norala, South Cotabato.” [Rule 4 §§2, 4](https://lawphil.net/courts/rules/am_19-10-20-sc_2019.html), [*Pilipino Telephone Corp. v. Tecson*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46189), [*Sweet Lines v. Teves*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1978/may1978/gr_37750_1978.html).
3. **Forfeiture.** “If we close your account for serious misuse, we do not refund unused paid time, unless the law requires a refund.” The right to suspend or terminate for a documented material breach is stronger than a right to keep the entire unused balance. Automatic forfeiture is vulnerable as a penalty, particularly on a newly purchased long plan. A court can reduce an iniquitous penalty or liquidated amount. Define the serious grounds and tie any offset to loss Pantas can substantiate. **Use:** “We may end access for a serious breach of these Terms. We may retain payment for access already supplied. Where Philippine law allows, we may deduct proven loss caused by the breach from an unused balance otherwise payable. Any remaining amount due will be refunded.” [Civil Code Arts. 1191, 1226, 1229, 1306, 2226–2227](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html), [Supreme Court, G.R. No. 238041](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2022/feb2022/gr_238041_2022.html), [RA 7394 Art. 52](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3302).
4. **User indemnity.** “If you break these Terms and this causes us loss, we may ask you to cover it, to the extent the law allows.” This is largely defensible as a claim for proven loss. Tighten causation and avoid language that implies automatic collection, uncapped third-party liability, or a personal payment obligation arising from a teen’s click. **Use:** “If you materially breach these Terms, we may recover the loss you caused and that we can prove, to the extent Philippine law allows.” [Civil Code Arts. 1170, 1306, 2200–2201](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html).
5. **Force majeure.** “We are not responsible for delay or failure caused by events outside our control, such as power or internet outages, failures of payment providers, banks, app stores or hosting providers, or government action.” “Those companies have their own terms and fees, and we are not responsible for them.” A vendor outage alone is insufficient. Article 1174 looks at foreseeability or avoidability, inability to perform normally, and Pantas’s own contribution to the loss. It does not erase mandatory remedies for a service buyers paid for. **Use:** “To the extent Philippine law allows, we are excused from delay or failure caused by an event beyond our reasonable control that we could not foresee or avoid and that prevents normal performance. This applies only to the extent the event caused the failure. Your statutory remedies still apply.” [Civil Code Arts. 1170–1174](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html), [*Sicam v. Jorge*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2007/aug2007/gr_159617_2007.html), [RA 11967 §§20, 23, 25](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902).
6. **Bank disputes.** “If you dispute a payment with your bank, card issuer or e-wallet, we may suspend the access that payment gave while the dispute is open.” “If the dispute is resolved in our favor, the access returns and ends on its original date.” “Please contact us before you file a dispute: we can often fix a problem faster.” The code acts on a PayMongo dispute event. It does not see every complaint lodged with a bank. It restores only the relevant unrefunded, eligible grant, and a short plan can expire during the dispute. A blanket suspension, followed by keeping the money after a long interruption, risks an unfairness or nonperformance claim on some facts. I found no Philippine ruling requiring a guaranteed extension for every dispute. **Use:** “When our payment provider reports a formal dispute, we may pause access funded by that payment while it is reviewed. If the payment is reversed, that access ends. If the payment stands and the affected access is still eligible, we restore any time remaining through its original end date, subject to remedies required by law. You can contact us for help and may report an unauthorized payment directly to your bank or wallet.” Reserve additional restrictions for evidence of fraud or abuse. 7. **No cash value.** “Pro access has no cash value.” Valid as a non-cash-out rule. It could be read to deny restitution or refund rights. **Use:** “Pro access is personal and cannot be transferred or exchanged for cash. Refund rights still apply.” [Civil Code Art. 1306](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html), [RA 11967 §20](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902).
8. **Feature changes and closure.** “We do not remove content you already paid for before your paid access ends, unless the law, a licensor, or a security or safety issue requires it.” “If we permanently end a Pro feature, or the whole service, while you have paid access, we refund the unused part of your payment.” The first line freezes individual content and the second arguably promises a whole-plan prorated refund for removing any feature. Neither is necessary. Preserve material advertised benefits and remedies. 9. **Voluntary duplicate, wrong-price and exam-date promises.** “it was an extra payment for a period or pass that has not started, such as a payment made twice;” “You may then keep the purchase at the correct price or get a full refund.” “If an exam body moves or cancels a sitting, tell us at support@pantas.study and we will review your Season Pass in good faith.” The code queues a second ordinary plan as paid time. Third-party materials belong to their respective owners.” Preserve rights applicable under law in the copying and reverse-engineering restrictions. [DPA §16(b)](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), [Intellectual Property Code §§175–176, 185, 189](https://ilaw.ipophil.gov.ph/pluginfile.php/146/mod_resource/content/2/Philippine%20IP%20Code.pdf).

The code supports weekly duration, Manila-time calendar clamping for Monthly and Yearly, a fixed-sitting pass, and a seven-day web trial surfaced on Monthly without payment details. **Single biggest legal risk:** Pantas creates and keeps an account-linked sensitive age classification before it has valid guardian permission, including after the guardian declines or never answers. Its ongoing purpose is routine checkout control. Section 13(f) is materially stronger for specific evidence needed in a capacity or authorization dispute than for that continuing operational use. A clearer notice makes the practice transparent. A prior lawful basis or a different workflow addresses the underlying risk.
