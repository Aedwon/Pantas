Passage
  1. Mangroves grow where rivers meet the sea. Their roots hold the mud in place, so the shore wears away more slowly.
  2. Fishers in Bohol noticed that catches rose in the years after a mangrove belt was replanted. Young fish hide among the roots until they are large enough to swim out.
  3. Not every planting succeeds. Seedlings set too low on the shore drown at high tide, and some planted species never belonged on that coast.
  4. The lesson most groups learned is simple: plant the right species at the right height, and protect the belt while it grows.

Adapted from a coastal management report, 2021
