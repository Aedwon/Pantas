## Core Concept

A shape's **boundary** is its outline. Its **interior** is the space inside that outline. A point on the boundary is different from a point in the interior or outside the shape.

Use the center of a drawn dot to locate the point. If that center is on a boundary, record “on” for that shape. If a thick line or blurry dot hides the center's position, the picture may not give enough information to decide.

## Examples

### #1: A Dot on a Boundary

A horizontal rectangle crosses a circle's right side. The rectangle's top and bottom edges pass above and below the circle's rightmost point. Dot P is centered exactly on that rightmost point, well inside the rectangle.

Dot Q is slightly to P's left, inside both shapes. Dot R is slightly to P's right, outside the circle but inside the rectangle. What record belongs to each dot?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: outline circle with a horizontal rectangle crossing its right side, rectangle left edge left of circle's rightmost point and right edge beyond it; three small dots on a horizontal line, Q just left of circle's rightmost point, P centered exactly at that point, R just right, all well inside rectangle; label P Q R without membership labels -->

**Solution:**

1. Check P against the circle. Its center is on the outline, so write “circle on.” Against the rectangle, write “rectangle in.”
2. Check Q. Its center is to the inside of the circle's outline and inside the rectangle. Write “circle in, rectangle in.”
3. Check R. Its center is beyond the circle's outline but still inside the rectangle. Write “circle out, rectangle in.”
4. To match P elsewhere, find a point on the circle's boundary that lies inside the rectangle. A nearby point just inside the circle would match Q's record instead.

If the center is unclear, do not assume it is inside.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Forcing One Answer

> Check each choice against the stated placement rule.

If several choices allow the same record, membership alone does not give a unique answer. Check for another stated condition or an unclear boundary point. Do not invent an extra rule about the shapes' overall arrangement.
