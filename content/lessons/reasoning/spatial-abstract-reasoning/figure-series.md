## Core Concept

A **figure series** shows figures in an order and asks what comes next or what belongs in a blank. Look for a change that keeps working from one figure to the next.

Track one feature at a time:

- number of sides or parts;
- which way the figure points;
- shading or fill;
- size;
- position;
- number of dots or other marks.

A feature can repeat, alternate, increase, decrease, move, or turn. More than one feature can change at the same time.

Check your rule against every change that is shown. If a rule works only once, it is not enough.

An **interleaved pattern** has two separate patterns mixed together. One pattern can use the first, third, fifth, and later odd positions. The other can use the second, fourth, sixth, and later even positions.

When the changing feature is a count, write the counts as a number sequence. For example, figures with $2$, $4$, and $6$ dots can be checked as the sequence $2, 4, 6$. This same skill helps when you move from visual counts to numerical sequence reasoning.

## Examples

### #1: Constant Rotation

A marked line points up, then upper-right, then right. If it keeps turning by the same amount in the same direction, which way should it point next?

<!-- FIGURE: {"kind": "figure-series", "items": [{"form": "square", "line": 0}, {"form": "square", "line": 45}, {"form": "square", "line": 90}], "blank": true, "semanticsLabel": "A series of three squares with rotating lines at 0, 45, and 90 degrees, followed by an empty frame."} -->

**Solution:**

The line turns $45^\circ$ clockwise each step. The next line should point lower-right.

### #2: Increasing Number of Sides

A triangle is followed by a square, then a pentagon. What is the next figure if the side-count pattern continues?

<!-- FIGURE: {"kind": "figure-series", "items": [{"form": "triangle"}, {"form": "square"}, {"form": "pentagon"}], "blank": true, "semanticsLabel": "A series of shapes with increasing sides: triangle, square, and pentagon, followed by an empty frame."} -->

**Solution:**

The side counts are $3, 4, 5$. The next count is $6$, so the next figure is a hexagon.

### #3: Alternating Fill

A black circle is followed by a white circle, then a black circle. What should come next if the fill keeps alternating?

<!-- FIGURE: {"kind": "figure-series", "items": [{"form": "circle", "fill": "black"}, {"form": "circle", "fill": "white"}, {"form": "circle", "fill": "black"}], "blank": true, "semanticsLabel": "A series of three circles alternating between black and white, followed by an empty frame."} -->

**Solution:**

The fill changes black, white, black. The next figure should be a white circle.

### #4: Two Features and Answer Choices

The figures use four sizes in this order: small, medium, large, extra large. The series is a small black triangle, a medium white triangle, then a large black triangle. Which choice comes next?

- A. extra-large white triangle
- B. extra-large black triangle
- C. large white triangle
- D. small white triangle

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: a figure series showing a small black triangle, a medium white triangle, a large black triangle, then a blank, with choices A extra-large white triangle, B extra-large black triangle, C large white triangle, and D small white triangle -->

**Solution:**

1. Track size. It moves small, medium, large, so the next defined size is extra large.
2. Track fill. It alternates black, white, black, so the next fill is white.
3. Choice A keeps both rules.

The choices help confirm the rule because the next size is already defined in the question.

### #5: Changing Position

A dot appears in the top-left corner of a square, then the top-right corner, then the bottom-right corner, then the bottom-left corner. If the dot keeps moving to the next corner clockwise, where should it appear next?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: five square frames in a row, with dots at top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left, then a blank fifth square -->

**Solution:**

The dot moves around the corners in a clockwise loop. After the bottom-left corner, it returns to the top-left corner.

### #6: Interleaved Rules

A series has these figures in order: a black circle with $1$ dot, a white square, a black circle with $2$ dots, a black square, a black circle with $3$ dots, then a blank. What should the blank show?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: six positions in one row, black circle with 1 dot, white square, black circle with 2 dots, black square, black circle with 3 dots, then a blank -->

**Solution:**

1. Look at the odd positions. They are black circles with $1$, $2$, and $3$ dots.
2. Look at the even positions. They are squares whose fill alternates white, black, then white.
3. The blank is an even position, so it should show a white square.

This series does not use one identical change between every pair of figures. Splitting the odd and even positions reveals the two rules.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Assuming One Short Series Has Only One Possible Description

> Test the simplest rule that fits every shown change and the answer choices.

A short series can sometimes be described in more than one way. Do not choose a rule that fails part of what is shown.

### #2: Tracking Only One Feature

> Size, fill, count, position, and direction can change at the same time.

A choice may match one feature but break another. Check each changing feature before choosing.

### #3: Guessing a Rotation from One Change

> Confirm both the amount of the turn and its direction.

One turn does not prove a full rotation rule. Use the other shown figures to check that the same turn continues.

### #4: Inventing a Size That Was Never Defined

> Do not create a new size level unless the figures or choices establish it.

If the series shows small, medium, and large only, an undefined next label may not be safe. Example $4$ works because the question defines the next size as extra large.

### #5: Missing an Interleaved Pattern

> If one rule will not fit every step, compare alternating positions.

Check the first, third, and fifth positions together, then the second, fourth, and sixth. Two simpler patterns may be mixed into one series.
