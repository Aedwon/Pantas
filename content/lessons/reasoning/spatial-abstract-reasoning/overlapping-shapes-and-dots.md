## Core Concept

When shapes **overlap**, they share some space. Their outlines split the drawing into **regions**, or separate areas. A dot-placement question asks where a dot could go in another drawing while keeping the same inside-and-outside pattern.

A shape's **boundary** is its outline. Its **interior** is the space inside that outline. A point on the boundary is different from a point in the interior or outside the shape.

A dot's **membership set** lists the shapes whose interiors contain its center. If the center is inside a circle and a rectangle but outside a triangle, record: circle in, rectangle in, triangle out. The outside check matters as much as the inside checks.

Use the center of a drawn dot to locate the point. If that center is on a boundary, record “on” for that shape. If a thick line or blurry dot hides the center's position, the picture may not give enough information to decide.

Check the starting dot against each named shape. Look for the same record in each choice. Shape positions and sizes can change.

With two dots, make two separate records. A choice must allow both placements in the same diagram. If a surrounding frame counts as a shape in the question, include it in each record too.

A region can be missing. For example, when a circle lies completely inside a triangle, there is no circle-interior space outside the triangle. Do not assume a needed region exists because both shapes are drawn.

## Examples

### #1: Labeling Regions of Two Shapes

Two equal circles, L and R, overlap side by side. L is on the left and R is on the right. Neither contains the other, and their outlines cross at two points.

Label the regions without labeling the outlines themselves. How can you name each region by the circles that contain it?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: two equal overlapping outline circles labeled L on the left and R on the right, centers separated by one radius, inside a larger viewing area that is not a tested shape; leave the left crescent, central lens, right crescent, and surrounding space unshaded and unlabeled -->

**Solution:**

1. Start in the left crescent, the curved part beside the overlap. It is inside L and outside R. Label it “L in, R out.”
2. Move into the middle lens, the shared area between the crossing points. It is inside both circles. Label it “L in, R in.”
3. Check the right crescent. It is outside L and inside R. Label it “L out, R in.”
4. Check the space beyond both circles. Label it “L out, R out.”

These are the $4$ membership regions in this drawing. A dot in the left crescent has the membership set L.

### #2: Labeling Regions of Three Shapes

Three equal circles are labeled T, L, and R. Their centers form an upright equilateral triangle, which has equal sides. T's center is at the top, L's at the lower left, and R's at the lower right.

A **radius** is the distance from a circle's center to its boundary. In this arrangement, the distance between neighboring centers equals one radius. This arrangement leaves a middle area shared by the three circles. Name the regions by membership, leaving the outlines aside.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: three equal outline circles T top, L lower left, R lower right, with centers at the vertices of an upright equilateral triangle whose side equals the common radius; show a clear central triple overlap and leave all regions unlabeled, with space beyond the circles -->

**Solution:**

1. Check the top cap, the part above both lower circles. Its label is “T in, L out, R out.” The far lower-left cap is “T out, L in, R out.” The far lower-right cap is “T out, L out, R in.”
2. Check the upper-left overlap beside the middle area. It is in T and L but outside R. Write “T in, L in, R out.”
3. Check the upper-right overlap beside the middle. Write “T in, L out, R in.” The bottom overlap below the middle is “T out, L in, R in.”
4. Check the middle area. It belongs to the interiors of the three circles: “T in, L in, R in.”
5. Finish with the space beyond the circles. Its label is “T out, L out, R out.”

This arrangement has $8$ membership regions. Other arrangements can have fewer. If the circles are separate, for instance, the shared regions are absent.

### #3: Finding a Match After the Shapes Move

A wide rectangle overlaps a large triangle. A small circle lies completely within their shared interior. A dot is at the small circle's center.

Which diagram allows a matching dot? In the choices, shapes described as separate have a clear gap. Shapes described as inside do not touch the enclosing outline.

- A. A small circle lies inside a rectangle. A triangle is separate from the rectangle.
- B. A small triangle lies inside a circle. The circle lies inside a tall rectangle.
- C. A small rectangle lies inside a triangle. A circle is separate from the triangle.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: stem wide rectangle overlapping large upright triangle with a small circle strictly inside their overlap and one centered dot; dot-free choices A circle strictly inside rectangle with triangle separate, B triangle strictly inside circle strictly inside tall rectangle, C rectangle strictly inside triangle with circle separate; do not highlight any region -->

**Solution:**

1. Record the starting dot: circle in, triangle in, rectangle in. We need a place inside the three shapes at once.
2. Check A. The circle is in the rectangle, but the triangle is separate. No point is inside the three shapes.
3. Check B. A point near the small triangle's center is inside the triangle. Since that triangle is inside the circle, the point is inside the circle too. The enclosing rectangle also contains it.
4. Check C. The separate circle cannot share an interior point with the triangle and rectangle.
5. Choose B. Put the dot inside the small triangle, away from its sides.

The new dot need not be at the circle's center. It needs the matching record.

### #4: Keeping One Shape Out

A circle overlaps a rectangle. A small triangle lies inside the circle's left side, away from the rectangle. A dot sits in the circle-and-rectangle overlap, to the right of the triangle.

Which diagram allows the same dot membership? Overlaps below have clear interior space. Separate shapes have a gap.

- A. A circle lies completely inside a rectangle, and a larger triangle encloses the rectangle.
- B. A circle and rectangle are separate, with a triangle between them.
- C. A circle and rectangle overlap, with a separate triangle above them.
- D. A rectangle lies completely inside a triangle. A circle crosses the rectangle's right side.
- E. A circle lies completely inside a triangle. A rectangle crosses the circle's left side.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: stem circle overlapping rectangle to its right, small triangle strictly inside circle's left crescent, dot in circle-rectangle overlap; dot-free choices A nested circle inside rectangle inside triangle, B circle triangle rectangle separated left to right, C overlapping circle and rectangle with separate triangle above, D rectangle strictly inside triangle with circle crossing rectangle's right side, E circle strictly inside triangle with rectangle crossing circle's left side; leave regions unshaded -->

**Solution:**

1. Record the dot: circle in, rectangle in, triangle out. Finding a shared circle-and-rectangle area is not enough. That area must extend outside the triangle.
2. In A, the triangle contains the rectangle. A point inside the rectangle cannot be outside the triangle. Reject A.
3. In B, the circle and rectangle have no shared interior. Reject B.
4. In C, place a point in the circle-and-rectangle overlap. The separate triangle does not contain it. C fits.
5. Check D too. The triangle contains the rectangle, so the required region is absent.
6. Check E. The triangle contains the circle, so a point inside the circle cannot be outside the triangle. Choose C.

D and E have overlaps, but they lack the required region.

### #5: Checking Two Dots Separately

A triangle overlaps a circle inside a square frame. The triangle's tip extends above the circle, and the circle extends below the triangle's base. Dot P is near the tip, above the circle. Dot Q is in the shared interior.

Which choice allows both placements? In each choice, the shapes lie fully inside the square frame without touching it.

- A. The triangle lies completely inside the circle, without touching it.
- B. The circle lies completely inside the triangle, without touching it.
- C. The triangle and circle are separate, with a gap.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: stem square frame containing overlapping upright triangle and circle, triangle tip above circle and circle bottom below triangle base, P inside triangle above circle and Q well inside shared interior; dot-free choices in square frames A triangle strictly inside circle, B circle strictly inside triangle, C separate triangle and circle with clear gap -->

**Solution:**

1. Record P: triangle in, circle out, frame in.
2. Record Q separately: triangle in, circle in, frame in. Both records must fit one choice.
3. Check A. It has space for Q. It has no triangle-interior space outside the circle, so P cannot fit.
4. Check B. Place Q inside the circle. That point is also inside the triangle. Place P inside the triangle near a corner, beyond the circle. Both remain inside the frame.
5. Check C. P can fit inside the triangle, but Q cannot fit because the shapes share no interior.
6. Choose B. It provides a place for each dot.

The shapes can change from overlapping to one inside another. Both dot records still fit.

### #6: A Dot on a Boundary

A horizontal rectangle crosses a circle's right side. The rectangle's top and bottom edges pass above and below the circle's rightmost point. Dot P is centered exactly on that rightmost point, well inside the rectangle.

Dot Q is slightly to P's left, inside both shapes. Dot R is slightly to P's right, outside the circle but inside the rectangle. What record belongs to each dot?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: outline circle with a horizontal rectangle crossing its right side, rectangle left edge left of circle's rightmost point and right edge beyond it; three small dots on a horizontal line, Q just left of circle's rightmost point, P centered exactly at that point, R just right, all well inside rectangle; label P Q R without membership labels -->

**Solution:**

1. Check P against the circle. Its center is on the outline, so write “circle on.” Against the rectangle, write “rectangle in.”
2. Check Q. Its center is to the inside of the circle's outline and inside the rectangle. Write “circle in, rectangle in.”
3. Check R. Its center is beyond the circle's outline but still inside the rectangle. Write “circle out, rectangle in.”
4. To match P elsewhere, find a point on the circle's boundary that lies inside the rectangle. A nearby point just inside the circle would match Q's record instead.

If the center is unclear, do not assume it is inside.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Copying Left or Right

> Keep the membership record when the shapes move.

A dot on the right in the starting drawing may need to go on the left in a choice. Test the surrounding shapes at the new point.

### #2: Forgetting the Outside Check

> Check excluded shapes as well as included shapes.

A triple overlap fails when the required dot must stay outside one shape. Example $4$ needs the circle and rectangle without the triangle.

### #3: Stopping After One Dot Fits

> Both dots must fit the same choice.

A choice can provide shared space for Q while leaving no place for P. Keep their records separate, as in Example $5$.

### #4: Forcing One Answer

> Check each choice against the stated placement rule.

If several choices allow the same record, membership alone does not give a unique answer. Check for another stated condition or an unclear boundary point. Do not invent an extra rule about the shapes' overall arrangement.
