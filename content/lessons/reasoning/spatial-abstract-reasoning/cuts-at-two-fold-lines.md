## Core Concept

### When Cuts Join Across a Fold

An **interior hole** lies fully inside the paper. A **notch** is a cut that opens onto an edge.

When a cut touches a **fold line**, unfolding puts the cut and its mirror copy edge-to-edge along that crease. They **join** into one connected opening instead of staying separate.

1. Fold a square vertically, then horizontally, and cut a quarter-circle at the corner touching **both** creases.
2. Open the horizontal fold: the quarter-circle joins its mirror copy into $1$ semicircle.
3. Open the vertical fold: the semicircle joins its mirror copy into **$1$ connected circular opening** at the center.

:::why
Why do $4$ cut layers make $1$ opening instead of $4$? All $4$ layers meet at the crease corner, so every reflected piece touches its neighbor along a cut edge.
:::

:::pair #split-a-crease-cut
Right: a cut at the corner where both creases meet joins into **One connected central opening**.
Wrong: pick **Four separate interior openings** because there are $4$ paper layers.
Four layers make $4$ separate holes only when the cut touches no crease. Check whether the cut sits on a fold first.
:::

:::check
A square is folded once vertically, and a triangle is cut with one whole side right on the vertical fold. How many openings appear when opened, and what shape is it?
:::answer
The answer is **one four-sided opening (a kite)**. The triangle and its mirror copy meet along that shared side on the fold.
:::

### Cuts on One Fold Line in a Multi-Fold Packet

When a cut touches **one** crease and stays away from the other creases, only the touched crease joins halves. Every untouched crease still doubles the number of separate openings.

<!-- FIGURE WANTED: square folded vertically left onto right, then horizontally bottom onto top, with a small semicircular notch centered on the vertical folded edge away from the horizontal crease; arrow opens horizontal fold first showing 2 notches on the vertical crease, arrow opens vertical fold next showing 2 separate circular openings centered on the vertical crease -->

1. Take a square folded twice (vertical, then horizontal) with a notch on the **vertical** fold only.
2. Open the untouched horizontal fold: $1$ notch doubles to **$2$ separate notches** on the vertical fold.
3. Open the touched vertical fold: each notch joins its copy across the fold, leaving **$2$ connected openings**.

In a $3$-fold packet (vertical, horizontal, vertical) with a cut on the **last vertical** crease only, opening that crease joins the cut into $1$ opening, and opening the two untouched folds doubles it twice ($1\times2\times2=4$ connected openings).

:::pair #merge-across-untouched-crease
Right: a cut on the vertical fold away from the horizontal crease gives **Two openings**.
Wrong: pick **One connected opening** by merging across both creases.
The horizontal crease never touches the cut, so opening it separates the top and bottom copies.
:::

:::pair #ignore-fold-join-in-three-folds
Right: in $3$ folds with a cut on the last vertical crease, copies join once to leave **Four connected openings**.
Wrong: pick **Eight separate openings** from $8$ layers.
Touching one crease joins pairs across that fold, reducing $8$ separate holes to $4$ connected openings.
:::

:::check
A square is folded vertically, then horizontally, then vertically again. A small quarter-circle is cut at the packet corner where the horizontal crease meets the last vertical crease, away from the outer edges. How many connected openings appear when fully opened?
:::answer
The answer is **two connected openings**. The $8$ layers meet in groups of $4$ at the corner where the two creases cross. Each group joins into $1$ circle, and $8 \div 4 = 2$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Cut at Both Fold Lines

A square is folded vertically in half, then horizontally in half. A small quarter-circle is cut through all layers from the corner where the two folded edges meet. Its center is that corner.

What opening appears when the sheet is opened?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: square folded left onto right, then bottom onto top; remove a quarter-circle from the packet corner formed by both folded edges, with arc centered at that corner; first unfolding shows a semicircular cut on remaining fold, full unfolding shows one circular center opening -->

**Solution:**

1. Notice the quarter-circle touches both creases at their meeting corner (the sheet center).
2. Open the horizontal fold: the quarter-circle and its copy join into **one semicircle**.
3. Open the vertical fold: the semicircle and its copy join into **one circular opening** at the center.

The open sheet has **one circular opening at the center**.

**Check:** Both creases pass through the cut, so all four quarter-circles meet at the center point to form one circle.

### #2: A Notch on a Folded Square

A square is folded vertically in half, then horizontally in half. A small semicircular notch is cut through all $4$ layers, centered on the vertical folded edge and away from the horizontal crease and outer edges. How many connected openings appear when the square is fully opened?

<!-- FIGURE WANTED: square folded vertically left onto right, then horizontally bottom onto top; small semicircular notch centered on the vertical folded edge halfway up, away from the horizontal crease and outer edges; unfolded state not shown -->

**Solution:**

1. Unfold in reverse order: open the **horizontal fold** first.
2. Since the notch stays away from the horizontal crease, reflecting across it makes **$2$ separate notches** on the vertical fold.

3. Open the **vertical fold**: each notch sits on that crease, so it joins its mirror copy across the fold.

The open square has **two connected openings**.

**Check:** Opening the untouched horizontal crease separates the notch into $2$ spots on the vertical crease, and opening the vertical crease joins each spot across that fold into $2$ connected openings.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Cut at the corner of two creases: copies join across both creases into $1$ center opening, not $4$. {#split-a-crease-cut}
- Cut on one crease only: untouched creases still double the count of separate openings. {#merge-across-untouched-crease}
- Three folds with a cut on one crease: join pairs across the touched crease first, leaving $4$ openings instead of $8$. {#ignore-fold-join-in-three-folds}
:::
