## Core Concept

A region can be missing. For example, when a circle lies completely inside a triangle, there is no circle-interior space outside the triangle. Do not assume a needed region exists because both shapes are drawn.

## Examples

### #1: Keeping One Shape Out

A circle overlaps a rectangle. A small triangle lies inside the circle's left side, away from the rectangle. A dot sits in the circle-and-rectangle overlap, to the right of the triangle.

Which diagram allows the same dot membership? Overlaps below have clear interior space. Separate shapes have a gap.

- A. A circle lies completely inside a rectangle, and a larger triangle encloses the rectangle.
- B. A circle and rectangle are separate, with a triangle between them.
- C. A circle and rectangle overlap, with a separate triangle above them.
- D. A rectangle lies completely inside a triangle. A circle crosses the rectangle's right side.
- E. A circle lies completely inside a triangle. A rectangle crosses the circle's left side.

**Solution:**

1. Record the dot: circle in, rectangle in, triangle out. Finding a shared circle-and-rectangle area is not enough. That area must extend outside the triangle.
2. In A, the triangle contains the rectangle. A point inside the rectangle cannot be outside the triangle. Reject A.
3. In B, the circle and rectangle have no shared interior. Reject B.
4. In C, place a point in the circle-and-rectangle overlap. The separate triangle does not contain it. C fits.
5. Check D too. The triangle contains the rectangle, so the required region is absent.
6. Check E. The triangle contains the circle, so a point inside the circle cannot be outside the triangle. Choose C.

D and E have overlaps, but they lack the required region.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Forgetting the Outside Check

> Check excluded shapes as well as included shapes.

A triple overlap fails when the required dot must stay outside one shape. Example $1$ needs the circle and rectangle without the triangle.
