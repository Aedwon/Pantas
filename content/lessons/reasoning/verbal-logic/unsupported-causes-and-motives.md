## Core Concept

### Separating Timing and Association from Cause

Two events together or in sequence do not prove cause.

- An **association** means two patterns appear together (busier periods had longer waits). It does *not* prove sole cause, nor that one change fixes everything.
- **Time order** ($A$ before $B$) supports sequence ("after"), not cause ("because").

Supported claim: "After reviewing a request for flexible hours, a supervisor approved it. The record gives no explanation or evidence of her reasons."

1. Sequence: reviewed first, approved second.
2. Supported claim: **She approved the request after reviewing it.**
3. Unsupported claim: reviewing *forced* agreement.

:::pair #timing-as-cause
Right: reviewing then approving supports **She approved the request after reviewing it.**
Wrong: She approved it because reviewing the request forced agreement.
Time order never proves cause. Report sequence ("after") instead of inventing "because."
:::

:::check
A bakery owner wrote, "Many regulars asked for home delivery, so we began delivering." Which claim is supported: (A) the bakery began delivering to match a rival shop, or (B) it began delivering because regulars asked for it?
:::answer
The answer is **(B)**. The owner states this reason, so it does not rest on timing or a guess. (A) names a reason nobody stated.
:::

### Checking Whether Two Changes Happened at Once

A fair causal test changes only one factor while holding others steady. When two things change at once, neither can take sole credit.

Spot a second change: "A garden's pechay grew faster after students changed its watering schedule. That same week, they moved the trays from shade into sun."

1. Change $1$: new watering schedule.
2. Change $2$: more light (**The move also changed the plants' light.**).
3. Neither factor was tested alone, so neither watering nor sunlight can claim sole credit.

:::why
Why does repeating the same mixed test on more plots still fail? If $100$ lowland plots get new trays and $100$ upland plots get old trays, elevation and tray type still change together. Only testing both trays at one elevation isolates the trays.
:::

:::pair #confounded-two-changes
Right: when watering and light changed together, **The move also changed the plants' light** shows why watering cannot take sole credit.
Wrong: The move proves that sunlight was the only cause of the increase.
Handing all credit to the second change repeats the same error in reverse. Neither factor was isolated.
:::

:::check
Pupils using a new reading app scored higher, but only they also had a tutor that month. Which change best isolates the app: (A) test more pupils the same way, (B) give both groups the tutor, or (C) ask pupils which they prefer?
:::answer
The answer is **(B)**. With the tutor in both groups, the app is the only difference. (A) repeats the mix, and (C) measures opinion, not scores.
:::

### Separating Actions from Unstated Motives

A **motive** is a person's inner reason for acting (fear, pride, or changing one's mind). Actions alone never prove a motive without stated or direct evidence.

Test the flexible-hours approval again: the record shows the review and approval, with zero reason given. Guessing she feared losing staff or wanted praise invents an unstated motive.

:::pair #guessed-motive
Right: with no recorded reason, report only **She approved the request after reviewing it.**
Wrong: She approved it because she feared losing experienced staff.
Fear sounds plausible, yet the record gives zero proof of it. State only observed actions unless a reason is given.
:::

:::check
Lito wrote, "I am leaving the choir because rehearsals clash with my work shift." A friend says Lito left because of the new conductor. Is the friend's claim supported?
:::answer
The answer is **no**. Lito's note gives a different reason. The claim about the conductor is a guess about Lito's mind, with no direct evidence.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Reporting Change Without Guessing Cause

Before a branch changed hours, few people arrived early. After it began opening earlier, records showed more early arrivals. No other details were recorded. Which conclusion is best supported?

(A) Opening earlier caused the rise in early visits.

(B) Early-morning visits were higher after the schedule change.

(C) Visitors came earlier because they disliked the old schedule.

**Solution:**

1. Facts: early arrivals were higher after the schedule changed.
2. Drop (A): other causes were not ruled out.
3. Drop (C): visitor motives were never recorded.
4. Choose **(B)**: reports the change with no invented cause or motive.

**Check:** Notice *after* in (B) replaces causal words like *caused* or *because*.

### #2: Separating Action from Motive

Carlo approved a revised schedule after reading staff feedback. No reason is given. Which conclusion is supported?

(A) Carlo approved it because he feared complaints.

(B) Carlo wanted to impress his director.

(C) Carlo approved the revised schedule after reading staff feedback.

**Solution:**

1. Recorded actions: read feedback, then approved schedule.
2. Drop (A) and (B): fear and impressing others are unstated motives.
3. Choose **(C)**.

**Check:** Every detail in (C) comes straight from the stated sequence with zero mind-reading.

### #3: Reading an Attendance Record

Across observed school days, rainier days had lower attendance than drier days. Which conclusion is best supported?

(A) Attendance was lower on rainier days because rain kept pupils home.

(B) Giving every pupil a raincoat always ends absences.

(C) Pupils attended less often on rainier days than on drier days.

**Solution:**

1. Data: attendance on rainier days versus drier days.
2. Drop (B): raincoats were never tested, and *always ends* is an unsupported promise.

:::try
Finish the last step: compare (A) and (C) against the data.
:::answer
The answer is **(C) Pupils attended less often on rainier days than on drier days.** The data show two patterns going together. Choice (A) repeats them but adds a cause the records never tested.

**Check:** (C) matches "went together in the records" without claiming an untested cause.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Swap "because" for "after" or "with."** When only timing or co-occurrence is given, cross out *because*, *caused*, and guessed emotions.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- One event before another, or two patterns together: report sequence or association, never cause. {#timing-as-cause}
- Two factors changing together: isolate one factor instead of crediting either alone. {#confounded-two-changes}
- Action with no stated reason: report the action without guessing inner motives. {#guessed-motive}
:::
