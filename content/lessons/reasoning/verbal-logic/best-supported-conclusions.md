## Core Concept

### Matching Scope to Who Was Measured

A **best-supported** conclusion has the strongest backing from the given facts. Every detail must stay inside what was measured.

Test a conclusion in four steps:

1. List the given facts.
2. Check *who* or *what* was measured.
3. Drop unmeasured groups, causes, or guarantees.
4. Pick the closest claim.

Supported claim: "A voluntary staff survey received only replies favoring a proposal. The report gives no data on non-respondents."

- Measured: responding volunteers.
- Unmeasured: non-respondents.
- Supported claim: **The responding volunteers favored the proposal.**

:::pair #generalize-sample-to-all
Right: from a voluntary survey with only favoring replies, conclude **The responding volunteers favored the proposal.**
Wrong: Every employee favored the proposal.
Replies from volunteers cannot speak for silent staff. Keep the claim inside the measured group.
:::

:::check
A counter log shows some customers used English and some used Filipino. Which claim does the log support?

(A) Most customers used their first language.

(B) Both languages were used at the counter.

(C) English was used more often than Filipino.
:::answer
The answer is **(B)**. The log shows both languages in use. It has no first-language data for (A) and no counts for (C).
:::

### Checking What the Count Actually Measures

Check *what* was counted; separate **most** (over half) from **most often** (top share).

Supported claim: "Residents marked hard crossings. The bakery junction appeared on more maps than any other spot. Crossing trips were not counted."

1. Counted: marked maps, not trips.
2. Top share can sit under $50\%$: $30$ of $100$ maps across $5$ spots ($30, 20, 20, 15, 15$) wins first place.
3. Claim: **The bakery junction was marked most often.**

:::why
Why can first place be under half? When choices split across four or five options, the highest share can be $30\%$ or $35\%$, beating every rival without reaching a majority.
:::

:::pair #most-vs-plurality
Right: when one corner appeared on more maps than any other, conclude **The bakery junction was marked most often.**
Wrong: Most residents who submitted maps marked the bakery junction.
First place does not mean over $50\%$. Also keep what was counted fixed: map checks do not count crossing trips.
:::

:::check
A class chose a pet: the dog got $55$ votes, the cat $30$, and the fish $15$ ($1$ vote per form). Did *most* forms pick the dog?
:::answer
The answer is **yes**. The dog got $55$ of $100$ forms, which is over half ($50$).
:::

### Avoiding Unmeasured Explanations and Combined Totals

Supported claim: "A school stocked more print than audio copies of a story, and print had more checkouts."

- Recorded fact: **Print copies had more recorded loans.**
- Unmeasured: choices under equal stock.

:::pair #silence-or-equal-guess
Right: unequal stock and more print loans support **Print copies had more recorded loans.**
Wrong: Given equal access, students would still choose print more often.
You cannot predict choices under equal supply when access was unequal. Report only the recorded loan count.
:::

:::check
Ana's pupils did better on short tests, Ben's on long tests. Which claim is supported?

(A) Ana's pupils did better overall.

(B) Ben's pupils did better overall.

(C) The better group depended on test length.
:::answer
The answer is **(C)**. Each group led on one kind of test. Overall, either group could lead, depending on how many tests of each length were taken.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Keeping Claims Inside a Trial

A branch tested appointments on several mornings. On those mornings, appointment visitors waited less than walk-ins. No cause was given. Which conclusion is best supported?

(A) Appointments will remove waiting time at every branch.

(B) Having an appointment appears to be linked with shorter waits at the branch during the test.

(C) Walk-in visitors caused the branch's delays.

**Solution:**

1. Facts: one branch, test mornings, shorter appointment waits.
2. Drop (A): promises zero wait at *every* branch.
3. Drop (C): invents an untested blame.
4. Choose **(B)**: stays inside the test.

**Check:** Every phrase in (B) matches a stated detail without adding branches or causes.

### #2: Subgroup Leads vs. Combined Totals

A plant trialed two fruit crates. Slotted crates had less damage on long routes; solid crates had less on short routes. No combined total is given. Which fits best?

**Solution:**

1. Split the evidence: long routes favor slotted crates; short routes favor solid crates.
2. Reject any claimed overall winner or tie: an overall comparison needs both damage rates and sample sizes for each route group.
3. Conclusion: **The better crate differed by route length.**

**Check:** Opposite winners on the two route lengths directly establish that the better crate depended on route length.

### #3: Reading a Later-Hours Trial

Before a later-hours trial, several workers arrived after closing. During the trial, those same workers used the library after their shifts during the added evening and weekend hours. Which conclusion is best supported?

(A) Later hours help all library visitors use the library.

(B) Later hours help some workers read more books.

(C) Some workers used the library during the added hours.

**Solution:**

1. Observed: several workers using the library during the added hours.
2. Drop (A): *several workers* does not cover *all library visitors*.

:::try
Finish the last step: compare (B) and (C) against what the trial actually measured.
:::answer
The answer is **(C) Some workers used the library during the added hours.** The trial saw exactly that. Choice (B) adds book counts nobody measured.

**Check:** Those added evening and weekend hours were unavailable before the trial, and "some workers" and "during the added hours" match the exact trial observations.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Audit every noun and count.** Match who was studied, what was counted, and where the test ran, then cross out anything wider.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A volunteer or small group: keep the claim inside the exact group measured. {#generalize-sample-to-all}
- First place ("more than any other"): report *most often*, never *most* (over half). {#most-vs-plurality}
- Unequal supply or missing weights: report the recorded totals without guessing fair-test or combined results. {#silence-or-equal-guess}
:::
