## Core Concept

In analytical reasoning, an **assumption** is an unstated premise that must be true for an argument or statement to hold. A **conclusion** is a logical claim that necessarily follows from given statements.

### Key Rules

- **Valid Assumption:** An assumption is valid if the argument falls apart when the assumption is negated.
- **Valid Conclusion:** A conclusion is valid if it must be true whenever the premises are true.
- **Avoid Overreach:** Do not assume facts outside the provided statement, and do not select conclusions that go beyond what the premises strictly justify.

## Examples

### #1: Identifying an Underlying Assumption

Consider the statement: "All employees who complete the training program will receive a promotion." Which unstated assumption must be true for this statement to hold?

(A) The training program is difficult to complete.
(B) Employees want to be promoted.
(C) Completion of the training program can be evaluated and verified.

**Solution:**
Test which assumption is necessary for the statement to make sense.

1. Option (A) is an opinion about difficulty; the statement does not depend on difficulty.
2. Option (B) concerns employee desires, not the administrative rule itself.
3. Option (C) must be true because if completion cannot be verified, the rule promising promotions upon completion cannot be implemented.
4. Therefore, Option (C) is a necessary underlying assumption.

### #2: Drawing a Valid Conclusion

Consider the premises: "All administrative officers are full-time employees. Maria is an administrative officer." What valid conclusion follows?

**Solution:**
Apply categorical deductive logic.

1. Premise 1 states that every administrative officer is a full-time employee.
2. Premise 2 places Maria in the group of administrative officers.
3. Combining Premise 1 and Premise 2, Maria must belong to the group of full-time employees.
4. Therefore, the valid conclusion is that Maria is a full-time employee.

### #3: Avoiding Invalid Logical Overreach

Given the statement: "Some inspectors are certified auditors," a student concludes: "Therefore, some inspectors are not certified auditors." Is this conclusion necessarily valid based strictly on the statement?

**Solution:**
Evaluate whether the conclusion must be true under formal logic.

1. "Some" in formal logic means "at least one."
2. Stating that at least one inspector is a certified auditor leaves open the possibility that all inspectors are certified auditors.
3. It does not strictly prove that there are inspectors who are not certified auditors.
4. Therefore, the conclusion goes beyond what is strictly proven and is not necessarily valid.
