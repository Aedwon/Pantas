## Core Concept

### Near-Synonyms

A **near-synonym** pair matches two words with very close meanings.

1. Look at *happy : joyful* and *angry : ______*.
2. *Happy* and *joyful* share a close meaning.
3. **Furious** means very angry, so it matches best among *calm*, *sad*, *nervous*, and *furious*.

:::check
Complete *begin : start* as *finish : ______* (*delay*, *end*, *open*, *try*).
:::answer
The answer is **end**. *Begin* and *start* have close meanings, and *finish* and *end* have close meanings.
:::

### Opposites

An **antonym** pair matches two words with opposite meanings.

1. Look at *hot : cold* and *fast : ______*.
2. *Hot* and *cold* are opposite temperatures, so *fast* pairs with **slow**.

:::pair #synonym-instead-of-opposite
Right: "Hot is to cold" matches **fast : slow**.
Wrong: "Hot is to cold" matches **fast : quick**.
*Quick* shares the idea of speed, yet the first pair contrasts meanings. Always check whether the first pair matches or opposes.
:::

:::check
Complete *brave : cowardly* as *generous : ______* (*kind*, *selfish*, *wealthy*, *polite*).
:::answer
The answer is **selfish**. *Brave* and *cowardly* are opposites, and *generous* and *selfish* are opposites.
:::

### Degree of Intensity

A **degree** pair steps from a mild form of a quality to a stronger form, or the reverse.

1. For *warm : hot* and *cool : ______*: *hot* is stronger than *warm*, so **cold** is stronger than *cool*.
2. For actions: *whisper* (quiet) to *shout* (loud) matches *stroll* (slow) to **sprint** (very fast).

:::why
Why treat degree separately from plain synonyms? A synonym keeps roughly the same strength, while a degree pair moves up or down along one scale.
:::

:::pair #wrong-degree-direction
Right: "Warm is to hot" matches **cool : cold**.
Wrong: "Warm is to hot" matches **cool : mild**.
*Mild* weakens coolness instead of strengthening it. Match the mild-to-strong direction.
:::

:::check
Complete *drizzle : downpour* as *breeze : ______* (*calm*, *gale*, *cloud*, *chill*).
:::answer
The answer is **gale**. A downpour is a stronger drizzle, and a gale is a stronger breeze.
:::

### Double Analogies

A **double analogy** has two blanks and two separate relationships. Solve each pair alone, and keep only the choice where both blanks fit in order.

1. Look at *map : cartographer* as *statue : ______*; *thermometer : temperature* as *barometer : ______*.
2. Pair $1$ (work to maker): a map is made by a cartographer, so a statue is shaped by a **sculptor**.
3. Pair $2$ (instrument to reading): a thermometer measures temperature, so a barometer measures **pressure**.
4. In order: **sculptor, pressure**.

:::pair #half-double-analogy
Right: for "Puppy is to dog as calf is to ______, in the same way that petal is to flower as ______ is to tree," choose **Cow, leaf**.
Wrong: choose **Cow, forest**.
The first blank (*cow*) is right, making the whole pair look safe. Check the second blank too: a forest holds trees and is not part of one.
:::

:::check
Solve in order: *baker : loaf* as *potter : ______*; *orchid : flower* as *quartz : ______* (*clay, mineral*, *vase, mineral*, *vase, jewelry*, *kiln, gemstone*).
:::answer
The answer is **vase, mineral**. A potter makes a vase, and quartz is a type of mineral.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Near-Synonym

Silent is to quiet as ancient is to ______.

(A) Old (B) Loud (C) Modern (D) Quick

**Solution:**

1. *Silent* and *quiet* have close meanings.
2. Among the choices, **old** matches *ancient* closely.
3. *Loud* and *modern* oppose those meanings.

The answer is **(A) Old**.

**Check:** Both pairs link words with similar meanings.

### #2: Degree

Cool is to cold as warm is to ______.

(A) Mild (B) Hot (C) Wet (D) Dry

**Solution:**

1. *Cold* is a stronger form of *cool*.
2. **Hot** is a stronger form of *warm*.
3. *Mild* weakens the quality, while *wet* and *dry* switch to moisture.

The answer is **(B) Hot**.

**Check:** Both pairs move from mild to intense (*cool* to *cold*, *warm* to *hot*).

### #3: Antonym or Contrast

Expand is to contract as accept is to ______.

(A) Receive (B) Reject (C) Allow (D) Choose

**Solution:**

1. *Expand* and *contract* are opposites.
2. The opposite of *accept* is **reject**.
3. *Receive* and *allow* are close in meaning to *accept*.

The answer is **(B) Reject**.

**Check:** *Accept* and *reject* contrast cleanly, matching *expand* and *contract*.

### #4: Double Analogy

Puppy is to dog as foal is to ______, while petal is to flower as ______ is to tree.

(A) Horse, leaf (B) Horse, forest (C) Cow, leaf (D) Goat, root

**Solution:**

1. First pair: a puppy grows into a dog, so a foal grows into a **horse**.
2. Drop (C) and (D), since neither starts with *horse*.
3. Second pair: a petal is part of a flower.

:::try
Finish the last step: compare *leaf* and *forest* to match part-to-whole for *tree*.
:::answer
The answer is **(A) Horse, leaf**. A leaf is part of a tree, while a forest holds many trees.

**Check:** Both completed halves hold in order: *foal : horse* and *leaf : tree*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Solve the first blank of a double analogy alone and cross out choices that miss it. Then test the remaining choices on the second blank.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Picking a synonym when the first pair contrasts: check whether meanings match or oppose, so *fast* pairs with *slow*, not *quick*. {#synonym-instead-of-opposite}
- Stepping the wrong way on a degree scale: match mild-to-strong, so *cool* goes to *cold*, not *mild*. {#wrong-degree-direction}
- Checking only half of a double analogy: test both blanks in order, so *calf* and *petal* give *cow, leaf*, not *cow, forest*. {#half-double-analogy}
:::
