## Core Concept

A **word analogy** asks you to identify a relevant relationship between one pair of words and find another pair that preserves that relationship in the same direction. Analogies are commonly written as "A is to B as C is to D," or $A:B::C:D$.

Natural-language relationships are rarely mathematically unique. Two words can be related in several ways, so the goal is to find the **most specific relationship that the item and answer choices support**, not to assume there is one metaphysically exact relationship between the words.

A useful method is to write a short relationship sentence for the first pair, then apply the same sentence to the candidate pair.

Common relationship types include:

- **Part to whole:** *petal : flower*.
- **Cause/product:** *fire : ash*.
- **Function:** *knife : cut*.
- **Category/type:** *rose : flower*.
- **Near-synonym:** *happy : joyful*.
- **Antonym/contrast:** *hot : cold*.
- **Degree:** *warm : hot*.
- **User/tool:** *carpenter : hammer*.
- **Creator or role/work:** *author : book*, where the relation may be "produces/writes" depending on the intended comparison.

Direction matters: *author : book* is not the same ordered relation as *book : author*.

## Examples

### #1: Function

Pen is to write as scissors is to ______.

(A) sharp (B) cut (C) paper (D) blade

**Solution:**

"A pen is used to write" maps most closely to "scissors are used to **cut**." Answer: **B**.

### #2: Role and Product

Author is to book as director is to ______.

(A) actor (B) movie (C) theater (D) camera

**Solution:**

A useful relationship is "an author has a central creative role in producing a book." Among the options, a director has a corresponding central creative role in producing/directing a **movie**. Answer: **B**.

This does not mean either person single-handedly "creates" the entire work; the analogy concerns the relevant role-to-work relationship.

### #3: Category

Hammer is to tool as oak is to ______.

(A) wood (B) tree (C) leaf (D) forest

**Solution:**

"A hammer is a type of tool" maps to "an oak is a type of **tree**." Answer: **B**.

### #4: Double Analogy

Puppy is to dog as calf is to ______, while petal is to flower as ______ is to tree.

(A) cow, leaf (B) cow, branch (C) cat, leaf (D) horse, root

**Solution:**

1. Puppy → dog is young animal → adult/kind; calf → **cow** is the intended matching pair among the choices.
2. Petal → flower is part → whole; **leaf** → tree matches that relation.
3. Answer: **A**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Write the relationship as a sentence before looking at choices.** Specific wording reduces vague associations.

**Match direction as well as category.** "Used by," "used for," "part of," and "contains" are not interchangeable.

**Use the choices to resolve ambiguity.** If the first pair supports several true relationships, the intended one is the relation that can be reproduced cleanly by one candidate pair.

**For double analogies, validate both relationships independently.**

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Choosing a Merely Associated Word

> Association is weaker than relational match. *Oak* is related to wood, but *oak : tree* better mirrors *hammer : tool*.

### #2: Demanding an "Exact" Real-World Relationship

> Natural concepts are messy. The test asks for the best structural/semantic correspondence among the available choices.

### #3: Reversing Direction

> Preserve the ordered relationship from the first term to the second.

### #4: Overstating a Role

> "Director : movie" does not mean the director is the movie's sole creator. Phrase the shared relationship only as strongly as both pairs support.
