## Core Concept

A **word analogy** asks you to find the relationship between one pair of words and match it with another pair. It may be written as "A is to B as C is to D," or $A:B::C:D$.

Two words can be related in more than one true way. Use the answer choices to find the clearest relationship that works in the same direction.

A good method is to turn the first pair into a short sentence. Then test the same sentence on each answer choice.

Common relationships include:

- **Part to whole:** *petal : flower*. A petal is part of a flower. Keep the direction from the part to the whole.
- **Cause or product:** *fire : ash*. Fire can produce ash. Ask what the first word makes, causes, or leaves.
- **Function:** *knife : cut*. A knife is used to cut.
- **Category or type:** *rose : flower*. A rose is a type of flower.
- **Near-synonym:** *happy : joyful*. The words have close meanings, though they may not mean exactly the same thing.
- **Antonym or contrast:** *hot : cold*. The words show opposite ideas.
- **Degree:** *warm : hot*. The second word shows a stronger degree of the same idea.
- **User and tool:** *carpenter : hammer*. The first word names a person who commonly uses the second thing.
- **Role and work:** *author : book*. An author writes a book.

Direction matters. *Author : book* is not the same relationship as *book : author*.

When two choices seem related, use a more exact sentence. "Is related to" is too broad, but "is part of" or "is used to" can separate the choices.

## Examples

### #1: Function

Pen is to write as scissors is to ______.

(A) sharp (B) cut (C) paper (D) blade

**Solution:**

"A pen is used to write" matches "scissors are used to cut." The answer is **B**.

### #2: Role and Product

Author is to book as director is to ______.

(A) actor (B) movie (C) theater (D) camera

**Solution:**

An author writes a book. In the same way, a director directs a movie. The answer is **B**.

### #3: Category

Hammer is to tool as oak is to ______.

(A) wood (B) tree (C) leaf (D) forest

**Solution:**

"A hammer is a type of tool" matches "an oak is a type of tree." The answer is **B**.

### #4: Part to Whole

Wheel is to bicycle as page is to ______.

(A) pencil (B) book (C) reader (D) ink

**Solution:**

A wheel is part of a bicycle. A page is part of a book, so the answer is **B**.

### #5: Cause or Product

Fire is to ash as grinding grain is to ______.

(A) flour (B) field (C) spoon (D) seed

**Solution:**

Fire can produce ash. Grinding grain can produce flour, so the answer is **A**.

### #6: Near-Synonym

Silent is to quiet as ancient is to ______.

(A) old (B) loud (C) modern (D) quick

**Solution:**

*Silent* and *quiet* have close meanings. *Ancient* and *old* also have close meanings, so the answer is **A**.

### #7: Degree

Cool is to cold as warm is to ______.

(A) mild (B) hot (C) wet (D) dry

**Solution:**

Cold is a stronger degree of cool. Hot is a stronger degree of warm, so the answer is **B**.

### #8: User and Tool

Painter is to brush as carpenter is to ______.

(A) hammer (B) wood (C) house (D) paint

**Solution:**

A painter commonly uses a brush. A carpenter commonly uses a hammer, so the answer is **A**.

### #9: Antonym or Contrast

Expand is to contract as accept is to ______.

(A) receive (B) reject (C) allow (D) choose

**Solution:**

*Expand* and *contract* are opposites. *Accept* and *reject* are also opposites, so the answer is **B**.

### #10: Double Analogy

Puppy is to dog as foal is to ______, while petal is to flower as ______ is to tree.

(A) horse, leaf (B) horse, forest (C) cow, leaf (D) goat, root

**Solution:**

1. A puppy grows into a dog. A foal grows into a **horse**.
2. Petal to flower is part to whole. **Leaf** to tree matches that relationship.
3. Both parts match only choice **A**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Write one short relationship sentence first.** This keeps you from choosing a word that is only loosely connected.

**Keep the direction.** "Part of" and "contains" point in opposite directions. "Uses" and "is used by" do too.

**Use the choices to settle a close call.** If more than one relationship is true, choose the specific one that one option matches cleanly.

**For double analogies, solve each pair on its own.** Keep only the choice that makes both relationships work.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Choosing an Associated Word

> A word can be related without matching the same relationship.

*Oak* is related to wood, but *oak : tree* better matches *hammer : tool*. Name the relationship before choosing.

### #2: Using a Vague Relationship

> "These words go together" is usually too weak.

Use a clear sentence such as "is part of," "is a type of," or "is used by." A precise sentence makes wrong choices easier to reject.

### #3: Reversing Direction

> Keep the first word and second word in the same roles.

If the first pair is user to tool, do not choose a tool-to-user pair. Direction is part of the answer.

### #4: Missing Degree

> Similar words may differ by strength.

If one word is a stronger form of another, match that change in strength. Do not treat every close meaning as a plain synonym.

### #5: Solving Only Half of a Double Analogy

> A double analogy needs both pairs to work.

A choice can fit the first blank and still fail the second. Check both before answering.
