## Core Concept

Trace the rope from its tied end to the hand to see which segments support the load.

In the trap below, the original lesson’s Example #3 is now #1, and its Example #4 is now #2.

## Examples

### #1: Counting the Supporting Segments

A $900\text{ N}$ load hangs from one movable pulley. The rope is tied to the ceiling, runs down and under the movable pulley, then up over a fixed pulley on the ceiling, and down to the hand. Ignore friction and the weight of the pulleys. What effort is needed, and how much rope must be pulled to raise the load $0.5\text{ m}$?

**Solution:**

1. Two segments pull on the movable pulley: the one tied to the ceiling and the one running up to the fixed pulley. The segment from the fixed pulley to the hand does not touch the load.
2. The ideal MA is $2$.

$$F_\text{effort}=\dfrac{900}{2}=450\text{ N}$$

3. The rope moves twice as far as the load: $2\times0.5=1\text{ m}$.

### #2: Three Supporting Segments

A $900\text{ N}$ load hangs from a frame that holds one movable pulley. One end of the rope is tied to the frame. The rope goes up and over a fixed pulley on the ceiling, down and under the movable pulley, then straight up to a person who pulls that end upward. Ignore friction and the weight of the pulleys and frame. What effort is needed, and how much rope must be pulled to raise the load $0.4\text{ m}$?

**Solution:**

1. Three segments pull up on the frame: the tied segment, the segment coming down from the fixed pulley, and the segment going up to the hand. The hand pulls the end that leaves the movable pulley, so it counts.
2. The ideal MA is $3$, even though there are only two pulleys.

$$F_\text{effort}=\dfrac{900}{3}=300\text{ N}$$

3. The rope moves three times as far as the load: $3\times0.4=1.2\text{ m}$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Counting Pulleys Instead of Segments

> For one continuous rope, ideal MA is the number of supporting segments, not the number of pulleys.

Example #4 has two pulleys but MA $3$. Example #3 has two pulleys but MA $2$. Count the rope segments that pull on the moving load.
