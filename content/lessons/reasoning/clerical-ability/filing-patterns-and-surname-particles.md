## Core Concept

### Finding the Rule from Samples

Some filing tasks show sample pairs and do not state the rule. To **infer** a rule is to work it out from the samples. Test your guess on each sample before you apply it.

Find the filed form of *Carlo Reyes* from two samples:

- Sample $1$: *Ana Cruz* becomes **Cruz, Ana**
- Sample $2$: *Ben Lim* becomes **Lim, Ben**
- Both samples move the surname to Unit $1$ and put a comma after it.
- So *Carlo Reyes* becomes **Reyes, Carlo**.

Follow the rule the question shows or states. Registries differ: one may put the given name first, skip linking words such as *and*, *of*, and *the*, or expand an abbreviation such as *St.* to *Saint* when sorting.

:::pair #wrong-convention
Right: under a given-name-first rule, file **Reyes, Alma**, **Santos, Belen**, **Santos, Cora**, and **Reyes, Zoe** last
Wrong: put **Santos, Cora** last because *Santos* comes after *Reyes*
The wrong choice uses a surname-first habit when the question states given-name-first order. Check which unit goes first before you sort.
:::

### Surnames with Particles

Some surnames begin with short words called **particles**, such as *De*, *Del*, *De la*, *De los*, *Van*, or *San*. Registries differ on how to file them, so follow the rule the task states.

In these questions, one rule treats the particles and the main name as **one surname unit** and ignores spaces only while comparing letters. The written record keeps its spaces.

Find the order of *De la Cruz*, *Delgado*, and *De la Rosa* under that rule:

- Comparison keys (spaces ignored): *delacruz*, *delgado*, *delarosa*.
- Letters $1$ to $3$ match in all three keys: *del*.
- Letter $4$ separates *delgado* from the other two: *a* comes before *g*.
- *delacruz* and *delarosa* first differ at letter $5$: *c* comes before *r*.
- Order: **De la Cruz**, then **De la Rosa**, then **Delgado**.

:::why
Why keep the spaces in the written entry if we ignore them to sort? Under this rule, the comparison key picks the shelf order. Removing spaces from *De la Rosa* would change the person's real spelling.
:::

:::pair #drop-particle
Right: under a rule treating surname particles as part of one unit, index *De la Rosa* as **De la Rosa** (one surname unit)
Wrong: index *De la Rosa* as **Rosa** only
When the rule keeps surname particles in one unit, dropping *De la* wrongly moves the record from *D* to *R*. Follow the particle rule stated in the task.
:::

:::pair #rewrite-surname
Right: compare letters as *delarosa*, and write the entry as **De la Rosa**
Wrong: rewrite the person's recorded surname as **Delarosa**
Under this rule, ignoring spaces is a comparison step. Keep the recorded spelling and spaces intact.
:::

:::check
A registry treats a particle as part of the surname and ignores spaces to compare. Sort *De los Santos, Rey*; *De los Reyes, Ana*; and *Delos Angeles, Pia*.
:::answer
The answer is **Delos Angeles, Pia**, **De los Reyes, Ana**, **De los Santos, Rey**. The keys are *delosangeles*, *delosreyes*, and *delossantos*. They match through letter $5$ and first differ at letter $6$: *a*, *r*, *s*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Inferring a Filing Rule

Find the filed form of "Paula J. Villanueva" from two samples: "Rosa M. Diaz" is filed as **Diaz, Rosa M.**, and "Ken Yu" is filed as **Yu, Ken**.

**Solution:**

1. Sample $1$: *Diaz* moves to the front with a comma, and *Rosa M.* follows as written.
2. Sample $2$: *Yu* moves to the front with a comma, and *Ken* follows.
3. Rule: surname, comma, then the given name and the initial as written.
4. Apply it to *Paula J. Villanueva*: **Villanueva, Paula J.**

### #2: Comparing Surnames with Particles

Find the filing order of *Santos, Mia*, *San Jose, Lito*, *Sanchez, Paz*, and *San Pedro, Raul*. The system treats a particle as part of one surname unit and ignores spaces only to compare. Keep each written spelling.

**Solution:**

1. Comparison keys: *santos*, *sanjose*, *sanchez*, *sanpedro*.
2. All four keys match through *san* (letters $1$ to $3$). They first differ at letter $4$: *t*, *j*, *c*, *p*.
3. Letter order: *c* (*Sanchez*), *j* (*San Jose*), *p* (*San Pedro*), *t* (*Santos*).
4. Order with the written spellings: **Sanchez, Paz**, **San Jose, Lito**, **San Pedro, Raul**, **Santos, Mia**.

### #3: Given-Name-First Rule

Find the filing order of *Dizon, Mara*, *Ong, Ian*, *Dizon, Ian*, and *Abad, Hugo* when the registry compares given name first and surname second.

**Solution:**

1. Unit $1$ is the given name: *Mara*, *Ian*, *Ian*, *Hugo*.
2. *Hugo*, *Ian*, and *Mara* first differ at letter $1$ ($H$, $I$, $M$), so **Abad, Hugo** is first and **Dizon, Mara** is last.
3. The two *Ian* entries tie on Unit $1$, so they first differ in Unit $2$, the surname.

:::try
Finish the last step: compare the two surnames, then write the full order.
:::answer
The answer is **Abad, Hugo**, **Dizon, Ian**, **Ong, Ian**, **Dizon, Mara**. *Dizon* comes before *Ong* ($D$ before $O$).
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A task states a different unit order: file by that order, even if the given name comes first. {#wrong-convention}
- Surname with particles (*De*, *Del*, *De la*), when the task keeps them in one unit: keep them in the surname unit. {#drop-particle}
- Spaces ignored to compare: keep the spaces in the written record. {#rewrite-surname}
:::
