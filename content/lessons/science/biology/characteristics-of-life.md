## Core Concept

Biology is the study of life. Defining "alive" is not as simple as checking one feature.

Biologists list a set of properties that living things show together. Use the list as a framework. A living thing does not have to show every property at every moment.

### The Seven Properties

**Cellular organization**: every known living organism is made of one or more **cells**. A cell is the smallest unit that can carry out the work of life.

A bacterium is one cell. A human is made of a very large number of cells.

Cells alone do not make something alive. A dead leaf is made of cells, but they no longer carry out the work of life.

**Energy processing (metabolism)**: **metabolism** means all the chemical reactions inside an organism. These reactions take in energy and materials, change them, and use them to keep the organism going. Plants get energy from light, animals from food, and some bacteria from chemicals.

**Homeostasis (regulation)**: **homeostasis** means keeping the inside conditions steady enough for life. Sweating when you are hot is an example. The conditions are kept within a range. They are not perfectly fixed.

**Growth and development**: an organism grows by making more cells or by making its cells larger. It also develops, which means it changes in an ordered way, such as a tadpole becoming a frog. Genes and the environment both guide these changes.

**Reproduction**: living things make new living things. In sexual reproduction, two **gametes** (sex cells, such as egg and sperm) fuse.

They usually come from two parents, but one parent can supply both, as in self-pollination in pea plants. In asexual reproduction, no gametes fuse, and one parent makes offspring.

This property belongs to living things as a group. One individual can be alive and still not reproduce, for example if it is too young or **sterile**. Sterile means unable to have offspring.

**Response to stimuli**: a **stimulus** is a change in the surroundings that an organism can detect, such as light, touch, heat, or a chemical. Organisms detect stimuli and respond. A plant bending toward light is a response.

**Evolutionary adaptation**: to **evolve** means that the inherited features of a **population** change over many generations. A population is a group of the same kind of organism living in one place.

An **adaptation** is an inherited feature that helps its owners survive and reproduce. This is a change in a population over generations. One individual cannot make it happen by needing it.

A single life-like behavior is not enough. Fire consumes fuel, grows, and spreads, so it copies several properties. A crystal grows.

Neither is made of cells, and neither regulates its inside conditions or reproduces as a living thing does.

### Direct and Inferred Evidence

Some properties can be seen in a short description. Others can only be **inferred**.

To infer means to reach a conclusion from the evidence you have plus facts you already know. A question may ask which properties are directly shown. Then list only what the description states.

### Seeds and Other Resting Forms

A dry seed does not grow, move, or react for a long time. It can still be alive.

A living seed is **dormant**, which means its life processes have slowed to a very low level. It still has living cells. When it gets enough water and warmth, it grows. A seed that has died, for example one that is very old or cooked, never grows.

So a property that is missing right now does not by itself mean something is not alive.

### Viruses at the Boundary

A virus is a tiny particle made of genetic material inside a protein coat. **Genetic material** is the DNA or RNA that carries instructions.

A virus is **acellular**, which means it has no cells. It cannot make copies of itself alone. It must enter a host cell and use that cell's machinery.

Viruses do have genetic material, and virus populations evolve. Introductory biology usually does not count them as living organisms, because they lack cells and cannot carry out their own metabolism. Some scientists still debate where to draw the line.

### Levels of Organization

Living things are built in levels. Each level is made of the level below it.

- **Cell**: the basic unit of life.
- **Tissue**: a group of similar cells doing one job, such as muscle tissue.
- **Organ**: different tissues working together as one part, such as the heart.
- **Organ system**: several organs working together, such as the circulatory system.
- **Organism**: one whole living thing.
- **Population**: organisms of one species living in the same area.
- **Community**: all the populations of different species living in the same area.
- **Ecosystem**: a community together with its nonliving surroundings, such as water, soil, air, and sunlight.

A single-celled organism is one cell and one organism at once. It has no tissues or organs. Plants also have tissues, organs, and organ systems.

Some books add one more level above the ecosystem, the biosphere, which is all of Earth's living things and their surroundings.

## Examples

### #1: Direct and Inferred Properties

A mushroom absorbs nutrients from decaying wood, grows, releases spores, and changes its growth in response to its surroundings. A **spore** is a tiny reproductive cell. Which properties of life are directly shown?

**Solution:**

1. Taking in and using nutrients shows energy processing (metabolism).
2. Getting bigger and changing form shows growth and development.
3. Releasing spores shows reproduction.
4. Changing growth because of the surroundings shows response to stimuli.
5. Cellular organization is not described. We infer it, because we know a mushroom is a fungus and fungi are made of cells.

### #2: Why Viruses Complicate the Definition

A virus carries genetic material, and virus populations evolve. It makes copies of itself inside a host cell. Why does introductory biology usually not classify it as a living organism?

**Solution:**

1. A virus has genetic material, and its population evolves.
2. It copies itself only by using the machinery of a host cell.
3. It is acellular and does not carry out its own metabolism or keep its own inside conditions steady.
4. It shows some properties of life but lacks cells and independent metabolism. So it sits at the boundary of the definition.

### #3: Living or Nonliving?

Explain why (a) a car engine that uses fuel and (b) a growing crystal are not alive just because each shows one life-like property.

**Solution:**

1. A car engine changes the energy in fuel. It has no cells, does not grow from within, does not regulate itself as an organism does, and does not make new engines.
2. A crystal grows when more of the same substance attaches to its outside. It does not take in materials and change them by chemical reactions, and it has no cells.
3. Living things show a combination of properties. One property alone is not enough.

### #4: An Individual That Cannot Reproduce

A mule is the offspring of a male donkey and a female horse. Almost all mules are sterile. Is a mule alive?

**Solution:**

1. A mule is made of cells.
2. It takes in food and uses it for energy, so it has metabolism.
3. It grows, keeps its body conditions steady, and responds to its surroundings.
4. Reproduction is a property of living things as a group. One individual that cannot reproduce is still alive. So a mule is alive.

### #5: A Dormant Seed

A dry bean seed sits in a jar for months. It does not grow or move. Can it still be alive? What evidence would show it?

**Solution:**

1. A seed that is still alive is made of living cells, even when it looks inactive.
2. Such a seed is dormant, so its metabolism is very slow but not zero.
3. Place it in moist soil in a warm place. If it sprouts and grows, it was alive. If it never does, it may be dead.
4. A missing property at one moment does not show that something is nonliving.

### #6: Adaptation in a Population

A farmer sprays an insecticide on a field. Most of the insects die. A few that carry an inherited resistance survive and breed. After many generations, most insects in the field are resistant. Did each insect adapt by trying to resist?

**Solution:**

1. Resistance was inherited. Those few insects already had it before the spraying.
2. They survived and reproduced more than the others, so they passed it on.
3. Over generations, the share of resistant insects in the population grew.
4. So evolution happened in the population, across generations. No single insect changed because it needed to.

### #7: Levels of Organization

Arrange these from smallest to largest: ecosystem, organ, tissue, community, cell, population, organism, organ system. Then name the level of "all the Nile tilapia in one lake."

**Solution:**

1. Smallest to largest: cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism, population, community, ecosystem.
2. Nile tilapia is one species, so all the Nile tilapia in one lake are one species in one area. That is a population.
3. All the fish, plants, and microbes in the lake would be a community.
4. Adding the water, sunlight, and soil would make it an ecosystem.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Check the seven properties as a group: cells, energy processing, regulation, growth and development, response, reproduction, and evolution. Fire and crystals may copy some of them, but they have no cells and lack the rest. Cells alone are not enough either: a dead leaf or a piece of wood is made of cells, or what is left of them, but shows none of the other properties. Judge the whole set together.

For viruses, remember two groups. They evolve and carry genetic material. They are also acellular and need a host cell to copy themselves.

When a description is incomplete, say which property is shown and which is only inferred. An organism that shows little activity may be dormant, not dead.

To order the levels, ask "is this made of the one before it?" A tissue is made of cells. An organ is made of tissues. A community is made of populations.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Requiring Every Individual to Reproduce

> Reproduction is a property of living things as a group, not a test that each individual must pass.

A sterile animal, such as a mule, or a young child, is still alive.

### #2: Treating One Life-Like Property as Proof

> Growth, movement, or energy use alone does not prove that something is alive.

Fire matches several properties: it uses fuel and grows. A crystal grows, and clouds move. None of them is made of cells.

### #3: Saying Viruses Are Simply "Dead"

> A virus is not an ordinary dead object. It is a particle that evolves and copies itself, but only inside a host cell.

Introductory biology usually does not classify viruses as living organisms. Their genetic material and evolution still set them apart from ordinary nonliving matter.

### #4: Confusing Adaptation with an Individual's Effort

> Evolutionary adaptation is a change in a population over generations.

An organism does not evolve a feature just by trying harder in its lifetime. An individual can change during its life, such as a plant growing toward light. That is not evolution.

### #5: Mixing Up Neighboring Levels

> A population is one species. A community is many species.

An organ is one body part. An organ system is several organs working together. A community has only living things. An ecosystem also includes the nonliving surroundings.
