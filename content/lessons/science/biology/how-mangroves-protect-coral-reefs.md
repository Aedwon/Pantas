## Core Concept

**Mangroves** are trees and shrubs that grow along some coasts in salty water. A **coral reef** is a hard structure built by tiny animals called corals.

**Runoff** is water that flows over land. After rain it can carry **sediment**, bits of soil or sand, into the sea. When runoff flows through mangroves, the roots can slow the water. Some sediment settles to the bottom near the roots. Less can reach the reef, where too much sediment can cover and harm corals.

**Pollutants** are substances that can harm living things or their surroundings. Some stick to bits of soil and settle with them. Other pollutants can still reach the reef. Follow the route the runoff takes to see whether it passes through the mangroves.

## Examples

### #1: Mud After Rain

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: coastal side view with a Bare Slope on the left, Mangroves at the water's edge in the middle, and a Coral Reef offshore on the right; show a Runoff arrow carrying mud from the slope through the roots toward the reef -->

Rain washes mud off a bare slope. The runoff flows through a band of mangroves before reaching the reef. How can the roots help protect the reef?

(A) They help mud settle before it reaches the reef.

(B) They turn mud into sand as water passes.

(C) They shade the corals from the sun.

**Solution:**

1. The roots slow the muddy water as it flows through them.
2. Some mud settles near the roots. With less mud reaching the reef, less can settle on the corals. Choose (A).

### #2: Pollution That Sticks to Soil

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: coastal side view with a Garden at left, Mangroves at the shore, and a Coral Reef at right; show a Runoff arrow through the roots and label specks on bits of soil as Pollutant stuck to soil -->

Water from a garden carries a harmful chemical stuck to bits of soil. The water passes through mangroves before it reaches the reef. Why might less of the chemical reach the corals?

(A) The chemical washes off as water slows.

(B) The soil settles and takes the chemical with it.

(C) The chemical breaks down when it enters the sea.

**Solution:**

1. Some bits of soil settle near the roots.
2. The chemical stuck to those bits settles with them. That keeps some of it from reaching the corals. Choose (B).

### #3: A Route Around the Roots

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: overhead coast map with land above sea, a strip labeled Mangroves on the west shore, a Drain Outlet on the east shore outside the strip, and a Coral Reef offshore; show a Runoff arrow from the drain straight to the reef, clear of the strip -->

A drain carries muddy runoff straight to the reef without passing the mangrove roots. Can those roots trap mud from this flow before it arrives?

**Solution:**

1. Trace the water from the drain to the reef.
2. The flow misses the roots. The roots cannot trap mud from that flow on its way to the reef.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Trace the runoff from land toward the reef. If it passes through the roots, ask what can settle there.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Expecting Roots to Catch It All

> Some sediment and pollutants can still get through.

Roots can help keep the water cleaner. A pollutant that stays mixed into the water may travel on to the reef.
