## Core Concept

### Mutations

A **mutation** is a change in the DNA sequence. A substitution swaps one base. An insertion or deletion adds or removes bases.

A substitution may change one amino acid, change nothing (because several codons stand for the same amino acid), or make a stop codon too early.

Inside a gene's coding sequence, an insertion or deletion of a number of bases that is not a multiple of $3$ shifts every later codon. That is a **frameshift**.

## Examples

Example 6 in the original lesson is the example in From DNA to Protein.

### #1: Effect of a Mutation

In the gene from Example 6, the last template base changes from $C$ to $T$. Codons $AAG$ and $AAA$ both stand for lysine. How does the protein change?

**Solution:**

1. The template now ends in $TTT$.
2. The matching mRNA codon is $AAA$.
3. $AAA$ stands for lysine, the same as $AAG$.

The protein does not change, so this is a silent substitution.
