## Core Concept

A balanced equation is normally written with the smallest whole-number coefficients. A coefficient of $1$ is not written.

If every coefficient can be divided by the same number, divide. So $4H_2+2O_2\rightarrow4H_2O$ is balanced but not in simplest form.

If a coefficient comes out as a fraction, multiply every coefficient by the number that clears it. Some equations need this, as Example #5 shows.

## Examples

### #1: Balancing with a Polyatomic Group

Balance:

$$Al(OH)_3+H_2SO_4\rightarrow Al_2(SO_4)_3+H_2O$$

**Solution:**

1. Al: the right side has $2$. Put $2$ before $Al(OH)_3$.
2. $SO_4$ stays together as a group. The right side has $3$ of them. Put $3$ before $H_2SO_4$.
3. Count H on the left: $2\times3=6$ from the hydroxide and $3\times2=6$ from the acid. That is $12$ H. Put $6$ before $H_2O$.
4. Check O. Left: $6+12=18$. Right: $12+6=18$.

$$2Al(OH)_3+3H_2SO_4\rightarrow Al_2(SO_4)_3+6H_2O$$

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Balance a bracketed group such as $SO_4$ or $OH$ as one unit. This works only if the group stays together on both sides.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Forgetting That Coefficients Multiply Everything

> A coefficient multiplies every atom in the formula.

In $3Ca(OH)_2$ there are $3$ Ca atoms, $6$ O atoms and $6$ H atoms. Do not apply the $3$ to only the first symbol.
