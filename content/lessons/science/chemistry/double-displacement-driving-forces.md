## Core Concept

### Double Displacement and Driving Forces

Swapping partners on paper does not prove that anything happens. The letters in $AB+CD\rightarrow AD+CB$ show only the swap. Write each new formula from the ion charges.

A double displacement reaction happens only when a **driving force** removes ions from the solution. There are four common ones.

- A **precipitate** forms. This is a solid that appears when two solutions are mixed because it does not dissolve.
- Water forms. This happens when an acid meets a hydroxide base, which is one kind of neutralization. See “Acid and Base Models.”
- A gas escapes.
- A weak electrolyte forms. This is a compound that stays mostly as molecules in water and gives few ions, such as a weak acid, unlike a strong acid such as $HCl$, which gives mostly ions. For example, $HCl+NaC_2H_3O_2\rightarrow NaCl+HC_2H_3O_2$ goes ahead because acetic acid, $HC_2H_3O_2$, is a weak acid.

When an acid reacts with a carbonate, a gas forms. The carbonic acid that first forms breaks down into water and carbon dioxide: $Na_2CO_3+2HCl\rightarrow2NaCl+H_2O+CO_2$.

## Examples

### #1: Is There a Driving Force?

Solutions of sodium chloride and potassium nitrate are mixed. Does a reaction occur?

**Solution:**

1. The swap gives $NaNO_3$ and $KCl$.
2. Nitrates and salts of Group $1$ metals dissolve in water, so both products stay dissolved. Nothing forms a solid, a gas, water or a weak electrolyte.
3. All four ions stay in solution. Every ion is a spectator ion, an ion that stays the same during the reaction, so there is no net ionic equation.

No reaction occurs. Writing the swapped formulas did not prove a reaction.

### #2: Neutralization and Gas Formation

Name the driving force in each reaction.

(a) $HCl+NaOH\rightarrow NaCl+H_2O$  
(b) $Na_2CO_3+2HCl\rightarrow2NaCl+H_2O+CO_2$

**Solution:**

1. (a) Water forms. This is double displacement, and also an acid-base reaction.
2. (b) $CO_2$ gas escapes. This is also double displacement.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

The reaction can still happen if water, a gas or a weak electrolyte forms, so check for those too. If the question asks about the activity series, check that the free metal is higher than the one it replaces.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Assuming Every Partner Swap Works

> A swap of ion partners needs a driving force.

If both new compounds are soluble, no precipitate forms. Also check whether water, a gas or a weak electrolyte (such as a weak acid) forms, as in neutralization, carbonate with acid, or a strong acid with an acetate salt. If none of these forms, and every ion stays dissolved, there is no reaction.
