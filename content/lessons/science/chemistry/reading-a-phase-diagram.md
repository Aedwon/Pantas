## Core Concept

A **phase** is a form of matter, such as solid, liquid, or gas. A **phase diagram** is a chart of which forms a substance has under different conditions. The kind in this lesson shows one substance at different temperatures and pressures.

Temperature tells how hot or cold it is. Pressure tells how much push there is on each bit of area. The same push on a smaller area gives more pressure.

A **region** is an area of the diagram. A point inside one labeled region shows one phase. Use the label printed in that region.

A line between two regions is a **boundary**. Between the ends of a boundary, both phases can exist at the temperature and pressure shown. At the far end of the liquid-gas line, called the **critical point**, liquid and gas become alike and no longer form two separate phases.

Where the three phase boundaries meet, all three phases can exist together. This is the **triple point**.

## Examples

### #1: Inside a Region

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: phase diagram with Pressure up and Temperature right, no ticks, three boundaries from an unlabeled junction extending down-left, steeply up-right, and gently up-right; end the gently rising line at a dot inside the plot; label the area left of the steep line Solid, the area between the two rising lines Liquid, and the bottom area Gas; place K between the rising lines before the dot and clear of both lines -->

Two lines rise to the right from the meeting point. The labels are Solid left of the steep line, Liquid between the rising lines, and Gas below the gently rising line.

K sits between the rising lines without touching either one. It is to the left of the dot at the end of the gently rising line.

Find K on the diagram and read the label of its region. Which phase does K show?

(A) Solid

(B) Liquid

(C) Gas

**Solution:**

1. Find K between the two rising lines. It sits inside an area without touching a boundary.
2. The label between those lines is Liquid. Choose (B).

### #2: On a Boundary

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: phase diagram with Pressure up and Temperature right, no ticks, three boundaries from an unlabeled junction extending down-left, steeply up-right, and gently up-right; end the gently rising line at a dot inside the plot; label the area left of the steep line Solid, the area between the two rising lines Liquid, and the bottom area Gas; place M halfway along the gently rising line -->

The labels are Solid left of the steep line, Liquid between the rising lines, and Gas below the gently rising line.

M is halfway along the gently rising line, before the dot at its far end. Which phases can exist together at M?

(A) Solid and liquid

(B) Solid and gas

(C) Liquid and gas

How many phases are there at the dot at the far end of the line?

**Solution:**

1. Find M on the gently rising line. Read the labels above and below it.
2. The labels say Liquid and Gas. M is between the ends of the line, so both phases can exist there. Choose (C).
3. The dot at the far end is the critical point. There, liquid and gas become alike and form one phase.

### #3: Points R, V, and T

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: phase diagram with Pressure up and Temperature right, no ticks, three boundaries from junction T extending down-left, steeply up-right, and gently up-right; end the gently rising line at a dot inside the plot; label the area left of the steep line Solid, the area between the two rising lines Liquid, and the bottom area Gas; place R on the down-left line and V on the steep line, both away from T -->

T sits where the three lines meet. R is on the line that goes down to the left from T. V is on the steep line that rises to the right from T.

The Solid label is above the down-left line and left of the steep line. The Liquid label is between the two rising lines. The Gas label is below the down-left line and the gently rising line.

At which of these points can solid and liquid exist together?

(A) R and T

(B) T and V

(C) R and V

**Solution:**

1. At R, read Solid above the line and Gas below it.
2. At V, read Solid to the left and Liquid to the right.
3. At T, all three labeled regions meet. Solid and liquid can exist together at T and V. Choose (B).

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Find the point and check whether it is at the critical point or the triple point. Between the ends of a boundary, read the two labels beside the line. A point inside a region gets its label from that region alone.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Guessing by Position

> Use the printed labels.

The shape of a region does not tell you its phase. Check the names beside the point on the diagram in the question.
