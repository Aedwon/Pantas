## Core Concept

A **phase** is a form of matter, such as solid, liquid, or gas. A **phase diagram** shows which forms a substance can have at different temperatures and pressures. Temperature tells how hot or cold it is. Pressure tells how much push there is on a given area.

A **region** is an area of the diagram. A point inside one labeled region shows one phase. Use the label printed in that region.

A line between two regions is a **boundary**. At a point along the line between Solid and Liquid, both can exist at that temperature and pressure. The same reading rule works for the other pairs. Where the three phase boundaries meet, all three phases can exist together. This is the **triple point**.

## Examples

### #1: Inside a Region

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: phase diagram with Pressure up and Temperature right, no ticks, three boundaries from an unlabeled junction extending down-left, steeply up-right, and gently up-right to the plot edges; label upper-left region Solid, upper-right wedge Liquid, and bottom region Gas; place K inside Liquid away from both lines -->

Point K lies inside the region labeled Liquid. Which phase does K show?

(A) Solid

(B) Liquid

(C) Gas

**Solution:**

1. K is inside an area and does not touch a boundary.
2. Read the label of that area. K shows liquid. Choose (B).

### #2: On a Boundary

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: phase diagram with Pressure up and Temperature right, no ticks, three boundaries from an unlabeled junction extending down-left, steeply up-right, and gently up-right to the plot edges; label upper-left region Solid, upper-right wedge Liquid, and bottom region Gas; place M on the gently rising line away from the junction -->

Point M is on the line between Liquid and Gas, away from the place where the three lines meet. Which phases can exist together at M?

(A) Solid and liquid

(B) Solid and gas

(C) Liquid and gas

**Solution:**

1. Read the labels on both sides of M.
2. They say Liquid and Gas. Liquid and gas can exist together at M. Choose (C).

### #3: Including the Meeting Point

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: phase diagram with Pressure up and Temperature right, no ticks, three boundaries from junction T extending down-left, steeply up-right, and gently up-right to the plot edges; label upper-left region Solid, upper-right wedge Liquid, and bottom region Gas; place R on the down-left line and V on the steep line, both away from T -->

R is on the Solid and Gas boundary. V is on the Solid and Liquid boundary. T sits where the three lines meet. At which of these points can solid and liquid exist together?

(A) R and T

(B) T and V

(C) R and V

**Solution:**

1. R touches Solid and Gas. V touches Solid and Liquid.
2. T touches all three regions, including Solid and Liquid. Choose (B), T and V.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Find the point, then read the labels of the regions it touches. A point inside a region gets its label from that region alone.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Guessing by Position

> Use the printed labels.

The shape of a region does not tell you its phase. Check the names beside the point on the diagram in the question.
