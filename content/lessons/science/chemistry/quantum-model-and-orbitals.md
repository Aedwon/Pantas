## Core Concept

### The Quantum-Mechanical Model

In the years after $1920$, scientists including **Louis de Broglie, Werner Heisenberg, Erwin Schrödinger, Max Born**, and others developed **quantum mechanics**.

De Broglie suggested in $1924$ that electrons can behave like waves. Schrödinger wrote his wave equation in $1926$. Born showed that it gives the probability of finding an electron somewhere. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle ($1927$) says that an electron's exact position and exact motion cannot both be known at the same time.

Because of this, an electron has no fixed path. The model gives an **orbital**: a region around the nucleus where an electron is likely to be found. An orbital has a shape. The letters s and p name types of orbital. An s orbital is a sphere. A p orbital has two lobes, like a dumbbell. Other types have other shapes. An orbital is not the same as Bohr's orbit.

The picture called the **electron cloud** is a shaded drawing of this probability. It is darker where the electron is more likely to be. It is not a real fuzzy cloud of material.

The idea of energy levels from Bohr stayed in the model. The fixed circular paths did not. The exact equations can be solved only for hydrogen-like atoms. For atoms with more electrons, scientists use approximations.

## Examples

### #1: Orbit or Orbital?

Which statement describes the modern model of an electron in an atom? (a) It moves on a fixed round path. (b) It is likely to be found in a region called an orbital. (c) It is a smeared cloud of material.

**Solution:**

1. (a) is Bohr's orbit, which the modern model dropped.
2. (c) is wrong because the electron cloud shows probability, not material.
3. (b) matches the modern model, so the answer is (b).

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating Orbit and Orbital as the Same Thing

> An orbit is a path. An orbital is a region of probability.

Bohr's model has orbits. The quantum model has orbitals. Test items may put one in place of the other.

### #2: Crediting Schrödinger Alone

> Schrödinger made a key contribution, but many researchers built quantum mechanics.

Models often grow through the work of many people.
