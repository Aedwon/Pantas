## Core Concept

When oxygen is short, burning is **incomplete**. Some carbon ends up as carbon monoxide $CO$, which is a poisonous gas, and some as soot, which is solid carbon $C$. The hydrogen of the fuel usually still forms water.

Fuels that also contain other elements give extra products. For example, sulfur burns mainly to form $SO_2$. Metals can burn as well: $2Mg+O_2\rightarrow2MgO$.

## Examples

### #1: Incomplete Combustion

Methane burns with too little oxygen. Write balanced equations if the carbon product is (a) $CO$ and (b) soot, $C$.

**Solution:**

1. (a) Start with $CH_4+O_2\rightarrow CO+H_2O$. Put $2$ before $H_2O$ for H. O on the right is now $1+2=3$.
2. A coefficient of $\dfrac{3}{2}$ for $O_2$ would be needed, so double everything.
3. (b) Start with $CH_4+O_2\rightarrow C+H_2O$. Put $2$ before $H_2O$. O on the right is $2$, so $O_2$ gets $1$.

$$2CH_4+3O_2\rightarrow2CO+4H_2O$$

$$CH_4+O_2\rightarrow C+2H_2O$$

Complete combustion needs $2$ units of $O_2$ per $CH_4$. Incomplete combustion uses less, which shows why short oxygen supply gives less $CO_2$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Saying All Combustion Gives $CO_2$ and $H_2O$

> That product set is for complete burning of a fuel with carbon and hydrogen, and at most oxygen besides.

With too little oxygen you can get $CO$ or soot. A fuel with sulfur also gives $SO_2$.
