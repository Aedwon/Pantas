## Core Concept

A **mixture** has two or more substances that are not chemically joined. The amounts can vary. Each substance keeps its own chemical identity, so a physical method can often separate them.

A **pure substance** (an element or a compound) has a fixed composition. A compound such as water has its atoms chemically bonded in a fixed ratio. Only a chemical change can break it into elements.

The parts of a mixture can still attract each other. Salt ions in water are held by water molecules, yet the salt is still salt. The difference between a mixture and a compound is chemical identity and composition, not whether the parts interact.

### Kinds of mixtures

A **homogeneous mixture** has the same composition throughout, even when you look closely. Sugar fully dissolved in water and clean air are examples. A homogeneous mixture of a solute in a solvent is a **solution**. The **solute** is the substance that dissolves. The **solvent** is the substance that does the dissolving. 

A solution does not have to be a liquid. Air is a gas solution, and brass is a solid solution.

A **heterogeneous mixture** has parts you can tell apart, with different composition in different places. Sand in water and vegetable soup are examples.

## Examples

### #1: Homogeneous or Heterogeneous?

Classify (a) a clear sugar-water solution, (b) vegetable soup with visible pieces, and (c) clean air.

**Solution:**

1. The sugar is dissolved, and the liquid is the same throughout. It is homogeneous.
2. Soup has pieces you can see and tell apart. It is heterogeneous.
3. Clean air has the same composition in every sample of ordinary size. It is a homogeneous mixture of gases.
