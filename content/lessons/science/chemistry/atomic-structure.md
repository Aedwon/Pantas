## Core Concept

An **atom** is the smallest piece of an element that still has the element's identity. Introductory chemistry builds every atom from three kinds of **subatomic particles**, which are particles smaller than an atom: **protons**, **neutrons** and **electrons**.

The center of the atom is the **nucleus**. It is very small, and it holds the protons and neutrons. The electrons are outside the nucleus.

Charge is measured in units of $e$, the size of the charge on one proton. A proton has charge $+1e$. An electron has charge $-1e$. A neutron has charge $0$, so it is neutral.

- **Protons**: in the nucleus; charge $+1e$.
- **Neutrons**: in the nucleus; charge $0$.
- **Electrons**: outside the nucleus, in regions called orbitals; charge $-1e$.

Atomic masses are measured in the **unified atomic mass unit**, written $u$. One $u$ is one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom. A proton and a neutron each have a mass of about $1\ u$. An electron has only about $\dfrac{1}{1836}$ of a proton's mass. So almost all of an atom's mass is in its nucleus.

An **orbital** is a region around the nucleus where an electron is likely to be found. Electrons do not travel on fixed paths like planets around the Sun.

### Atomic Number and Mass Number

The **atomic number**, $Z$, is the number of protons in the nucleus. It decides which element the atom is. Every atom with $6$ protons is carbon, and no other element has $6$ protons.

A **nucleon** is a proton or a neutron. A **nuclide** is one kind of nucleus, fixed by its number of protons and its number of neutrons. The **mass number**, $A$, is the number of nucleons in a nuclide:

$$A=Z+N$$

Here $N$ is the number of neutrons. Rearranged:

$$N=A-Z$$

$A$ is a count, so it is always a whole number. The mass of the atom in $u$ is close to $A$, but it is not exactly equal to $A$.

A nuclide is written as the element symbol with $A$ at the upper left and $Z$ at the lower left. For chlorine with $A=35$ and $Z=17$, this is ${}^{35}_{17}\text{Cl}$. It can also be written as chlorine-35.

A **neutral atom** has no net charge, so it has as many electrons as protons. An ion does not, as the Ions section shows.

### Isotopes

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element that have different numbers of neutrons. They have the same $Z$ but different $N$, so they have different mass numbers.

Carbon-12 and carbon-14 both have $6$ protons. Carbon-12 has $6$ neutrons and carbon-14 has $8$.

Hydrogen shows a low edge case. Hydrogen-1 has $1$ proton and $0$ neutrons. Hydrogen-2 has $1$ proton and $1$ neutron. Hydrogen-3 has $1$ proton and $2$ neutrons. So a nucleus can have no neutrons at all, but only hydrogen-1 does this.

Neutral atoms of two isotopes of one element have the same number of electrons. For that reason, isotopes of an element behave in nearly the same way in chemical reactions.

### Average Atomic Mass

Most elements in nature are a mixture of isotopes. The **relative atomic mass** (also called standard atomic weight) is the average mass of the atoms in a natural sample of the element, in $u$. It is the decimal number shown for the element on most periodic tables.

It is a **weighted average**. An isotope that is more common counts more. To find it, multiply each isotope's mass by its fraction of the sample, then add:

$$\text{average mass}=\sum(\text{fraction}\times\text{isotope mass})$$

A fraction is the percent divided by $100$. The fractions add up to $1$.

The average is not the mass number of any one atom. The exact abundances of isotopes can vary a little between samples. Some elements have no standard value, and many tables show a bracketed mass number for them instead of a decimal.

### Ions

An **ion** is an atom or a group of bonded atoms that has a net electric charge. An ion has a different number of electrons from protons.

An atom that loses electrons has more protons than electrons, so it becomes a positive ion called a **cation**. An atom that gains electrons becomes a negative ion called an **anion**. Metal atoms usually form cations. Nonmetal atoms usually form anions.

The charge, in units of $e$, is the number of protons minus the number of electrons:

$$\text{charge}=\text{protons}-\text{electrons}$$

The charge is written at the upper right, with the number first and then the sign, as in $\text{Mg}^{2+}$. A charge of $1$ is written as just $+$ or $-$, as in $\text{Na}^{+}$.

Ions form when electrons move. The nucleus does not change, so $Z$ and $N$ stay the same. The hydrogen ion $\text{H}^{+}$ is a hydrogen-1 atom that has lost its only electron, so it has $1$ proton and no electrons.

A **polyatomic ion** is a group of bonded atoms with a net charge, such as $\text{NH}_4^{+}$ and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$. The same formula works if you add up the protons of all the atoms and the electrons of the whole group.

## Examples

### #1: Subatomic Particle Counts

A neutral chlorine-35 atom has $Z=17$ and $A=35$. How many protons, neutrons and electrons does it have?

**Solution:**

1. Protons equal $Z$: $17$.
2. Neutrons:

$$N=35-17=18$$

3. The atom is neutral, so electrons equal protons: $17$.

### #2: Hydrogen-1 and Zero Neutrons

How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in a neutral hydrogen-1 atom? Hydrogen has $Z=1$.

**Solution:**

1. Protons equal $Z$: $1$.
2. The mass number is $1$, so neutrons:

$$N=1-1=0$$

3. The atom is neutral, so electrons: $1$.

### #3: Identifying Isotopes

Two nuclei each contain $6$ protons. One contains $6$ neutrons and the other contains $8$ neutrons. What is the relationship between them?

**Solution:**

1. Both have $Z=6$, so both are carbon.
2. They have different $N$, so they are different isotopes of carbon.
3. Their mass numbers are $6+6=12$ and $6+8=14$. They are carbon-12 and carbon-14.

### #4: Average Atomic Mass

A made-up element has two natural isotopes. Mass $63\ u$ makes up $70\%$ of the atoms, and mass $65\ u$ makes up $30\%$. Calculate its average atomic mass.

**Solution:**

1. Change the percents to fractions: $0.70$ and $0.30$.
2. Multiply each mass by its fraction and add:

$$0.70\times63+0.30\times65=44.1+19.5$$

3. The average atomic mass is $63.6\ u$.

The answer is between $63$ and $65$ and closer to $63$, because the lighter isotope is more common.

### #5: Ion Charge from Particle Counts

A sulfur atom has $16$ protons. It gains $2$ electrons. What is the charge on the ion?

**Solution:**

1. The electron count becomes $16+2=18$.
2. Charge:

$$16-18=-2$$

3. The ion is $\text{S}^{2-}$. It is an anion.

### #6: Electron Count from Charge

A calcium-40 ion has $20$ protons and a charge of $2+$. How many electrons and neutrons does it have?

**Solution:**

1. Use charge equals protons minus electrons:

$$+2=20-\text{electrons}$$

2. So electrons:

$$20-2=18$$

3. Neutrons do not change when an ion forms:

$$N=40-20=20$$

### #7: Particles in a Nuclide Symbol with a Charge

How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in the ion $^{56}_{26}\text{Fe}^{3+}$?

**Solution:**

1. The lower number is $Z$, so protons: $26$.
2. Neutrons:

$$N=56-26=30$$

3. The charge is $+3$, so there are $3$ more protons than electrons:

$$26-3=23$$

### #8: Polyatomic Ion

How many protons and electrons are in one $\text{NH}_4^{+}$ ion? Nitrogen has $Z=7$ and hydrogen has $Z=1$.

**Solution:**

1. Protons: one nitrogen atom and four hydrogen atoms.

$$7+4\times1=11$$

2. The charge is $+1$, so there is one fewer electron than protons.

$$11-1=10$$

### #9: Telling Species Apart

Each species below is described by its protons, neutrons and electrons. Which two are isotopes of the same element?

- P: $17$ protons, $18$ neutrons, $17$ electrons
- Q: $17$ protons, $20$ neutrons, $17$ electrons
- R: $17$ protons, $18$ neutrons, $18$ electrons
- S: $18$ protons, $22$ neutrons, $18$ electrons

**Solution:**

1. Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
2. P, Q and R all have $17$ protons, so they are the same element.
3. P and Q have different neutron numbers ($18$ and $20$), so P and Q are isotopes.
4. P and R have the same neutron number. R has one extra electron, so R is an ion of the same nuclide as P, with charge $17-18=-1$.
5. S has $18$ protons, so it is a different element.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For a given nuclide, use $N=A-Z$. The lower number is $Z$ and the upper number is $A$.

For any ion, use charge equals protons minus electrons. A positive charge means electrons are fewer than protons. A negative charge means electrons are more than protons.

To tell what changed, check which count differs. A different neutron number with the same proton number is an isotope. A different electron number with the same proton number is an ion. A different proton number is a different element.

For an average atomic mass, the answer must lie between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses. It is closer to the mass of the more common isotope.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Drawing Electrons on Literal Planetary Orbits

> Ring diagrams of the atom are simple pictures of energy levels, not real paths.

In the modern model, electrons are described by orbitals, where they are likely to be found. Do not read a ring diagram as an electron's actual path.

### #2: Confusing Mass Number and Periodic-Table Atomic Mass

> Mass number is a whole-number count for one nuclide. The decimal on the periodic table is a weighted average.

Use the value that the problem names. If the problem gives "chlorine-35", use $A=35$ for the neutron count. Do not round the table value for that.

### #3: Saying Isotopes or Ions Change Proton Count

> If the proton number changes, the element changes.

Isotopes differ in neutron number. Ions differ in electron number. Both keep the same $Z$.

### #4: Forgetting That Ions Can Be Groups of Atoms

> An ion can be one atom or a bonded group of atoms with a net charge.

Examples are $\text{Na}^{+}$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ and $\text{NH}_4^{+}$. For a group, add the protons of all its atoms before you use the charge formula.

### #5: Reading the Sign of the Charge Backwards

> A positive ion has lost electrons, so it has fewer electrons than protons.

A charge of $3+$ means $3$ electrons fewer than protons, not $3$ more. Check the sign before you subtract.
