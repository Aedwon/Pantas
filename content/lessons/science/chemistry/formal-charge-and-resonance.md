## Core Concept

**Formal charge** is the charge an atom would have if every bonding pair were shared equally, so each atom owns its nonbonding electrons and half of each bond. It helps you compare possible structures:

$$FC=V-L-\dfrac{B}{2}$$

Here $V$ is the atom's valence electrons, $L$ is its nonbonding electrons, which are the electrons in its lone pairs plus a single unpaired electron if it has one, and $B$ is the electrons in its bonds. Prefer the structure with formal charges nearest zero. The charges must add up to the charge of the species.

Some species need **resonance**. Two or more valid Lewis structures use the same atom order but differ in where the double or triple bonds and lone pairs sit. The real molecule is a blend of them. Equivalent structures give equal bond lengths, as in carbonate below.

## Examples

### #1: Formal Charge and Resonance of $CO_3^{2-}$

Find a Lewis structure for the carbonate ion, $CO_3^{2-}$, with its formal charges.

**Solution:**

1. Valence electrons: $4+3(6)+2=24$.
2. Carbon is in the center with three single bonds. Each oxygen gets three lone pairs. That uses all $24$ electrons, but carbon has only six.
3. Make one oxygen a double bond to carbon. That oxygen keeps two lone pairs.
4. Use $FC=V-L-\dfrac{B}{2}$, where $B$ counts the bonding electrons. Carbon has four bonds, so $B=8$:

$$FC_{C}=4-0-\dfrac{8}{2}=0$$

5. The double-bond oxygen has $L=4$ and $B=4$:

$$FC_{O}=6-4-\dfrac{4}{2}=0$$

6. Each single-bond oxygen has $L=6$ and $B=2$:

$$FC_{O}=6-6-\dfrac{2}{2}=-1$$

7. The total is $0+0-1-1=-2$, which matches the ion.
8. Any of the three oxygens can be the double-bond one. So there are three resonance structures, and the real ion has three equal $C-O$ bonds, because the structures are equivalent.
