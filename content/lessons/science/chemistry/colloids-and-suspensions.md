## Core Concept

A **suspension** is a heterogeneous mixture with particles big enough to settle out if left alone. Muddy water is one. Its larger particles are caught by filter paper.

A **colloid** is in between. Its particles are too small to settle or to be seen, but large enough to scatter a beam of light. Milk and fog are colloids.

A light beam shining through a colloid shows its path, which is called the Tyndall effect. A true solution does not scatter light this way.

Colloid particles are small enough to pass through ordinary filter paper. So a colloid cannot be separated by ordinary filtering, even though it is not a true solution.

## Examples

### #1: Milk or Muddy Water?

A flashlight beam is shone through two clear containers. In one, the beam's path is visible and the liquid does not settle. In the other, particles slowly sink to the bottom. Which is a colloid, and which is a suspension?

**Solution:**

1. A visible light path means the particles scatter light but stay mixed. This is a colloid, such as milk.
2. Particles that settle out on standing are a suspension, such as muddy water.
3. Filter paper will hold back the suspension particles. It will not hold back the colloid particles.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Judging a Mixture by Its Looks

> A mixture that looks uniform can still be a colloid, and a clear liquid can still be a mixture.

Clear tap water or air is a mixture even though it looks like one thing. A colloid looks uniform but scatters light and passes through filter paper. Do not call something a pure substance just because it looks smooth.
