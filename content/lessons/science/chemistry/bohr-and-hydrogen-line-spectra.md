## Core Concept

### Bohr and Quantized Hydrogen Levels

Hot hydrogen gas gives off light. When this light is split by a prism, it is not a smooth rainbow. It is a few thin bright lines of single colors, called a **line spectrum**. Hydrogen shows four main lines in the visible range.

In $1913$, **Niels Bohr** added a new idea to the nuclear atom: an electron in an atom can have only certain energies. Each allowed energy is an **energy level**. This is called **quantization**, because the energy comes in fixed amounts and not in any amount. A **photon** is one packet of light, and its energy decides its color.

In Bohr's model, an electron in a level does not give off light.

When it drops to a lower level, it gives off a photon whose energy equals the difference between the two levels. When it absorbs a photon of exactly that energy, it rises to a higher level. Only certain energy differences exist, so only certain colors appear. This matches the lines of hydrogen.

The Bohr model works well for hydrogen and for ions with one electron, such as He$^+$. An **ion** is an atom or group of atoms with an electric charge, and an atom becomes one by gaining or losing electrons. It does not correctly describe atoms with many electrons. It also treats electrons as particles on fixed circular paths, which the later model replaced.

## Examples

### #1: Why Hydrogen Gives Lines

A student says that hot hydrogen should give a smooth rainbow. Explain why it gives separate lines.

**Solution:**

1. In Bohr's model, an electron has only certain energy levels.
2. When it drops from one level to a lower one, it gives off a photon with energy equal to the difference.
3. Only certain differences exist, so only certain photon energies and colors appear.
4. For example, the drop from level $3$ to level $2$ gives the red line.
5. These separate colors show as lines, not a smooth rainbow.

### #2: What Did Bohr Actually Explain Well?

A student says, "Bohr's fixed-orbit model accurately explains the spectra and electron structures of all atoms." Correct the statement.

**Solution:**

1. Bohr's model explained the line spectrum of hydrogen well. It also works for ions with one electron.
2. It does not correctly describe atoms with many electrons.
3. Modern chemistry uses orbitals, not fixed circular paths. The idea of energy levels stayed.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating Bohr Orbits as the Current Model

> Bohr's model is important and useful for hydrogen-like atoms.

Modern atomic structure uses orbitals, not fixed circular paths.
