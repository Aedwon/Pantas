## Core Concept

### Electronegativity and Bond Polarity

**Electronegativity** is how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons toward itself. It generally rises toward the upper right of the periodic table, leaving out the noble gases, and fluorine is highest.

If two bonded atoms differ in electronegativity, the shared electrons sit closer to one atom. That atom is partly negative ($\delta^-$) and the other is partly positive ($\delta^+$). The bond is **polar**.

The bigger the difference, the more polar the bond.

A polar bond is a **dipole**: two equal and opposite partial charges a short distance apart. We picture it as a pull of electrons toward the more electronegative atom. A very large difference gives an ionic bond, and books use different cutoffs, so treat any cutoff as a guide.

## Examples

### #1: Bond Polarity

The electronegativities are $H=2.20$, $C=2.55$, $O=3.44$, and $F=3.98$. Which bond is most polar: $C-H$, $O-H$, or $H-F$? Which atom is partly negative in it?

**Solution:**

1. $C-H$: $2.55-2.20=0.35$.
2. $O-H$: $3.44-2.20=1.24$.
3. $H-F$: $3.98-2.20=1.78$.
4. $H-F$ has the largest difference, so it is most polar. Fluorine pulls the electrons, so $F$ is $\delta^-$.
