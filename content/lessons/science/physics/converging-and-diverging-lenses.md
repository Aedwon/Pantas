## Core Concept

### Lenses

A **lens** forms images by refraction. A **convex lens** is thicker in the middle. A **concave lens** is thinner in the middle.

For a glass or plastic lens in air, a convex lens is converging and a concave lens is diverging. A lens has a focal length $f$ too. A thin lens has a focal point on each side. We take the object on the left and the light moving to the right.

<!-- FIGURE: {"kind":"physics-energy-diagram","type":"lens-comparison","illustrationKey":"science/physics/light-and-optics#2","convexBehavior":"parallel rays bend inward to one focus point","concaveBehavior":"parallel rays spread outward"} -->

This rule depends on the lens having a larger $n$ than its surroundings. A convex-shaped lens made of a material with a lower $n$ than the medium around it diverges light instead. An air bubble shaped like a convex lens inside water is one case. Its $f$ is negative, because $f$ follows converging or diverging, not the shape.

## Examples

### #1: Convex Lens Making a Real Image

A convex lens in air has a focal length of $15$ cm. A slide is $20$ cm from the lens. Describe the image.

**Solution:**

The slide is between $f$ and $2f$. The lens brings the rays together on the far side. It makes a real, inverted image that is larger than the slide.

### #2: A Magnifying Glass and a Concave Lens

(a) A magnifying glass in air has a focal length of $5$ cm. An insect is $4$ cm from it. (b) A concave glass lens in air has a focal length of magnitude $10$ cm. An object is $30$ cm from it. Describe both images.

**Solution:**

1. The insect is inside the focal length. The rays leave the magnifying glass spreading apart. They appear to come from a larger, upright insect, so the image is virtual.
2. The concave lens spreads the rays. They appear to come from a smaller, upright object on the same side as the real object. This image is virtual too.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating Lens Shape Alone as Universal

> Whether a lens converges depends on its shape and on its $n$ compared with the surroundings.

The familiar "convex converges, concave diverges" rule is for a lens with a larger $n$ than the medium around it, such as glass in air.
