## Core Concept

Each formula in Motion with Constant Acceleration leaves out one quantity.

Use the formula that leaves out the quantity you do not need. One leaves out $\Delta x$, one leaves out $v$, one leaves out $t$, and one leaves out $a$.

They do not work if the acceleration changes during the motion.

## Examples

### #1: The Equation Without Time

A car moving in the positive direction has $v_0=10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $a=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. It travels $\Delta x=60$ m. Find its final velocity.

**Solution:**

1. Time is not given, so use the equation that leaves out $t$:

$$v^2=10^2+2(2)(60)=340$$

2. Taking the square root gives $v=\pm18.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
3. The car speeds up in the positive direction, so $v=+18.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Before you pick an equation, list $v_0$, $v$, $a$, $t$, and $\Delta x$. Mark which are known and which you want. Pick the equation that has these and leaves out the missing one.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Using the Equations When Acceleration Changes

> The four equations need constant acceleration.

If the velocity-time graph is curved, the acceleration is changing and the equations do not apply. If the motion has separate parts where $a$ is constant, use the equations on each part.
