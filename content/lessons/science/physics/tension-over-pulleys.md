## Core Concept

Two objects tied to the ends of a tight rope have accelerations of the same size, if the rope passes only over fixed pulleys. Over a pulley the directions can differ: one object moves up while the other moves down, so give each its own sign.

A movable pulley changes this. If one end of the rope is fixed and a block hangs from a movable pulley, that block's acceleration is half the acceleration of the free end. A heavy rope or a rough pulley also breaks these rules.

## Examples

### #1: Two Blocks Over a Pulley

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ block A and a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ block B hang from the two ends of an ideal rope. The rope passes over an ideal fixed pulley. Use $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The blocks are released from rest. Find the acceleration and the tension.

**Solution:**

1. The rope is tight and the pulley is fixed, so both blocks have accelerations of the same size $a$. A is heavier, so A moves down and B moves up.
2. Write the second law for each block, with its own sign. For A, take down as positive. The weight is $3\times 9.8=29.4\,\mathrm N$ down and the tension $T$ is up.

$$29.4-T=3a.$$

3. For B, take up as positive. The weight is $2\times 9.8=19.6\,\mathrm N$ down and the tension is up.

$$T-19.6=2a.$$

4. Add the two equations. The tension cancels.

$$29.4-19.6=5a\Rightarrow a=\dfrac{9.8}{5}=1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.$$

5. Put $a$ into the equation for B.

$$T=19.6+2\times 1.96=23.52\,\mathrm N.$$

6. Check with A: $29.4-23.52=5.88=3\times 1.96$.

The tension is $23.52\,\mathrm N$, between the two weights. The acceleration is not $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, because the rope holds each block back.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Do not use this over a pulley, where one block moves up and the other moves down. Write the second law for each block, with its own sign for the direction it moves (see example #7).
