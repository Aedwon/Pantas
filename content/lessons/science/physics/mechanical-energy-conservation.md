## Core Concept

### Conservation of Energy

Energy is never created or destroyed. It changes form or moves from place to place. The total energy of an isolated system stays constant. An isolated system is a group of objects with no energy entering or leaving it from outside. If a motor or a push from outside adds energy, the system is not isolated, and its total energy rises if more energy comes in than leaves.

**Mechanical energy** is $KE+PE$. In this lesson, $PE$ is gravitational potential energy. A spring also stores energy, and that stored energy counts in $PE$ too. A **conservative force**, such as gravity, can give back the energy it takes. If only conservative forces do work, mechanical energy stays the same:

$$KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f$$

## Examples

### #1: Conservation of Energy on a Slide

A $10\ \text{kg}$ child starts from rest at the top of a frictionless slide, $3\ \text{m}$ above the bottom. Find the speed at the bottom. Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.

**Solution:**
With no friction, mechanical energy stays constant. Choose the bottom as $PE=0$.

1. Energy at the top. The child starts at rest, so $KE_i=0$.

$$PE_i = (10)(9.8)(3) = 294\ \text{J}$$

2. At the bottom, all of it is kinetic energy.

$$294 = \dfrac{1}{2}(10)v^2$$

3. Solve for $v$.

$$v^2 = 58.8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v \approx 7.67\ \text{m/s}$$

Check the mass. Writing $mgh=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$, the $m$ cancels, so a lighter child would reach the same speed.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

If an object starts from rest and only gravity does work, mass cancels. Then $mgh=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ gives $v=\sqrt{2gh}$, where $h$ is the drop in height. It fails if the object already has speed or if any other force does work.
