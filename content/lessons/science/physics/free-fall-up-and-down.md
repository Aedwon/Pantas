## Core Concept

### Free Fall

**Free fall** is motion with only gravity acting. This includes an object going up after a throw. Near Earth's surface, the acceleration is $g\approx9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ toward the ground.

This ignores air resistance. Then all objects fall with the same acceleration, whatever their mass.

For vertical motion we write the position as $y$ and the displacement as $\Delta y$. The equations above work the same way with $y$ in place of $x$.

If up is positive, $a=-g$. If down is positive, $a=+g$. The acceleration stays the same on the way up, at the top, and on the way down.

## Examples

### #1: Dropped Object

A rock is dropped and falls for $3$ s. Down is positive and $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the distance fallen and the final velocity.

**Solution:**

1. "Dropped" means it is released, not pushed. So $v_0=0$.
2. Distance, using $\Delta y=v_0t+\dfrac12at^2$ with $v_0t=0$:

$$\Delta y=\dfrac12(9.8)(3^2)=44.1\text{ m}$$

3. Velocity:

$$v=0+(9.8)(3)=29.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$$

4. Down is positive, so the rock moves downward.

### #2: Thrown Upward

A ball is thrown straight up at $19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Up is positive and $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the time to the top, the greatest height, and the velocity when the ball returns to its starting level.

**Solution:**

1. Here $a=-9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At the top, $v=0$.
2. Time to the top:

$$t=\dfrac{0-19.6}{-9.8}=2.0\text{ s}$$

3. Greatest height:

$$\Delta y=\dfrac{19.6+0}{2}(2.0)=19.6\text{ m}$$

4. At the starting level, $\Delta y=0$, so $v^2=19.6^2$. The ball is moving down, so $v=-19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Words in the problem give hidden values. "Starts from rest" or "is dropped" means $v_0=0$. "Comes to a stop" means $v=0$. At the top of a straight-up throw, $v=0$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Zero Velocity Means Zero Acceleration

> At the top of a straight-up throw, $v=0$ but $a=-9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ (up is positive).

The velocity is changing at that instant, so the acceleration is not zero.

### #2: Heavier Objects Fall Faster

> Without air resistance, every object falls with the same acceleration.

In air, drag changes the motion. A feather falls slowly because of air, not because it is light.
