## Core Concept

Earth moves in several ways at the same time. Three of them matter here: **rotation**, **revolution**, and **precession**. Each one explains a different thing we notice.

Some words first. Earth's **axis** is the imaginary line through the North Pole and the South Pole. The **equator** is the imaginary circle around Earth halfway between the poles.

The part of Earth north of the equator is the **Northern Hemisphere**. The part south of it is the **Southern Hemisphere**.

### Rotation

**Rotation** is Earth's spin around its own axis. Earth spins toward the east.

The daily trip of the Sun, Moon, and stars across the sky is only apparent. It happens because the ground under us turns.

Seen from most places on Earth, the Sun, Moon, and stars therefore appear to rise on the eastern side of the sky and set on the western side.

One turn relative to the Sun takes about $24$ hours on average. This gives the familiar **solar day**, with day and night.

Relative to the distant stars, one turn takes about $23$ hours $56$ minutes. This is the **sidereal day**. It is about $4$ minutes shorter because Earth also moves along its orbit during the day.

After one turn, Earth must turn a little more, about $1^\circ$, before the Sun crosses the local meridian again, at solar noon.

The same fact explains why a given star rises about $4$ minutes earlier each night. After a month it rises about $2$ hours earlier.

## Examples

### #1: Solar Day and Sidereal Day

A star is directly overhead at midnight on one night. About when will it be directly overhead the next night, by clock time, and why?

**Solution:**
1. Relative to the stars, Earth turns once in about $23$ hours $56$ minutes.
2. The clock day is $24$ hours, so the star returns to the same place about $4$ minutes earlier by the clock.
3. The star is overhead at about $11{:}56$ PM. Earth moves along its orbit as it spins, so the Sun needs about $4$ more minutes of turning to cross the local meridian again.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Keep the roles separate: **rotation gives day and night**, **revolution with tilt gives seasons and the year**, **precession slowly changes the direction of the axis**.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Solar Day vs. Sidereal Day

> The familiar day is about $24$ hours relative to the Sun. Relative to the stars, it is about $23$ hours $56$ minutes.

The two values differ because Earth also moves along its orbit while it rotates. Do not treat the $24$-hour solar day as the time of one full spin.
