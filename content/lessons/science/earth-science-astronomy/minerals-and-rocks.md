## Core Concept

A **mineral** is a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with an orderly internal structure and a characteristic chemical composition or limited compositional range. Quartz, feldspar, calcite, and olivine are common minerals.

A **rock** is an aggregate of one or more minerals or other consolidated/undifferentiated mineral matter. Some rocks are dominated by one mineral; others contain many minerals. Rocks are classified mainly by how they form.

### Igneous Rocks

**Igneous rocks** form when molten rock cools and solidifies.

- **Intrusive** igneous rocks cool below the surface, generally slowly enough to grow visible crystals. Granite is a common example.
- **Extrusive** igneous rocks cool at or near the surface. Rapid cooling generally produces small crystals; very rapid cooling can produce volcanic glass such as obsidian, which lacks a crystalline mineral structure.

### Sedimentary Rocks

**Sedimentary rocks** form when sediments are deposited and then lithified through compaction and cementation, or when minerals precipitate chemically/biologically. Examples include sandstone, shale, and limestone. Fossils are especially common in sedimentary rocks because the temperatures and pressures involved are usually much lower than in igneous or high-grade metamorphic settings.

### Metamorphic Rocks

**Metamorphic rocks** form when existing rock is changed by heat, pressure, and/or chemically active fluids **without wholesale melting**. Examples include limestone changing to marble, sandstone changing to quartzite, and shale changing to slate under suitable conditions.

### The Rock Cycle

The **rock cycle** is a network, not a single fixed loop. Any rock exposed at the surface can weather into sediment. Buried rocks can undergo metamorphism. Rocks that melt can later cool into igneous rock. A transformation requires the relevant process and materials; simply naming any three rock types does not make every direct pathway equally plausible.

## Examples

### #1: Mineral or Rock?

Quartz is a naturally occurring inorganic crystalline solid with a characteristic composition, while granite is an aggregate containing quartz, feldspar, and other minerals. Which is the mineral and which is the rock?

**Solution:**
Quartz is a mineral. Granite is a rock made of multiple minerals.

### #2: Cooling Rate and Texture

Two magmas have similar composition. One cools slowly deep underground; the other erupts and cools quickly at the surface. Which one is more likely to have larger visible crystals?

**Solution:**
The slowly cooled intrusive rock is more likely to develop larger crystals because crystals have more time to grow. The rapidly cooled extrusive rock generally has finer crystals or, if quenched extremely rapidly, a glassy texture.

### #3: A Valid Rock-Cycle Pathway

Describe a plausible pathway that turns granite into quartzite through a sedimentary stage.

**Solution:**
1. Granite exposed at the surface weathers and erodes, releasing mineral grains including quartz.
2. Quartz-rich sand can be transported, deposited, compacted, and cemented into sandstone.
3. If that sandstone is later buried and subjected to sufficient heat and pressure without melting, it can metamorphose into quartzite.
4. This is a coherent pathway because the sedimentary precursor of quartzite is sandstone. Marble, by contrast, forms mainly from carbonate rocks such as limestone or dolostone.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Classify a rock by **formation process**, not merely by appearance: cooled melt → igneous; lithified/precipitated sedimentary material → sedimentary; solid-state change under heat/pressure/fluids → metamorphic.

A common metamorphic pair to remember is **limestone → marble** and **sandstone → quartzite**.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Forgetting “Inorganic” in the Mineral Definition

> A mineral is not simply any natural solid crystal.

Introductory mineral definitions include naturally occurring, inorganic, solid, ordered internal structure, and characteristic composition.

### #2: Saying Metamorphism Means Melting

> Metamorphism changes a rock while it remains largely solid.

If the rock melts and later solidifies, the resulting rock is igneous.

### #3: Assuming Every Rock-Cycle Arrow Is a Direct One-Step Transformation

> Rock-cycle paths must respect the material and process involved.

Granite-derived quartz-rich sediment can become sandstone and then quartzite; it does not simply become limestone merely because both are sedimentary rocks.
