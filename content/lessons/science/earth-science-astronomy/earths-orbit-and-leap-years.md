## Core Concept

### Revolution and Axial Tilt

**Revolution** is Earth's trip around the Sun along its path, called its **orbit**. One trip measured against the stars takes about $365.26$ days.

Calendars follow the seasonal year instead, about $365.24$ days, from one March equinox to the next. It is slightly shorter because of precession (explained below).

### Leap Years

A calendar year has a whole number of days, but the seasonal (tropical) year is about $365.24$ days. The extra $0.24$ of a day adds up to about one day every four years. A **leap year** has $366$ days, with February $29$, to catch up.

The **Gregorian calendar**, the one in common use today, sets this rule:

1. A year divisible by $4$ is a leap year.
2. But a year divisible by $100$ (a century year) is not a leap year.
3. Unless it is also divisible by $400$. Then it is a leap year.

Adding a day every fourth year is slightly too much, so the century rule removes a few. Over $400$ years there are $97$ leap years, which gives an average year of $365.2425$ days, very close to the seasonal year of $365.24$ days.

## Examples

### #1: Rotation vs. Revolution

Classify each statement as caused mainly by Earth's rotation or Earth's revolution: (a) the daily rising and setting of the Sun, and (b) the yearly cycle of seasons.

**Solution:**
1. The daily rising and setting happens because Earth spins once a day. This is rotation.
2. The yearly cycle of seasons comes from Earth's trip around the Sun with a tilted axis. This is revolution, together with the axial tilt.

### #2: Leap-Year Check

Which of these years are leap years in the Gregorian calendar: $2000$, $1900$, $2024$, and $2100$?

**Solution:**
1. $2024$ is divisible by $4$ and is not a century year, so it is a leap year.
2. $1900$ is divisible by $100$ but not by $400$, so it is not a leap year.
3. $2000$ is divisible by $400$, so it is a leap year.
4. $2100$ is divisible by $100$ but not by $400$, so it is not a leap year.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For leap years, test $4$ first. If the year ends in two zeros, test $400$ instead. A century year such as $1700$ or $2100$ that is not divisible by $400$ is not a leap year.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Every Fourth Year Has a Century Exception

> Leap years are not simply every fourth year.

Century years such as $1900$ and $2100$ are skipped unless divisible by $400$. So $2000$ was a leap year, but $1900$ was not.
