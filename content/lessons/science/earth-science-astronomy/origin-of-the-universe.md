## Core Concept

### The Big Bang Model

The **Big Bang model** describes an early universe that was extremely hot and dense and has been expanding and cooling for about **13.8 billion years**. It is misleading to picture a bomb exploding from one location into pre-existing empty space. In modern cosmology, the expansion is an expansion of space itself, and there is no known central point in ordinary space from which all galaxies fly outward.

Important evidence includes:

- **Cosmic expansion:** on large scales, more distant galaxies generally show larger cosmological redshifts, consistent with an expanding universe.
- **Cosmic microwave background (CMB):** faint microwave radiation fills the sky and is interpreted as relic radiation from the hot early universe, released when the universe cooled enough for neutral atoms to form about $380{,}000$ years after the Big Bang.
- **Primordial light elements:** the observed abundances of hydrogen, helium, and some lithium broadly match predictions of Big Bang nucleosynthesis.


The Big Bang model describes the universe's early evolution from a hot dense state. It does not by itself establish what, if anything, “caused” the Big Bang or what preceded the earliest times current physics can describe.

### Formation of the Solar System

The leading **nebular model** says the Solar System began forming about **4.6 billion years ago** from part of a cold molecular cloud of gas and dust. Gravity caused the region to collapse. As it contracted, conservation of angular momentum helped it spin faster and flatten into a **protoplanetary disk**.

Most material collected at the center to form the young Sun. In the surrounding disk, dust and solid particles collided and accumulated into larger bodies such as planetesimals and planetary embryos. Continued accretion and dynamical interactions produced the planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and other Solar System bodies. The details of planet formation remain an active research field, so the textbook “nebular hypothesis” is a simplified model of a complex process.

## Examples

### #1: Big Bang Misconception

A diagram shows all matter exploding outward from one location into an already existing empty universe. What is wrong with this picture?

**Solution:**
1. The Big Bang was not a conventional explosion from a center into surrounding empty space.
2. Modern cosmology describes the universe itself—including space—as expanding from an earlier hot, dense state.
3. Large-scale expansion is observed everywhere, with no identified central point of expansion in ordinary three-dimensional space.

### #2: What the CMB Tells Us

Why is the cosmic microwave background important evidence for the Big Bang model?

**Solution:**
The CMB is relic radiation from a time when the young universe had cooled enough for light to travel freely. Its nearly uniform presence across the sky and measured spectrum are strong evidence that the universe was once much hotter and denser.

### #3: From Cloud to Planets

Put these Solar System formation stages in a sensible order: protoplanetary disk, molecular cloud collapse, planetesimals, planets.

**Solution:**
1. A region of a molecular cloud collapses under gravity.
2. The collapsing material forms a rotating protoplanetary disk with the young Sun at the center.
3. Solid particles accrete into planetesimals and larger embryos.
4. Continued growth and interactions produce planets and other bodies.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Keep the two origin models separate: **Big Bang → evolution of the universe**, while **nebular/protoplanetary-disk model → formation of our Solar System much later**.

For Big Bang evidence, remember **expansion + CMB + light-element abundances**. For Solar System formation, remember **cloud collapse → rotating disk → accretion**.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: The Big Bang Was Not a Firecracker in Empty Space

> There is no known spatial center of the universe's expansion.

The “explosion” analogy can create the wrong geometry.

### #2: CMB Came Before the First Stars

> The CMB was released roughly $380{,}000$ years after the Big Bang, before the first stars formed.

### #3: A Nearby Supernova Trigger Is Possible, Not a Required Fact

> Some models invoke a nearby stellar event as a possible trigger for collapse of the presolar cloud, but the essential nebular model does not require treating one specific trigger as established.
