## Core Concept

**Latitude** tells how far north or south a place is from the **equator**, the line halfway between the North and South Poles. Read $15^\circ$ N as “fifteen degrees north of the equator.” The small circle means degrees, N means north, and S means south.

On a map with latitude lines, read the line at each place or find the lines it lies between.

At the same time of year, places at nearby latitudes on the same side of the equator usually get a similar number of daylight hours. The Sun also usually reaches about the same height in the sky around local midday. Near the poles, a small change in latitude can mean a big change in hours of daylight.

Clouds can change how much sunlight reaches the ground. Similar sunlight patterns make similar **climates**, or usual weather over many years, more likely when other conditions are alike.

**Sea level** is the usual height of the sea's surface. **Altitude** tells how high a place is compared with sea level. High places tend to be cooler.

The sea can keep coastal temperatures from changing as much as those far inland. Winds can bring moist air from the sea or dry air from land.

## Examples

### #1: Three Places on a Map

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: map grid with horizontal lines labeled 45° N, 30° N, 15° N, and Equator from top to bottom; place P in the west just below 15° N, Q in the east just above 15° N, and R on 45° N directly north of P -->

P sits just below the $15^\circ$ N line, between that line and the equator. Q sits just above it, between $15^\circ$ N and $30^\circ$ N. R sits on the $45^\circ$ N line.

Which pair has the best latitude evidence for similar climates?

(A) P and Q

(B) P and R

(C) Q and R

**Solution:**

1. Check the nearby lines. P and Q are both close to $15^\circ$ N. R is much farther north at $45^\circ$ N.
2. P and Q are closest in latitude, so they are likely to have the most similar sunlight patterns.
3. The map gives no other climate information, so similar sunlight is the strongest clue that P and Q have similar climates. Choose (A).

### #2: Same Latitude, Different Heights

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: map with a horizontal 20° N line and an Equator line below it; place Low Town and Hill Town at separate spots on 20° N; label Low Town near sea level and Hill Town well above sea level -->

Low Town is near sea level. Hill Town is well above sea level. Both are on $20^\circ$ N.

Which town would you expect to be cooler if other conditions are alike?

**Solution:**

1. Both towns share a latitude and have similar sunlight patterns.
2. Hill Town is higher. It will likely be cooler.

### #3: Shore and Inland

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: map with the sea at left and dry land at right; place Shore and Inland on the same horizontal latitude line above the Equator, with wind arrows from sea toward Shore and from dry land toward Inland -->

Shore and Inland share a latitude. Winds often reach Shore from the sea and Inland from dry land. What could explain why Shore gets more rain?

(A) Shore is closer to the equator.

(B) Winds bring moist air to Shore.

(C) Inland gets longer days.

**Solution:**

1. The towns share a latitude, so that clue alone cannot explain the rain.
2. Winds from the sea can bring moist air to Shore. That can help explain the extra rain. Choose (B).

### #4: By the Sea and Far Inland

Bay Town and Field Town are on the same latitude line and both are near sea level. Bay Town is by the sea. Field Town is far inland.

Which town would usually have smaller temperature changes from season to season?

**Solution:**

1. The towns have similar latitude and height. Bay Town is closer to the sea.
2. Seawater warms and cools more slowly than land. This helps keep temperatures in Bay Town from changing as much.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Compare latitude first when the map gives only locations. Use any extra clues about height, water, or winds when the question gives them.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Promising a Match

> A latitude line gives a clue.

Two places on the same line can still have different climates. A mountain town may be colder than a low town on the same line.
