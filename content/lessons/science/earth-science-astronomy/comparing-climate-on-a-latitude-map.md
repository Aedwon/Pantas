## Core Concept

**Latitude** tells how far north or south a place is from the **equator**, the line halfway between the North and South Poles. On a map with latitude lines, read the line at each place or find the lines it lies between.

Places at similar latitudes on the same side of the equator have similar day lengths and Sun heights at the same time of year. Clouds can change how much sunlight reaches the ground. Similar sunlight patterns make similar **climates**, or usual weather over many years, more likely when other conditions are alike.

**Altitude** means height above sea level. High places tend to be cooler. The sea can keep coastal temperatures from changing as much as those far inland. Winds can bring moist air from the sea or dry air from land.

## Examples

### #1: Matching Latitude Lines

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: map grid with horizontal lines labeled 45° N, 15° N, and Equator from top to bottom; place P in the west and Q in the east on 15° N, and R on 45° N directly north of P -->

P and Q lie on $15^\circ$ N. R is directly north of P on $45^\circ$ N. Which pair has the best latitude evidence for similar climates?

(A) P and Q

(B) P and R

(C) Q and R

**Solution:**

1. P and Q share a latitude line.
2. They have similar patterns of sunlight across the year. Choose (A), P and Q.

### #2: Same Latitude, Different Heights

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: map with a horizontal 20° N line and an Equator line below it; mark Low Town on a plain and Hill Town high on a mountain at separate spots on 20° N -->

Low Town lies on a plain. Hill Town lies high on a mountain. Both are on $20^\circ$ N. Which town would you expect to be cooler if other conditions are alike?

**Solution:**

1. Both towns share a latitude and have similar sunlight patterns.
2. Hill Town is higher. It will likely be cooler.

### #3: Winds From the Sea

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: map with the sea at left and dry land at right; place Shore and Inland on the same horizontal latitude line above the Equator, with wind arrows from sea toward Shore and from dry land toward Inland -->

Shore and Inland share a latitude. Winds often reach Shore from the sea and Inland from dry land. What could explain why Shore gets more rain?

(A) Shore is closer to the equator.

(B) Winds bring moist air to Shore.

(C) Inland gets longer days.

**Solution:**

1. The towns share a latitude, so that clue alone cannot explain the rain.
2. Winds from the sea can bring moist air to Shore. That can help explain the extra rain. Choose (B).

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Compare latitude first when the map gives only locations. Use any extra clues about height, water, or winds when the question gives them.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Promising a Match

> A latitude line gives a clue.

Two places on the same line can still have different climates. A mountain town may be colder than a low town on the same line.
