## Core Concept

The **water cycle** describes how water constantly moves between the oceans, the atmosphere, and the land, in an endless loop with no true starting point.

**Evaporation**: heat from the Sun turns liquid water (mostly from the oceans) into water vapor, a gas, which rises into the atmosphere. Most of the water vapor in our atmosphere comes from evaporation off the oceans.

**Transpiration**: plants release water vapor into the air through their leaves. Evaporation and transpiration together are sometimes called "evapotranspiration."

**Condensation**: as water vapor rises and cools, it turns back into tiny liquid water droplets, forming clouds. This is the opposite process of evaporation.

**Precipitation**: when cloud droplets combine and grow heavy enough, they fall back to Earth as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

**Infiltration and runoff**: precipitation that lands on the ground either soaks into the soil (**infiltration**, which feeds groundwater) or flows over the surface into streams, rivers, and eventually back to the ocean (**runoff**).

### Basic Oceanography

Oceans cover most of Earth's surface and hold about $96\%$ of all the water on the planet. Ocean water is **saline** (salty), unlike most water in lakes and rivers.

Ocean water is not uniform. It has layers based on temperature and density: warmer, sunlit water near the surface, and colder, denser water in the deep ocean, where sunlight cannot reach. Ocean water also constantly moves in **currents**, driven by wind, differences in water density, and Earth's rotation, which help redistribute heat around the planet.

## Examples

### #1: Naming the Water Cycle Stage

Water vapor rises from the ocean surface, cools as it rises higher into the atmosphere, and turns back into tiny liquid droplets that form a cloud. Which stage of the water cycle does "turning back into tiny liquid droplets" describe?

**Solution:**
Match the described change (vapor to liquid) to its stage name.

1. Water vapor (a gas) turning into liquid droplets is the opposite of evaporation.
2. This gas-to-liquid change, forming clouds, is called condensation.
3. The answer: condensation.

### #2: Infiltration vs. Runoff

After a heavy rainstorm, some water soaks slowly into the soil, while the rest flows quickly over the ground surface into a nearby river. Identify which process is which.

**Solution:**
Match the described motion (soaking in vs. flowing over the surface) to the correct term.

1. Water soaking slowly into the soil is infiltration.
2. Water flowing over the surface into a river is runoff.
3. Both processes can happen to the same rainfall at the same time, depending on how quickly the soil can absorb water.

### #3: Why Most of Earth's Water Vapor Comes from the Ocean

Explain why most of the water vapor found in Earth's atmosphere comes from the ocean rather than from lakes, rivers, or plants.

**Solution:**
Compare the surface area and exposure to sunlight of each water source.

1. Oceans cover the vast majority of Earth's surface and contain about $96\%$ of all the water on the planet.
2. Since evaporation depends on the amount of exposed water surface heated by the Sun, the ocean's enormous surface area makes it by far the largest source of evaporation.
3. Lakes, rivers, and plant transpiration also add water vapor to the atmosphere, but their combined contribution is much smaller than the ocean's, simply because there is so much more ocean surface exposed to sunlight.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Remember the water cycle stages as a loop with four main stops: evaporation (liquid to gas, going up), condensation (gas to liquid, forming clouds), precipitation (falling back down), and runoff/infiltration (moving across or into the ground). If a question describes a state change, match "liquid to gas" with evaporation and "gas to liquid" with condensation.

For ocean facts, anchor the one big number: oceans hold about $96\%$ of Earth's water. Any question asking "where is most of Earth's water" points straight to the ocean.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Mixing Up Evaporation and Condensation

> Evaporation: Liquid to gas (rising). Condensation: Gas to liquid (forming clouds).

These are opposite processes, and questions often swap their descriptions. If the process is warming water into vapor, it is evaporation; if it is cooling vapor into visible droplets or clouds, it is condensation.

### #2: Believing Transpiration Only Involves Animals or the Ground

> Transpiration specifically refers to water vapor released from plants through their leaves.

Some students confuse transpiration with general evaporation from soil or water bodies. Transpiration is specifically the plant-based release of water vapor, which is why it is often paired with evaporation as "evapotranspiration" when discussing total water vapor sources.

### #3: Thinking Freshwater Lakes Hold Most of Earth's Water

> The ocean holds about $96\%$ of Earth's water; freshwater lakes and rivers hold only a small fraction.

A choice suggesting that lakes or rivers are the largest water reservoir is incorrect. The vast majority of Earth's water, and nearly all of it that is liquid, is contained within the salty oceans.
