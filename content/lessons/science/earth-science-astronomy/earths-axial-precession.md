## Core Concept

### Precession

**Precession** is the slow change in the direction that Earth's axis points. It is caused mainly by the pull of the Moon and the Sun on Earth's equatorial bulge. Earth is slightly wider at the equator than from pole to pole, and the pull acts on this bulge.

Think of a spinning toy top that slowly wobbles in a circle. Earth's axis does the same in a very slow way.

The tilt angle stays about the same, but the direction of the axis traces a cone. One full circle takes about $26{,}000$ years.

The axis points to the north celestial pole now. That pole is close to the star Polaris, so Polaris is our "North Star".

Because of precession, the pole slowly moves among the stars. Thousands of years ago a different star, Thuban, was nearest the pole, and in about $12{,}000$ years the star Vega will be.

Precession is far too slow to change the days or the seasons within a lifetime. Its only effect on the calendar is that the seasonal year is about $20$ minutes shorter than the year measured against the stars.

## Examples

### #1: Precession

Which statement about precession is correct? (A) Earth's axis changes direction slowly, over about $26{,}000$ years. (B) Earth's tilt angle changes from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ every year. (C) Earth's spin reverses direction every century. (D) Earth's orbit changes from circle to line over a year.

**Solution:**
1. Precession is a slow change in the direction the axis points, with the tilt angle staying about the same.
2. One full cycle takes about $26{,}000$ years.
3. Only choice (A) says this.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Precession Does Not Cause Yearly Seasons or Yearly Sky Changes

> Precession changes the direction of the axis over thousands of years, not over months.

The seasons come from tilt and revolution. The change in which constellations are seen across one year comes from revolution. Precession does change which star is closest to the celestial pole, and over thousands of years it shifts which constellations are seen in each season.
