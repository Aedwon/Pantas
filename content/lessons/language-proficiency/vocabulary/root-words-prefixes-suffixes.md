## Core Concept

A **root** or **base** is the main meaningful part of a word. An **affix** is a word part added to a base to change its meaning or job in a sentence.

A **stem** is the form that another affix is added to. In many school questions, the root, base, and stem may look the same, so focus on the meaningful part the word is built around.

Common affix positions include:

- **Prefix / unlapi:** before the base, as in *unhappy*.
- **Infix / gitlapi:** inside the base, as in *sumulat* from *sulat*.
- **Suffix / hulapi:** after the base, as in *careless*.
- **Kabilaan:** affixes appear on both sides of the base, as in *kabataan* from *bata*.
- **Laguhan:** a word uses a prefix, an infix, and a suffix around the base.

Word structure is the way meaningful parts are joined in a word. A word's origin is where it came from in an older language or earlier form. These clues can help, but letters that only look familiar are not enough.

Before you split a word, ask two questions. Does the remaining base have a real meaning? Does the added part make sense in that word?

### Useful English Word Elements

These word parts appear in many English words:

- **mal-**: bad, badly, or faulty, as in *malfunction* and *malpractice*.
- **bene-/ben-**: good or well, as in *benefit* and *benevolent*.
- **omni-**: all, as in *omnivorous*.
- **chron-/chrono-**: time, as in *chronology*.
- **pseudo-**: false or imitation, as in *pseudoscience*.

Do not split every word that begins with the same letters. A word part is useful only when it truly belongs to the word and helps explain its meaning.

For example, *un-* works as a prefix in *unhappy* because *happy* is a meaningful base and *un-* changes it to the opposite idea. Do not split *uncle* into *un-* and *cle*. The letters *un* are there, but *cle* is not the meaningful base of the word.

### English Suffixes

A **suffix** comes after a base. It can change meaning, and it can also change the kind of word you get.

- **-ful** means "full of" or "having," as in *helpful*. It often forms an adjective.
- **-less** means "without," as in *careless*. It often forms an adjective.
- **-ness** forms a noun for a state or quality, as in *kindness* from *kind*.
- **-ly** often forms an adverb that tells how an action happens, as in *quickly* from *quick*.

These patterns are clues, not promises for every word. Always check the whole word and the sentence.

### Filipino Affixes

Filipino affixes can change both meaning and grammar. The same affix can work differently in different words, so use the sentence as a guide.

In *sumulat* from *sulat*, *-um-* helps form a verb in which the doer carries out the action. In *Sumulat siya ng liham*, the word means that the person wrote a letter.

In *sinulat* from *sulat*, *-in-* helps form a verb that puts attention on what was written. In *Sinulat niya ang liham*, the letter is the thing that was written.

In *kabataan* from *bata*, *ka-...-an* helps form a noun. *Kabataan* refers to youth or young people.

Do not remove affixes one by one just because they are at the outside of a word. Look for the meaningful base and check whether the whole word still makes sense.

## Examples

### #1: Meaning from a Prefix

The report called the claim pseudoscientific. What does the word part in bold, **pseudo-**, most likely add to the meaning?

A) ancient  
B) false or imitation  
C) complete  
D) fast

**Solution:**

1. *Pseudo-* means false or imitation.
2. So **pseudoscientific** describes something that seems scientific but is not genuine science. The answer is **B**.

### #2: Root Word in Filipino

Tukuyin ang salitang-ugat ng salitang naka-bold: **pinagsumikapan**.

A) sikap  
B) pinag  
C) sumikap  
D) sikapan

**Solution:**

1. Hanapin ang bahaging may pangunahing kahulugan sa salita.
2. Ang salitang **pinagsumikapan** ay binuo sa salitang-ugat na **sikap**. Ang sagot ay **A**.

### #3: Affixes on Both Sides

Which word most clearly shows affixes placed on both sides of its base?

A) kabataan  
B) pag-ibig  
C) sumulat  
D) bahay

**Solution:**

1. The base of **kabataan** is *bata*.
2. The parts *ka-* and *-an* appear on both sides of *bata*. This is **kabilaan**, so the answer is **A**.

### #4: Meaning and Word Type from a Suffix

In the sentence "Her **kindness** comforted the child," what does *-ness* do?

A) It makes *kind* mean "not kind."  
B) It turns *kind* into a noun for the quality of being kind.  
C) It turns *kind* into a verb.  
D) It makes *kind* mean "full of kindness."

**Solution:**

1. The suffix *-ness* can form a noun for a state or quality.
2. **Kindness** names the quality of being kind. The answer is **B**.

### #5: Rejecting a False Split

Which analysis is more reliable?

A) *uncle* = *un-* + *cle* because it begins with *un*  
B) *unhappy* = *un-* + *happy* because *happy* is a real base and *un-* changes its meaning  
C) Both analyses are equally reliable  
D) Any word beginning with *un* must contain the prefix *un-*

**Solution:**

1. A useful split should leave a meaningful base and explain the whole word.
2. *Happy* is a meaningful base, and *un-* changes its meaning in *unhappy*. The answer is **B**.

### #6: Meaning from Context

The speaker's **circumlocution** made a simple answer long and indirect. Which meaning best fits the word in bold?

A) direct statement  
B) indirect or roundabout expression  
C) silence  
D) shouting

**Solution:**

1. The sentence says the answer became long and indirect.
2. **Circumlocution** means an indirect or roundabout way of speaking. The answer is **B**.

### #7: Contrasting Word Elements

**Malevolent** describes harmful intent, while **benevolent** describes kind intent. Which pair best matches the meanings of the words in bold?

A) harmful and kind  
B) slow and fast  
C) old and new  
D) loud and quiet

**Solution:**

1. *Mal-* can carry a bad or harmful idea, while *bene-* can carry a good or kind idea.
2. The pair **harmful and kind** matches that contrast. The answer is **A**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Use a word part only when the base makes sense.** If your split leaves a meaningless piece, stop and check again.

**Check the sentence.** A prefix or suffix gives a clue, but context tells you whether that clue fits the whole word.

**For Filipino, find the meaningful base first.** A word may contain several affixes, and their form can change with the word.

**For English suffixes, ask two things.** What meaning does the suffix add, and does it change the word into a noun, adjective, adverb, or another form?

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: False Word Splits

> Familiar letters do not prove that a real prefix or root is present.

*Unhappy* can be split into *un-* and *happy*. *Uncle* should not be split into *un-* and *cle* just because the first letters match.

### #2: Treating a Prefix as the Whole Meaning

> A prefix gives only part of a word's meaning.

Knowing that *mal-* can suggest something bad does not give the full meaning of *malady, malign,* or *malpractice*. Use the whole word and its context.

### #3: Assuming Similar Words Share One Origin

> Similar spelling does not prove that two words come from the same root.

English, Spanish, and Filipino share many borrowed words. Check meaning and real word structure instead of trusting appearance alone.

### #4: Confusing Affix Positions

> A prefix comes before a base, an infix goes inside it, and a suffix comes after it.

Filipino words can also use several affixes at once. Find the base before naming the pattern.

### #5: Trusting a Hidden Smaller Word

> A smaller word inside a longer spelling is not automatically its root.

A useful analysis must explain the larger word's meaning. Do not rely on letter matching alone.
