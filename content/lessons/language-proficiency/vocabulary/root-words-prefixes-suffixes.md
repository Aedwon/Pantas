## Core Concept

A **root** carries a word's main meaning. A **base** is the word or part to which another part is added. In *unhappy*, *happy* is both the root and the base.

An **affix** is a part added to a base to change its meaning or job. A **stem** is the form to which the next affix is added. In *helpfulness*, *helpful* is the stem before *-ness* is added.

A **prefix** comes before the base: *un-* in *unhappy*. A **suffix** comes after it: *-less* in *careless*.

To **infer** a meaning is to work it out from clues. Word parts can help you infer meaning, but a familiar set of letters is not always a real word part.

Ask whether your split leaves a meaningful base. Then check whether the parts explain the whole word.

In *unhappy*, *un-* means "not," and *happy* is a real base. In *uncle*, the first letters are also *un*, but *cle* is not its base. The split does not explain the word.

### Useful English Word Elements

Some word parts come from older languages. They may carry meaning even when they cannot stand alone as English words.

- **mal-** suggests bad, badly, or faulty: *malfunction*, *malpractice*.
- **bene-/ben-** suggests good or well: *benevolent*, *benefit*.
- **omni-** means all: *omnivorous* describes eating both plant and animal food.
- **chron-/chrono-** relates to time: *chronology* is the order of events in time.
- **pseudo-** means false or imitation: *pseudoscience*.
- **circum-** means around, and **locut-** relates to speaking: *circumlocution* is an indirect, wordy way of speaking.

These parts give clues, not the whole meaning of every word. Do not force a split in a word such as *benign* just because it begins with *ben*. Learn the full word when the rest of the split does not help.

### English Suffixes

A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea. An **adjective** describes a noun. An **adverb** can describe how an action happens, and a **verb** tells an action or state.

The suffix **-ful** means "full of" or "having." *Helpful* describes someone or something that gives help, so it is an adjective.

The suffix **-less** means "without." *Careless* describes someone who does not take enough care. It is also an adjective.

The suffix **-ness** makes a noun naming a state or quality. *Kind* describes a person, while *kindness* names the quality of being kind.

The suffix **-ly** often makes an adverb. In "She spoke quietly," *quietly* tells how she spoke.

These patterns have limits. *Friendly* ends in *-ly*, but it describes a person or thing, so it is an adjective. Check the job the word does in the sentence.

### Mga Panlapi sa Filipino

Ang **salitang-ugat** (root word) ang nagdadala ng pangunahing kahulugan. Ang **panlapi** (affix) ay idinadagdag dito upang makabuo ng salita.

Ang **unlapi** (prefix) ay nasa unahan, gaya ng *pag-* sa *pag-ibig*. Ang **gitlapi** (infix) ay nasa loob, gaya ng *-um-* sa *sumulat*. Ang **hulapi** (suffix) ay nasa hulihan, gaya ng *-an* sa *sulatan*.

Sa *sumulat*, ang ugat ay *sulat*. Sa "Sumulat siya ng liham," ang *-um-* ay tumutulong bumuo ng salitang nagsasaad ng kilos ng gumawa. Dito, tapos na ang pagsulat.

Sa *sinulat*, ang ugat ay *sulat* din. Sa "Sinulat niya ang liham," ang *-in-* ay tumutulong ipakita ang nagawang kilos. Ang liham ang binibigyang-pansin.

Hindi iisa ang gamit ng isang panlapi sa lahat ng salita. Basahin ang buong pangungusap upang malaman ang kahulugan at gamit nito.

Ang **kabilaan** (affixes on both sides) ay may panlapi sa unahan at hulihan. Sa *kabataan*, nasa magkabilang panig ng *bata* ang *ka-* at *-an*. Tumutukoy ang salita sa mga bata o sa panahon ng pagiging bata.

Ang **laguhan** (prefix, infix, and suffix together) ay may panlapi sa unahan, loob, at hulihan. Sa *pinagsumikapan*, makikita ang *pinag-*, *-um-*, at *-an* sa paligid ng ugat na *sikap*. Ang bagay na pinagsumikapan ay pinaglaanan ng pagsisikap.

Hanapin ang ugat na nagpapaliwanag sa kahulugan ng buong salita. Huwag magtanggal ng mga letra dahil lamang nasa unahan o hulihan ang mga ito.

## Examples

### #1: A Prefix Clue

What does the prefix add to the meaning of the word in bold, **pseudoscientific**?

A) ancient  
B) false or imitation  
C) complete  
D) fast

**Solution:**

1. *Pseudo-* means false or imitation.
2. **Pseudoscientific** describes something presented as science that is not genuine science. Answer: **B**.

### #2: Finding a Root

Tukuyin ang salitang-ugat ng salitang nakadiin: **pinagsumikapan**.

A) sikap  
B) pinag  
C) sumikap  
D) sikapan

**Solution:**

1. Hanapin ang bahaging nagdadala ng pangunahing kahulugan.
2. Ang **sikap** ang ugat. May dagdag na panlapi pa ang *sumikap* at *sikapan*. Sagot: **A**.

### #3: Panlapi sa Magkabilang Panig

Aling salita ang may panlaping kabilaan?

A) kabataan  
B) pag-ibig  
C) sumulat  
D) bahay

**Solution:**

1. Ang ugat ng *kabataan* ay *bata*.
2. Nasa unahan ang *ka-* at nasa hulihan ang *-an*. Sagot: **A**.

### #4: A Suffix That Makes a Noun

In "Her **kindness** comforted the child," what does the suffix in the word in bold do?

A) It adds the meaning "not kind."  
B) It forms a noun naming the quality of being kind.  
C) It forms a verb meaning "to become kind."  
D) It forms an adverb telling how an action happens.

**Solution:**

1. *-Ness* makes a noun naming a state or quality.
2. **Kindness** names the quality of being kind. Answer: **B**.

### #5: False and Valid Splits

Which word analysis is valid?

A) uncle = un- + cle  
B) unhappy = un- + happy  
C) benefit = be- + nefit  
D) chronology = chrono- + log + y

**Solution:**

1. *Happy* is a meaningful base, and *un-* changes its meaning to "not happy."
2. This split explains the whole word. The other splits rely on misleading letter groups, such as the English word *log*. Answer: **B**.

### #6: Parts and Context Together

The official's reply was criticized for its **circumlocution**. Which meaning best fits the word in bold?

A) direct statement  
B) indirect, wordy expression  
C) complete silence  
D) angry shouting

**Solution:**

1. *Circum-* suggests around, and *locut-* relates to speaking.
2. **Circumlocution** means an indirect, wordy way of speaking. Answer: **B**.

### #7: Contrasting Elements

Which pair best gives the meanings of the words in bold, in order: **malevolent**, **benevolent**?

A) wishing harm; wishing good  
B) acting slowly; acting quickly  
C) very old; very new  
D) speaking loudly; speaking softly

**Solution:**

1. *Mal-* suggests bad or harmful, while *bene-* suggests good or well.
2. **Malevolent** means wishing harm, and **benevolent** means wishing good or being kind. Answer: **A**.

### #8: Panlaping Laguhan

Anong uri ng panlapi ang makikita sa salitang nakadiin: **pinagsumikapan**?

A) unlapi lamang  
B) gitlapi lamang  
C) kabilaan  
D) laguhan

**Solution:**

1. Ang ugat ay *sikap*. May *pinag-* sa unahan, *-um-* sa loob, at *-an* sa hulihan.
2. May panlapi sa tatlong puwesto, kaya **laguhan** ito. Sagot: **D**.

### #9: Meaning and Word Job

Which statement correctly describes the words in bold in "The **helpful** clerk answered **quickly**"?

A) Helpful names a quality; quickly names a person.  
B) Helpful describes the clerk; quickly tells how the clerk answered.  
C) Helpful tells how the clerk answered; quickly describes the clerk.  
D) Helpful means without help; quickly means without speed.

**Solution:**

1. *-Ful* gives *helpful* the idea of giving help. It describes the clerk, so it is an adjective.
2. *-Ly* makes *quickly* tell how the clerk answered. It is an adverb. Answer: **B**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Use word parts to narrow the meaning. Then read the whole sentence to check your choice.

Ask what a suffix adds and what job the new word does. A meaning clue and a sentence clue should agree.

Sa Filipino, hanapin muna ang salitang-ugat. Pagkatapos, tingnan ang puwesto ng bawat panlapi at ang kahulugan ng buong salita.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Trusting Familiar Letters

> Matching letters do not prove that a real word part is present.

*Unhappy* contains *un-* meaning "not." *Uncle* does not. The split must help explain the word.

### #2: Guessing the Whole Meaning from One Part

> A prefix gives only part of a word's meaning.

*Malady*, *malign*, and *malpractice* have different meanings. A bad or harmful idea alone does not tell you which meaning the sentence needs.

### #3: Assuming Similar Spellings Have One Origin

> Words that look alike may have different roots.

Check known word parts and meaning. Appearance alone cannot prove how a word was formed.

### #4: Pagkalito sa Puwesto ng Panlapi

> Maaaring may higit sa isang panlapi ang salita.

Hanapin muna ang ugat. Sa kabilaan, nasa unahan at hulihan ang mga panlapi. Sa laguhan, mayroon din sa loob.

### #5: Treating Every Ending as a Fixed Rule

> An ending does not always tell you the word's job.

*Quickly* is an adverb in "She answered quickly." *Friendly* is an adjective in "a friendly clerk." Read how the word is used.
