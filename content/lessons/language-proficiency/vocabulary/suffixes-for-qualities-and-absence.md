## Core Concept

### Suffixes -ful and -less

A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea. An **adjective** describes a noun.
The suffix **-ful** often means "full of" or "having." *Helpful* describes someone or something that gives help, so it is an adjective.

The suffix **-less** means "without." *Careless* describes someone who does not take enough care.
:::pair #ful-less
Right: *Helpful* has *-ful*, which means having. *Careless* has *-less*, which means lacking.
Wrong: *Helpful* has *-ful*, which means without. *Careless* has *-less*, which means full of.
In these words, *-ful* adds help and *-less* takes away care. They are opposites here.
:::

:::check
What does the suffix in *hopeless* mean?
:::answer
The answer is without. *Hopeless* means without hope.
:::

### Suffix -ness

The suffix **-ness** makes a noun naming a state or quality.

Let's change *kind* into a noun that names a quality:

- *Kind* describes a person: "a kind teacher."
- *Kind* + *-ness* = *kindness*, the quality of being kind: "Her kindness helped."

:::pair #ness-noun
Right: In "her kindness," the suffix *-ness* names a quality.
Wrong: In "her kindness," the suffix *-ness* describes a kind person.
*Kindness* is a noun. It names the quality.
:::

:::check
In "her fairness," what job does *fairness* do?
:::answer
The answer is a noun naming a quality. *-Ness* changes the adjective *fair* into a noun.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Suffix That Makes a Noun

In "Her **kindness** comforted the child," what does the suffix in the word in bold do?

A) It adds the meaning "not kind."

B) It forms a noun naming the quality of being kind.

C) It forms a verb meaning "to become kind."

D) It forms an adverb telling how an action happens.

**Solution:**

1. **Kindness** names the quality of being kind. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** "Her habit of being kind comforted the child" keeps the meaning.

### #2: The Night Guard

The **sleepless** guard watched the gate all night. Which choice gives the job and meaning of the word in bold?

A) an adjective meaning full of sleep

B) an adjective meaning without sleep

C) a noun naming the quality of sleep

D) an adverb meaning in a sleepy way

**Solution:**

1. Break **sleepless** into its base and its suffix.

:::try
Finish the last step: what does the suffix mean, and which choice gives the job and meaning of *sleepless* in this sentence?
:::answer
The base is *sleep*, and the suffix is *-less*, which means "without." *Sleepless* describes the guard, so it is an adjective meaning without sleep. The answer is **B**.

**Check:** "The guard without sleep watched the gate all night" keeps the meaning.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Ask what a suffix adds and what job the new word does.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- -ful and -less: in words like *helpful*, -ful means having, and -less means lacking. {#ful-less}
- -ness: it makes a noun that names a quality, not a describing word. {#ness-noun}
:::
