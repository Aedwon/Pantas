## Core Concept

College entrance exams will test you on words you do not know. This is normal and expected. You do not need to read the whole dictionary to pass.

Test makers always leave hidden hints inside the sentence. These hints are called **context clues**. Think of it like a puzzle. The words before and after the hard word will help you guess its meaning.

Your goal is to become a detective. You must look for signal words that point to the correct definition. If you can find the clue, you can find the answer.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

> Remember the word **ideas** to find the right clue.

* **I for Inference:** You must guess the meaning based on the story happening in the whole sentence.
* **D for Definition:** The sentence tells you the exact meaning of the word. Look for commas or the word *is*.
* **E for Example:** The sentence lists examples of the hard word. Look for *such as* or *including*.
* **A for Antonym:** The sentence shows the opposite meaning. Look for signal words like *but*, *unlike*, or *however*.
* **S for Synonym:** The sentence uses an easier word that means the same thing. Look for the word *or* or *and*.

## Examples

### #1: Synonym Clue

Which of the following is the closest meaning of the word in bold?
The king's **edict**, or official command, forced all citizens to pay higher taxes to the crown.
A) request
B) suggestion
C) law
D) secret

**Solution:**

1. Find the hard word. The word is *edict*.
2. Look at the words right after it. The sentence says "or official command".
3. The word *or* is a synonym signal. It means the writer is giving you another word for *edict*.
4. Look at the choices. A command is like a rule or a law. The correct answer is **C**.

### #2: Example Clue

Choose the best meaning for the italicized word.
The forest is full of *nocturnal* creatures, such as owls, bats, and flying foxes, that hunt under the moonlight.
A) dangerous
B) flying
C) active at night
D) making loud noises

**Solution:**

1. Find the signal words. The sentence uses "such as". This means examples are coming.
2. Look at the examples. Owls, bats, and flying foxes.
3. What do these animals have in common? The sentence says they "hunt under the moonlight."
4. Find the choice that matches hunting in the moonlight. The correct answer is **C**.

### #3: The Antonym Clue

Identify the meaning of the word in bold.
Unlike his **gregarious** sister who loved attending grand balls and chatting with everyone, Simon preferred the quiet isolation of his library.
A) sociable
B) intelligent
C) shy
D) angry

**Solution:**

1. Find the signal word. The sentence starts with "Unlike". This is an antonym clue.
2. It means Simon is the opposite of his sister.
3. Simon likes "quiet isolation". He likes being alone.
4. The sister must be the opposite. She loves "attending grand balls and chatting".
5. Find the choice that means someone loves chatting and being with people. The correct answer is **A**.

### #4: Inference Clue

Select the word that best captures the meaning of the term in bold.
The politician’s speech was full of **platitudes**; the audience yawned at the repetitive and meaningless phrases they had heard a thousand times before.
A) original ideas
B) angry insults
C) cliché statements
D) false promises

**Solution:**

1. There are no easy signal words like *but* or *such as* here. You must look at the whole story.
2. What did the audience do? They yawned.
3. Why did they yawn? Because the phrases were "repetitive and meaningless". They had heard them a thousand times.
4. What do you call a phrase that is overused and boring? It is a cliché.
5. The correct answer is **C**.

### #5: Filipino Context Clues

Alin sa mga sumusunod ang kasingkahulugan ng salitang may salungguhit?
Pilit na **ikinubli** ni Maria ang kanyang mga luha sa pamamagitan ng pagtalikod sa kanyang mga kaibigan.
A) ipinakita
B) itinago
C) ipinagmalaki
D) iniyak

**Solution:**

1. Find the hard word. The word is *ikinubli*.
2. Look at the action in the sentence. Maria turned her back (pagtalikod) on her friends.
3. Why do you turn your back when you have tears (luha)? You want to hide them.
4. Find the Filipino word for hide. The correct answer is **B**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**The Positive/Negative Test.** Ask yourself if the hard word is a "good" word or a "bad" word. If a teacher is yelling in the sentence, the word is bad. Cross out all the choices that are good or happy words.

**The Plug-In Trick.** Treat the hard word like a blank space. Plug each choice into the blank. Read the sentence in your head. Usually, the wrong answers will sound very silly.

**Hunt for Signals First.** Do not stare at the hard word. Your eyes should jump to words like *but*, *although*, *because*, or *such as*. These words do the heavy lifting for you.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Secondary Meaning

> Do not pick a definition just because it is the most common one. Read the sentence!

Test makers love to use simple words in weird ways. The word *bark* usually means the sound a dog makes. But in a sentence about trees, it means the outer wood. Always plug the choice back into the sentence to see if it makes sense.

### #2: Look-alike Words

> If a choice looks or sounds exactly like the hard word, it is usually a trap.

If the hard word is *belligerent*, a trap choice might be *belly*. The test maker hopes you will panic and pick a word that looks familiar. Ignore how the word looks. Focus only on the clues in the sentence.

### #3: Ignoring the Contrast Word

> Missing the word "however" or "but" will make you pick the exact wrong answer.

If you miss an antonym signal, you will pick a synonym instead. Always scan the start and middle of the sentence for words that change the direction of the thought.
