## Core Concept

A **context clue** is information near an unfamiliar word that helps you work out its likely meaning. Context may give the exact meaning, or it may only narrow the choices.

Common clue types include:

- **Definition or restatement:** the text explains the word in another way.
- **Example:** the text gives examples that show what the word includes.
- **Synonym or near-synonym:** a nearby word or phrase has the same or almost the same meaning.
- **Contrast or antonym:** words such as *but, unlike,* or *however* show an opposite idea.
- **Inference:** the situation, actions, results, or tone point to a likely meaning.

A sentence can use more than one clue. A signal word can help, but it does not prove the answer by itself.

When two choices still seem possible, use smaller clues:

- **Word partners:** some words often appear together. Nearby words can point to the intended meaning.
- **Connotation:** two words can have a similar basic meaning but a different feeling, such as warm, cold, polite, or rude.
- **Register:** some words sound formal, while others sound casual. A legal notice, school memo, or friendly chat may use different words for the same basic idea.
- **Word's job in the sentence:** check whether the blank acts like a thing, an action, or a describing word. This can remove choices that do not fit the grammar.

## Examples

### #1: Definition / Restatement Clue

The king's **edict**, or official command, required all citizens to pay the tax. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) request  
B) suggestion  
C) official order  
D) secret

**Solution:**

1. The words *or official command* explain **edict** in another way.
2. An official order matches that restatement. Answer: **C**.

### #2: Example Clue

The forest contains many **nocturnal** animals, such as owls, bats, and flying foxes, that are active after dark. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) dangerous  
B) flying  
C) active at night  
D) noisy

**Solution:**

1. The sentence gives examples and says the animals are active after dark.
2. These clues show that **nocturnal** means active at night. Answer: **C**.

### #3: Contrast Clue

Unlike his **gregarious** sister, who loved meeting people and attending parties, Simon preferred quiet isolation. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) sociable  
B) intelligent  
C) shy  
D) angry

**Solution:**

1. *Unlike* shows a contrast between the sister and Simon.
2. Her love of meeting people shows that **gregarious** means sociable. Answer: **A**.

### #4: Inference Clue

The politician's speech was full of **platitudes**; the audience yawned at the repetitive, familiar phrases they had heard many times before. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) original ideas  
B) angry insults  
C) clichéd statements  
D) false promises

**Solution:**

1. The phrases are described as repetitive, familiar, and heard many times before.
2. Those details show that **platitudes** are clichéd statements. Answer: **C**.

### #5: Filipino Context

Pilit na **ikinubli** ni Maria ang kaniyang mga luha sa pamamagitan ng pagtalikod sa kaniyang mga kaibigan. Ano ang malamang na ibig sabihin ng salitang naka-bold?

A) ipinakita  
B) itinago  
C) ipinagmalaki  
D) iniyak

**Solution:**

1. Tumalikod si Maria upang hindi makita ng mga kaibigan ang kaniyang luha.
2. Ipinapakita ng kilos na ang **ikinubli** ay nangangahulugang *itinago*. Sagot: **B**.

### #6: Register as a Clue

The formal notice stated that all visitors must **cease** using mobile phones inside the testing room. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) stop  
B) begin  
C) record  
D) hide

**Solution:**

1. The sentence is a formal notice giving a rule about phone use.
2. **Cease** is a formal word meaning stop, which fits the sentence. Answer: **A**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Blank out the unfamiliar word.** Ask what meaning the sentence needs before looking at the choices.

**Use several clues together.** A contrast word, an example, and the result of an action can support the same meaning.

**Put the choice back into the sentence.** Check both the meaning and the word's job in the sentence.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Assuming Context Always Gives an Exact Meaning

> Some sentences support only a broad meaning or more than one possible sense.

If two choices still fit, compare word partners, feeling, formality, and grammar. Choose the one that best matches the whole sentence.

### #2: Reducing Meaning to Positive vs. Negative Tone

> A sentence sounding "good" or "bad" is not enough to prove a word's meaning.

Two negative words can mean different things. Use the details around the target word.

### #3: Choosing by Similar Spelling

> Words that look alike do not always mean the same thing.

Judge a choice by the sentence and meaning, not by spelling alone.

### #4: Ignoring Contrast and Reference Words

> Words such as *however, unlike, this,* and *therefore* can connect ideas and point to useful clues.

Read the whole sentence or passage to see what relationship the word creates.
