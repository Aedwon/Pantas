## Core Concept

A **context clue** is information near an unfamiliar word that helps you work out its likely meaning. Context may give the exact meaning, or it may only narrow the choices.

Common clue types include:

- **Definition or restatement:** the text explains the word in another way.
- **Example:** the text gives examples that show what the word includes.
- **Synonym or near-synonym:** a nearby word has the same or almost the same meaning.
- **Contrast or antonym:** the text shows an opposite idea. Words such as *but*, *unlike*, and *however* can signal this link.
- **Inference:** you work out a meaning from the situation, actions, or results. To infer means to use given details to reach an idea that is not stated directly.

A sentence can use more than one clue. A signal word helps, but it does not prove the answer by itself.

When two choices still seem possible, check smaller clues:

- **Word partners:** some words often appear together. In *file a complaint*, *file* means submit, not smooth a surface.
- **Connotation:** a word can carry a feeling beyond its basic meaning. *Slim* and *scrawny* both suggest being thin, but *scrawny* suggests being too thin.
- **Register:** wording can be formal or casual. *Request* sounds more formal than *ask for*. An official letter may help you recognize a formal meaning, but formality alone cannot prove it.
- **Grammar:** check the word's job. Does the sentence need a thing, an action, or a describing word?

First guess the meaning from the sentence. Then put a possible answer in place of the target word. It must keep the meaning and fit the grammar.

## Examples

### #1: Definition or Restatement

The king's **edict**, or official command, required all citizens to pay the tax. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) request  
B) suggestion  
C) official order  
D) secret

**Solution:**

1. The words *or official command* explain **edict**.
2. An official order matches that meaning. Answer: **C**.

### #2: Examples and a Description

The forest contains many **nocturnal** animals, such as owls, bats, and flying foxes, that are active after dark. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) dangerous  
B) flying  
C) active at night  
D) noisy

**Solution:**

1. The sentence gives animal examples and says they are active after dark.
2. These details show that **nocturnal** means active at night. Answer: **C**.

### #3: A Nearby Synonym

The clerk was **courteous** throughout the interview. She remained polite even when an applicant spoke rudely. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) hurried  
B) respectful  
C) uncertain  
D) talkative

**Solution:**

1. The nearby word *polite* describes the clerk's behavior again.
2. *Respectful* has a similar meaning. Answer: **B**.

### #4: Contrast

Unlike his **gregarious** sister, who loved meeting people and attending parties, Simon preferred quiet isolation. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) sociable  
B) intelligent  
C) shy  
D) angry

**Solution:**

1. *Unlike* shows a contrast between the sister and Simon.
2. Her love of meeting people shows that **gregarious** means sociable. Answer: **A**.

### #5: Meaning from Details

The politician's speech was full of **platitudes**; the audience yawned at the repetitive, familiar phrases they had heard many times before. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) original ideas  
B) angry insults  
C) clichéd statements  
D) false promises

**Solution:**

1. The phrases are familiar and have been repeated many times.
2. Clichéd statements are worn-out sayings that lack fresh thought. That meaning fits **platitudes**. Answer: **C**.

### #6: Filipino Context

Pilit na **ikinubli** ni Maria ang kaniyang mga luha sa pamamagitan ng pagtalikod sa kaniyang mga kaibigan. Ano ang malamang na ibig sabihin ng salitang naka-bold?

A) ipinakita  
B) itinago  
C) ipinagmalaki  
D) iniyak

**Solution:**

1. Tumalikod si Maria upang hindi makita ng mga kaibigan ang kaniyang luha.
2. Ipinapakita ng kilos na ang **ikinubli** ay nangangahulugang *itinago*. Sagot: **B**.

### #7: Formal Wording

The official letter asked the applicant to **furnish** proof of residence before the office could process the request. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) decorate  
B) inspect  
C) provide  
D) withhold

**Solution:**

1. *Furnish* can mean put furniture in a room or provide something. Here, its partner is *proof of residence*.
2. The office needs the applicant to provide a document. This use of *furnish* fits the formal letter.
3. Put the answer back: “The official letter asked the applicant to provide proof of residence.” *Provide* fits after *to* and keeps the meaning. Answer: **C**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Hide the unfamiliar word.** Ask what meaning the sentence needs before looking at the choices.

**Use several clues together.** A contrast, an example, and the result of an action may support the same meaning.

**Put the choice back.** Check the full sentence for meaning and grammar. For a noun or describing word, keep that same job when replacing it.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Expecting an Exact Definition

> Context may support only a broad meaning.

If two choices still fit, compare word partners, feeling, formality, and grammar. A short sentence may not give enough detail to settle every possible meaning.

### #2: Using Only a Good or Bad Feeling

> A positive or negative feeling does not give the whole meaning.

Two negative words can mean different things. Use the details around the target word to decide which meaning fits.

### #3: Choosing by Similar Spelling

> Words that look alike do not always mean the same thing.

Similar spelling may come from a shared word origin, or the words may only happen to look alike. Check the sentence before choosing.

### #4: Ignoring Links Between Ideas

> Words such as however, unlike, this, and therefore can point to clues.

Read the whole passage. *However* may introduce a contrast, while *this* may point back to an idea explained earlier.

### #5: Using Formality Alone

> Many different words can fit a formal sentence.

A formal setting helps you recognize a word's use. You still need a meaning that fits what the sentence says.
