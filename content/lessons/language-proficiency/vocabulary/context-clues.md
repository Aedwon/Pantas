## Core Concept

A **context clue** is information in the surrounding text that helps a reader infer the meaning of an unfamiliar word or phrase. Context can narrow a word's likely meaning, but it does not always provide enough evidence for an exact dictionary definition. When precision matters and a reference is available, verify the word afterward.

Useful clue types include:

- **Definition or restatement:** the text directly explains the word.
- **Example:** examples reveal what belongs to the category.
- **Synonym or near-synonym:** a nearby expression supplies similar meaning.
- **Contrast or antonym:** words such as *but, unlike,* or *however* establish an opposing relationship.
- **Inference:** the broader situation, actions, consequences, and tone support a likely interpretation.

These categories can overlap. A sentence may contain more than one clue, and signal words do not guarantee a particular clue type by themselves.

## Examples

### #1: Definition / Restatement Clue

The king's **edict**, or official command, required all citizens to pay the tax.

A) request  
B) suggestion  
C) official order  
D) secret

**Solution:**

The phrase "or official command" directly restates *edict*. The best answer is **C**.

### #2: Example Clue

The forest contains many *nocturnal* animals, such as owls, bats, and flying foxes, that are active after dark.

A) dangerous  
B) flying  
C) active at night  
D) noisy

**Solution:**

The examples and the phrase "active after dark" support **C**.

### #3: Contrast Clue

Unlike his **gregarious** sister, who loved meeting people and attending parties, Simon preferred quiet isolation.

A) sociable  
B) intelligent  
C) shy  
D) angry

**Solution:**

*Unlike* sets up contrast, and the sister's behavior shows sociability. **A** is the best fit.

### #4: Inference Clue

The politician's speech was full of **platitudes**; the audience yawned at the repetitive, familiar phrases they had heard many times before.

A) original ideas  
B) angry insults  
C) clichéd statements  
D) false promises

**Solution:**

The description "repetitive, familiar phrases" supports **C**. The sentence gives enough evidence to infer the intended sense without relying only on the audience's emotional reaction.

### #5: Filipino Context

Pilit na **ikinubli** ni Maria ang kaniyang mga luha sa pamamagitan ng pagtalikod sa kaniyang mga kaibigan.

A) ipinakita  
B) itinago  
C) ipinagmalaki  
D) iniyak

**Solution:**

Turning away so others will not see the tears supports **B**, *itinago*.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Replace the unfamiliar word with a blank.** Ask what meaning the surrounding sentence requires before looking at the choices.

**Use several clues together.** A contrast word, an example, and the outcome of an action can reinforce one interpretation.

**Plug the candidate back in.** The best choice should preserve both the grammar and the sentence's intended meaning.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Assuming Context Always Gives an Exact Meaning

> Some contexts support only a broad meaning or several plausible senses.

If two choices remain plausible, use finer clues such as collocation, connotation, register, and the grammatical role of the word.

### #2: Reducing Meaning to Positive vs. Negative Tone

> A word's meaning cannot reliably be inferred just by calling the sentence "good" or "bad."

Two negative-sounding choices may differ sharply in meaning, and a neutral word can appear in an unpleasant situation.

### #3: Treating Look-Alike Choices as Automatic Traps

> Similar spelling can reflect real etymological relationships or pure coincidence.

Judge the choice by context and meaning, not by a claim about how test writers supposedly design distractors.

### #4: Ignoring Contrast and Reference Words

> Words such as *however, unlike, this,* and *therefore* can supply important structural clues, but interpret them in the context of the whole sentence or passage.
