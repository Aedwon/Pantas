## Core Concept

### Checking Longer Words by Syllable

Say the word slowly and listen for its beats. Then check the base, ending, and doubled letters.

We split *beginning* into its beats.

- Say it: *be-gin-ning*.
- The first vowel sounds weak, so sound alone does not prove it.
- The known base *begin* gives the letters *b-e-g-i-n*.

Now we check the ending of *beginning*.

- *Begin* ends in a stressed vowel plus one consonant. Before *-ing* or *-ed*, that consonant usually doubles, but not *w*, *x*, or *y*.
- So the final *n* doubles before *-ing*: *begin* + *n* + *-ing* gives *beginning*.

:::pair #known-base
Right: The definite answer is in the report.
Wrong: The definate answer is in the report.
The weak vowel sounds the same either way. The related word *finite* shows the vowel is *i*.
:::

Use this process for a long word:

1. Say the word and listen for each syllable.
2. Find a base or word part you know.
3. Match the sounds to letter patterns you know.
4. Check stress, suffix changes, and doubled letters.
5. Learn any part that sound or a rule cannot predict.

Now we apply the process to *occurrence*.

- Say it: *oc-cur-rence*. Split it: *occur* + *-ence*.
- *Occur* ends in a stressed vowel plus one consonant, so its *r* doubles.
- *occur* + *r* + *-ence* gives *occurrence*.

:::pair #doubled-pairs
Right: The committee will accommodate every request.
Wrong: The comittee will acommodate every request.
The spoken sound does not show a doubled letter. Check each pair: *cc* and *mm* in *accommodate*, *mm* and *tt* in *committee*.
:::

:::check
Add *-ing* to *regret* and write the new word.
:::answer
The answer is *regretting*. The base *regret* ends in a stressed vowel plus one consonant, so its final *t* doubles before *-ing*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Long Word in Context

Choose the correctly spelled word to complete the sentence: “Yesterday, Ana ______ the window seat.”

A) prefered

B) preferred

C) prefferred

D) preferrd

**Solution:**

1. Say the word in beats: *pri-furd*. The beats do not give every letter.
2. Recall the base *prefer*.

:::try
Finish the spelling of *prefer* with *-ed* and choose the correct option.
:::answer
The answer is **B**, *preferred*. The base ends in a stressed vowel plus one consonant, so its final *r* doubles before *-ed*.

**Check:** Read it back: "Yesterday, Ana preferred the window seat." Base *prefer*, doubled *r*, ending *-ed*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Compare related forms such as *begin/beginning* and *refer/referred*. Say the base aloud to find its stressed syllable before deciding whether to double the last letter.

Break a long word into spoken parts. Use a known base to check letters that are hard to hear. Check the ending separately.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A weak vowel you cannot hear: check it against a known base, as in *definite* from *finite*. {#known-base}
- A word with doubled letters: check each pair, as in *accommodate* with *cc* and *mm*. {#doubled-pairs}
- A long word: use sound to split it, then recall the standard letters. {#checking-longer-words-by-syllable}
:::
