## Core Concept

### Alliteration

**Alliteration** repeats the same starting sound, usually a consonant, in nearby words, as in *busy bees buzzed*. Sound matters in alliteration, not just spelling.

Here is alliteration on one line: *Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.* Many nearby words start with the *p* sound.

:::pair #repeated-sound
Right: "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers" is alliteration.
Wrong: "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers" is onomatopoeia.
No word here imitates a noise. A repeated starting sound is alliteration.
:::

:::check
Which device is in "Cold winds crept closer"?
:::answer
The answer is alliteration. *Cold*, *crept*, and *closer* all start with the *k* sound.
:::

### Onomatopoeia

**Onomatopoeia** uses a word that sounds like the sound it names, such as *buzz* or *clang*.

:::check
Which device is the word *thud*?
:::answer
The answer is onomatopoeia. The word sounds like the noise it names.
:::

### Oxymoron

An **oxymoron** puts words with opposing meanings together, such as *jumbo shrimp*.

:::pair #opposing-words
Right: "Deafening silence" is an oxymoron.
Wrong: "Deafening silence" is hyperbole.
The phrase joins two opposing words. It does not simply stretch the truth.
:::

:::check
Which device is in the phrase "cruel kindness"?
:::answer
The answer is oxymoron. *Cruel* and *kindness* have opposite meanings.
:::

## Examples

### #1: The Snakes

“Silver snakes silently slid across the sand.” Which sound device is used most clearly?

A) Alliteration  
B) Onomatopoeia  
C) Metaphor  
D) Oxymoron

**Solution:**

1. Several nearby words begin with the same *s* sound.
2. Repeating a starting consonant sound is alliteration. Answer: **A**.

**Check:** Say the line aloud. *Silver snakes silently slid* all start with *s*.

### #2: The Firecracker

“The firecracker went **bang** beside the gate.” Which device is shown by the word in bold?

A) Onomatopoeia  
B) Simile  
C) Metaphor  
D) Oxymoron

**Solution:**

1. **Bang** imitates the sudden noise it names.
2. A word that imitates a sound is onomatopoeia. Answer: **A**.

**Check:** Say *bang* aloud. It sounds like the noise.

### #3: The Plan

“The plan was an **open secret**.” Which literary device is created by the phrase in bold?

A) Personification  
B) Simile  
C) Oxymoron  
D) Onomatopoeia

**Solution:**

1. Name the meaning of each word in the phrase.

:::try
Finish the last step: compare the two meanings and choose the device.
:::answer
*Open* means known to all, and *secret* means hidden. These meanings oppose each other, so “open secret” is an oxymoron. The answer is **C**.

**Check:** “The plan was an open secret” says the plan was both known to all and hidden. That clash marks an oxymoron.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Check the effect.** Ask whether the words compare things, give a human quality, stretch the truth, repeat a sound, or join opposing meanings.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A repeated starting sound: alliteration, not onomatopoeia. {#repeated-sound}
- Two opposing words together: oxymoron, not hyperbole. {#opposing-words}
- A sound word such as *clang* or *bang*: check if the word imitates a noise, which is onomatopoeia. {#onomatopoeia}
:::
