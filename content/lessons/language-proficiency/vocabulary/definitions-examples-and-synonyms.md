## Core Concept

### Definitions and Examples

A **context clue** is information near an unfamiliar word that helps you work out its likely meaning.
- **Definition or restatement:** the text explains the word in another way.
- **Example:** the text gives examples of what the word includes.

We find the meaning of *candid* from a restatement.

- Sentence: “Her **candid** remarks, or honest opinions, offended some people.”
- *or honest opinions* explains *candid remarks*.
- So *candid* means honest: “Her honest remarks offended some people.”

:::pair #restatement-not-reaction
Right: *Candid* means honest.
Wrong: *Candid* means rude.
The remarks offended people, but that is their effect. The restatement gives the meaning.
:::

:::check
“The chef used various **utensils**, such as spatulas, tongs, and whisks.” What does *utensils* mean?
:::answer
The answer is tools. The examples are all tools for preparing food.
:::

### Nearby Synonyms and Sentence Meaning

- **Synonym or near-synonym:** a nearby word has the same or almost the same meaning.

We read the meaning of *sanction* from the sentence.

- Sentence: “The committee's **sanction** allowed the policy to proceed, since it had full official approval.”
- *Full official approval* says it was allowed, so *sanction* means approval here: “The committee's approval allowed the policy to proceed.”

:::pair #sentence-meaning
Right: *Sanction* means approval here.
Wrong: *Sanction* means punishment here.
*Sanction* can also mean a penalty. This sentence says the policy was allowed.
:::

:::check
“The instructions were **concise**, giving every rule in brief, clear words.” What does *concise* mean?
:::answer
The answer is brief and clear. The nearby words *brief, clear* explain *concise*: “The instructions were brief and clear.”
:::

## Examples

### #1: The King's Edict

The king's **edict**, or official command, required all citizens to pay the tax. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) request

B) suggestion

C) official order

D) secret

**Solution:**

1. The words *or official command* explain **edict**.
2. So it means official order. Answer: **C**.

**Check:** “The king's official order required all citizens to pay the tax.” It still makes sense.

### #2: The Forest Animals

The forest contains many **nocturnal** animals, such as owls, bats, and flying foxes, that are active after dark. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) dangerous

B) flying

C) active at night

D) noisy

**Solution:**

1. The examples are animals active after dark.
2. So **nocturnal** means active at night. Answer: **C**: “The forest contains many animals active at night, such as owls, bats, and flying foxes.”

**Check:** Each example, owls, bats, and flying foxes, fits active at night.

### #3: The Clerk's Interview

The clerk was **courteous** throughout the interview. She remained polite even when an applicant spoke rudely. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) hurried

B) respectful

C) uncertain

D) talkative

**Solution:**

1. Find the word in the next sentence that describes the clerk's behavior.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice matches that word's meaning?
:::answer
The word is *polite*, so the answer is **B**, *respectful*. Both describe the same good behavior.

**Check:** “The clerk was respectful throughout the interview.” It still makes sense.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Hide the unfamiliar word.** Ask what meaning the sentence needs.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A restatement, often after *or*: take the meaning from it, not from how people react. {#restatement-not-reaction}
- A word with more than one meaning: pick the meaning the sentence needs. {#sentence-meaning}
- A nearby synonym: read the unfamiliar word through it, then check the sentence. {#nearby-synonyms-and-sentence-meaning}
:::
