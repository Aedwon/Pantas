## Core Concept

### One-Syllable Words

In many one-syllable words, double the last consonant before *-ing* or *-ed*. The base must end in one vowel letter followed by one consonant letter.

This ending is called the **consonant-vowel-consonant** pattern. Check the letters at the end, not the sound.

Take *run*.

1. *R* is a consonant, *u* is a vowel, and *n* is a consonant.
2. The pattern fits, so double the *n*.
3. Add *-ing*.

*Run* becomes *running*.

Do not double final *w*, *x*, or *y*.

- *Mix* becomes *mixing*.
- *Stay* becomes *staying*.

:::check
Spell *hop* plus *-ing* and *box* plus *-ing*.
:::answer
The answers are *hopping* and *boxing*. *Hop* ends in *o* plus *p*, so *p* doubles; *box* ends in *x*, so it does not double.
:::

### Stress in Longer Words

In most longer words, double only if the last syllable is stressed.

- *Begin* becomes *beginning*.
- *Refer* becomes *referred*.

:::pair #begin-stress
Right: Beginning
Wrong: Begining
The last syllable of *begin* is stressed, so the final *n* doubles.
:::

:::pair #refer-stress
Right: Referred
Wrong: Refered
The last syllable of *refer* is stressed, so the final *r* doubles before *-ed*.
:::

*Visit* has stress on its first syllable. Its final *t* does not double in *visiting*. The final letter pattern alone is not enough.

:::pair #visit-stress
Right: Visiting, beginning
Wrong: Visitting, beginning
*Visit* is stressed on its first syllable, so its *t* stays single.
:::

:::why
The stressed last syllable is the one you hear strongly. A doubled consonant is a common spelling clue for a short stressed vowel. An unstressed last syllable needs no help.
:::

These are useful patterns, not rules for every suffix. Check a standard spelling when the pattern does not settle the answer.

:::check
Spell *open* plus *-ed*.
:::answer
The answer is *opened*. *Open* has stress on its first syllable, not its last, so the final *n* does not double.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A One-Syllable Base

We choose the spelling of *run* plus *-ing*.

A) runing  
B) running  
C) runeing  
D) runnning

**Solution:**

1. *Run* has one syllable.
2. It ends in one vowel letter, *u*, then one consonant letter, *n*.
3. Double the final *n* before *-ing*.

The spelling is **running**. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Say *run-ning*. The doubled *n* is a clue that *u* is short.

### #2: A Stressed Final Syllable

We choose the spelling of *begin* plus *-ing*.

A) beginning  
B) begining  
C) beggining  
D) begininning

**Solution:**

1. Say *begin* in two beats: *be-gin*.
2. The last beat is stressed.
3. The base ends in one vowel letter, *i*, then one consonant letter, *n*.
4. Double that *n* before *-ing*.

The spelling is **beginning**. Answer: **A**.


### #3: An Unstressed Final Syllable

We choose the spelling of *visit* plus *-ing*.

A) visitting  
B) visiteing  
C) visiting  
D) vissiting

**Solution:**

1. Say *visit* in two beats: *vis-it*.
2. The stress is on the first beat, not the last.
3. Add *-ing* without doubling the final *t*.

The spelling is **visiting**. Answer: **C**.


### #4: Limits of Doubling

We choose the pair in which *fix* plus *-ing* and *play* plus *-ing* are both spelled correctly.

A) fixxing; playing  
B) fixing; playying  
C) fixxing; playying  
D) fixing; playing

**Solution:**

1. Check the final letter of *fix*. It is *x*.
2. Check the final letter of *play*. It is *y*.

:::try
Finish the last step: apply the *w*, *x*, and *y* rule to both words and choose the right pair.
:::answer
The answer is **D**, **fixing; playing**. Final *w*, *x*, and *y* do not double before *-ing*.

**Check:** Neither *fix* nor *play* doubles its last letter, and nothing is dropped before *-ing*.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Longer word ending in one vowel and one consonant: check the stress first, as in *beginning*. {#begin-stress}
- Stressed last syllable ending in one vowel and one consonant: usually double the final letter before *-ed*, as in *referred*. {#refer-stress}
- Stress on an earlier syllable: keep one letter, as in *visiting*. {#visit-stress}
:::
