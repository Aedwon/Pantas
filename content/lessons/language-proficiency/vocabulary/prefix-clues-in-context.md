## Core Concept

### Word Parts as Clues

A **prefix** is a word part added at the beginning of a word. For example, *un-* can mean *not*, as in *unfair*.

:::pair #prefix-meaning
Right: In "unfair treatment," *unfair* means not fair.
Wrong: In "unfair treatment," *unfair* means not finished.
The prefix *un-* means *not*, and the base is *fair*.
:::

Not every opening group of letters is a prefix. For *un-* meaning *not*, a real word must be left, as *fair* is in *unfair*. *Uncle* does not mean "not cle." Other prefixes can attach to parts that cannot stand alone, such as *in-* in *include*.

:::pair #real-prefix
Right: *Uncle* has no base *cle*, so *un-* is not a prefix.
Wrong: Every initial *un* means *not*, so *uncle* means "not cle."
*Cle* is not a word, so *un-* is not a prefix in *uncle*. Opening letters alone prove nothing.
:::

:::why
For *un-* meaning *not*, the prefix changes the meaning of a real base word. If nothing real is left after the opening letters, there is no prefix to read.
:::

:::check
In "The water was **unsafe** to drink," which real word is left after *un-*, and what does *unsafe* mean?
:::answer
The answer is *safe*, so *unsafe* means *not safe*. *Safe* is a real base word, *un-* means *not*, and *not safe* fits the sentence.
:::

## Examples

### #1: The Directions

The team read the **unclear** directions three times and still could not follow them. Which phrase is closest in meaning to the word in bold?

A) left out a step

B) printed too small

C) hard to understand

D) not needed

**Solution:**

1. Split the word into its prefix and its base.

:::try
Finish the last step: say what each part means and put them together. Which phrase fits?
:::answer
*Un-* means *not*, and *clear* is a real word, so *unclear* means not clear: hard to understand. The answer is **C**.

**Check:** Put the phrase in the sentence: "The team read the hard-to-understand directions three times and still could not follow them." It makes sense.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Check prefix clues against context. The full word must still fit the sentence.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Opening letters like *un-* meaning *not*: check that a real word is left. It is in *unfair*, not in *uncle*. {#real-prefix}
- A meaning from the prefix: test it in the sentence, as in *unfair* meaning not fair. {#prefix-meaning}
:::
