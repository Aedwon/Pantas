## Core Concept

### Word Parts as Clues

A **prefix** is a word part added at the beginning of a word. For example, *un-* can mean *not*, as in *unfair*.

Use the prefix as a clue. Then check the whole word in the sentence.

:::pair #prefix-meaning
Right: not fair
Wrong: not finished
The prefix *un-* means *not*, and the base is *fair*. *Not finished* is the meaning of *incomplete*, a different word.
:::

Not every opening group of letters is a prefix. *Uncle* does not mean “not cle.” Word parts help only when they belong to the word and give a meaning that fits.

:::pair #real-prefix
Right: Uncle has no base cle.
Wrong: Every initial un means not.
*Cle* is not a word, so *un-* is not a prefix in *uncle*. Opening letters alone prove nothing.
:::

:::why
A prefix changes the meaning of a real base word. If nothing real is left after the opening letters, there is no prefix to read.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Checking a Prefix in Context

We find the meaning of **incomplete** and check it against the sentence.

The report was **incomplete** because it lacked the final page. Which phrase is closest in meaning to the word in bold?

A) not finished  
B) not correct  
C) not clear  
D) not needed

**Solution:**

1. Split the word: *in-* plus *complete*.
2. Here, *in-* means *not*, and *complete* is a real word.
3. The word means not complete, or not finished.

:::try
Finish the last step: does the sentence agree with that meaning?
:::answer
Yes. A report that lacks its final page is not finished. The answer is **A**.

**Check:** Put the phrase in the sentence: "The report was not finished because it lacked the final page." It makes sense.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Check prefix clues against context. A word part can suggest a meaning. The full word must still fit the sentence.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Opening letters like *un-*: check that a real base word is left, as in *uncle*. {#real-prefix}
- A meaning from the prefix: test it in the sentence, as in *unfair* meaning not fair. {#prefix-meaning}
:::
