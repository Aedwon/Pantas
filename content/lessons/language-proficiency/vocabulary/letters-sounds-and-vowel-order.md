## Core Concept

Spelling has patterns, but some words must be learned one by one. Use a pattern to check a word.

### Letters and Sounds

The letters **a, e, i, o, u** are the main **vowel** letters in English. Vowel sounds are made without blocking the flow of air. Sometimes *y* also works as a vowel, as in *my*.

Most other letters usually stand for **consonant** sounds.

A **syllable** is a beat in a word. It usually has one main vowel sound. **Stress** is the extra force used to say one syllable.

- *Begin* has stress on its last syllable.
- *Visit* has stress on its first syllable.

Letters and sounds do not always match one for one.

- In *chief*, the letters *ie* stand for one vowel sound.
- In *manage*, the final *e* has no sound of its own.

:::check
How many main vowel sounds does *field* have?
:::answer
The answer is one. The letters *ie* together make one vowel sound, so *field* has one syllable.
:::

### The Limited I Before E Rhyme

The rhyme “i before e except after c” helps with words such as *believe*, *receive*, and *deceive*. It does not work for every English word.

We check the vowel order in *believe* and *receive*.

- *Believe*: no c before the pair, so *ie* gives *believe*.
- *Receive*: c before the pair, so *ei* gives *receive*.

:::pair #belief-order
Right: The thief held a belief.
Wrong: The thief held a beleif.
There is no c before the pair, so write *ie*. Check the family *believe* and *belief*.
:::

*Weird*, *seize*, *science*, and *height* do not fit the simple rhyme. Words like *neighbor* and *weigh* also use *ei* when the pair makes the long-*a* sound. Learn these spellings as whole words.

:::pair #seize-exception
Right: The goalkeeper tried to seize the ball.
Wrong: The goalkeeper tried to sieze the ball.
*Seize* is an exception. Write *ei* even though no c comes before it.
:::

:::check
Which spelling is correct: *cieling* or *ceiling*?
:::answer
The answer is *ceiling*. It has the long-*e* sound, and a *c* comes before the pair, so the rhyme hints at *ei*. This is also its standard spelling.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Vowel Order

We find the misspelled word in a list of four.

A) achieve

B) percieve

C) sleigh

D) niece

**Solution:**

1. In *achieve*, no c comes right before the pair, so *ie* is right.

:::try
Finish the checking: check the other three words and name the misspelled one.
:::answer
The answer is **B**, *percieve*. It has the long-*e* sound and a *c* before the pair, so the rhyme hints at *ei*: *perceive*. *Sleigh* is correct because *ei* spells its long-*a* sound. *Niece* has the long-*e* sound and no c before the pair, so *ie* is correct.

**Check:** Spell B as *perceive*. It now matches *receive*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Use patterns to check a spelling. For an exception, recall the whole word instead of forcing the pattern.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- No c before the pair in a long-*e* word: the rhyme hints at *ie*, as in *belief*. Check the usual spelling. {#belief-order}
- A known exception such as *seize*: keep *ei*, because the rhyme does not cover every word. {#seize-exception}
:::
