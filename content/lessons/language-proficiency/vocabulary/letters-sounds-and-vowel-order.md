## Core Concept

Spelling has patterns, but some words must be learned one by one. Use a pattern to check a word. Do not let it overrule a standard spelling you know.

### Letters and Sounds

The letters **a, e, i, o, u** are the main **vowel** letters in English. Vowel sounds are made without blocking the flow of air. Sometimes *y* also acts as a vowel, as in *my*.

The other letters are **consonant** letters. Consonant sounds usually involve blocking or narrowing the flow of air with the lips, tongue, or teeth. Examples include the sounds of *p*, *t*, and *s*.

A **syllable** is a beat in a word with one main vowel sound. **Stress** is the extra force used to say one syllable.

- *Begin* has stress on its last syllable.
- *Visit* has stress on its first syllable.

Letters and sounds do not always match one for one.

- In *chief*, the letters *ie* stand for one vowel sound.
- In *manage*, the final *e* has no sound of its own.

:::check
How many main vowel sounds does *chief* have?
:::answer
The answer is one. The letters *ie* together make one vowel sound, so *chief* has one syllable.
:::

### The Limited I Before E Rhyme

The rhyme “i before e except after c” helps with words such as *believe*, *receive*, and *conceive*. It does not work for every English word.

Here is the rhyme on two words.

- *Believe*: no c before the pair, so write *ie*.
- *Receive*: c before the pair, so write *ei*.

:::pair #belief-order
Right: The thief held a belief.
Wrong: The thief held a beleif.
There is no c before the pair, so write *ie*. Check the family *believe* and *belief*.
:::

*Weird*, *seize*, *science*, and *height* do not fit the simple rhyme. Learn these spellings as whole words.

:::pair #seize-exception
Right: The goalkeeper tried to seize the ball.
Wrong: The goalkeeper tried to sieze the ball.
*Seize* is an exception. Write *ei* even though no c comes before it.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Vowel Order

We find the misspelled word in a list of four.

A) chief  
B) concieve  
C) neighbor  
D) seize

**Solution:**

1. In *chief*, no c comes before the pair, so *ie* is right.
2. In *concieve*, a c comes before the pair, so the standard spelling is *conceive*, with *ei*.
3. *Neighbor* is a standard spelling.
4. *Seize* is an exception, so *ei* is right.
5. Only *concieve* is misspelled. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Spell B as *conceive*. It now matches *receive*.

:::try
Which spelling is correct: *recieve* or *receive*? Use the rhyme.
:::answer
The answer is *receive*. A c comes before the pair, so write *ei*.

**Check:** *Receive* matches *conceive*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Use patterns to check a spelling. For an exception, recall the whole word instead of forcing the pattern.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- No c before the pair: write *ie*, as in *belief*. {#belief-order}
- A known exception such as *seize*: keep *ei*, because the rhyme does not cover every word. {#seize-exception}
:::
