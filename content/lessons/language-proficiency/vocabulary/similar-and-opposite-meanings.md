## Core Concept

### Synonyms

A **synonym** is a word or phrase with the same or a closely related meaning in a sentence.
Words with close meanings can differ in strength. Choose the word that keeps the meaning and fits the sentence.

:::check
In "She made a bold move and took a big risk," which word keeps the meaning of *bold*: *daring*, *polite*, *tired*, or *mild*?
:::answer
The answer is *daring*: "She made a daring move and took a big risk." Both words mean willing to take risks.
:::

### Word Pairings

A **collocation** is a group of words that people commonly use together. English uses *heavy rain*, not *strong rain*. For a large amount, *heavy* is a usual partner. For an intense flavor, *strong* is, as in *strong tea*.

:::pair #heavy-rain
Right: The road closed during heavy rain.
Wrong: The road closed during powerful rain.
English still pairs *heavy* with *rain*.
:::

:::check
Which pairing sounds natural in standard English: *heavy traffic* or *thick traffic*?
:::answer
The answer is *heavy traffic*. A large amount of traffic takes *heavy*.
:::

### Antonyms

An **antonym** is a word with an opposite or contrasting meaning. The right opposite must match the meaning used in the sentence.

Let's find the antonym of *mitigate* in "The new rules mitigate the problem":

- *Mitigate* makes a problem less severe.
- Its antonym makes the problem more severe.
- *Aggravate* does that: "The new rules aggravate the problem."

:::pair #mitigate-opposite
Right: The opposite of *mitigate* is *aggravate*.
Wrong: The opposite of *mitigate* is *alleviate*.
*Alleviate* means to ease, so it is a synonym, not an opposite.
:::

:::check
Which word is an antonym of *abundant*: *scarce*, *plentiful*, *common*, or *large*?
:::answer
The answer is *scarce*. *Abundant* means plentiful, and *scarce* means hard to find enough of.
:::

## Examples

### #1: The Word Ephemeral

Choose the word closest in meaning to the word in bold: **ephemeral**.

A) eternal

B) fleeting

C) glowing

D) rigid

**Solution:**

1. **Ephemeral** means lasting for a very short time.
2. *Eternal* and *rigid* do not mean that, and *glowing* is about light.
3. **Fleeting** has the closest meaning. The answer is **B**.

**Check:** Read it in a sentence: "The rainbow was ephemeral, gone in minutes." *Fleeting* fits.

### #2: The Word Vague

Select the word most nearly opposite to the word in bold: **vague**.

A) precise

B) unclear

C) hazy

D) general

**Solution:**

1. *Unclear*, *hazy*, and *general* are close to *vague*. They are not opposites.
2. **Precise** means exact and clear. The answer is **A**.

**Check:** "The directions were precise" says the reverse of "The directions were vague."

### #3: A Cup of Coffee

Which phrase sounds most natural in standard English for coffee with a very intense flavor?

A) heavy coffee

B) strong coffee

C) mighty coffee

D) hard coffee

**Solution:**

1. Test each choice with *coffee*.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice is the natural English pairing?
:::answer
The answer is **B**, **strong coffee**. Coffee with an intense flavor takes *strong*, not *heavy*, *mighty*, or *hard*.

**Check:** "I need a cup of strong coffee." It sounds natural in standard English.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A task that asks for an opposite: pick the word that reverses the meaning, not a synonym. {#mitigate-opposite}
- A word pairing: use the usual partner, as in *heavy rain*. {#heavy-rain}
- A synonym choice: check that it keeps the meaning and fits the sentence. {#synonyms}
:::
