## Core Concept

### Vowel Suffixes and Soft Sounds

A **suffix** is a word part added at the end of a base. The **base** is the word or part to which the suffix is added.

A final silent *e* is often dropped before a suffix that begins with a vowel.

- *Manage* becomes *managing*.
- *Hope* becomes *hoping*.

Sometimes *e* stays to help keep a sound. In *noticeable*, the *e* stays after *c*. This keeps the soft *s* sound of *c*. After *g*, *courage* keeps its *e* before *-ous* the same way.

:::pair #soft-g
Right: *Courageous* is spelled correctly. The *e* stays after *g*.
Wrong: *Courageous* is misspelled. Drop the *e* before *-ous*.
Without the *e*, the *g* would sound hard.
:::

:::check
Spell *believe* plus *-able*.
:::answer
The answer is *believable*. *-able* starts with a vowel and *v* needs no soft sound, so drop the silent *e*.
:::

### Exceptions Before Consonant Suffixes

Most words keep a final silent *e* before a suffix that starts with a consonant, such as *care* becoming *careful*.
- *True* becomes *truly*, even though *-ly* begins with a consonant.
- *Argue* becomes *argument*, which also loses its *e*.

:::pair #truly
Right: Truly
Wrong: Truely
*True* is an exception. It loses the *e* before *-ly*.
:::

:::why
Learn *truly* and *argument* as exceptions. No simple rule covers them, so memory is the only tool.
:::

:::check
Spell *hope* plus *-ful* and *safe* plus *-ly*.
:::answer
The answers are *hopeful* and *safely*. Both suffixes start with a consonant, and neither word is an exception, so both keep the *e*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Dropping a Silent E

We choose the spelling of *manage* plus *-ing*.

A) manageing  
B) managing  
C) mannaging  
D) managging

**Solution:**

1. *Manage* ends in a silent *e*.
2. *-ing* starts with a vowel.
3. Drop the final *e*.

The spelling is **managing**. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** *Manage* without its final *e*, plus *-ing*, is *manag* + *ing*. Only one *i* comes before *ng*.

### #2: Keeping a Silent E

We choose the spelling of *notice* plus *-able*.

A) noticable  
B) noticeable  
C) noticeible  
D) notiseable

**Solution:**

1. The base is *notice*, and the suffix is *-able*.
2. The *e* sits after *c*.
3. Keep the *e* so the *c* keeps its soft *s* sound.

The spelling is **noticeable**. Answer: **B**.


### #3: An Exception Before Ly

We choose the spelling of *true* plus *-ly*.

A) truely  
B) trueley  
C) truly  
D) truley

**Solution:**

1. The base is *true*, and the suffix is *-ly*.
2. *-ly* starts with a consonant, so we might keep the *e*.
3. This word is an exception. It loses its final *e*.

The spelling is **truly**. Answer: **C**.


### #4: A Consonant Suffix After Silent E

Choose the correctly spelled word to complete the sentence: "The staff could not hide their ______ about the new office."

A) excitment  
B) excitemant  
C) excitement  
D) excitmant

**Solution:**

1. Identify the base and suffix: *excite* plus *-ment*.
2. *-ment* begins with a consonant. Is *excite* an exception?

:::try
Finish the last step: apply the spelling rule for *excite* plus *-ment* and choose the correct option.
:::answer
Choice **C**, **excitement**. *-ment* starts with a consonant, and *excite* is not one of the exceptions, so it keeps its silent *e*.

**Check:** Read it back: "The staff could not hide their excitement about the new office." *Excitement* keeps the *e*, as *careful* does.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Vowel suffix with no soft sound to keep: often drop the *e*, as in *managing*. A few words, such as *dyeing*, keep it. {#vowel-suffixes-and-soft-sounds}
- Silent *e* after *c* or *g* before *-able* or *-ous*: keep the *e* for the soft sound, as in *noticeable* and *courageous*. {#soft-g}
- Exceptions before *-ly* and *-ment*: drop the *e* in *truly* and *argument*. {#truly}
:::
