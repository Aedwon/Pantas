## Core Concept

### Adding Suffixes

A **suffix** is a word part added at the end of a base. The **base** is the word or part to which the suffix is added.

A final silent *e* is often dropped before a suffix that begins with a vowel.

- *Manage* becomes *managing*.
- *Hope* becomes *hoping*.

Sometimes *e* stays to help keep a sound. In *noticeable*, the *e* stays after *c*. This keeps the sound of *s* heard at the end of *notice*, often called the soft sound of *c*.

Some forms must be learned separately.

- *True* becomes *truly*, even though *-ly* begins with a consonant.
- *Argue* becomes *argument*, with no final *e* from the base.

:::pair #truly
Right: Truly
Wrong: Truely
Many words keep the *e* before a suffix that starts with a consonant. *True* is an exception, so it loses the *e* before *-ly*.
:::

:::why
Learn *truly* and *argument* as exceptions. No simple rule covers them, so memory is the only tool.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Dropping a Silent E

We choose the spelling of *manage* plus *-ing*.

A) manageing  
B) managing  
C) mannaging  
D) managging

**Solution:**

1. *Manage* ends in a silent *e*.
2. *-ing* starts with a vowel.
3. Drop the final *e*.

The spelling is **managing**. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Say it: *man-a-jing*. No *e* sound is heard before *-ing*.

### #2: Keeping a Silent E

We choose the spelling of *notice* plus *-able*.

A) noticable  
B) noticeable  
C) noticeible  
D) notiseable

**Solution:**

1. The base is *notice*, and the suffix is *-able*.
2. The *e* sits after *c*.
3. Keep the *e* so the *c* keeps its soft *s* sound.

The spelling is **noticeable**. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Without the *e*, *c* would read as a hard *k* sound before *a*.

### #3: An Exception Before Ly

We choose the spelling of *true* plus *-ly*.

A) truely  
B) trueley  
C) truly  
D) truley

**Solution:**

1. The base is *true*, and the suffix is *-ly*.
2. *-ly* starts with a consonant, so we might keep the *e*.
3. This word is an exception. It loses its final *e*.

The spelling is **truly**. Answer: **C**.

**Check:** *Truly* is one of the exceptions to learn.

### #4: An Exception Before Ment

Choose the correctly spelled word to complete the sentence: “The report presents a strong ______ for extending office hours.”

A) arguement  
B) argumant  
C) argument  
D) arguemant

**Solution:**

We build the word from *argue* plus *-ment*.

1. The base is *argue*, and the suffix is *-ment*.
2. This word is an exception. The final *e* does not stay.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice is *argue* plus *-ment* with no *e* before the suffix?
:::answer
Choice **C**, **argument**.

**Check:** *Argument* is one of the exceptions to learn, like *truly*.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- *e* after *c* before *-able*: keep the *e*, as in *noticeable*.
- Vowel suffix, no sound to protect: drop the *e*, as in *managing*.
- *True* plus *-ly*: drop the *e*, as in *truly*. {#truly}
- *Argue* plus *-ment*: drop the *e*, as in *argument*.
:::
