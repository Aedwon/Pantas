## Core Concept

### Vowel Suffixes and Soft Sounds

A **suffix** is a word part added at the end of a base. The **base** is the word or part to which the suffix is added.

A final silent *e* is often dropped before a suffix that begins with a vowel.

Let's add *-ing* to each base:

- *Manage* + *-ing*: drop the *e*, giving *managing*.
- *Hope* + *-ing*: drop the *e*, giving *hoping*.

Sometimes *e* stays before *a* or *o* to help keep a sound. In *noticeable*, the *e* stays after *c*. This keeps the soft *s* sound of *c*. After *g*, *courage* keeps its *e* before *-ous* the same way.

:::pair #soft-g
Right: *Courageous* is spelled correctly. The *e* stays after *g*.
Wrong: *Courageous* is misspelled. Drop the *e* before *-ous*.
Without the *e*, the *g* would sound hard.
:::

:::check
Spell *believe* plus *-able*.
:::answer
The answer is *believable*. *-able* starts with a vowel and *v* needs no soft sound, so drop the silent *e*.
:::

### Consonant Suffixes

Most words keep a final silent *e* before a suffix that starts with a consonant, such as *care* becoming *careful*. A few words, such as *true* and *argue*, are exceptions:

- *True* + *-ly*: the *e* drops even though *-ly* begins with a consonant, giving *truly*.
- *Argue* + *-ment*: the *e* drops too, giving *argument*.

:::pair #truly
Right: Truly
Wrong: Truely
*True* is an exception. It loses the *e* before *-ly*.
:::

:::why
Learn *truly* and *argument* as exceptions. No simple rule covers them, so memory is the main tool.
:::

:::check
Spell *use* plus *-ful* and *safe* plus *-ly*.
:::answer
The answers are *useful* and *safely*. Both suffixes start with a consonant, and neither word is an exception, so both keep the *e*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Manage

Choose the spelling of *manage* plus *-ing*.

A) manageing

B) managing

C) mannaging

D) managging

**Solution:**

1. *Manage* ends in a silent *e*.
2. *-ing* starts with a vowel.
3. Drop the final *e*.

The spelling is **managing**. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** *Manag* + *ing*: the *g* is followed directly by *-ing*, with no leftover *e*.

### #2: Notice

Choose the spelling of *notice* plus *-able*.

A) noticable

B) noticeable

C) noticeible

D) notiseable

**Solution:**

1. The base is *notice*, and the suffix is *-able*.
2. The *e* sits after *c*.
3. Keep the *e* so the *c* keeps its soft *s* sound.

The spelling is **noticeable**. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** *Notice* + *able* keeps both parts whole: *notice* then *able*.

### #3: True

Choose the spelling of *true* plus *-ly*.

A) truely

B) trueley

C) truly

D) truley

**Solution:**

1. The base is *true*, and the suffix is *-ly*.
2. *-ly* starts with a consonant, so we might keep the *e*.
3. This word is an exception. It loses its final *e*.

The spelling is **truly**. Answer: **C**.

**Check:** *Tru* + *ly*: no *e* is left between *u* and *l*.

### #4: A Sentence Blank

Choose the correctly spelled word to complete the sentence: "The staff could not hide their ______ about the new office."

A) excitment

B) excitemant

C) excitement

D) excitmant

**Solution:**

1. Find the base and the suffix in the word the sentence needs.

:::try
Finish the last step: apply the spelling rule to that base and suffix and choose the correct option.
:::answer
Choice **C**, **excitement**: *excite* plus *-ment*. *-ment* starts with a consonant, and *excite* is not one of the exceptions, so it keeps its silent *e*.

**Check:** Read it back: "The staff could not hide their excitement about the new office." *Excitement* keeps the *e*, as *careful* does.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Vowel suffix such as *-ing*: often drop the *e*, as in *hoping* and *managing*. {#vowel-suffixes-and-soft-sounds}
- Silent *e* after *c* or *g* before *-able* or *-ous*: usually keep the *e* for the soft sound, as in *noticeable* and *courageous*. {#soft-g}
- Exceptions such as *truly* and *argument*: drop the *e*. {#truly}
:::
