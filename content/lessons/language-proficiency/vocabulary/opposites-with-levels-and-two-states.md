## Core Concept

### Gradable Opposites

**Gradable opposites** have levels between them. *Hot* and *cold* are opposites, but *warm* and *cool* fall between them.

Here is the ladder from top to bottom.

- Hot
- Warm
- Cool
- Cold

Something that is not hot may still be warm. So *not hot* does not always mean *cold*.

:::pair #not-hot
Right: The water is not hot. It may be warm.
Wrong: The water is not hot. It must be cold.
Warm and cool sit between hot and cold. *Not hot* does not pick one of them.
:::

:::check
A cup is not cold. Must it be hot?
:::answer
The answer is no. It may be cool or warm, because the levels between cold and hot are still possible.
:::

### Complementary Opposites

**Complementary opposites** divide a stated system into two states. If those are the only states allowed, ruling out one leaves the other.

- The record has two labels: alive and dead.
- A fish is not alive.
- So the label is dead.

Use that rule only when the stated system allows no middle state. A stone is not alive, but calling it dead would not fit this use.

:::pair #two-labels
Right: The log has only "available" and "unavailable." A terminal that is not available is "unavailable."
Wrong: The log has only "available" and "unavailable." A terminal that is not available is "partially available."
The log allows two labels. A middle label is not one of them.
:::

:::check
A switch has only two states: on and off. If the switch is not on, what state is it in?
:::answer
The answer is off. With only two states allowed, ruling out *on* leaves *off*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A System with Two States

A record classifies each fish as either alive or dead, with no other category. A fish is recorded as not **alive**. Which opposite of the word in bold must describe its state in this record?

A) weak  
B) asleep  
C) injured  
D) dead

**Solution:**

1. The record allows only *alive* or *dead*.
2. Ruling out *alive* leaves **dead**. The answer is **D**.

**Check:** The record uses only *alive* and *dead*. Since the fish is not *alive*, *dead* is the only label left.

### #2: Opposites with Middle Levels

The box is not **large**. Given the meaning of the word in bold, which statement must be true based on this sentence alone?

A) The box is tiny.  
B) The box is medium.  
C) The box is small.  
D) The exact size is not given.

**Solution:**

1. *Large* and *tiny* are opposites with middle sizes between them.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice matches what we know from "not large" alone?
:::answer
The answer is **D**, **the exact size is not given**. A, B, and C each force one size, when *tiny*, *medium*, or *small* are all possible.

**Check:** A medium box is not large, and a tiny box is not large, so no single size is forced.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For opposites, check the relationship. Are there middle levels, only two allowed states, or two people in opposite roles? Do not use a two-state rule when middle levels are possible.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Opposites with middle levels: "not hot" does not have to mean "cold." {#not-hot}
- A system with only two labels: ruling out one leaves the other. {#two-labels}
:::
