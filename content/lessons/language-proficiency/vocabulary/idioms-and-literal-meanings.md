## Core Concept

### Idioms and Literal Meanings

An **idiom** is a familiar expression whose meaning may differ from the meanings of its separate words. *Back to square one* means returning to the beginning of a process.

A **literal** meaning is the direct meaning of the words. The same words can be literal in one setting and an idiom in another. Read what happens around the phrase to decide.

We read an idiom against its sentence.

- Sentence: “After failing the exam three times, Maria decided to go back to square one and restart her review.”
- The word *restart* shows what happens.
- So *back to square one* means to start over.

:::pair #start-over
Right: *Back to square one* means to start over.
Wrong: *Back to square one* means to abandon the attempt.
Maria restarts her review. She does not quit.
:::

:::check
“Dana asked the reporter to keep her plan private, and he promised not to spill the beans.” What does *spill the beans* mean?
:::answer
The answer is to reveal a secret. Dana wants the plan kept private, so *spill the beans* means telling it.
:::

### Filipino Idioms (Sawikain)

Ang **sawikain** (idiom) ay pahayag na may nakasanayang kahulugan. Maaaring iba ito sa tuwirang kahulugan ng bawat salita. Ang *naghigpit ng sinturon* ay maaaring mangahulugang nagtipid.

Ngayon, basahin natin ang sawikain sa pangungusap nito.

- Pangungusap: “Bumaba ang kita ng pamilya, kaya naghigpit sila ng sinturon at ipinagpaliban ang pagbili ng bagong kagamitan.”
- Bumaba ang kita, at ipinagpaliban ang bili.
- Kaya ang *naghigpit ng sinturon* ay nagtipid.

:::pair #naghigpit-ng-sinturon
Right: Ang *naghigpit ng sinturon* ay nagtipid.
Wrong: Ang *naghigpit ng sinturon* ay naghanda sa paglalakbay.
Hindi ito tungkol sa totoong sinturon. Ang kahulugan ay ayon sa sitwasyon ng pamilya.
:::

:::check
Hindi sumama sa kainan si Ben at sinabing, “Butas ang bulsa ko ngayon.” Ano ang ibig sabihin ng *butas ang bulsa*?
:::answer
Ang sagot ay walang pera. Hindi siya sumama sa kainan, kaya walang perang pambayad.
:::

## Examples

### #1: An English Idiom

After losing his unsaved research paper, Mark knew he was **back to square one**. What does the phrase in bold mean?

A) To play a board game  
B) To return to the beginning  
C) To become angry  
D) To give up permanently

**Solution:**

1. Mark lost his work and must start again.
2. **Back to square one** means to return to the beginning. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Put *had to start again* in place of *was back to square one*: “Mark knew he had to start again.”
### #2: The Same Words Used Literally

During a board game, Mark moved his piece **back to square one** after drawing a penalty card. What does the phrase in bold mean in this sentence?

A) He restarted his research.  
B) He stopped playing the game.  
C) He returned his piece to the first space.  
D) He forgot the rules of the game.

**Solution:**

1. The sentence describes a piece moving on a game board.
2. The words name an actual move to the first space. The meaning is literal. Answer: **C**.


### #3: Sawikain sa Pangungusap

Nagalit agad si Tina sa isang simpleng biro dahil **balat-sibuyas** siya. Ano ang ibig sabihin ng salitang binigyang-diin?

A) Madaling masaktan ang damdamin  
B) Mahilig magluto  
C) Mabagal kumilos  
D) Matalino sa klase

**Solution:**

1. Maliit na biro lang ang ikinagalit ni Tina.

:::try
Tapusin ang huling hakbang: aling pagpipilian ang angkop sa sitwasyong iyan?
:::answer
Ang sagot ay **A**, **madaling masaktan ang damdamin**. Kapag maliit na bagay ang ikinagagalit, ang ibig sabihin ng *balat-sibuyas* ay madaling masaktan ang damdamin.

**Check:** Palitan ang salita: “Nagalit agad si Tina sa isang simpleng biro dahil madaling masaktan ang damdamin niya.” Tugma ito sa sitwasyon.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Start with context.** An idiom's known meaning must fit what happens in the sentence.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- An idiom such as *back to square one*: take the meaning from what happens in the sentence. {#start-over}
- A Filipino idiom: read the situation, not the real object. {#naghigpit-ng-sinturon}
- The same phrase in a new setting: check whether the meaning is literal or an idiom. {#idioms-and-literal-meanings}
:::
