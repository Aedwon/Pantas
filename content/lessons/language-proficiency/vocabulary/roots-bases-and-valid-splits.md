## Core Concept

### Roots, Bases, Affixes, and Stems

A **root** carries a word's main meaning. A **base** is the word or part to which another part is added. In *unhappy*, *happy* is both the root and the base.

An **affix** is a part added to a base to change its meaning or job. A **stem** is the form to which the next affix is added. A **prefix** comes before the base: *un-* in *unhappy*. A **suffix** comes after it: *-less* in *careless*.

Here is *helpfulness* built one step at a time.

- Start with *help*.
- Add *-ful* to make *helpful*.
- Add *-ness* to *helpful* to make *helpfulness*.
- *Helpful* is the stem before *-ness* is added.

:::pair #stem-before-suffix
Right: In *helpfulness*, the stem for *-ness* is *helpful*.
Wrong: In *helpfulness*, the stem for *-ness* is *-ness*.
A stem is the form that receives the suffix. The suffix is not the stem.
:::

:::check
In *hopelessness* (built from *hope*, then *-less*, then *-ness*), what is the stem before *-ness*?
:::answer
The answer is *hopeless*. *-Ness* is added to *hopeless*, so *hopeless* is the stem.
:::

### Valid Splits and False Prefixes

Word parts can help you work out meaning, but a familiar set of letters is not always a real word part.

Ask whether your split leaves a meaningful base. Then check whether the parts explain the whole word.

- In *unhappy*, *un-* means "not."
- *Happy* is a real base.
- The split explains the word.

In *uncle*, the first letters are also *un*, but *cle* is not its base. The split does not explain the word. Even when a real base is left, check which prefix is used.

:::pair #inflammable
Right: *Inflammable* means easily ignited. Its *in-* comes from *inflame* ("cause to catch fire"), not "not."
Wrong: *Inflammable* means protected from fire. The *in-* is read as "not."
Here *in-* is not the negative prefix *in-* ("not"). Read the sentence too.
:::

:::check
In *ready*, what is left after the letters *re*, and is *re-* a real prefix?
:::answer
The answer is *ady*, and no. *Ady* is not a base with a meaning, so *re-* changes nothing.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Four Splits

We test four splits and keep the one that explains its word.

A) pretty = pre- + tty  
B) replay = re- + play  
C) mother = mo- + ther  
D) unite = un- + ite

**Solution:**

1. In A, *tty* is not a base with a meaning. The split fails.

:::try
Finish the last step: check the other three splits. Which one is valid, and why?
:::answer
The answer is **B**. In C, *ther* is not a base, and in D, *ite* is not a base, so those splits fail. In B, *play* is a meaningful base, and *re-* changes it to "play again," so the split explains the whole word.

**Check:** Join the parts of B. *Re-* plus *play* gives *replay*, which means "play again."
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Use word parts to narrow the meaning. Then read the whole sentence to check your choice.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- The stem for a suffix: take the form before it, such as *helpful* for *-ness*. {#stem-before-suffix}
- Matching starting letters: check that removing them leaves a real base, as *happy* does and *cle* does not. {#valid-splits-and-false-prefixes}
- *Inflammable*: the *in-* comes from *inflame* ("cause to catch fire"), not "not." {#inflammable}
:::
