## Core Concept

### -ly as an Adverb

The suffix **-ly** often makes an adverb. In "She spoke quietly," *quietly* tells how she spoke.

- *Quiet* describes a noun.
- Add *-ly* to make *quietly*.
- *Quietly* tells how an action happens.

:::pair #ly-adverb
Right: In "The officer read the instructions quietly," *quietly* tells how the officer read.
Wrong: In "The officer read the instructions quietly," *quietly* tells which officer was present.
An adverb tells about the action. It does not pick out a person.
:::

:::check
In "He walked slowly," what does *slowly* tell the reader?
:::answer
The answer is how he walked. *Slowly* is an adverb.
:::

### -ly as an Adjective

This does not always hold. *Friendly* ends in *-ly*, but it describes a person or thing, so it is an adjective. Check the job the word does in the sentence.

:::pair #friendly-adjective
Right: In "the friendly receptionist," *friendly* is an adjective.
Wrong: In "the friendly receptionist," *friendly* is an adverb of manner.
*Friendly* describes the noun *receptionist*. It does not tell how an action happens.
:::

:::check
In "The lively crowd cheered loudly," which *-ly* word tells how the crowd cheered?
:::answer
The answer is *loudly*. It tells how the action happens, so it is an adverb. *Lively* describes the noun *crowd*, so it is an adjective.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Meaning and Word Job

Which statement correctly describes the words in bold in "The **helpful** clerk answered **quickly**"?

A) Helpful names a quality; quickly names a person.  
B) Helpful describes the clerk; quickly tells how the clerk answered.  
C) Helpful tells how the clerk answered; quickly describes the clerk.  
D) Helpful means without help; quickly means without speed.

**Solution:**

1. *-Ful* gives *helpful* the idea of giving help. It describes the clerk, so it is an adjective.
2. *-Ly* makes *quickly* tell how the clerk answered. It is an adverb.
3. Only B gives both jobs correctly. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Replace the words in bold: "The clerk who gives help answered fast." Both meaning and grammar match **B**.

### #2: The Garden

In "A **lovely** garden surrounded the house," what job does the word in bold do?

A) It names the act of surrounding.  
B) It tells how the house was built.  
C) It describes the garden.  
D) It names a group of houses.

**Solution:**

1. The ending *-ly* does not settle the job. See how **lovely** is used in the sentence.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice names the job *lovely* does?
:::answer
The answer is **C**, **it describes the garden**. *Lovely* sits before the noun *garden*, so it is an adjective despite its *-ly* ending.

**Check:** "A lovely garden surrounded the house." *Lovely* describes the noun *garden*, not an action.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A word ending in -ly that tells how an action happens: adverb, as in "read quietly." {#ly-adverb}
- A word ending in -ly that describes a noun: adjective, as in "a friendly clerk." {#friendly-adjective}
:::
