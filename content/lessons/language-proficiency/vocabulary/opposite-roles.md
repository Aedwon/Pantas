## Core Concept

### Opposite Sides of One Exchange

**Converse opposites** describe the same relationship from different roles.

Let's switch the viewpoint from the seller to the buyer:

- Ana sells a book to Ben.
- Ben buys the book from Ana.

The book stays the same. The person doing the action changes.

:::pair #same-exchange
Right: If the office bought a printer from a supplier, the supplier sold the printer.
Wrong: If the office bought a printer from a supplier, the supplier bought the printer.
The office is the buyer, so the supplier is on the other side of the exchange and sold it.
:::

Do the same for borrow and lend.

:::pair #borrow-lend
Right: If Ana borrows a book from Ben, Ben lends the book to Ana.
Wrong: If Ana borrows a book from Ben, Ben returns the book to Ana.
*Borrow* and *lend* are two sides of one exchange. *Return* is a later step, not the other side.
:::

:::why
The exchange has two sides. Each word tells what one side does.
:::

:::check
If Dina lends a pen to Leo, which verb names Leo's role: *borrows*, *returns*, or *lends*?
:::answer
The answer is *borrows*. Leo takes the pen from Dina. *Lend* and *borrow* are two sides of one exchange.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Gift

Nina gave a gift to Rico. From Rico's point of view, what did he do?

A) gave the gift to Nina

B) received the gift from Nina

C) wrapped the gift for Nina

D) returned the gift to Nina

**Solution:**

1. Nina is the giver.
2. Rico is on the other side of the same exchange.

:::try
Finish the last step: what is Rico's role?
:::answer
Rico is the receiver. He **received the gift from Nina**. The answer is **B**.

**Check:** Test D: Rico cannot return a gift he never had, so D does not fit.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- One person sells: the other buys, as in the supplier and the office. {#same-exchange}
- One person borrows: the other lends. {#borrow-lend}
:::
