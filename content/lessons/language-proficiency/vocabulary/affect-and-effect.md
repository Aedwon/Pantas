## Core Concept

Similar words can have different jobs. Check both the meaning and the job needed in the sentence.

### Affect vs. Effect

A **verb** tells an action or state, such as *change* or *seem*. A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea, such as *teacher* or *result*.

**Affect** usually means "to influence." It is a verb.

- Rain can affect travel.

**Effect** usually means "a result." It is a noun.

- The delay had a serious effect.

:::pair #influence-verb
Right: The drought will affect the crop yield.
Wrong: The drought will effect the crop yield.
The sentence needs a verb that means influence. *Effect* is usually the result noun. As a verb, it means bring about.
:::

The **base form** of a verb is its plain form, such as *affect*, without endings such as *-s* or *-ed*. Use this form after *will*, *can*, or *may*.

:::pair #base-form
Right: The drought will affect the crop yield.
Wrong: The drought will affects the crop yield.
After *will*, the verb stays in its base form. Do not add *-s*.
:::

:::check
Choose the correct word: Heavy traffic can *(affect / effect)* our arrival time.
:::answer
The answer is *affect*. The sentence means influence, and *can* needs the base form.
:::

### Less Common Uses

As a verb, *effect* means "to bring about."

- The reform effected change.

:::pair #bring-about
Right: The mediator effected an agreement yesterday.
Wrong: The mediator affected an agreement yesterday.
The mediator made the agreement happen. *Affected* only means influenced.
:::

In psychology, *affect* can be a noun for the outward signs of emotion, such as a person's voice and face.

:::check
Choose the correct word: During the exam, the patient showed a flat *(affect / effect)* with little facial expression.
:::answer
The answer is *affect*. In psychology, the noun *affect* names outward signs of emotion.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Influence

The prolonged drought will severely ______ crop yield.

A) affect  
B) effect  
C) affects  
D) effects

**Solution:**

1. The word after *will* must be a verb in its base form. This removes C and D.
2. The drought influences the crop yield. The meaning is "influence."
3. *Affect* means influence. Answer: **A**.

**Check:** Read it back: "The drought will affect crop yield." *Effect* would need a different meaning, "bring about."

### #2: A Result

The new schedule had a positive ______ on attendance.

A) affect  
B) effect  
C) affected  
D) effected

**Solution:**

1. The words *a positive* come before the gap. A noun must follow.
2. C and D are verbs. A, *affect*, is usually a verb too. This leaves B.
3. The noun *effect* means a result. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** "The new schedule had a positive effect on attendance."
### #3: Bringing About Change

Which sentence uses the word in bold correctly?

A) The rain will **effect** our travel plans.  
B) The new law **effected** major changes in procedure.  
C) The medicine had no **affect**.  
D) The delay had a serious **affect** on service.

**Solution:**

1. In A, the rain influences plans. It needs *affect*, so A is wrong.
2. In C and D, a noun is needed after *no* and *serious*. It names a result, so *effect* is right. C and D are wrong.
3. In B, the law made changes happen. As a verb, *effect* means bring about, so *effected* fits. Answer: **B**.

**Check:** Replace the bold word in B with "brought about." "The new law brought about major changes" keeps the meaning.

### #4: The Nurse's Notes

The nurse recorded the patient's anxious ______ during the visit.

A) affect  
B) effect  
C) affected  
D) effected

**Solution:**

1. The word *anxious* comes before the gap, and *the patient's* shows a noun is needed. This removes C and D.
2. Both A and B can be nouns.

:::try
Finish the last step: choose A or B.
:::answer
The answer is *affect*, choice **A**. In psychology, *affect* is a noun for outward emotion.

**Check:** Read it back: "The nurse recorded the patient's anxious affect during the visit." Anxiety shown in face and voice is outward emotion, so *affect* fits.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Start with meaning for *affect* and *effect*. Ask whether the sentence means influence, result, bring about, or an outward sign of emotion.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A verb that means influence: use *affect*, in its base form after *will*, as in "will affect travel." {#influence-verb}
- A verb that means bring about: use *effect*, as in "effected change." {#bring-about}
- A noun that means a result: use *effect*. In psychology, the noun for outward emotion is *affect*. {#less-common-uses}
:::
