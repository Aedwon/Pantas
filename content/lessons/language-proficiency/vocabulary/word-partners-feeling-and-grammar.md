## Core Concept

### Word Partners and Feeling

- **Word partners:** some words often appear together. In *file a complaint*, *file* means submit, not smooth a surface.
- **Connotation:** a word can carry a feeling beyond its basic meaning. *Slim* and *scrawny* both suggest being thin, but *scrawny* suggests being too thin.

Let's use the partners *fee* and *invoice* to read *charged* in this sentence: “The hotel charged the guest for an extra night, and the fee appeared on the final invoice.”

- The partners point to *billed*: “The hotel billed the guest for an extra night, and the fee appeared on the final invoice.”

:::pair #charged-partner
Right: The hotel charged the guest for an extra night, and the fee appeared on the final invoice. Here *charged* means billed.
Wrong: The hotel charged the guest for an extra night, and the fee appeared on the final invoice. Here *charged* means accused.
The partner words *extra night* and *fee* point to billing. *Accused* usually goes with a crime.
:::

:::check
In "break a promise," what does *break* mean?
:::answer
The answer is fail to keep. The partner *promise* is something you keep or fail to keep.
:::

### Register and Grammar

- **Register:** wording can be formal or casual. *Request* sounds more formal than *ask for*.- **Grammar:** check the word's job. Does the sentence need a thing, an action, or a describing word?

First guess the meaning from the sentence. Then put a possible answer in place of the target word. It must keep the meaning and fit the grammar.

:::pair #furnish-grammar
Right: Please furnish proof of residence.
Wrong: Please furniture proof of residence.
In a request like this, a verb follows *please*. *Furnish* is the verb here. *Furniture* is a noun.
:::

*Qualified* has more than one meaning. Look at *qualified support* in “The residents gave the project qualified support: they favored it only if drainage repairs were completed first.”

:::pair #qualified-meaning
Right: *Qualified support* means limited support.
Wrong: *Qualified support* means certified support.
The phrase *only if* shows a limit. *Certified* is a different meaning of *qualified*.
:::

:::check
In "The workers will strike unless the company raises their pay," what does *strike* mean?
:::answer
The answer is stop working as a protest. The words *workers* and *unless the company raises their pay* show a demand.
:::

## Examples

### #1: The Court Clerk

The court clerk will **serve** the notice to the tenant on Monday. What does the word in bold most likely mean?

A) wait on tables

B) deliver

C) work as

D) be useful to

**Solution:**

1. *Serve* has more than one meaning.
2. Here, its partner is *notice*.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice fits the partner and the grammar in *serve the notice to the tenant*?
:::answer
The answer is **B**, **deliver**. *Serve* can mean bring food or work as a duty. *Deliver* is a verb that takes *the notice* as its object and *to the tenant* after it.

**Check:** Put *deliver* back: “The court clerk will deliver the notice to the tenant on Monday.” It keeps the meaning and the grammar.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Put the choice back.** Check the full sentence for meaning and grammar.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A word with a partner: let the partner pick the meaning, as *extra night* and *fee* pick *billed*. {#charged-partner}
- A replacement word: keep the same job, such as a verb for a verb. {#furnish-grammar}
- A word with more than one meaning: use the detail around it, as *only if* picks *limited*. {#qualified-meaning}
:::
