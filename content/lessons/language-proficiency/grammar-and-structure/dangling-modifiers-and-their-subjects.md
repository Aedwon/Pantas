## Core Concept

### Matching an Opening Phrase to the Main Subject

When a sentence begins with a subjectless opening phrase that describes a person or thing in the sentence (*"Driving through the mountains,"* *"Exhausted from the hike,"* *"To meet the deadline,"*), the phrase attaches to the **main subject**, the subject of the main clause. Ask who does it or is in that state.

A **dangling modifier** happens when the main subject is not the one the phrase describes. In *"After finishing the marathon, a warm bath felt amazing,"* the main subject is *a warm bath*, making the sentence say the bath finished the race.

An opening phrase is fine when the main subject can logically do that action (*"Working toward the deadline, the editors requested more time"*).

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: two stacked sentences: top "After finishing the marathon, [a warm bath] felt amazing." with a crossed line from "finishing" to "a warm bath" labeled "dangling: bath cannot finish a race"; bottom "After finishing the marathon, [she] enjoyed a warm bath." with a teal arrow from "finishing" to "she" labeled "matching main subject" -->

:::pair #passive-or-later-pronoun
Right: *"After finishing the marathon, she enjoyed a warm bath."*
Wrong: Write *"After finishing the marathon, a warm bath was enjoyed by her."* or *"After finishing the marathon, the warmth of the bath amazed her."*
Putting *her* in *"by her"* or *"amazed her"* does not fix the phrase. The runner must be the main subject (*she*).
:::

:::pair #activity-noun-subject
Right: *"Excited about the trip, we packed the suitcases a week early."* and *"Exhausted from the hike, the campers quickly pitched their tents."*
Wrong: Write *"Excited about the trip, packing the suitcases was finished a week early."* or *"Exhausted from the long hike, pitching the tents took the campers an hour."*
Making an activity (*packing*, *pitching*) the main subject leaves the phrase dangling, since activities cannot feel excited or tired. Name the people (*we*, *the campers*) as the main subject.
:::

:::check
Does *"Reading the departure board, the train arrived late"* have a dangling modifier?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. *The train* is the main subject, and a train cannot read a board.
:::

### Two Ways to Fix a Dangling Modifier

Fix a dangling opening phrase in two ways:

1. **Change the main subject:** *"Rushing to catch the bus, my coffee spilled"* -> *"Rushing to catch the bus, **I** spilled my coffee."*
2. **Turn the phrase into a full clause:** *"While approaching the gate, the barking of a dog startled Liza"* -> *"As **Liza approached** the gate, the barking of a dog startled her."*

*While* or *after* before an *-ing* word dangles unless the main subject is the doer: *"While walking home, Ben found a wallet."*

:::why
Why can *"As Liza approached the gate, the barking of a dog startled her"* have a different main subject? *"As Liza approached the gate"* is a full clause with its own subject *Liza* and verb *approached*, so *the barking* does not take over Liza's action.
:::

:::pair #while-ing-no-subject
Right: *"As Liza approached the gate, the barking of a dog startled her."* and *"After the guests arrived, dinner was served."*
Wrong: Write *"While approaching the gate, the barking of a dog startled Liza."* or *"After arriving at the house, dinner was served to the guests in the hall."*
*While* or *after* before an *-ing* word supplies no subject. Name the actor inside the opening clause, or make that actor the main subject.
:::

:::check
Which avoids a dangling modifier: *"While walking to class, a bird landed on Ana's shoulder"* or *"While Ana walked to class, a bird landed on her shoulder"*?
:::answer
The answer is **"While Ana walked to class, a bird landed on her shoulder."** Naming *Ana* inside the opening clause leaves *a bird* free to be the main subject.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Diagnosing a Dangling Modifier

What is the problem in *"Having studied all night, the exam seemed easy"*?

**Solution:**

1. *"Having studied all night"* needs a person who studied.
2. The main subject is *the exam*, which cannot study.
3. So *"Having studied all night, the exam seemed easy"* **dangles**: *the exam* is the wrong doer.

**Check:** Ask who studied all night: *the exam* cannot.

### #2: Fixing With the Main Subject

Revise *"Rushing to catch the bus, my coffee spilled everywhere"* so the runner is the main subject.

**Solution:**

1. Identify who was rushing: *I*.
2. Put *I* right after the comma: **"Rushing to catch the bus, I spilled my coffee everywhere."**

**Check:** *I* can rush; *my coffee* cannot.

### #3: Comparing Two Openings

Choose the sentence without a dangling modifier:
(A) *"While crossing the bridge, the strong wind blew Marco's hat away."*
(B) *"As Marco crossed the bridge, the strong wind blew his hat away."*

**Solution:**

1. Compare the openings: *"While crossing the bridge"* (A) vs. *"As Marco crossed the bridge"* (B).
2. Ask who crossed the bridge in each version.

:::try
Finish: choose (A) or (B), and prove why it avoids a dangling modifier.
:::answer
The answer is **(B) "As Marco crossed the bridge, the strong wind blew his hat away."** Inside (B)'s opening clause, *Marco* is the subject and *crossed* is the finite verb, so *the strong wind* can safely serve as the main subject. In (A), *"While crossing the bridge"* lacks a subject, wrongly making *the strong wind* cross the bridge.

**Check:** Ask "Who crossed the bridge?" Sentence (B) answers *Marco* right inside the opening clause before the comma.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Read the opening phrase and check the main subject. If it does not fit, change the main subject or turn the phrase into a full clause.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Putting the doer in *by her* or an object: make the doer the main subject. {#passive-or-later-pronoun}
- Using an activity noun like *packing* or *pitching* as the main subject: name the people doing or feeling the phrase. {#activity-noun-subject}
- Opening with *while* or *after* plus an *-ing* word: add a subject inside the opening clause, or match the main subject. {#while-ing-no-subject}
:::
