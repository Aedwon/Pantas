## Core Concept

### Joining Independent Clauses with a Semicolon

A **semicolon (;)** joins two closely related independent clauses without a joining word: *"The train was late; the passengers remained calm."* Each side has a subject and finite verb and can stand alone.

Joining two independent clauses with only a comma is a **comma splice**: *"The concert was amazing, everyone stayed until the end."* Fix a comma splice in one of three ways:

1. Replace the comma with a semicolon (;).
2. Replace the comma with a period (.).
3. Add a coordinating conjunction (*and, but, so, yet, or, for, nor*) after the comma.

Condition: a semicolon here needs an **independent clause on both sides**. Never put a semicolon before a dependent clause (*"; because she visits..."*), before a phrase (*"; after regular servicing"*), or right after *and* (*"and;"*).

:::pair #comma-splice-same-topic
Right: Fix *"The concert was amazing, everyone stayed until the end"* by replacing the comma with a semicolon (;).
Wrong: Keep the comma because both clauses concern the same concert.
Sharing a topic does not let a comma alone join two complete clauses in formal writing. Test both halves as standalone sentences.
:::

:::pair #semicolon-before-dependent-part
Right: "She loves reading; she visits the library every weekend."
Wrong: "She loves reading; because she visits the library every weekend."
*Because she visits the library every weekend* has a subject and verb, yet *because* makes it dependent. A semicolon cannot join a main clause to a dependent clause.
:::

:::check
Can a semicolon join *"The machine runs smoothly"* and *"after regular servicing"*?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. *"After regular servicing"* is a phrase with no subject or finite verb, so it cannot follow a semicolon here.
:::

### Separating Complex List Items

When list items already contain commas, commas alone blur the items together. Keep commas inside each item and place semicolons between the full items.

Look at *"We visited Manila, Philippines; Tokyo, Japan; and Seoul, Korea."* Each city-country pair keeps its inside comma, and semicolons divide the three pairs.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: marked-up list "We visited [Manila, Philippines] ; [Tokyo, Japan] ; and [Seoul, Korea]." with each city-country pair bracketed, the internal commas shown in gray, and the boundary semicolons highlighted in teal -->

:::why
Why switch to semicolons for a list with inside commas? Semicolons act as heavier dividers than commas, so the reader sees three two-part items instead of six separate words.
:::

:::pair #semicolons-in-complex-list
Right: "We visited Manila, Philippines; Tokyo, Japan; and Seoul, Korea."
Wrong: "We visited Manila, Philippines, Tokyo, Japan, and Seoul, Korea." or "We visited Manila; Philippines, Tokyo; Japan, and Seoul; Korea."
All commas blur the pairs into six places, while semicolons inside each pair split each city from its country. Keep commas inside pairs and put semicolons between pairs.
:::

:::check
Where do the semicolons go in *"The delegates came from Calamba, Laguna / Vigan, Ilocos Sur / and Malolos, Bulacan"*?
:::answer
The answer is **After *Laguna* and after *Ilocos Sur***. Each city-province pair keeps its internal comma, and semicolons divide the three pairs.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Fixing a Comma Splice

Identify and fix the error: *"The concert was amazing, everyone stayed until the end."*

**Solution:**

1. Test the first half: *"The concert was amazing"* stands alone.
2. Test the second half: *"everyone stayed until the end"* stands alone.
3. Replace the comma splice with a semicolon (;).

Corrected sentence: **"The concert was amazing; everyone stayed until the end."**

**Check:** Put a period at the semicolon: *"The concert was amazing."* *"Everyone stayed until the end."* Both are complete sentences.

### #2: Why a Semicolon Fails Before a Phrase

Why is the semicolon faulty in *"The machine runs smoothly; after regular servicing"*?

**Solution:**

1. *"The machine runs smoothly"* has subject *the machine* and finite verb *runs*, so it is independent.
2. *"After regular servicing"* has no subject and no finite verb, so it is a phrase.
3. Because the second half is not an independent clause, the semicolon is faulty.

**Check:** Read *"After regular servicing."* alone. It leaves the reader waiting, proving it cannot stand as a sentence.

### #3: Punctuating a List with Internal Commas

Which sentence uses semicolons to separate city-country pairs while keeping commas inside each pair?

(A) "We visited Manila, Philippines, Tokyo, Japan, and Seoul, Korea."
(B) "We visited Manila; Philippines, Tokyo; Japan, and Seoul; Korea."
(C) "We visited Manila, Philippines; Tokyo, Japan; and Seoul, Korea."

**Solution:**

1. Group each whole pair: (1) *Manila, Philippines*, (2) *Tokyo, Japan*, (3) *Seoul, Korea*.
2. Keep the internal comma inside each pair.

:::try
Finish the last step: explain why (A) and (B) fail, and select the correct choice.
:::answer
The answer is **(C) "We visited Manila, Philippines; Tokyo, Japan; and Seoul, Korea."** Choice (A) mixes inside and outside commas, and (B) splits each city from its country with semicolons.

**Check:** Bracket each pair in (C): *[Manila, Philippines]*, *[Tokyo, Japan]*, *[Seoul, Korea]*. Commas stay inside; semicolons sit at the pair boundaries.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Cover the semicolon and test both sides as standalone sentences; if either side needs the other, drop the semicolon. For complex lists, bracket each full item first, then place semicolons only between brackets.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Two independent clauses joined by only a comma: fix the comma splice with a semicolon, period, or comma plus joining word. {#comma-splice-same-topic}
- Semicolon before a *because*-clause or phrase: check that both sides can stand alone as full sentences. {#semicolon-before-dependent-part}
- List of items with internal commas: keep commas inside each item and put semicolons between items. {#semicolons-in-complex-list}
:::
