## Core Concept

### Apostrophes for Ownership

An **apostrophe (')** can show ownership. Always name the **owner** first, check whether it is singular or plural, then add the ending:

1. Singular noun: add *'s*: *Maria* -> *"Maria's book."*
2. Plural noun ending in *s*: add only *'* after the *s*: *teachers* -> *"the teachers' room."*
3. Plural noun not ending in *s*: add *'s*: *children* -> *"the children's books."*

For a singular name ending in *s* (such as *James*), many style guides add *'s* (*"James's bag"*), while some allow *"James' bag"*. Follow the rule stated in the question.

:::pair #singular-name-possessive
Right: "Maria's book was left on the table."
Wrong: "Marias book was left on the table." or "Marias' book was left on the table."
*Marias* drops the apostrophe, while *Marias'* treats *Maria* as plural. Start with singular *Maria* and add *'s*.
:::

:::check
Write ownership for several students: "the *(student's / students' / students's)* lockers."
:::answer
The answer is **Students'**. The plural owner *students* already ends in *s*, so add only an apostrophe after *s*.
:::

### Apostrophes in Contractions

A **contraction** drops letters. Put the apostrophe **where letters drop** in *do not*, *it is*, and *it has*:

1. *do not* drops *o* -> *don't*.
2. *it is* drops *i* -> *it's*.
3. *it has* drops *ha* -> *it's*.

Test *it's* by expanding it to *it is* or *it has*.

:::pair #contraction-apostrophe-spot
Right: "I don't want to go outside today."
Wrong: "I dont want to go outside today." or "I do'nt want to go outside today."
*Dont* drops the apostrophe, and *do'nt* places it before *n* instead of over the missing *o* in *do not*.
:::

:::check
In *"It's too cold outside,"* does *it's* mean *it is* or *it has*?
:::answer
The answer is **It is**. *"It is too cold outside"* fits, while *"It has too cold outside"* does not.
:::

### Its vs. It's

**Its** (no apostrophe) shows ownership: *"The company announced its new product line yesterday."* **It's** (with an apostrophe) always stands for *it is* or *it has*.

:::why
Why does possessive *its* have no apostrophe? Possessive pronouns such as *his*, *hers*, *ours*, *yours*, *theirs*, and *its* never take apostrophes.
:::

:::pair #its-vs-its-contraction
Right: "The company announced its new product line yesterday."
Wrong: "The company announced it's new product line yesterday." or "The company announced its' new product line yesterday."
*"Announced it is new product line"* breaks the sentence, and *its'* is never standard. Ownership for *it* uses *its* with no apostrophe.
:::

:::check
Choose the right form: "The dog wagged *(its / it's)* tail."
:::answer
The answer is **Its**. *"Wagged it is tail"* fails, and the sentence shows ownership.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Singular, Plural, and Irregular Plural Owners

Finish each sentence with ownership: (a) The bike belongs to Leo. It is ___ bike. (b) The bus is used by the players. It is the ___ bus. (c) The coats belong to the men. They are the ___ coats.

**Solution:**

1. For (a), *Leo* is singular, so add *'s*: **"It is Leo's bike."**
2. For (b), *players* is plural ending in *s*, so add *'*: **"It is the players' bus."**
3. For (c), *men* is plural without *s*, so add *'s*: **"They are the men's coats."**

**Check:** Read "the bus used by the players": the same owner, *players*, appears with its apostrophe after the *s*.

### #2: Expanding a Contraction

In *"It's been a long day,"* what does *it's* mean?

**Solution:**

1. Test *it is*: *"It is been a long day"* fails.
2. Test *it has*: *it's* means *it has*, because **"It has been a long day"** fits.

**Check:** Read *"It has been a long day."* The helping verb *has* pairs directly with *been*.

### #3: Choosing Between Its and It's

Choose the form that completes the sentence: *"The bakery changed ___ opening hours last week."*

(A) Its

(B) It's

(C) Its'

(D) It has

**Solution:**

1. Read each choice in the blank between *changed* and *opening hours*.

:::try
Finish the last step: choose the form that completes the sentence.
:::answer
The answer is **(A) Its**: **"The bakery changed its opening hours last week."** The hours belong to the bakery, so ownership is needed. (B) expands to *"it is opening hours"* and (D) to *"it has opening hours"*, and neither fits. (C) *Its'* is invalid; *its* with no apostrophe shows ownership.

**Check:** Swap in a possessive noun: *"the bakery's opening hours."* It means the same, so **Its** is right.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For a choice between *its* and *it's*, expand *it's* to *it is* or *it has*; if neither fits and the word shows ownership, use *its*. For noun possessives, write the owner word first, then choose *'s* (singular or irregular plural) or *'* (plural ending in *s*).

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Singular owner such as *Maria*: write *Maria's*, never *Marias* or *Marias'*. {#singular-name-possessive}
- Contraction such as *don't*: put the apostrophe over the omitted letter, never before *n* or at the end. {#contraction-apostrophe-spot}
- Ownership for *it*: test *it is* and *it has* first, then use *its* with no apostrophe. {#its-vs-its-contraction}
:::
