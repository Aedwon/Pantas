## Core Concept

### Correlative Conjunctions: Matching Both Sides

**Correlative conjunctions** work in pairs (such as *either...or*, *neither...nor*, *not only...but also*, *both...and*). Make the two parts the pair joins match in grammatical form:

1. Two adjectives: *"The policy is not only practical but also affordable."*
2. Two *to + verb* phrases: *"She wanted both to travel and to save money."*
3. Two base verbs after *will*: *"The company will neither raise prices nor cut jobs."*

In *"The festival was not only fun but also it was educational,"* *fun* (an adjective) follows *not only*, while *it was educational* (a full clause) follows *but also*. Drop *it was* to leave two adjectives: *"The festival was not only fun but also educational."*

Also check the two choices after *either* and *or*. *Either* before a shared verb is allowed but can hide the contrast. In *"Applicants may either submit online or by mail,"* *either* sits before *submit*, *or* before *by mail*. Moving *either* shows the two choices, *online* and *by mail*: *"Applicants may submit either online or by mail."*

:::pair #mismatched-correlative-pair
Right: Revise *"The festival was not only fun but also it was educational."* to **"The festival was not only fun but also educational."**
Wrong: Change *"it was educational"* to *"to educate people,"* or change *"fun"* to *"enjoyably."*
Match *fun*, an adjective: *to educate people* fails, and *enjoyably* leaves the clause *it was educational*.
:::

:::pair #misplaced-correlative-connector
Right: *"Applicants may submit either online or by mail."*
Wrong: *"Applicants may either submit online or a mailed application."*
*A mailed application* is a noun phrase, so it does not match *submit online*. Make the two choices match: *online* and *by mail*.
:::

:::check
Choose the matching verb in "The shop will neither lower prices nor ___ discounts": *(offer / offering / offers)*
:::answer
**The shop will neither lower prices nor offer discounts.** The shared helping verb *will* governs both base verbs: *will lower* and *will offer*.
:::

### Comparisons: Comparing Like to Like

Comparisons with *than* or *as* should compare the same kind of thing or activity on both sides.

In *"Reading a book is more relaxing than to watch television,"* the left side uses *-ing* (*reading*) while the right switches to *to watch*. Change *to watch* to *watching*: *"Reading a book is more relaxing than watching television."*

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: two balanced comparison cards joined by "is lower than": left card "The cost of a bus trip" tagged "cost"; right card "the cost of a taxi trip" tagged "cost", with "a taxi driver" and "taking a taxi" crossed out below -->

:::why
Why can you not say *"Her report is longer than him"*? A report is writing, while *him* is a person. Comparing *her report* to *his report* keeps the comparison fair.
:::

:::pair #comparing-unlike-things
Right: *"The cost of a bus trip is lower than the cost of a taxi trip."*
Wrong: *"The cost of a bus trip is lower than taking a taxi to the same destination."*
The left side names a cost, while the right switches to an activity (*taking a taxi*).
:::

:::check
Which ending keeps *"Swimming daily is healthier than ___"* parallel: *"to sit all day"*, *"sitting all day"*, or *"a sofa"*?
:::answer
**Swimming daily is healthier than sitting all day.** *Swimming daily* and *sitting all day* are both *-ing* activities.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Fixing a Correlative Pair

Identify and fix the error: *"He is both intelligent and he works hard."*

**Solution:**

1. Find the pair: *both...and*.
2. After *both* sits the adjective *intelligent*; after *and* sits the clause *he works hard*.
3. Replace the clause with a matching adjective: **"He is both intelligent and hardworking."**

**Check:** Read each description after *is*: *is intelligent* and *is hardworking* match.

### #2: Moving *Either* for Clarity

Revise *"He either wanted to win the race or to finish in the top three"* so *either* and *or* introduce the two goals.

**Solution:**

1. After *either* sits *wanted to win the race*; after *or* sits *to finish*.
2. Move *either* after the shared verb: *"He wanted either to win the race or to finish in the top three."*

**Check:** Read each goal after *wanted*: *wanted to win the race* and *wanted to finish in the top three*.

### #3: Fixing Two Errors in One Passage

Fix both errors in: *"The room is neither dark nor it is small. The rent is lower than the apartment next door."*

**Solution:**

1. In sentence 1, *neither* is followed by the adjective *dark* and *nor* by the clause *it is small*. Replace the clause: *"The room is neither dark nor small."*

:::try
Finish the last step: the writer compares what it costs to rent each apartment for one month. Which rewrite of sentence 2 is fair?

(A) *"The rent is lower than the deposit for the apartment next door."*

(B) *"The rent is lower than the rent for the apartment next door."*
:::answer
**B: "The rent is lower than the rent for the apartment next door."** Now a rent is compared with a rent.

**Check:** In A, the right side is a deposit, so the rent is compared with a different cost.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For *than* and *as*, name the thing on each side (*cost* vs. *cost*).

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Correlative pairs (*not only...but also*, *both...and*): make the two parts they join match in form. {#mismatched-correlative-pair}
- Two choices after *either* or *neither*: make them match, such as *online* and *by mail*. Moving the connector can show them more clearly; if they already match (*either to win or to lose*), leave it. {#misplaced-correlative-connector}
- Comparisons with *than* or *as*: compare like with like, such as two costs. {#comparing-unlike-things}
:::
