## Core Concept

### Active Versus Passive Voice

First find the **subject** and the whole **verb group**. In **active voice**, the subject usually does the action: "The dog **chased** the ball."

This lesson covers the common **passive voice**, where the verb group is *be* + a past participle and the subject is the one affected: "The ball **was chased** by the dog." The **agent** (the doer, *the dog*) may follow *by* or be left out. The participle can name an action (*was chased*) or a state (*is loved*).

Judge voice by the whole verb group, not by the object or the doer after *by*.
:::pair #check-subject-not-by-phrase
Right: "The dog chased the ball" is active voice, and "The ball was chased by the dog" is passive voice.
Wrong: Call "The dog chased the ball" passive voice because the ball receives the action, or call "The ball was chased by the dog" active voice because the dog is the actor.
Look at the whole verb group, not the object or the doer after *by*.
:::

:::check
Is "The chef cooked the meal" active or passive voice?
:::answer
The answer is **Active voice**. The verb group is just *cooked*, with no *be* + past participle, so it is active.
:::

### The Be + Past Participle Pattern

Passive voice usually uses **be + past participle** (*is checked*, *was chased*, *had been submitted*, *have been changed*). The helper keeps the tense clear:

- "The report **had already been submitted** by the intern." (Past perfect + passive voice.)

Watch out for **linking constructions**. A form of *be* plus an adjective describes a state, not an action: "She **is** happy" links *she* to the adjective *happy*. Some participles also work as adjectives, so "The window was broken" can describe a state.

:::pair #be-plus-adjective
Right: "She is happy" is not passive because *is* links the subject to an adjective.
Wrong: Think *happy* must be a past participle whenever it follows a form of *be*.
Check whether the word after *be* is a participle (*was invited*, *is loved*) or an adjective that describes the subject (*is happy*).
:::

:::check
Which sentence is passive: "The package was heavy" or "The package was delivered"?
:::answer
The answer is **"The package was delivered."** *Delivered* is a past participle naming an action done to the package, while *heavy* is an adjective.
:::

### When Passive Voice Fits Best

Use passive voice when the agent is unknown, obvious, or less important than the result: "The access codes **have been changed**."
:::why
Why do formal notices often use passive voice? When readers need to know what changed rather than who changed it, naming an unverified actor would add false detail.
:::

:::pair #keep-unknown-agent-passive
Right: If the notice asks for present perfect passive and the doer is unknown, write "The access codes have been changed."
Wrong: Invent a doer ("The administrator has changed the access codes") or use simple past ("The access codes were changed").
Do not invent an actor when the doer is unknown. *Were changed* is also passive, but it is simple past, and the notice asks for present perfect (*have been changed*).
:::

:::check
No one knows who misplaced the files. Which fits better: "The cleaner lost the files" or "The files were lost"?
:::answer
The answer is **"The files were lost."** Passive voice reports what happened without inventing an actor. Naming the cleaner would assume who misplaced the files.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Active, Passive, and Linking Clauses

Label each sentence: (a) "The nurse checked the chart." (b) "The chart was checked by the nurse." (c) "He is calm."

**Solution:**

1. **Active voice**: "The nurse checked the chart." The subject *the nurse* does the checking.
2. **Passive voice**: "The chart was checked by the nurse." *Was checked* is *be* + a past participle.
3. **Linking, not passive**: "He is calm." *Is* links *he* to the adjective *calm*.

**Check:** Only (b) can be rewritten with its actor as subject ("The nurse checked the chart"). *Calm* has no actor.

### #2: Tense and Voice Together

In "Before the inspector arrived, the permits had been signed by the director," identify the tense and voice of *had been signed*.

**Solution:**

1. Find the verb group: *had been signed*.
2. *Had* plus *been* shows **past perfect**.
3. *Been signed* is *be* + a past participle, so the voice is **passive**: "Before the inspector arrived, the permits had been signed by the director."

**Check:** Change it to active: "the director had signed the permits." The tense stays past perfect.

### #3: A Notice About the Lobby Doors

A notice must report that the lobby doors are now locked and that this matters now. Nobody knows who locked them. Choose between "The lobby doors will be locked" and "The lobby doors have been locked."

**Solution:**

1. Neither sentence names a doer, so the missing actor cannot decide.

:::try
Finish the last step: which sentence fits, and why?
:::answer
The answer is **"The lobby doors have been locked."** *Have been locked* is passive (be + past participle) in the present perfect, so it links the locking to now. *Will be locked* is passive too, but it looks ahead.

**Check:** A reader can try the doors at this moment, and only *have been locked* matches what they find.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Find the whole verb group. A *be*-passive is *be* plus a past participle (*was chased*, *is loved*), not an adjective describing a state, such as *calm* or *broken*.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- An object after the verb or an actor after *by*: judge voice from the whole verb group, not other nouns. {#check-subject-not-by-phrase}
- A form of *be* before a describing word like *happy*: test whether it is an adjective (linking), not a passive participle. {#be-plus-adjective}
- Unknown doer in a formal notice: keep passive voice in the right tense instead of inventing an actor. {#keep-unknown-agent-passive}
:::
