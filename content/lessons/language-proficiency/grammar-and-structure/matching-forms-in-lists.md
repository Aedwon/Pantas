## Core Concept

### Matching Forms in Lists and Pairs

**Parallelism**, or **parallel structure**, means items doing the same job in a list or pair use matching grammatical forms:

- **-ing gerunds:** *"Her hobbies include painting, dancing, and singing."* *"The recipe requires chopping onions, mincing garlic, and boiling water."*
- **Infinitives (*to + verb*):** *"The coach told the team to run faster, to jump higher, and to try harder."*
- **Nouns or noun phrases:** *"The committee values accuracy, fairness, and transparency."* Topic names are an exception: *-ing* nouns and a plain noun phrase can sit together, as in *"The workshop covers budgeting, marketing, and sales strategy."*
- **Matching verb tenses in a compound predicate:** *"She opened the folder, checked the total, and signed the form."* Finite verbs sharing a subject keep the same tense when they describe the same time.

Never mix a list item with a full clause (*"tools will be bought"*), a *how-to* clause (*"how to plan sales"*), or a new verb predicate that changes the meaning.

:::pair #fix-only-two-items
Right: In *"Her hobbies include painting, to dance, and singing,"* **Change *to dance* to *dancing*.**
Wrong: Change *painting* to *to paint*, or change *singing* to *to sing*.
Changing *painting* leaves *singing* odd; changing *singing* leaves *painting* odd. Compare all three items and fix the one outlier.
:::

:::pair #changing-meaning-in-pair
Right: Revise *"She enjoys hiking and to swim"* to *"She enjoys hiking and swimming."*
Wrong: Revise it to *"She enjoys hiking and swims in the lake."* or *"She enjoys hiking and to swim in the lake."*
*"And swims"* makes *swims* a second main verb for *she*, so it no longer says she enjoys swimming. Keep both activities in the *-ing* form after *enjoys*.
:::

:::pair #noun-list-vs-clause
Right: Fix *"The report covers the company's history, analyzing its financial growth, and its plans for the future"* by **deleting *analyzing***.
Wrong: Change *its plans for the future* to *planning for the future*, or change *analyzing its financial growth* to *it grew financially*.
Two items (*the company's history* and *its plans for the future*) are noun phrases, and *it grew financially* inserts a full clause. Deleting *analyzing* makes all three matching noun phrases.
:::

:::check
Which completes *"The camp teaches swimming, climbing, and ___"*: *"to paddle"*, *"how to paddle"*, or *"paddling"*?
:::answer
The answer is **paddling**. *Swimming* and *climbing* are *-ing* forms, so *paddling* matches them.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Fixing an Outlier in a List of Infinitives

Find and fix the error: *"The coach told the team to run faster, jumping higher, and to try harder."*

**Solution:**

1. List the three actions after *"told the team"*: *to run faster*, *jumping higher*, *to try harder*.
2. Two items use *to + verb*, while *jumping* uses *-ing*.
3. Change *jumping* to *to jump*: **"The coach told the team to run faster, to jump higher, and to try harder."**

**Check:** Read each item after *told the team*: all three match.

### #2: Matching Two Choices After Between

Revise *"We must choose between repairing the roof and a new roof will be installed"* so both choices match.

**Solution:**

1. After *between*, the first option is an *-ing* phrase (*repairing the roof*), while the second is a full clause (*a new roof will be installed*).
2. Change the second option to a matching *-ing* phrase: **"We must choose between repairing the roof and installing a new roof."**

**Check:** Read both sides right after *between*: *"between repairing the roof"* and *"between installing a new roof."* Both use *-ing* phrases.

### #3: Catching a Hidden Mismatch in a Long List

Find the hidden error in *"The brochure lists the hotel's rooms, offering free breakfast, and its prices,"* and explain why changing *"the hotel's rooms"* to *"touring the hotel's rooms"* is wrong.

**Solution:**

1. Separate the three items after *"The brochure lists"*: (1) *the hotel's rooms*, (2) *offering free breakfast*, (3) *its prices*.
2. Check the form of each item.

:::try
Finish the last step: name the item that does not match, explain why changing item (1) fails, and give the fix.
:::answer
**Delete *offering*.** Items (1) and (3) are noun phrases, and item (2) is the outlier. Changing item (1) to *"touring the hotel's rooms"* leaves item (3) (*its prices*) unmatched. Deleting *offering* gives three noun phrases: *"the hotel's rooms,"* *"free breakfast,"* and *"its prices."*

**Check:** Read *"The brochure lists"* before each repaired item: *"the hotel's rooms,"* *"free breakfast,"* *"its prices."* All three share the noun-phrase form.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Stack list items under their shared lead-in words (*"The report covers..."*, *"We must choose between..."*). Read each item after the lead-in to confirm every item uses the same form and keeps the sentence's meaning.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Changing an already matching item in a three-item list: compare all three items and fix the single outlier. {#fix-only-two-items}
- Replacing an *-ing* item with a finite verb like *swims*: keep both items inside the shared structure so meaning stays intact. {#changing-meaning-in-pair}
- Mixing an *-ing* phrase or full clause into a list of noun phrases: match every item to the surrounding noun phrases. {#noun-list-vs-clause}
:::
