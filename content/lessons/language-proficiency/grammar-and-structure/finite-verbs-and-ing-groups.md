## Core Concept

### Finite Verbs Carry Tense

A **finite verb** is a verb form that carries tense (present or past) and matches a subject, such as *walks, walked, is,* or *was*. Compare *"Ana walks home"* with *"Ana walked home"*: the verb changes when time changes.

A **nonfinite verb** does not show tense by itself. Three nonfinite forms appear often in sentences:

- **-ing participles** used inside progressive verb groups or descriptions (*is preparing*, *running quickly*)
- **-ing gerunds** acting as nouns, such as after a preposition (*before closing*)
- **Infinitives** made of *to* plus the base verb (*to submit*, *to finish*)

In *"The assistant is preparing the documents to submit before closing,"* the helper **is** carries present tense and matches *the assistant*. *Preparing* and *closing* stay unchanged even if you switch to past time (*was preparing*), and *to submit* has no tense form. So *is* is the finite verb.

:::pair #ing-is-not-finite
Right: In *"The assistant is preparing the documents to submit before closing,"* **is** supplies the finite verb form.
Wrong: Pick **preparing**, **to submit**, or **closing** as the finite verb form.
*Preparing* and *closing* are *-ing* forms, and *to submit* is an infinitive. None of those shows tense by itself; only *is* changes to *was* when the sentence shifts to past time.
:::

:::check
Which word supplies the finite verb form in *"The clerks were sorting the files to mail before noon"*?
:::answer
The answer is **were**. Shifting to present time turns *were* into *are*, while *sorting* and *to mail* stay the same.
:::

### Why Standalone -ing Groups Are Not Sentences

A group built on an *-ing* word without a finite helper verb, such as *"running quickly through the park,"* is a **nonfinite verb group**. It names an action, yet it has no tense-carrying verb and no subject paired with a finite verb.

Compare *"Running quickly through the park"* (incomplete nonfinite group) with *"Ana is running quickly through the park"* (*Ana* is the subject and *is* supplies the finite tense form).

:::why
Why do standalone *-ing* groups look like full sentences? An action word like *running* plus a place phrase like *through the park* makes a clear mental picture. Most formal sentences still need a stated subject and a finite verb carrying tense (a command like *Close the door* leaves its subject understood).
:::

:::pair #ing-group-not-clause
Right: In a formal task requiring a complete sentence, *"running quickly through the park"* is **A nonfinite verb group**.
Wrong: Classify *"running quickly through the park"* as an **Independent finite clause**, a **Dependent finite clause**, or a **Prepositional phrase**.
Without a finite helper like *is* or *was*, *running* carries no tense, so the group cannot be any kind of finite clause. And because *running quickly* leads the whole group, *through the park* is only part of it, not the whole label.
:::

:::check
Can *"Checking the forms before noon"* stand alone as a complete formal sentence?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. *Checking* is a nonfinite *-ing* form with no stated subject and no tense-carrying helper like *is* or *was*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Finding the Finite Verb in a Verb Group

Which is a complete formal sentence: *"Running quickly through the park"* or *"Lia was running quickly through the park"*?

**Solution:**

1. In *"Running quickly through the park,"* *running* has no helper verb carrying tense and no stated subject. It is a nonfinite verb group.
2. In *"Lia was running quickly through the park,"* *Lia* is the subject and *was* carries past tense.
3. The second entry is the complete formal sentence.

**Check:** Shift the second sentence to present time: *was* changes to *is* (*"Lia is running"*), proving *was* is the finite verb.

### #2: Sorting -ing Words and Infinitives

In *"The assistant is preparing the documents to submit before closing,"* identify which word supplies the finite verb form.

**Solution:**

1. Find the main subject: *The assistant*.
2. Look at the main verb group *is preparing*: *is* carries present tense and agrees with *the assistant*, while *preparing* is an *-ing* participle.
3. Check the remaining action words: *to submit* is an infinitive and *closing* is an *-ing* noun after *before*, so neither carries tense.

The word that supplies the finite verb form is **is**.

**Check:** Shift the sentence to past time: only *is* changes (*"The assistant was preparing the documents to submit before closing"*).

### #3: Completing an -ing Fragment

Revise *"Preparing the final report for the committee"* into a complete formal sentence by adding a subject and a finite verb.

**Solution:**

1. *Preparing* alone is nonfinite, so the group has no subject and no tense.
2. Add a subject and a tense-carrying verb.

:::try
Finish the last step: write the complete sentence using a finite helper with *preparing*, then test it by shifting the tense.
:::answer
**The coordinator is preparing the final report for the committee.** *The coordinator* is the subject, and *is* supplies present tense.

**Check:** Shift the sentence to past time: *"The coordinator was preparing the final report for the committee."* *Is* changes to *was*, confirming a real finite verb is present.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Run the **Today / Yesterday test** on any verb-shaped word. Shift the sentence between present (*today*) and past (*yesterday*). A finite verb usually changes form (*is*/*was*, *prepares*/*prepared*), though a few look the same in both (*put*). *-ing* words and *to + verb* infinitives never change.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Multi-word verb like *is preparing*: pick the tense-carrying helper (*is*), not the *-ing* word or an infinitive. {#ing-is-not-finite}
- Standalone *-ing* group like *"running quickly through the park"*: label it a nonfinite verb group, not a finite clause or a prepositional phrase. {#ing-group-not-clause}
:::
