## Core Concept

### Groups and Joined Antecedents

A **collective noun** names a group, such as *company* or *team*. Use *it* or *its* when the group acts as one unit: "The company announced its policy."

Some uses, especially in British English, treat a group as its members and use a plural pronoun. To make that meaning clear, name the members: "The team members packed their bags."

Antecedents joined by *and* are normally plural: "Ana and Ben brought their snacks."

With *or* or *nor*, the sentence may mix a single person and a group. A pronoun can then leave the meaning unclear. When editing, name the people meant or remove the pronoun if it adds nothing.

For example, "Neither the players nor the coach brought his or their required equipment" is awkward. "Neither the players nor the coach brought the required equipment" keeps the main meaning clear.

## Examples

### #1: A Group as One Unit

Choose the correct form: "The company announced (its / his) new policy yesterday."

**Solution:**

1. The company is an organization acting as one unit.
2. Use *its*: "The company announced its new policy yesterday."

### #2: Antecedents Joined by And

Choose the correct form: "Liza and Carlo, who share one project, finished (her / their) project early."

**Solution:**

1. Liza and Carlo share the project.
2. Use *their* to show that the project belongs to both. "Liza and Carlo, who share one project, finished their project early."

### #3: Mixed Number with Nor

Edit this sentence for clear pronoun use. Keep the meaning that neither side brought the required equipment: "Neither the players nor the coach brought his or their required equipment."

**Solution:**

1. *Players* is plural, but *coach* is singular. "His or their" makes the sentence hard to read.
2. The pronoun adds no needed meaning here. Write: "Neither the players nor the coach brought the required equipment."

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating Every Group the Same Way

> A group may act as one unit or as individual members.

"The company changed its policy" treats it as a unit. "The members changed their plans" clearly points to the people.
