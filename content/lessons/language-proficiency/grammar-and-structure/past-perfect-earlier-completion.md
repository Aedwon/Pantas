## Core Concept

### Marking Earlier Completion in the Past

**Past perfect** uses *had* plus a past participle (*had cooked*, *had written*, *had finished*). Use it to show an action was already complete by a past event or time:

- "By the time the guests arrived, she **had cooked** the entire meal."
- "She **had written** the letter by noon yesterday."

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal timeline showing Event 1 marked "had cooked (earlier past, finished first)" before Event 2 marked "guests arrived (later past)", both to the left of "now" -->

*Had* stays the same for every subject. Put *had* on the action that was already finished at the later past point.

:::pair #mark-earlier-past
Right: By the time the guests arrived, she had cooked the entire meal.
Wrong: Use simple past (*cooked*) when the prompt asks for past perfect, or present (*cooks*).
*Arrived* puts the sequence in the past, so present *cooks* breaks the timeline. The requested form, *had cooked*, shows cooking finished before the guests arrived.
:::

:::check
Choose the correct form: "Ana *(had called / has called)* the client before the meeting started yesterday."
:::answer
The answer is **Had called**. *Before the meeting started yesterday* is a past reference point, so earlier completion uses *had* rather than *has*.
:::

### When Time Words Already Show Order

Past perfect is often optional when *before* or *after* already shows order. Compare simple past and past perfect with *before*:

- "She **finished** the report before her boss **asked** for it." (Simple past, clear order.)
- "She **had finished** the report before her boss **asked** for it." (Past perfect, stresses earlier completion.)

:::why
Why are both sentences right with *before*? The word *before* already tells the reader which action came first. Past perfect is still helpful when you want to stress that the first action was completely done, or when the sentence has no clear order word like *before*.
:::

:::pair #before-already-orders
Right: "She finished the report before her boss asked for it" is grammatical because *before* clearly orders the two past events.
Wrong: Claim that any action that happened first must always use past perfect instead, or that the later event must always use past perfect instead of simple past.
Simple past often works if the time word already gives the order. Past perfect marks an action already finished at a later past point, but it is not required.
:::

:::check
In "The tailor measured the coat before the client paid," which event happened first, and which word shows it?
:::answer
**Measuring** came first, and *before* shows it. So *measured* and *paid* need no *had*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Showing Earlier Completion Before a Past Point

Choose the past-perfect form to make earlier completion explicit: "By the time the bus left, Ben ___ (buy) his ticket."

**Solution:**

1. Find the later past point: *the bus left* (simple past).
2. Check when buying happened: it was already complete by the time the bus left.
3. Build past perfect with *had* plus the past participle *bought*: **had bought**.

Correct: "By the time the bus left, Ben had bought his ticket."

**Check:** Retell it in order: Ben bought the ticket, then the bus left.

### #2: Two Past Actions in One Sentence

Complete "When Rita reached the shop, the owner ___ home" with *went* or *had gone*. The owner was already gone.

**Solution:**

1. Identify the two past actions: *reached* and the owner leaving.
2. Mark Rita's arrival as the later past point: *reached the shop*.

:::try
Finish the last step: which form fits, and why?
:::answer
The answer is **had gone**: "When Rita reached the shop, the owner **had gone** home." The owner left before Rita reached the shop, so *had* marks leaving as finished first.

**Check:** Swap in *went*: "the owner went home" would suggest he left after she arrived.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Put two past events on a timeline. Use *had* + past participle on the action completed first. If *before* or *after* already makes the order clear, simple past is often valid too, unless the question asks for past perfect.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A prompt that asks for the past-perfect form: put *had* + past participle on the action finished first. {#mark-earlier-past}
- A sentence with *before* or *after* joining two simple past verbs: simple past often works if the time word already gives the order. {#before-already-orders}
:::
