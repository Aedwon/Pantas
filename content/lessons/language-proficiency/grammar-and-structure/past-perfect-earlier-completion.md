## Core Concept

**Past perfect** uses *had + past participle*. It shows that an action was already complete before a past point: "She had written the letter by noon yesterday."

Past perfect is not needed for every earlier past event. Both "She finished before he arrived" and "She had finished before he arrived" are possible. *Before* already gives the order, while *had finished* stresses earlier completion.

## Examples

### #1: Marking Earlier Completion

Choose the correct form: By the time the meeting started yesterday, Ana *(had finished / has finished / will finish)* the report. She handed out copies as the meeting began.

**Solution:**

1. The meeting started in the past. The report was already complete at that point, so copies were ready to hand out.
2. Choose *had finished*: "By the time the meeting started yesterday, Ana had finished the report."
3. *Had* and the past participle *finished* show completion before that past point.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Requiring Past Perfect for Every Earlier Event

> Words such as *before* can already show the order of past events.

"She finished before he arrived" is correct. "She had finished before he arrived" also works and stresses earlier completion.
