## Core Concept

The **subject** tells who or what a clause is about. The **verb** tells what the subject does or is.

In English, **subject-verb agreement** means that some verb forms change to match the subject. "The dog *barks*" uses a singular subject. "The dogs *bark*" uses a plural subject.

**Number** means singular or plural. A singular subject names one person or thing. A plural subject names more than one.

**Person** means who is speaking or being spoken about. First person uses *I* or *we*. Second person uses *you*.

Third person uses words such as *he, she, it,* or *they*.

Some English verbs show person as well as number. Compare "I *am* ready," "you *are* ready," and "she *is* ready." Also compare "I *have* time" with "he *has* time."

A few situations make agreement harder.

**Words inside an intervening phrase do not become the subject.** In "The basket of apples *is* fresh," the subject head is *basket*. The word *apples* is inside the *of*-phrase.

**Collective nouns** such as *team, family,* and *committee* can name a group as one unit or its members. In American English, singular agreement is common when the group acts as one unit: "The team *is* practicing."

Plural agreement can occur when the members are treated separately, especially in British English. Use the meaning and the variety of English used in the passage.

**Indefinite pronouns** such as *everybody, someone,* and *nothing* normally take a singular verb. "Everybody *is* welcome."

Words such as *all, some, most,* and *none* can take singular or plural agreement based on what they refer to. "Some of the water *is* gone." "Some of the cookies *are* gone."

**Compound subjects** joined by *and* are usually plural. "Ana and Ben *are* here."

With *or, either...or, nor,* and *neither...nor*, standard edited English often makes the verb agree with the nearer subject. "Neither the teacher nor the students *were* late." If the result sounds awkward, rewrite the sentence in a clearer way.

### Filipino is Different

Sa Filipino, hindi karaniwang nagpapalit ang anyo ng pandiwa dahil lamang isahan o maramihan ang paksa. Kaya hindi dapat direktang ilipat ang tuntuning English na *is/are* o *barks/bark* sa pangungusap na Filipino.

Ang **aspekto** (aspect) ay tumutulong magpakita kung tapos na, nagaganap, o hindi pa nagaganap ang kilos. Tingnan ang oras ng pangyayari at anyo ng pandiwa. Halimbawa: "Kumain si Ana kahapon," "Kumakain si Ana ngayon," at "Kakain si Ana mamaya."

Ang **pokus** (voice/focus) ay tumutulong magpakita kung aling kalahok ang sentro ng pandiwa. Sa "Nagluto si Mara ng sopas," si Mara ang gumagawa ng kilos. Sa "Niluto ni Mara ang sopas," ang sopas ang sentro.

Mahalaga rin ang **pananda** (marker). Ang *ang* o *si* ay karaniwang kasama ng **parirala** (phrase) na nasa pokus. Halimbawa: "Kumain **ang bata** ng mangga."

Ang *ng* ay maaaring magmarka ng **layon** (object) na hindi nasa pokus o ng pagmamay-ari. Sa "Kumain ang bata **ng mangga**," ang mangga ang layon ng kilos. Sa "bahay **ng guro**," ipinapakita ng *ng* ang pagmamay-ari o ugnayan.

Ang *sa* ay karaniwang ginagamit sa lugar, direksiyon, o tagatanggap. Sa "Pumunta ang bata **sa paaralan**," ipinapakita ng *sa* ang direksiyon o lugar na pinuntahan.

## Examples

### #1: Person Agreement

Choose the correct verb: I *(am / is)* ready, but she *(am / is)* still preparing.

**Solution:**

1. *I* takes *am* in this form of *be*.
2. *She* takes *is*.
3. Correct: "I *am* ready, but she *is* still preparing."

### #2: Ignoring an Intervening Phrase

Choose the correct verb: The list of items on the shelf *(is / are)* long.

**Solution:**

1. The subject head is *list*.
2. *Items* is inside an *of*-phrase and does not control the verb.
3. *List* is singular, so the correct verb is *is*.

### #3: Collective Noun

Choose the natural American-English form when the committee acts as one unit: The committee *(has / have)* reached a decision.

**Solution:**

1. The sentence treats the committee as one unit.
2. Singular *has* fits that meaning in American English.
3. Correct: "The committee *has* reached a decision."

### #4: Indefinite Pronoun

Choose the correct verb: Everyone in the two offices *(needs / need)* a new ID.

**Solution:**

1. The subject is *everyone*.
2. *Everyone* normally takes a singular verb.
3. Correct: "Everyone in the two offices *needs* a new ID."

### #5: Quantifier with an *of*-Phrase

Choose the correct verb: Most of the water in the tank *(has / have)* evaporated.

**Solution:**

1. *Most* refers to the amount of water.
2. *Water* is treated as singular here.
3. Correct: "Most of the water *has* evaporated."

### #6: Compound Subject with *and*

Choose the correct verb: The supervisor and the clerk *(is / are)* checking the forms.

**Solution:**

1. The subject has two people joined by *and*.
2. That compound subject is plural.
3. Correct: "The supervisor and the clerk *are* checking the forms."

### #7: *Neither...nor*

Choose the standard edited form: Neither the manager nor the employees *(was / were)* aware of the change.

**Solution:**

1. The nearer subject is *employees*.
2. *Employees* is plural, so *were* agrees with it.
3. Correct: "Neither the manager nor the employees *were* aware of the change."

### #8: Aspekto at Pananda sa Filipino

Piliin ang pangungusap na nagsasaad ng kilos na nagaganap ngayon: "Kumain si Lea ngayon" o "Kumakain si Lea ngayon."

**Solution:**

1. Ang salitang *ngayon* ay nagsasaad ng kasalukuyang panahon.
2. Ang *kumakain* ay anyong nagsasaad ng kilos na nagaganap.
3. Wasto: "Kumakain si Lea ngayon." Si Lea ang pariralang may panandang *si*.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Find the subject first.** Do not match the verb to the nearest noun unless that noun is really the subject.

**Check the subject's number and person.** Ask whether it is singular or plural, then check whether the verb has a special form for *I, you,* or third person.

**With *and*, expect a plural subject in most cases.** With *or/nor*, check the nearer subject in standard edited English.

**With collective nouns and quantifiers, check the meaning.** The verb may depend on whether the sentence means one unit, separate members, one amount, or several countable things.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Matching an Intervening Noun

> The noun closest to the verb is not automatically the subject.

"The box of chocolates *is* on the table" agrees with *box*, not *chocolates*.

### #2: Treating Collective-Noun Agreement as Universal

> Collective nouns can take different agreement based on meaning and the variety of English.

Do not mark a plural form wrong without checking the context. American English often uses a singular verb when the group acts as one unit.

### #3: Making *everybody* Plural for Verb Agreement

> Indefinite pronouns such as *everybody* and *someone* normally take singular verbs.

This rule is about the verb. Modern English can still use singular *they* or *their* later to refer to the person.

### #4: Forcing *or/nor* Agreement into an Awkward Sentence

> The nearer-subject rule is useful, but clarity still matters.

If mixed singular and plural subjects make the sentence sound awkward, rewrite it so the agreement is clear.

### #5: Importing English Agreement into Filipino

> Filipino does not generally change a verb just because the subject is singular or plural.

Check aspekto, pokus, and pananda as Filipino grammar features. Do not look for an English-style *is/are* match.
