## Core Concept

### Reason and Contrast Connectors, and Avoiding Double Connectors

A **connector** joins clauses and shows how their ideas relate:

- **Reason** (*because, since*): explains why something happened. *"We stayed inside because it was raining."*
- **Contrast** (*although, even though, though*): introduces a fact that goes against the expected result. *"Although she was tired, she finished her homework."*

**Never double-connect.** A subordinating connector (*although, because*) already joins one dependent clause to one main clause. A coordinating connector (*but, so*) joins two independent clauses. Never use both together, as in *"Although it rained, but we walked"* or *"Because the store was closed, so we went home."* Pick one connector and keep one main clause.

:::why
Why does *"Because it rained, so we stayed inside"* fail? *Because* makes *"it rained"* wait for a main clause, while *so* expects a main clause before it. Drop *so* so *"we stayed inside"* serves as the main clause.
:::

:::check
Fix *"Although the team practiced, but it lost"*: keep *although* alone, or keep both *although* and *but*?
:::answer
The answer is **Keep *although* alone**. *"Although the team practiced, it lost."* Dropping *but* removes the double connector.
:::

### Comma Placement by Clause Order

Compare comma placement by order:

1. **Dependent clause first:** Add a comma after it: *"Because it was raining, we stayed inside."*
2. **Essential reason second:** Skip the comma: *"We stayed inside because it was raining."*

:::pair #comma-by-clause-order
Right: *"Because it was raining, we stayed inside."* (comma after *raining*) and *"We stayed inside because it was raining."* (no comma before *because*).
Wrong: Claim *"No: the comma should be removed entirely"* for the opening clause, or *"Yes: a comma is always required regardless of clause order"* for the second.
Comma use depends on clause order. Put a comma after an opening dependent clause; skip it before a following essential *because* clause.
:::

:::check
Where does a comma belong in *"Because it was raining we stayed inside"*?
:::answer
The answer is **After *raining***. *"Because it was raining, we stayed inside."* An opening dependent clause takes a comma before the main clause.
:::

### Completing a Dependent-Clause Fragment

In formal writing, a dependent clause standing alone with a period is a **fragment**, an incomplete sentence: *"Although the teacher explained the lesson twice."*

To fix a dependent-clause fragment, attach a true **independent clause** with a subject, finite verb, and no subordinating word at the front. Adding another dependent clause (*while, because*), a noun phrase (*our journey back home*), or a prepositional phrase (*after losing*) still leaves a fragment.

:::pair #fragment-is-not-sentence
Right: In formal prose, *"Although the teacher explained the lesson twice"* is **a dependent-clause fragment**.
Wrong: Call *"Although the teacher explained the lesson twice"* **a complete independent sentence** or **a run-on with two main clauses**.
*The teacher explained* has a subject and verb, yet *Although* turns the single clause into a fragment waiting for a main clause. One clause cannot be a run-on.
:::

:::pair #two-dependent-clauses
Right: *"Because the store was closed, we went home."*
Wrong: Write *"Because the store was closed, while we walked back home."* or *"Although she reviewed it carefully, because the manager still had questions."*
Both clauses start with subordinating words (*because, while, although*). Joining two dependent clauses leaves the sentence with no main clause.
:::

:::check
Which completes *"If the records are accurate, ___"* as a full sentence: *"the totals will match"* or *"when the totals finally match"*?
:::answer
The answer is **the totals will match**. *The totals* is the subject and *will match* is the finite verb with no subordinating word at the front.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Fixing a Fragment Split by a Period

Revise *"Although the manager reviewed the proposal carefully. She still had questions."* into one complete formal sentence.

**Solution:**

1. *Although* makes the first group a dependent fragment.
2. *"She still had questions"* is an independent clause.
3. Join them with a comma: *"Although she reviewed it carefully, the manager still had questions."*

**Check:** Cover *"Although she reviewed it carefully,"* and read *"the manager still had questions."* The main clause stands alone.

### #2: Checking Comma Order

Decide whether the comma placement is right in (a) *"Because it was raining, we stayed inside."* and (b) *"We stayed inside because it was raining."*

**Solution:**

1. In (a), the *because* clause comes first, so the comma after *raining* is right.
2. In (b), the main clause comes first and the reason clause is essential, so no comma before *because* is right.

**Check:** Move the *because* clause in (b) to the front; a comma appears after *raining*, matching (a).

### #3: Adding a Contrasting Main Clause

Complete *"Even though the team had practiced for weeks, ___"* with one option: (a) *"because it lost the match"*, (b) *"after losing the match"*, or (c) *"it lost."*

**Solution:**

1. Choice (a) starts with *because*, adding a second dependent clause.
2. Choice (b) is a prepositional phrase with no subject-verb clause.

:::try
Finish the last step: check choice (c) *"it lost"* and write the full sentence.
:::answer
**Even though the team had practiced for weeks, it lost.** *It* is the subject and *lost* is the finite verb with no subordinating word, giving the contrasting main clause.

**Check:** Drop *"Even though the team had practiced for weeks,"* and read *"It lost."* It stands alone as a main clause.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Count subordinating words (*because, although, when, while, if*). Every sentence needs at least one clause with no subordinating word, and never pair *although* with *but* or *because* with *so*.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Reversing clause order with *because*: put a comma after an opening *because* clause, and skip it before a closing essential *because* clause. {#comma-by-clause-order}
- Dependent clause ending with a period in formal prose: treat it as a fragment and attach it to a main clause. {#fragment-is-not-sentence}
- Two clauses both starting with subordinating words: drop the second connector so one clause is independent. {#two-dependent-clauses}
:::
