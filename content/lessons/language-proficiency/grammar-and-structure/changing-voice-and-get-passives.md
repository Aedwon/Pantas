## Core Concept

### Switching Between Active and Passive Voice

To change active to passive, move the **object** (what receives the action) into the subject spot. Use the matching form of *be* plus the past participle, and add *by* plus the agent if needed.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: two stacked sentences showing role swap: top "The committee [actor] approved [simple past] the new budget [receiver]." with crossing arrows to bottom "The new budget [subject] was approved [simple past passive] by the committee [agent]." -->

To change passive to active, make the agent the subject and drop passive *be*. A verb needs an object to allow a standard passive form: "The child slept" has no object, so it has no passive partner. Switch "The committee approved the new budget" between active and passive:

- Active: "The committee **approved** the new budget."
- Passive: "The new budget **was approved** by the committee."

:::pair #swap-roles-correctly
Right: Changing "The novel was written by a famous author" to active gives "A famous author wrote the novel."
Wrong: Keep the nouns in place ("The novel wrote a famous author") or reverse them into "The committee was approved by the new budget during the meeting."
Match the verb to the real doer and real receiver. A novel cannot write an author, and a budget cannot approve a committee.
:::

:::check
Change to active voice: "The meal was cooked by the chef."
:::answer
The answer is **"The chef cooked the meal."** *The chef* becomes the subject, and simple past *was cooked* becomes *cooked*.
:::

### Keeping the Same Time and Aspect

Changing voice only swaps the subject role. Keep the same time and keep completed actions simple rather than ongoing:

- Completed past: "The novel **was written** by a famous author." -> "A famous author **wrote** the novel."
- Completed past: "The committee **approved** the new budget." -> "The new budget **was approved** by the committee."

:::pair #preserve-tense-and-aspect
Right: "The committee approved the new budget" becomes "The new budget was approved by the committee."
Wrong: Shift to present ("The new budget is approved by the committee during its meetings") or ongoing ("A famous author was writing the novel").
Keep past time in the past (*was approved*, *wrote*), and keep completed actions simple rather than *-ing*.
:::

:::check
Change to passive voice in past time: "The clerk checked the forms."
:::answer
The answer is **"The forms were checked by the clerk."** Plural *the forms* takes *were* plus *checked*.
:::

### Get-Passives Versus Be-Passives

A **get-passive** uses *get* plus a past participle (*got broken*, *got delivered*): "The window **got broken** during the storm." Change *get* to match the time (*gets*, *got*, *will get*).

A *get*-passive is common in speech and informal writing. In formal reports and notices, replace *get* with a *be*-passive ("The window **was broken** during the storm").

Like *be*, *get* followed by an adjective is not passive: "The package **got heavy**" describes a state, while "The package **got delivered**" is passive.

:::why
Why do exams test *got broken* against *got heavy*? The word *got* can mean "became" (before an adjective), "obtained" (before a noun), or act as a passive helper (before a past participle). Reading *got broken* as one verb group keeps the role clear.
:::

:::pair #get-plus-participle
Right: In "The window got broken during the storm," *got broken* is a get-passive.
Wrong: Call it a linking construction with *got* meaning ownership of an object, or an active clause with the window as the actor causing the break.
Read *got broken* together. *The window* received the breaking, and *broken* is a past participle, not a noun owned or a state adjective.
:::

:::check
Which uses a get-passive: "The parcel got wet" or "The parcel got delivered"?
:::answer
The answer is **"The parcel got delivered."** *Delivered* is a past participle naming an action done to the parcel, while *wet* is an adjective.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Passive to Active Without Losing Tense

Rewrite in active voice while keeping the completed past action: "The novel was written by a famous author."

**Solution:**

1. Move *a famous author* to the subject spot and *the novel* after the verb.
2. Match simple past *was written* to simple past *wrote*.

Correct: "A famous author wrote the novel."

**Check:** Turn it back to passive: *the novel* becomes the subject and *wrote* becomes *was written* by *a famous author*.

### #2: Active to Passive Without Losing Tense

Rewrite in passive voice while keeping the past event: "The committee approved the new budget."

**Solution:**

1. Move *the new budget* to the subject spot.
2. Form the simple past passive *was approved* to agree with singular *the new budget*.
3. Add *by the committee* for the agent.

Correct: "The new budget was approved by the committee."

**Check:** In both sentences, *the committee* approves, *the new budget* receives approval, and the verb stays simple past.

### #3: Spotting a Get-Passive and Rewriting It Formally

In "The window got broken during the storm," classify the action clause, then rewrite it for a formal report.

**Solution:**

1. Check the subject *the window*: it receives the breaking, so the clause is passive.
2. Check the verb group: *got* plus the past participle *broken* forms a **get-passive**.

:::try
Finish the last step: how do you rewrite "The window got broken during the storm" as a formal *be*-passive?
:::answer
The answer is **"The window was broken during the storm."** Replace past *got* with singular past *was*, keeping *broken* and past time unchanged.

**Check:** Check all three points: *the window* receives the action, *was broken* keeps past time, and *was* replaces informal *got*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

When changing voice, first swap who acts and who receives, then match the exact time and aspect. With *get*, read the whole verb group: *get* + past participle is a *get*-passive, while *get* + adjective is a state.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Swapping voice between subject and object: put the real actor in the active subject spot and the real receiver in the passive subject spot. {#swap-roles-correctly}
- Rewriting a past verb in another voice: keep simple past as simple past (*wrote*, *was approved*), without shifting to present or *-ing*. {#preserve-tense-and-aspect}
- A clause with *got* plus a past participle like *got broken*: read both words as a get-passive, and use *was* or *were* in formal writing. {#get-plus-participle}
:::
