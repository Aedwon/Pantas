## Core Concept

### Switching Between Active and Passive Voice

To change active to passive, move the **object** (what receives the action) into the subject spot. Only an active clause with an object or prepositional object can do this ("The child slept" has none). Use the matching form of *be* plus the past participle, and add *by* plus the agent if needed.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: two stacked sentences showing role swap: top "The committee [actor] approved [simple past] the new budget [receiver]." with crossing arrows to bottom "The new budget [subject] was approved [simple past passive] by the committee [agent]." -->

Change "The committee approved the new budget" to passive:

- Active: "The committee **approved** the new budget."
- Passive: "The new budget **was approved** by the committee."

To switch back, make the agent the subject and drop passive *be*.

:::pair #swap-roles-correctly
Right: Changing "The novel was written by a famous author" to active gives "A famous author wrote the novel."
Wrong: Keep the nouns in place ("The novel wrote a famous author") or reverse them into "The committee was approved by the new budget during the meeting."
Match the verb to the real doer and real receiver. A novel cannot write an author, and a budget cannot approve a committee.
:::

:::check
Change to active voice: "The meal was cooked by the chef."
:::answer
The answer is **"The chef cooked the meal."** *The chef* becomes the subject, and simple past *was cooked* becomes *cooked*.
:::

### Keeping the Same Time and Aspect

Change the voice of each sentence and keep its tense and aspect:

- Simple past: "The novel **was written** by a famous author." -> "A famous author **wrote** the novel."
- Simple past: "The committee **approved** the new budget." -> "The new budget **was approved** by the committee."

:::pair #preserve-tense-and-aspect
Right: "The committee approved the new budget" becomes "The new budget was approved by the committee."
Wrong: Shift to present ("The new budget is approved by the committee during its meetings") or ongoing ("A famous author was writing the novel").
Simple past stays simple past (*was approved*, *wrote*), not present or *-ing*.
:::

:::check
Change to passive voice in past time: "The clerk checked the forms."
:::answer
The answer is **"The forms were checked by the clerk."** Plural *the forms* takes *were* plus *checked*.
:::

### Get-Passives Versus Be-Passives

A **get-passive** uses *get* plus a past participle (*got broken*, *got delivered*): "The window **got broken** during the storm."

A *get*-passive is common in speech. In formal reports, replace *get* with a *be*-passive ("The window **was broken** during the storm").

*Get* plus an adjective is not passive: "The package **got heavy**" describes a state. *Get* plus a participle is passive only when the subject receives an action: "The package **got delivered**" is passive, but "She **got tired**" is not, because *tired* works as an adjective.

:::why
Why do exams test *got broken* against *got heavy*? The word *got* can mean "became" (before an adjective), "obtained" (before a noun), or act as a passive helper (before a past participle naming an action done to the subject). Reading the verb group and the event together keeps the role clear.
:::

:::pair #get-plus-participle
Right: In "The window got broken during the storm," *got broken* is a get-passive.
Wrong: Call it a linking construction with *got* meaning ownership of an object, or an active clause with the window as the actor causing the break.
*The window* received the breaking, and *broken* is a past participle, not a noun owned or a state adjective.
:::

:::check
Which uses a get-passive: "The bike got old" or "The bike got stolen"?
:::answer
The answer is **"The bike got stolen."** *Stolen* is a past participle naming an action done to the bike, while *old* is an adjective.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Passive to Active Without Losing Tense

Rewrite in active voice while keeping the completed past action: "The novel was written by a famous author."

**Solution:**

1. Move *a famous author* to the subject spot and *the novel* after the verb.
2. Match simple past *was written* to simple past *wrote*.

Correct: "A famous author wrote the novel."

**Check:** Turn it back: *the novel* becomes the subject and *wrote* becomes *was written*.

### #2: Active to Passive Without Losing Tense

Rewrite in passive voice while keeping the past event: "The committee approved the new budget."

**Solution:**

1. Move *the new budget* to the subject spot.
2. Form the simple past passive *was approved* to agree with singular *the new budget*.
3. Add *by the committee* for the agent.

Correct: "The new budget was approved by the committee."

**Check:** Both sentences keep *the committee* as approver and the verb in simple past.

### #3: Spotting a Get-Passive and Rewriting It Formally

In "The wooden fence got damaged during the typhoon," classify the action clause, then rewrite it for a formal report.

**Solution:**

1. Check what happened to *the wooden fence*: something damaged it, so it receives the action.
2. Check the verb group: *got* plus the past participle *damaged* names that action, so the clause is a **get-passive**.

:::try
Finish the last step: how do you rewrite "The wooden fence got damaged during the typhoon" as a formal *be*-passive?
:::answer
The answer is **"The wooden fence was damaged during the typhoon."** Replace past *got* with singular past *was*, keeping *damaged* and past time unchanged.

**Check:** Check all three points: *the wooden fence* receives the action, *was damaged* keeps simple past time, and *was* replaces informal *got*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

When changing voice, first swap who acts and who receives, then match the time and aspect. With *get*, ask whether the subject receives an action: *get* + a participle naming that action is a *get*-passive, while *get* + adjective is a state.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Swapping voice between subject and object: put the real actor in the active subject spot and the real receiver in the passive subject spot. {#swap-roles-correctly}
- Rewriting a past verb in another voice: keep simple past as simple past (*wrote*, *was approved*), not present or *-ing*. {#preserve-tense-and-aspect}
- *Got* plus a participle like *got broken* that names what happened to the subject: read it as a get-passive, and use *was* or *were* in formal writing. {#get-plus-participle}
:::
