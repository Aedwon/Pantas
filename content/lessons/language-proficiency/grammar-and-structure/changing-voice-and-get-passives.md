## Core Concept

### Switching Between Active and Passive Voice

To change active to passive for an action like approving a budget, move the **object** (what receives the action) into the subject spot. Only an active clause with an object and a verb that allows a passive can do this ("The child slept" has no object, and *have lunch* has no passive). Use the matching form of *be* plus the past participle.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: two stacked sentences showing role swap: top "The committee [actor] approved [simple past] the new budget [receiver]." with crossing arrows to bottom "The new budget [subject] was approved [simple past passive] by the committee [agent]." -->

Change "The committee approved the new budget" to passive:

- Active: "The committee **approved** the new budget."
- Passive: "The new budget **was approved** by the committee."

To switch back, if the passive names the doer, make the doer the subject and rebuild the active verb in the same tense and aspect.

:::pair #swap-roles-correctly
Right: Changing "The novel was written by a famous author" to active gives "A famous author wrote the novel."
Wrong: Keep the nouns in place ("The novel wrote a famous author") or reverse them into "The committee was approved by the new budget during the meeting."
Match the verb to the real doer and real receiver.
:::

:::check
Change to active voice: "The meal was cooked by the chef."
:::answer
The answer is **"The chef cooked the meal."** *The chef* becomes the subject, and simple past *was cooked* becomes *cooked*.
:::

### Keeping the Same Time and Aspect

Change the voice of each sentence and keep its tense and aspect:

- Ongoing present: "The crew **is repairing** the road." -> "The road **is being repaired** by the crew."

:::pair #preserve-tense-and-aspect
Right: "The novel was written by a famous author" becomes "A famous author wrote the novel."
Wrong: Shift to ongoing ("A famous author was writing the novel").
Simple past stays simple past (*was written*, *wrote*).
:::

:::check
Change to passive voice in past time, keeping *the clerk*: "The clerk checked the forms."
:::answer
The answer is **"The forms were checked by the clerk."** Plural *the forms* takes *were* plus *checked*, and *by the clerk* keeps the doer.
:::

### Get-Passives Versus Be-Passives

A **get-passive** uses *get* plus a past participle (*got broken*, *got delivered*): "The window **got broken** during the storm."

In formal reports, replace *get* with a *be*-passive ("The window **was broken** during the storm").

*Get* plus an adjective is not passive: "The package **got heavy**" means it became heavy. *Get* plus a participle is passive only when the subject receives an action: "The package **got delivered**" is passive, but "She **got tired**" is not, because *tired* works as an adjective.

:::why
Why do exams test *got broken* against *got heavy*? The word *got* can mean "became" (before an adjective), "obtained" (before a noun), or act as a passive helper (before a past participle naming an action done to the subject). Reading the verb group and the event together keeps the role clear.
:::

:::pair #get-plus-participle
Right: In "The window got broken during the storm," *got broken* is a get-passive.
Wrong: Call it a linking construction with *got* meaning ownership of an object, or an active clause with the window as the actor causing the break.
*The window* received the breaking, and *broken* is a past participle, not a noun owned or an adjective.
:::

:::check
In "The bike got old" and "The bike got stolen," which verb group names something done to the bike?
:::answer
The answer is **got stolen**. In "The bike got stolen," *stolen* is a past participle naming an action done to the bike. In "The bike got old," *old* is an adjective.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Passive to Active Without Losing Tense

Rewrite in active voice while keeping the completed past action: "The report was typed by an intern."

**Solution:**

1. Move *an intern* to the subject spot and *the report* after the verb.
2. Match simple past *was typed* to simple past *typed*.

Correct: "An intern typed the report."

**Check:** Before and after, an intern is the one who typed the report, and the action stays in the past.

### #2: Active to Passive Without Losing Tense

Rewrite in passive voice while keeping the past event: "The mayor signed the new law."

**Solution:**

1. Move *the new law* to the subject spot.
2. Form the simple past passive *was signed* to agree with singular *the new law*.
3. Add *by the mayor* for the agent.

Correct: "The new law was signed by the mayor."

**Check:** Both sentences keep *the mayor* as signer and the verb in simple past.

### #3: A Line from a Storm Report

Look at "The wooden fence got damaged during the typhoon."

**Solution:**

1. Check what happened to *the wooden fence*: something damaged it, so it receives the action.
2. Check the verb group: *got* plus the past participle *damaged* names that action, so the clause is a **get-passive**.

:::try
Finish the last step: replace only *got* to make the sentence formal.
:::answer
The answer is **"The wooden fence was damaged during the typhoon."** Only *got* changes to *was*; *damaged* stays.

**Check:** Compare with the original: the fence was damaged during the typhoon both times, so the time did not change.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

To change voice, swap the doer and receiver (to active only when the doer is named, to passive only when the verb allows it), then keep the time and aspect. With *get*, ask whether the subject receives an action: *get* + a participle naming that action is a *get*-passive, while *get* + adjective shows a change (*became*).

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Swapping voice between subject and object: put the real actor in the active subject spot and the real receiver in the passive subject spot. {#swap-roles-correctly}
- Rewriting a past verb in another voice: keep simple past as simple past (*wrote*, *was approved*), not present or *-ing*. {#preserve-tense-and-aspect}
- *Got* plus a participle like *got broken* that names what happened to the subject: read it as a get-passive, and use *was* or *were* in formal writing. {#get-plus-participle}
:::
