## Core Concept

For **obligation**, *must* and *have to* show a strong requirement. "You must submit the form" and "You have to submit the form" both say it is required. Use *has to* with *he, she, it,* or a singular noun, and *had to* for a past requirement.

*Should* usually gives advice: "You should check your answers." *Must not* means something is forbidden. *Do not have to* means it is not required, but it may still be allowed.

For **possibility**, *may, might,* and *could* can all mean that something is possible: "It might rain later." They can overlap, so meaning alone may not select just one of them.

*Must* can also express a strong conclusion: "The lights are on, so someone must be home." Here, the speaker uses what they see to decide what is very likely true. This use of *must* does not give an order.

## Examples

### #1: Requirement and Advice

Choose the forms that fit the stated meanings: Attendance is required, so all employees *(have to / should)* attend. Checking notes is advice, so they *(must / should)* review their notes beforehand.

**Solution:**

1. A requirement calls for *have to*.
2. Advice calls for *should*.

### #2: Not Required or Forbidden

Visitors may bring a printed copy, but it is optional. Choose the matching sentence: "Visitors do not have to bring a copy" or "Visitors must not bring a copy."

**Solution:**

1. Optional means allowed but not required.
2. Choose "Visitors do not have to bring a copy." *Must not* would forbid it.

### #3: Possibility

Choose the modal that presents the change as possible, without saying it is required: The schedule *(might / must)* change if the office closes.

**Solution:**

1. The requested meaning is possibility.
2. Choose *might*: "The schedule might change if the office closes."

### #4: A Conclusion from Evidence

What does the word in bold express: ability, permission, obligation, or a strong conclusion?

"Lina's signed receipt is on the desk, so she **must** be back from the delivery."

**Solution:**

1. The signed receipt gives the speaker a reason to believe Lina is back.
2. Choose *a strong conclusion*. The speaker is deciding what is very likely true. The sentence gives no order to Lina.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Confusing Advice, a Rule, and a Ban

> *Should*, *must*, and *must not* express different meanings.

*Should* usually gives advice. *Must* can state a requirement, while *must not* forbids an action. *Do not have to* leaves the action optional.
