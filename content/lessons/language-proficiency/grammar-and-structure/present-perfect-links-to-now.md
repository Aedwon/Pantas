## Core Concept

### Forming Present Perfect

A **past participle** is the verb form used after *has, have,* or *had* (and in passives). Regular verbs add *-d* or *-ed* (*finished*, *submitted*), while irregular verbs use forms like *written*, *eaten*, and *broken*.

**Present perfect** uses *has* or *have* plus a past participle. Use *has* with *he, she, it*, or a singular noun, and *have* with *I, you, we, they*, or a plural noun:

- "She **has written** three letters this week."
- "They **have finished** the report."

:::pair #full-verb-group
Right: In "She has written three letters this week," *has written* is present perfect.
Wrong: Call *has written* simple past (ignoring *has*) or past perfect (confusing *has* with *had*).
Read the full verb group, not just *written*. Present *has* or *have* plus a past participle is present perfect, while *had* forms past perfect.
:::

:::check
Choose the right form: The team *(has finished / has finish)* the audit today.
:::answer
The answer is **Has finished**. After *has*, always use the past participle (*finished*), never the base form (*finish*).
:::

### Linking the Past to Now

Present perfect connects a past action or state to now. Use it for:

1. A present result: "She **has finished** the report, so it is ready now."
2. Experience up to now: "Mara **has visited** Cebu twice."
3. A situation continuing to now: "We arrived last month and **have lived** here ever since."
4. Repeated events in an open period: "The printer **has stopped** twice this week."

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal timeline showing "last month" with a solid teal arrow extending to "now" labeled "have lived here ever since (present perfect)", below a marked point at "yesterday afternoon" labeled "submitted (simple past)" -->

:::why
Why does "We arrived last month and have lived here ever since" change verb forms? Moving happened and ended last month (*arrived*). Living here started then and is still true now (*have lived*).
:::

:::pair #still-true-now
Right: We arrived last month and have lived here ever since.
Wrong: We had lived here until last month, and then we left.
*Had lived* describes a stay that already ended. When a situation continues into now, use *have* or *has* plus the past participle.
:::

:::check
Choose the form for a stay continuing to now: "She *(has lived / had lived)* here since June."
:::answer
The answer is **Has lived**. Her stay continues into the present, so present perfect links June to now.
:::

### Finished Past Time Uses Simple Past

When a sentence names a finished past time (*yesterday*, *last week*, *in 2022*), use **simple past**, not present perfect:

- "The committee **submitted** its report yesterday afternoon." (*Yesterday afternoon* is finished.)
- "The committee **has submitted** its report." (Focuses on the present result with no finished past time.)

:::pair #finished-past-time
Right: The committee submitted its report yesterday afternoon.
Wrong: The committee has submitted (or *has submit*) its report yesterday afternoon.
*Yesterday afternoon* names a finished past period, so use simple past (*submitted*). Also, *has* never takes a base verb (*submit*).
:::

:::check
Choose the right verb: The printer *(stopped / has stopped)* working yesterday.
:::answer
The answer is **Stopped**. *Yesterday* names a finished past time, so choose simple past.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Identifying Present Perfect

Identify the tense in "She has written three letters this week."

**Solution:**

1. Find the full verb group: *has written*.
2. Check the helper: present *has* pairs with the past participle *written*.
3. Match the form (*has* + past participle) to its tense: **present perfect**.

**Check:** Verify *has written* directly: present *has* plus past participle *written* links past writing up to now with *this week* and no finished past time word, confirming **present perfect** (whereas simple past uses *wrote* alone and past perfect uses *had written*).

### #2: Choosing Simple Past for a Finished Time

Choose the right verb form: "The committee ___ its report yesterday afternoon."

**Solution:**

1. Spot the time clue: *yesterday afternoon*.
2. Decide if that period is finished: yesterday afternoon is over.
3. Rule out present perfect (*has submitted*), *has submit*, and future (*will submit*), leaving simple past **submitted**.

**Check:** Pair *yesterday afternoon* with a second verb: "I read it yesterday afternoon" also uses simple past.

### #3: Past Arrival with a Continuing Stay

The speakers arrived last month and still live here. Choose the clearer sentence: "We arrive last month and have lived here ever since," "We had lived here until last month, and then we left," or "We arrived last month and have lived here ever since."

**Solution:**

1. Match the arrival (*last month*) to simple past *arrived*, ruling out present *arrive*.
2. Check whether the stay ended or still continues: they *still live here*, ruling out *had lived ... and then we left*.

:::try
Finish the last step: which sentence fits both timelines, and why do its two verbs differ?
:::answer
The answer is **"We arrived last month and have lived here ever since."** *Arrived* places the move in the finished past, and *have lived* links the ongoing stay to now.

**Check:** Test each clause: *arrived last month* matches a completed move, and *have lived here ever since* confirms they still live here now.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Read the time clue first. A finished past time (*yesterday*, *last month*) takes simple past, while a link to now (*since June*, *ever since*, *this week*, *so far*) takes *has* or *have* plus a past participle.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A verb with *has* or *have* plus a past participle: read the whole group as present perfect, not simple past or past perfect. {#full-verb-group}
- A situation continuing into now: use *has* or *have* + past participle, not *had*. {#still-true-now}
- A finished past time like *yesterday afternoon*: choose simple past (*submitted*), never present perfect or *has* + base verb. {#finished-past-time}
:::
