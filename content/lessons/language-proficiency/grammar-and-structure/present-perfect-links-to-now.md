## Core Concept

### Forming Present Perfect

A **past participle** is the verb form used after *has, have,* or *had*. Regular verbs add *-d* or *-ed* (*finished*, *saved*), sometimes doubling a letter first (*submitted*), while irregular verbs use forms like *written*, *eaten*, and *broken*.

**Present perfect** uses *has* or *have* plus a past participle. Use *has* with *he, she, it*, or a singular noun, and *have* with *I, you, we, they*, or a plural noun:

- "She **has written** three letters this week."
- "They **have finished** the report."

:::pair #full-verb-group
Right: In "She has written three letters this week," *has written* is present perfect.
Wrong: Call *has written* simple past (ignoring *has*) or past perfect (confusing *has* with *had*).
Read the full verb group, not just *written*. Present *has* or *have* plus a past participle is present perfect, while *had* forms past perfect.
:::

:::check
Choose the right form: The nurses *(have cleaned / have clean)* the ward today.
:::answer
The answer is **Have cleaned**. After helper *have*, use the past participle (*cleaned*), never the base form (*clean*).
:::

### Linking the Past to Now

Present perfect connects a past action or state to now. Common uses:

1. A present result: "She **has finished** the report, so it is ready now."
2. Experience up to now: "Mara **has visited** Cebu twice."
3. A situation continuing to now: "We arrived last month and **have lived** here ever since."
4. Repeated events in an open period: "The printer **has stopped** twice this week."

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal timeline showing "last month" with a solid teal arrow extending to "now" labeled "have lived here ever since (present perfect)", below a marked point at "yesterday afternoon" labeled "submitted (simple past)" -->

:::why
Why does "We arrived last month and have lived here ever since" change verb forms? The arrival happened and ended last month (*arrived*). Living here started then and is still true now (*have lived*).
:::

:::pair #still-true-now
Right: We arrived last month and have lived here ever since.
Wrong: We had lived here until last month, and then we left.
Here the words *until last month* and *then we left* show that the stay ended. When a situation continues into now, use *have* or *has* plus the past participle.
:::

:::check
Our neighbor still works at the bank. Choose the form: "Our neighbor *(has worked / had worked)* there since 2020."
:::answer
The answer is **Has worked**. The job continues into the present, so present perfect links 2020 to now.
:::

### Finished Past Time

For one dated event in a finished past time (*yesterday, last week, in 2022*), choose **simple past** over present perfect. Compare:

- "The committee **submitted** its report yesterday afternoon."
- "The committee **has submitted** its report." (Present result, no finished time.)

:::pair #finished-past-time
Right: The committee submitted its report yesterday afternoon.
Wrong: The committee has submitted (or *has submit*) its report yesterday afternoon.
*Yesterday afternoon* names a finished past period. Here, between *submitted* and *has submitted*, choose *submitted*. *Has* also needs a past participle, not *submit*.
:::

:::check
Choose the right verb: The bus *(arrived / has arrived)* late last night.
:::answer
The answer is **Arrived**. *Last night* names a finished past time, so choose simple past.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Verb Group

Identify the tense in "The team has repaired three roofs this month."

**Solution:**

1. Find the full verb group: *has repaired*.
2. *Has* + past participle *repaired* makes **present perfect**: "The team **has repaired** three roofs this month."

**Check:** *This month* is still open, so a link to now fits.

### #2: A Verb and a Time Clue

Choose the right verb form for one event: "The mayor ___ the new bridge last Friday." (opened / has opened)

**Solution:**

1. Spot the time clue: *last Friday*, a finished time.
2. Rule out *has opened*, leaving simple past **opened**: "The mayor opened the new bridge last Friday."

**Check:** "I saw it last Friday" also uses simple past.

### #3: Choosing Between Three Sentences

Dan's club membership began in May and continues today. Choose the correct sentence: "Dan joins the club in May and has been a member ever since," "Dan had been a member until May, and then he left," or "Dan joined the club in May and has been a member ever since."

**Solution:**

1. The membership *continues today*, ruling out *had been ... and then he left*.

:::try
Finish the last step: which sentence fits both timelines, and why do its two verbs differ?
:::answer
The answer is **"Dan joined the club in May and has been a member ever since."** *Joined* places the joining in the finished past, and *has been* links the ongoing membership to now.

**Check:** Ask whether Dan is on the member list today. Yes, so *has been a member* fits.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Read the time clue first. For one dated event in a finished past time (*yesterday*, *last month*), choose simple past over present perfect. A link to now (*since June*, *ever since*) takes *has* or *have* plus a past participle once you check that the reference point is now. An open period like *this week* often takes present perfect, but simple past can fit a completed event (*She wrote a letter this week*).

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A verb with *has* or *have* plus a past participle: read the whole group as present perfect, not simple past or past perfect. {#full-verb-group}
- A situation continuing into now: use *has* or *have* + past participle, not *had*. {#still-true-now}
- One dated event in a finished past time like *yesterday afternoon*: choose simple past (*submitted*) over present perfect; *has* needs a past participle. {#finished-past-time}
:::
