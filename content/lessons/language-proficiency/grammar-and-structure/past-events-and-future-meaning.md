## Core Concept

### Simple Past for Completed Events

**Simple past** places an event in past time: "She **wrote** a memo yesterday." Regular verbs add *-d* or *-ed* (*walked*, *finished*), while most irregular verbs change form (*wrote*, *ate*, *bought*).

The past forms of *be* are *was* (with *I, he, she, it*, or a singular noun) and *were* (with *you, we, they*, or a plural noun). Check the time clue in the sentence before choosing the verb:

- "Yesterday, she **walked** to the store and **bought** some bread." (*Yesterday* keeps both actions in the past.)

:::pair #keep-stated-past-time
Right: Yesterday, she walked to the store and bought some bread.
Wrong: Change *bought* to *buys* while keeping *yesterday*, or change both action verbs to future forms with *will*.
Matching two verbs in the wrong time breaks the clue *yesterday*. Match every verb to the stated timeline first.
:::

:::check
Choose the correct verb: Last Friday, the clerk *(files / filed)* the form.
:::answer
The answer is **Filed**. *Last Friday* sets a past timeline, so the verb stays in simple past.
:::

### Future Meaning with Will

**Simple future** is the common school label for *will* plus the base form of a verb when it points to the future: "She **will write** a letter tomorrow." Use it for things such as a prediction, a promise, a future fact, or a decision made as you speak.

Never add *-s*, *-ed*, or *-ing* to the verb after *will*. Always use the plain base form (*will close*, *will help*).

:::pair #will-is-simple-future
Right: In "She will write a letter tomorrow," *will write* is simple future.
Wrong: Call *will write* present perfect just because it uses a helping word before *write*.
*Will* plus the base form shows future meaning, not present perfect (which uses *has* or *have* plus a past participle).
:::

:::check
Choose the correct form: I think the office *(will close / will closes)* early tomorrow.
:::answer
The answer is **Will close**. *Tomorrow* points to the future, and *will* always takes the plain base form *close*.
:::

### When a Tense Shift Is Correct

Usually keep one tense for same-time actions; shift tenses when time clues change:

- Same time: "Yesterday, she **walked** to the store and **bought** bread." (Both are yesterday.)
- Different times: "I **studied** yesterday, and I **will practice** tomorrow." (Past moves to future.)

:::why
Why is a tense shift allowed in "I studied yesterday, and I will practice tomorrow"? Verb tense follows real time, not a flat rule about matching words. When the sentence moves from yesterday to tomorrow, the verb must move too.
:::

:::pair #justified-tense-shift
Right: "I studied yesterday, and I will practice tomorrow" is justified because the two time clues set different timelines.
Wrong: Say no, because every coordinated sentence must use one tense throughout.
Joining two clauses with *and* does not force them into the same time. Keep one tense when both actions share the same timeline.
:::

:::check
Is the tense shift justified in "The report was late yesterday, but it is ready now"?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. *Yesterday* calls for past *was*, and *now* calls for present *is*.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Fixing a Broken Past Timeline

Both events happened yesterday. Correct the sentence: "Yesterday, she walks to the store and bought some bread."

**Solution:**

1. Find the time clue: *Yesterday* places the whole sentence in past time.
2. Check the first verb: *walks* is simple present, which clashes with *yesterday*.
3. Change *walks* to simple past *walked*, and keep *bought*.

Correct: "Yesterday, she walked to the store and bought some bread."

**Check:** Read both verbs with the time clue: "Yesterday, she walked" and "Yesterday, she bought." Both match past time.

### #2: Naming a Verb Form

Identify the verb tense in "We will visit the museum on Friday."

**Solution:**

1. Find the full verb group before *the museum*: *will visit*.
2. Check the helper *will*: it marks a future action.
3. Match the form *will* + base verb to its label: **simple future**.

**Check:** Swap *on Friday* for *tomorrow*: "We will visit the museum tomorrow" still points forward.

### #3: Judging a Tense Shift Across Clauses

Decide whether the tense shift in "We cleaned the room last Saturday, and we paint the walls last Saturday" is justified.

**Solution:**

1. Check the first clause: *cleaned* matches the time clue *last Saturday*.

:::try
Finish the job: check the second clause, then say whether the shift is justified and why.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the shift is not justified.** Both clauses share the clue *last Saturday*, which is past, so *paint* must become *painted*: **"We cleaned the room last Saturday, and we painted the walls last Saturday."** (*Will paint* would fit a future clue such as *next week*.)

**Check:** Read each verb with its clue: *cleaned last Saturday* and *painted last Saturday* are both past.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Spot every time word first (*yesterday*, *last week*, *now*, *tomorrow*). Usually keep verbs in the same tense when they share one time, and allow a shift whenever a new time word moves the event to a different time.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A past clue like *yesterday*: put every verb for that time in a past form that fits its action. {#keep-stated-past-time}
- A verb with *will* plus the base form for a future time: label it simple future, and never add *-s* or *-ed*. {#will-is-simple-future}
- Two clauses joined by *and* with different time clues: allow the tense to shift with the timeline. {#justified-tense-shift}
:::
