## Core Concept

### Sentence Openings and Proper Names

Capitalize the **first word** of every sentence and every **proper noun** (such as a specific person, city, country, organization, month, or day).

In *"We visited Manila last summer,"* *We* begins the sentence and *Manila* is a specific city, so both take capitals. Other words (*visited*, *summer*) stay lowercase.

:::check
Choose the correctly capitalized sentence: *"They Flew to cebu in March."* or *"They flew to Cebu in March."*
:::answer
The answer is **"They flew to Cebu in March."** *They* begins the sentence, *Cebu* is a city name, *March* is a month, and *flew* stays lowercase.
:::

### Titles Before Names vs. Generic Roles

Under the standard convention, capitalize a formal title such as *President* **directly before a person's name**: *"President Reyes"* or *"President Cruz."*

In this style, a **generic job role** without a name after it stays lowercase: *"the president"* or *"the doctor."*

:::pair #title-before-name-vs-role
Right: "We met President Reyes yesterday." and (as a generic role) "We met the president yesterday."
Wrong: "We met president Reyes yesterday at the company office." or "We met the President yesterday during the afternoon visit."
Before *Reyes*, *President* sits right in front of a name and takes a capital *P*. After *the* with no name, *president* is a generic role and stays lowercase here.
:::

:::check
Fix the capitals: *"The form needed principal Tan's signature, and the Principal signed it."*
:::answer
The answer is **"The form needed Principal Tan's signature, and the principal signed it."** Capitalize *Principal* right before *Tan*, and lowercase *principal* after *the*.
:::

### Quoted Sentences vs. Quoted Words

In stand-alone direct speech, capitalize the first word of a **quoted complete sentence**: *She said, "Please close the door."*
Keep an **ordinary word mentioned as a word** inside a sentence lowercase (unless it is a proper name, a title, or *I*): *The word "meeting" appears twice.*
:::pair #quoted-word-capitalized
Right: "The word \"meeting\" appears twice."
Wrong: "The word \"Meeting\" appears twice in the short announcement."
Quotation marks around one ordinary word do not start a new sentence. Keep the embedded quoted word lowercase.
:::

:::pair #quoted-sentence-lowercase
Right: "She said, \"Please close the door.\""
Wrong: "She said, \"please close the door.\"" or "She said, \"Please Close The Door.\""
*"Please close the door"* is a full spoken sentence, so capitalize *Please*, but not every word.
:::

:::check
Choose the correct sentence: *The teacher wrote the word "garden" on the board.* or *The teacher wrote the word "Garden" on the board.*
:::answer
The answer is **The teacher wrote the word "garden" on the board.** The quote is one ordinary word named inside the sentence, not a complete quoted sentence or a name, so it stays lowercase.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Capitals in a Title and Surname

Choose the correctly capitalized sentence:

(A) "I called mayor Lopez this morning."

(B) "I called Mayor lopez this morning."

(C) "I called Mayor Lopez this morning."

**Solution:**

1. (A) leaves *mayor* lowercase before *Lopez*.
2. (B) leaves the surname *lopez* lowercase.
3. (C) capitalizes sentence-start *I*, before-name title *Mayor*, and surname *Lopez*.

The correct answer is **(C)**: "I called Mayor Lopez this morning."

**Check:** Swap the name for a generic role: "I called the mayor this morning" has a lowercase *m*. With *Lopez* back, *Mayor* and *Lopez* both take capitals.

### #2: Quoted Sentence vs. Quoted Word

Fix the quotes: *He said, "please wait here." The word "Weather" appears twice.*

**Solution:**

1. *"Please wait here"* is a full sentence, so capitalize *Please*.
2. *"Weather"* is one quoted word inside a sentence, so lowercase it to *weather*.

Corrected text: **He said, "Please wait here." The word "weather" appears twice.**

**Check:** Read *He said* alone, then *Please wait here* alone. Then read *The word weather appears twice*: *weather* sits mid-sentence.

### #3: Fixing Multiple Errors Together

Fix every error in this sentence:

*the mayor met senator Garcia on friday and said, "the clinic opens soon."*

**Solution:**

1. Capitalize sentence start *The*.
2. Capitalize the day *Friday*.
3. Capitalize *The* to open the quoted sentence.

:::try
Finish the last step: fix the title before *Garcia* and write the full corrected sentence.
:::answer
The answer is **The mayor met Senator Garcia on Friday and said, "The clinic opens soon."** *Senator* sits directly before *Garcia*, so it takes a capital *S*.

**Check:** Take away the name: *"met the senator"* has a lowercase *s*. The capital depends on *Garcia* coming right after.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Check four spots: sentence start, specific names, titles directly before a name, and quotes (stand-alone direct speech gets an opening capital; an ordinary word mentioned as a word stays lowercase).

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Formal title before a name vs. after *the*: capitalize *President Reyes*, lowercase *the president*. {#title-before-name-vs-role}
- Ordinary word mentioned as a word inside a sentence: keep it lowercase, like *"meeting"*. {#quoted-word-capitalized}
- Quoted full sentence as direct speech: capitalize the opening word (*"Please close the door."*), not every word; usual capitals for names and *I* still apply. {#quoted-sentence-lowercase}
:::
