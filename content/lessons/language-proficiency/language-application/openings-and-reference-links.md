## Core Concept

### Choosing the Opening Sentence

**Paragraph organization** means putting sentences in an order that makes the ideas easy to follow.
An **opening sentence** often names the topic or situation. It should make sense before the reader sees the other sentences. A sentence with *this*, *that* or *these* points back to an earlier sentence, so it rarely opens a scrambled set.

Which sentence opens?

(A) "Their boots were covered in mud by the time they reached the camp."

(B) "A group of hikers set out before sunrise to climb the ridge."

1. (A) says "Their boots." Whose boots? No earlier sentence says.
2. (B) names the hikers and what they do. It makes sense alone.
3. (B) opens, and (A) follows.

A story or a set of steps can still open with an event or action, not a broad statement. A pronoun can open only when something outside the set, such as a title, already names it.

:::pair #reference-before-source
Right: "The shop's water bill doubled. After that increase, the owner checked the pipes."
Wrong: "After that increase, the owner checked the pipes. The shop's water bill doubled."
*That increase* points back to the bill.
:::

:::check
Which sentence opens? (A) "Those boxes were stacked by the window." (B) "The new teacher unpacked six boxes of books."
:::answer
The answer is (B). It names the boxes. *Those boxes* in (A) needs an earlier sentence that names them.
:::

### Following Reference Words

**Reference words**, such as *it, this, these* and *they*, point to something already named. A noun with *the*, *that* or *those* can point back too, as in *the study* or *those replies*. If a sentence says *this problem*, ask what problem. The sentence that names it usually comes earlier.

Put in order:

(A) "This water then flows into the village tank."

(B) "Rain collects on the school roof and runs into a pipe."

(C) "The tank holds enough for the dry months."

1. (B) names the rain, the roof and the pipe. It needs no earlier sentence.
2. *This water* in (A) points to the rain water in (B).
3. *The tank* in (C) points to the village tank in (A).
Rain collects on the school roof and runs into a pipe. This water then flows into the village tank. The tank holds enough for the dry months.

:::pair #the-noun-link
Right: "A student began a study of park birds. The study counted forty kinds."
Wrong: "The study counted forty kinds. A student began a study of park birds."
*The study* is a link even without *this* or *it*.
:::

:::check
Put in order: (A) "That change saved the school money." (B) "The school switched to solar panels last year."
:::answer
The answer is B, then A. *That change* in (A) points to the switch to solar panels in (B): "The school switched to solar panels last year. That change saved the school money."
:::

## Examples

### #1: Building a Reference Chain

Arrange the sentences into a coherent paragraph.

(A) This competition led to conflicts between the groups.

(B) As the settlements grew, competition for these resources increased.

(C) Several groups settled near a river for water and fertile soil.

(D) Some of these conflicts later became wars.

**Solution:**

1. (C) names the groups and resources. It needs no earlier sentence.
2. *These resources* in (B) point to the water and soil in (C).
3. *This competition* in (A) points to the competition in (B).
4. *These conflicts* in (D) point to the conflicts in (A).
Several groups settled near a river for water and fertile soil. As the settlements grew, competition for these resources increased. This competition led to conflicts between the groups. Some of these conflicts later became wars.

**Check:** Each pointing phrase follows its source.

### #2: A Short Story

Arrange the sentences in the best order.

(A) Her heart sank when she saw the empty shelf.

(B) Rosa had saved all week to buy the last copy of a book.

(C) The clerk said the last copy on that shelf had sold an hour earlier.

**Solution:**

1. (A) says "Her heart." Whose? It needs an earlier sentence.
2. (B) names Rosa and the book, so it opens.
3. (C) explains the empty shelf in (A), so it follows (A).
Rosa had saved all week to buy the last copy of a book. Her heart sank when she saw the empty shelf. The clerk said the last copy on that shelf had sold an hour earlier.

**Check:** Rosa saves, finds the shelf empty, hears why.

### #3: A Set of Three

Arrange the sentences in the best order.

(A) These grants let schools buy new lab equipment.

(B) Last year the district set aside money for science grants.

(C) Teachers said the equipment made experiments safer.

**Solution:**

1. (A) says "These grants." Which grants? It needs an earlier sentence.

:::try
Finish the last step: what is the full order?
:::answer
The answer is B, A, C. (B) names "science grants" and makes sense alone. *The equipment* in (C) points to "new lab equipment" in (A), so (C) comes after (A): "Last year the district set aside money for science grants. These grants let schools buy new lab equipment. Teachers said the equipment made experiments safer."

**Check:** Each pointing phrase (*These grants*, *the equipment*) now comes after the sentence that names it.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A sentence that starts with *this*, *that* or *these*: usually put the sentence that names the thing first (name, then pointer). {#reference-before-source}
- A phrase such as *the study* or *those replies* that names a noun introduced elsewhere in the set: put the first mention before it (first mention, then phrase). {#the-noun-link}
- No pointing word and no broad statement: a story or steps may open with an event (follow the links the text gives). {#choosing-the-opening-sentence}
:::
