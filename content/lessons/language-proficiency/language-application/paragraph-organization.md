## Core Concept

**Paragraph organization** means putting sentences in an order that makes the ideas easy to follow. Read every sentence before choosing an order. Look for links that make one sentence depend on another.

### Clues to Order

An **opening sentence** often names the topic or situation. It should make sense before the reader sees the other sentences. Still, a story can open with an event or question instead of a broad statement.

**Reference words**, such as *it, this, these,* and *they*, often point to something already named. If a sentence says *this problem*, ask what problem it means. In a scrambled set with no other context, the sentence naming that problem usually comes earlier.

**Transitions** show links between ideas. *However* shows contrast; *therefore* shows a result. *For example* introduces a case that helps explain an idea, while *in addition* adds a point.

A transition does not give an exact position by itself. Read the meaning of the sentences it connects. A pronoun can also begin a paragraph if the reader already knows who or what it means.

**Chronology** is time order. Dates and words such as *before* and *after* show when events happen. In a procedure, check which step must be done before the next one can work.

A **cause** explains why something happens. An **effect** is the result. The cause may come first, but a writer can also state the result and then explain its cause.

### Understanding the Ordered Text

The **main idea** is the main point the text makes about its topic. An important **detail** supports or explains that point. Choose a main idea that covers the whole text without adding a claim the text does not make.

To **infer** means to reach an idea from clues in the text. An inference need not repeat a sentence, but the text must support it. A possible guess with no clue is not enough.

Notice the kind of text. A story may follow events, instructions may follow steps, and an opinion may give a claim with reasons. An explanation may show how or why something happens.

### Filipino: Pag-aayos at Pag-unawa sa Talata

Hanapin ang malinaw na ugnayan ng mga pangungusap. Ang *ito, iyon, siya,* at *sila* ay maaaring tumukoy sa naunang binanggit. Ang salitang *dito* ay maaaring tumukoy sa lugar na nabanggit na.

Ang *una, pagkatapos, sumunod,* at *sa wakas* ay tumutulong sa pagkakasunod-sunod. Ngunit hindi sapat ang *pagkatapos* upang malaman ang eksaktong puwesto. Tingnan kung aling pangyayari ang kailangang mauna.

Ang **sanhi** (cause) ay dahilan ng pangyayari. Ang **bunga** (effect) ay resulta nito. Karaniwang ipinapakilala ng *dahil* at *sapagkat* ang sanhi; maaaring ipakilala ng *kaya* ang bunga.

Ang **paksa** (topic) ang pinag-uusapan. Ang pangunahing ideya ang mahalagang sinasabi tungkol dito. Ang mga salitang susi ay tumutulong upang maunawaan ang diwa, ngunit basahin ang mga ito sa buong pangungusap.

Sa pagpapaliwanag, sabihin ang ideya sa sariling salita. Huwag magdagdag ng impormasyong wala sa binasa. Sa paggawa ng **balangkas** (outline), ilagay ang pangunahing ideya at ang mga detalyeng sumusuporta rito.

## Examples

### #1: Building a Reference Chain

Arrange the sentences into a coherent paragraph. **Coherent** means the ideas connect clearly.

(A) This competition led to conflicts between the groups.
(B) As the settlements grew, competition for these resources increased.
(C) The groups settled near a river for water and fertile soil.
(D) Some of these conflicts later became wars.

**Solution:**

1. (C) names the groups, river, and resources without needing an earlier sentence.
2. *These resources* in (B) points to the water and soil in (C).
3. *This competition* in (A) points to the competition in (B).
4. *These conflicts* in (D) points to the conflicts in (A).
5. The order is **C → B → A → D**.

### #2: General Idea and Illustration

Arrange the sentences in the best order.

(A) For example, a large-print copy can help a reader who struggles with small text.
(B) This larger copy presents the same information in an easier-to-read form.
(C) Offices can offer documents in forms that meet different readers' needs.

**Solution:**

1. (C) gives the general idea: offering different forms of documents.
2. (A) gives a specific example of that idea.
3. *This larger copy* in (B) refers to the large-print copy in (A).
4. The order is **C → A → B**.

### #3: Reading a Contrast

Read: "Many staff members supported the new policy. **However**, not everyone agreed with it." What does the word in bold tell you?

**Solution:**

1. **However** shows contrast.
2. Many staff members agreed, but some did not. The second sentence makes that difference clear.
3. The word shows the link between the ideas. It does not set a fixed sentence number.

### #4: Ordering a Procedure

Arrange the steps in the best order.

(A) Finally, submit the signed form to the records desk.
(B) Check the completed fields for errors before signing.
(C) First, write your name and contact details in the form's fields.
(D) After checking the form, sign it at the bottom.

**Solution:**

1. (C) fills the fields. (B) must follow because it checks those completed fields.
2. (D) must follow the check and adds the signature.
3. (A) submits the signed form, so it comes last.
4. The order is **C → B → D → A**.

### #5: Ordering Events by Time

Arrange the sentences in time order.

(A) By noon, the missing parcel had reached its owner.
(B) At opening time that morning, the clerk found an unclaimed parcel.
(C) An hour after finding it, she called its owner using the number on its label.

**Solution:**

1. Finding the parcel in (B) comes first.
2. The call in (C) happens an hour after that discovery.
3. The parcel reaching its owner in (A) completes the account.
4. The order is **B → C → A**.

### #6: Cause Before Effect

Which order makes the link clear?

(A) As a result, several streets were flooded.
(B) Heavy rain fell for hours without stopping.

**Solution:**

1. (B) gives the cause, the long period of rain.
2. *As a result* in (A) points back to that cause.
3. Use **B → A**.

### #7: Effect Before Cause

Which order makes the link clear?

(A) Several classes were canceled that morning.
(B) The reason for these cancellations was that floodwater had blocked the school road.

**Solution:**

1. (A) states the effect.
2. *These cancellations* in (B) points back to (A) and introduces the reason.
3. Use **A → B**. Cause does not always come first.

### #8: Main Idea in an Explanation

Read: "Many offices use digital forms. These forms reduce repeated handwriting and make records easier to store. They can also be sent quickly between departments."

What is the main idea?

(A) Handwriting is always slow.
(B) Digital forms can make office work easier.
(C) Every department uses the same form.
(D) Paper records should never be used.

**Solution:**

1. The details describe benefits of digital forms.
2. **(B)** covers those benefits without adding a claim the text does not support.

### #9: Important Detail in a Notice

Read: "The records desk will close at noon on Friday for equipment repairs. Requests received after noon will be processed on Monday. Online submission will remain open."

When will a request received on Friday afternoon be processed?

**Solution:**

1. Friday afternoon is after the noon cutoff.
2. The notice says those requests will be processed **on Monday**. Online submission staying open does not change that date.

### #10: Inference in a Story

Read: "Nina reached the office before it opened. She held a folder of forms and checked the posted list of required documents. When the guard opened the door, she joined the application line."

What can be inferred about Nina's purpose?

**Solution:**

1. Her forms, document check, and choice of line are clues.
2. She likely came **to submit an application**. The story does not tell us whether it will be approved.

### #11: Main Point in an Opinion

Read: "The office should keep a paper option for forms. Some visitors have no internet access. Others need help filling in the fields. A paper option would let these visitors request service too."

What does the writer want, and what reasons support it?

**Solution:**

1. The writer wants **the office to keep a paper option**.
2. The reasons are that some visitors lack internet access and others need help with the fields.
3. These reasons support access to service. They do not claim that digital forms are useless.

### #12: Pagkakasunod-sunod at Pagtukoy

Ayusin ang mga pangungusap.

(A) Sa wakas, inilagay niya ang nilagdaang liham sa sobre.
(B) Una, isinulat ni Lea ang liham.
(C) Binasa niya itong muli upang itama ang mali bago niya ito lagdaan.
(D) Nang matapos ang pagwawasto, nilagdaan niya ang liham.

**Solution:**

1. Sa (B), isinulat muna ni Lea ang liham. Ito ang tinutukoy ng *ito* sa (C).
2. Sinasabi ng (C) na nauna ang pagbasa at pagwawasto sa paglagda.
3. Sa (D), tapos na ang pagwawasto kaya maaari nang lumagda.
4. Sa (A), inilagay ang nilagdaang liham sa sobre.
5. Ang ayos ay **B → C → D → A**.

### #13: Sanhi at Bunga

Ayusin ang mga pangungusap. Pagkatapos, pagsamahin ang mga ito gamit ang *dahil*.

(A) Kaya hindi natuloy ang palaro.
(B) Malakas ang ulan buong umaga.

**Solution:**

1. Ang malakas na ulan ang sanhi. Ang hindi pagtuloy ng palaro ang bunga.
2. Ang ayos ay **B → A**.
3. Maaaring pagsamahin ang mga ito: **"Hindi natuloy ang palaro dahil malakas ang ulan buong umaga."**

### #14: Paksa, Salitang Susi, at Balangkas

Basahin: "Mahalaga ang silid-aklatan sa pag-aaral. Dito maaaring manghiram ng aklat ang mga mag-aaral. Maaari rin silang magsaliksik gamit ang mga **sanggunian**. May tahimik ding lugar para sa pagbabasa."

Ano ang paksa? Ano ang ibig sabihin ng salitang nakadiin sa talata? Ipaliwanag ang pangunahing ideya at gumawa ng maikling balangkas.

**Solution:**

1. Ang paksa ay **silid-aklatan**. Ang pangunahing ideya ay ang tulong nito sa pag-aaral.
2. Ang **sanggunian** dito ay materyal na pinagkukunan ng impormasyon. Kaugnay ito ng pagsasaliksik.
3. Sa sariling salita: **"Nakakatulong ang silid-aklatan dahil may mga aklat, mapagkukunan ng impormasyon, at lugar para sa tahimik na pagbabasa."**
4. Maaaring ganito ang balangkas:

- Pangunahing ideya: Tulong ng silid-aklatan sa pag-aaral.
- Detalye: Pagpapahiram ng aklat.
- Detalye: Mga sanggunian para sa pagsasaliksik.
- Detalye: Tahimik na lugar para sa pagbabasa.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Find strong links first. A phrase such as *these cancellations* needs the sentence that names the cancellations.

For steps, ask what must already be done. A form must be signed before it can be submitted as a signed form.

Read the whole order once more. A good pair can still be in the wrong place in the full paragraph.

For comprehension, return to the text. Separate what it states from what its clues support.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Banning Pronouns from Openings

> A pronoun can begin a paragraph when its meaning is already known.

In a scrambled set with no outside context, an unexplained pronoun is a clue to look for an earlier sentence. It is not a rule for every kind of writing.

### #2: Treating Transitions as Position Codes

> A transition shows a link, not an exact position.

*Afterward* tells you that something follows another event. Check the actual events to decide which comes next.

### #3: Forcing a General Opening

> Not every paragraph starts with a broad topic sentence.

Stories and instructions can begin with an event or action. Follow the kind of text and the links it provides.

### #4: Forcing Cause Before Effect

> An effect can come before its cause.

Words such as *the reason for this* can introduce a later explanation. Follow that signal.

### #5: Adding a Guess

> An inference needs clues from the text.

Do not add reasons, results, or facts just because they are possible. Use only what the passage supports.
