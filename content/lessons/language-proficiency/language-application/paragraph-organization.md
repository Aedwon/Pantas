## Core Concept

**Paragraph organization** means putting sentences in an order that makes the ideas easy to follow. The public CSE scope includes paragraph organization. The exact item format can vary, so focus on how ideas connect.

Use these clues:

**Opening idea.** A good first sentence often introduces the topic or situation. It should make sense even if the reader has not seen the other sentences yet.

**Reference words.** Words such as *it, this, these,* and *they* often point back to something already named. If a sentence starts with *this problem*, ask, "What problem?" The sentence that names it may need to come first.

**Transitions.** Words such as *however, therefore, for example, meanwhile,* and *in addition* show how ideas relate. Use the meaning of the whole paragraph, not the transition word alone.

**Chronology and procedure.** Time words such as *first, next, after,* and *finally* can show the order of events or steps.

**Cause and effect.** A cause explains why something happened. An effect is the result. Writers can give the cause first or the effect first, so follow the words and meaning.

After arranging a paragraph, check the **main idea**, the important details, and what you can **infer**. To infer means to reach a reasonable idea from clues in the text. A paragraph can be a story, explanation, instruction, or opinion, so do not expect every paragraph to use the same pattern.

### Filipino: Pag-aayos ng Talata

Sa Filipino, hanapin din ang malinaw na ugnayan ng mga pangungusap. Ang mga salitang *ito, iyon, siya,* at *sila* ay maaaring tumukoy sa naunang binanggit na tao o bagay.

Ang mga salitang *una, pagkatapos, sumunod,* at *sa wakas* ay tumutulong sa pagkakasunod-sunod. Ang *dahil, sapagkat,* at *kaya* ay maaaring magpakita ng sanhi at bunga.

Pagkatapos ayusin ang talata, tukuyin ang paksa at mahahalagang detalye. Hanapin din ang mga salitang susi at ipaliwanag ang ideya gamit ang sariling salita. Maaari ring gumawa ng maikling balangkas: pangunahing ideya muna, saka ang mga detalyeng sumusuporta rito.

## Examples

### #1: Finding the Best Opening

Which sentence is the strongest opening for this set?

(A) "This eventually led to conflicts between different groups."
(B) "As cities grew, competition for these limited resources increased."
(C) "Ancient civilizations often settled near rivers because of fertile soil and reliable water."
(D) "Some of these conflicts escalated into full-scale wars."

**Solution:**

1. Sentence (C) introduces the topic without pointing back to an earlier idea.
2. The words *this* and *these* in the other sentences need ideas that have not yet been named.
3. Choose **(C)** as the opening.

### #2: Tracking a Reference

What best follows sentence (C)?

**Solution:**

1. Sentence (B) says "these limited resources."
2. That phrase can point back to fertile soil and reliable water in sentence (C).
3. So **(B)** best follows (C).

### #3: Completing the Chain

Place the remaining two sentences after C and B.

**Solution:**

1. Sentence (A) says the competition "led to conflicts."
2. Sentence (D) then says "these conflicts" became wars.
3. The full order is **C → B → A → D**.

### #4: Reading a Transition

A paragraph contains: "However, not everyone agreed with the new policy." What does the word in bold, **however**, tell you?

**Solution:**

1. **However** shows a contrast.
2. An earlier idea probably gave a different view, such as support for the policy.
3. The word alone does not tell you the exact sentence that must come right before it. Check the meaning of the whole paragraph.

### #5: Chronological Order

Arrange the steps in the best order.

(A) "Finally, submit the signed form to the records desk."
(B) "Next, check that all required fields are complete."
(C) "First, write your name and contact details on the form."
(D) "After checking the form, sign it at the bottom."

**Solution:**

1. *First* starts the procedure, so (C) comes first.
2. *Next* gives the second step, so (B) follows.
3. *After checking* points back to (B), so (D) comes next.
4. *Finally* ends the procedure with (A).
5. The order is **C → B → D → A**.

### #6: Cause Before Effect

Which order is clearest?

(A) "As a result, several streets were flooded."
(B) "Heavy rain fell for hours without stopping."

**Solution:**

1. "As a result" tells you that sentence (A) gives an effect.
2. Sentence (B) gives the cause.
3. The clearest order is **B → A**.

### #7: Effect Before Cause

Which order is clearest?

(A) "Several classes were canceled that morning."
(B) "The reason was simple: floodwater had blocked the road to the school."

**Solution:**

1. Sentence (A) gives the effect first.
2. Sentence (B) clearly says it will explain the reason.
3. The order **A → B** works because the second sentence signals the cause.

### #8: Main Idea After Ordering

Read this ordered paragraph:

"Many offices now use digital forms. These forms can reduce repeated handwriting and make records easier to store. They can also be sent quickly between departments."

What is the main idea?

(A) Handwriting is always slow.
(B) Digital forms can make office work easier.
(C) Every department uses the same form.
(D) Paper records should never be used.

**Solution:**

1. All three sentences describe benefits of digital forms.
2. Only (B) covers the whole paragraph.
3. Choose **(B)**.

### #9: Pagkakasunod-sunod

Ayusin ang mga pangungusap.

(A) "Sa wakas, inilagay niya ang liham sa sobre."
(B) "Una, isinulat ni Lea ang liham."
(C) "Pagkatapos, binasa niya itong muli."
(D) "Sumunod, pinirmahan niya ang liham."

**Solution:**

1. Ang *una* ay palatandaan ng simula, kaya (B) ang una.
2. Ang *pagkatapos* ay kasunod ng unang hakbang, kaya (C) ang ikalawa.
3. Ang *sumunod* ay tumutukoy sa kasunod na hakbang, kaya (D) ang ikatlo.
4. Ang *sa wakas* ay huling hakbang, kaya (A) ang huli.
5. Ang ayos ay **B → C → D → A**.

### #10: Sanhi at Bunga

Aling pangungusap ang dapat mauna?

(A) "Kaya hindi natuloy ang palaro."
(B) "Malakas ang ulan buong umaga."

**Solution:**

1. Ang *kaya* ay nagpapakita ng bunga.
2. Ang malakas na ulan ang sanhi.
3. Ang tamang ayos ay **B → A**.

### #11: Paksa at Balangkas

Basahin: "Maraming mag-aaral ang gumagamit ng silid-aklatan. Dito sila nakakabasa, nakapagsasaliksik, at nakapag-aaral nang tahimik."

Ano ang pangunahing paksa?

(A) tahimik na lugar
(B) gamit ng silid-aklatan sa pag-aaral
(C) bilang ng mga aklat
(D) oras ng klase

**Solution:**

1. Ang unang pangungusap ay tungkol sa paggamit ng silid-aklatan.
2. Ang ikalawang pangungusap ay nagbibigay ng mga dahilan at halimbawa.
3. Piliin ang **(B)**.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Build links between sentences.** Mark reference words, transitions, repeated words, time clues, and cause-and-effect clues.

**Find what each sentence needs.** If a sentence says *this change*, find the sentence that explains the change.

**Test the full order.** A pair of sentences can sound fine together but still make the whole paragraph confusing.

**Check the main idea last.** Once the order is clear, make sure every sentence supports the paragraph's main point.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: "A Pronoun Can Never Come First"

> A pronoun can begin a real paragraph when the reader already knows what it refers to.

In a self-contained ordering question, an unexplained reference is only a clue that the sentence may not belong first.

### #2: Treating Transition Words as Fixed Position Codes

> A transition shows a relationship, not an exact sentence number.

Use the whole meaning. *However* shows contrast, while *therefore* often shows a result.

### #3: Assuming Every Paragraph Starts with a General Topic Sentence

> Stories, questions, quotations, and other openings can be valid.

Choose the sentence that best fits the paragraph's actual pattern.

### #4: Forcing Cause Before Effect

> A writer can give the effect first and explain the cause afterward.

Look for signals such as "the reason was" or "because" before deciding the order.
