## Core Concept

### Choosing A, An and The

A **countable noun** names something you can count as separate items, such as one form or several forms. Use *a* or *an* for one such item that is not yet identified. Choose by the first sound of the next word, not its first letter.

- *hour* starts with a vowel sound, so *an hour*.
- *university* starts with a consonant sound, so *a university*.

Use **the** when the context identifies which thing is meant. Let's fill the blanks in "I received a form. ______ form was blank."

1. The first sentence introduces the form with *a*.
2. The second sentence points to that same form.
3. A thing already introduced takes *the*.

I received a form. The form was blank.

:::pair #article-by-spelling
Right: an unopened box
Wrong: a unopened box
*Unopened* begins with a vowel sound, so it takes *an*. Say the word aloud before choosing.
:::

:::check
Choose the pair: "Mia gave ___ honest answer. Everyone trusted ___ answer." *(an, the / a, the / the, an)*
:::answer
*an, the*. *Honest* begins with a vowel sound (the *h* is silent), so the first blank takes *an*. The second mention points to the same answer, so it takes *the*. Full sentences: "Mia gave an honest answer. Everyone trusted the answer."
:::

### Prepositions in Fixed Phrases

**Prepositions**, such as *in, on, at, to,* and *for*, show links between words. Many go with one word in a fixed phrase: *interested in*, *responsible for*, *good at*. Check the word before the blank, then recall its usual partner.

Let's fill the blank in "The nurse is good ______ calming patients."

1. The word before the blank is *good*.
2. Its usual partner is *at*.

The nurse is good at calming patients.

:::pair #preposition-pattern
Right: She is interested in music.
Wrong: She is interested on music.
Many words keep one usual preposition. *On* fits other phrases, such as *focused on*, but not *interested*.
:::

:::check
Choose the preposition: "The coach is proud ___ the team." *(of / at / for)*
:::answer
*of*. The fixed phrase is *proud of*: "The coach is proud of the team."
:::

### Matching Forms in Lists and Comparisons

**Parallel structure** means items doing the same job use the same form. A list can use *reading, writing, and speaking*. A comparison can use *She prefers reading to watching television*.

Let's fill the blank in "Mara enjoys swimming, running, and ______."

1. *Swimming* and *running* both end in *-ing*.
2. The blank is the third item in the same list.
3. Use an *-ing* word, such as *cycling*.

Mara enjoys swimming, running, and cycling.

:::pair #mixed-list-form
Right: The job needs sorting, packing, and shipping.
Wrong: The job needs sorting, packing, and to ship.
The third item changed form. Every item in the list uses *-ing*.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "The course teaches planning, budgeting, and ___." *(negotiate / negotiating / to negotiate)*
:::answer
*negotiating*. *Planning* and *budgeting* end in *-ing*, so the third item does too: "The course teaches planning, budgeting, and negotiating."
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Phrase with Responsible

Choose the best completion: "The assistant is responsible ______ updating the visitor log."

(A) of

(B) for

(C) to

(D) with

**Solution:**

1. The word before the blank is *responsible*.
2. Its usual partner is *for*.

Choose **(B)**: "The assistant is responsible for updating the visitor log."

**Check:** Try one swap: "responsible of updating the visitor log" is not a standard phrase.

### #2: Articles and Context

Choose the correct pair: "A teacher gave Ben a worksheet. He finished ______ worksheet before ______ unit test."

(A) a, the

(B) the, an

(C) a, an

(D) the, a

**Solution:**

1. The first blank points to the worksheet already introduced. Use **the**.
2. The test is new. *Unit* begins with a consonant sound, so use **a**.

Choose **(D)**: "He finished the worksheet before a unit test."

**Check:** Swap the first article: "He finished a worksheet" no longer points to the worksheet already named.

### #3: A Comparison of Habits

Choose the best completion: "Ken prefers walking to ______ the bus."

(A) ride

(B) riding

(C) to ride

(D) rode

**Solution:**

1. *Prefers* compares two activities.
2. Name the form of the first activity.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice has that form?
:::answer
The answer is **(B) riding**: "Ken prefers walking to riding the bus."

**Check:** *Walking* and *riding* both end in *-ing*, so the two activities match.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- *a* or *an* before a word: say the word and listen to its first sound (*an unopened box*). {#article-by-spelling}
- A word before the blank with a fixed partner: use that partner (*interested in*). {#preposition-pattern}
- A list with matching items: give the blank the same form as the others (*sorting, packing, shipping*). {#mixed-list-form}
:::
