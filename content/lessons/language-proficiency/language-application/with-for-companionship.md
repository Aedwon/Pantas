## Core Concept

A **preposition** is a word that shows how one part of a sentence connects to another. Use *with* to show who joins someone in an action. In “I painted the sign with Niko,” both people took part in painting.

*For* can show who the action helps. “I painted the sign for Niko” says I did it for his benefit. *To* can show where someone is going or who gets something. “I handed the sign to Niko” tells who got it.

## Examples

### #1: Working Together

Both students helped make the model. Choose the best word to complete the sentence.

“I built the model ______ my cousin.”

(A) for

(B) with

(C) from

**Solution:**

1. Both people helped build the model. The blank tells who joined the speaker.
2. Use *with*. Choose (B).

### #2: Joining or Receiving

Ana and Luis built a kite together. Ben was the judge. Choose the best pair of words.

“Ana built the kite ______ Luis and handed it ______ Ben.”

(A) to, with

(B) for, with

(C) with, to

**Solution:**

1. Luis helped Ana build the kite. Use *with Luis*.
2. Ben got the kite from Ana. Use *to Ben*. Choose (C).

### #3: Two Blanks in One Sentence

Sari and her aunt sorted donations together. Choose the best pair of words.

“Sari packed books ______ her aunt, ______ they finished before noon.”

(A) for, because

(B) with, and

(C) with, or

**Solution:**

1. Her aunt took part, so the first blank takes *with*.
2. The second blank joins two things that happened. Use *and*. Choose (B).

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Ask “Who did it together?” If the words after the blank name the person who joined in, try *with*.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Naming a Tool

> Read what comes after *with* before deciding what it means.

In “I cut the paper with scissors,” *with* tells what tool I used. When it shows companionship, it tells who joined me.
