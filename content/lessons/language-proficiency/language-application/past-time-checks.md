## Core Concept

### Time Clues and Who Acts

**Tense** places an action in time. Use the **simple past** for a finished past event marked by clues like *yesterday* or *last week*, or for events told in order: "She finished yesterday."

Check who does the action. When the subject receives the action instead of doing it, use passive *was* or *were* plus a past participle: "The parcels **were collected** by the driver last week." Parcels cannot collect themselves, and *last week* keeps the verb in the past.

:::pair #past-time-clue
Right: "The documents were delivered by the courier yesterday."
Wrong: "The documents are being delivered by the courier yesterday."
*Yesterday* marks a finished past event, so present ongoing *are being delivered* does not fit.
:::

:::check
Choose the correct form: The signed forms *(were mailed / are being mailed)* to the office last Monday.
:::answer
*Were mailed*: "The signed forms were mailed to the office last Monday." *Last Monday* places the finished action in the past, and *forms* receives the action.

**Check:** Turn it active: "Someone mailed the signed forms last Monday." The time and the meaning still match.
:::

### One Action Meets Another

**Aspect** shows whether an action was ongoing or already done at a past moment. Use the **past progressive** (*was* or *were* plus *-ing*) when an action was still in progress as another past event happened: "Mina **was checking** the files when the alarm rang."

:::check
The lights went out at ten, before the clerk finished. Choose the right form: At ten, she *(was filing / had filed)* the receipts.
:::answer
*Was filing*: "At ten, she was filing the receipts." She had not finished, so the filing was still in progress when the lights went out.

**Check:** Join the two events with *when*: "She was filing the receipts when the lights went out." The two actions overlap.
:::

### Order of Two Past Events

The **past perfect** uses *had* plus a **past participle** (the form used after *have, has,* or *had*). Regular past participles end in *-ed* (*reviewed, finished*), while irregular ones vary (*written* from *write*, *gone* from *go*, *caught* from *catch*).

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: timeline with two points: earlier X labeled "had already resolved (past perfect)" to the left of later X labeled "finished inspecting (simple past)" -->

Use *had* + past participle on the **earlier** of two past events when a sentence highlights that earlier completion: "The cook **had washed** the pans before the first order came in."

Do not add *had* to every past action. When *before* or *after* already makes the order clear, simple past can be enough unless a question specifically asks for past perfect to mark earlier completion. Do not put *had* on the later event instead of the earlier one.

:::why
Why does *had review* fail even when the timeline needs past perfect? After *had*, the next verb is a past participle (*reviewed*), not the plain base verb.
:::

:::pair #participle-after-had
Right: "The analysts had reviewed the data before they caught the error."
Wrong: "The analysts had review the data before they caught the error."
After *had*, use the past participle *reviewed*, not the base verb *review*.
:::

:::pair #which-event-gets-had
Right: "By the time the committee finished inspecting, the team had already resolved the errors."
Wrong: "By the time the committee had finished inspecting, the team already resolved the errors."
The resolving finished first, and the inspection is the later point. Put *had* on the earlier action, not the later one.
:::

:::check
Choose the right verb form: Before the doors opened, the staff *(had arrange / had arranged)* all the chairs.
:::answer
*Had arranged*: "Before the doors opened, the staff had arranged all the chairs." After *had*, use the past participle *arranged*.

**Check:** Test with *have*: "I have arranged it" sounds right, while "I have arrange it" does not.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Time Clue and Who Does the Action

Choose the correct form: "Last Friday, the packages *(were sorted / are being sorted / have sorted)* by the night crew."

**Solution:**

1. Check the time clue: *last Friday* places the finished event in the past, ruling out *are being sorted*.
2. Check who acts: packages cannot sort anything, so passive voice is needed, ruling out *have sorted*.
3. Choose **were sorted**: "Last Friday, the packages were sorted by the night crew."

**Check:** Turn it active to test: "The night crew sorted the packages last Friday." Both time and passive meaning match.

### #2: Checking the Participle After Had

Identify the error: "Before the rain began, the volunteers had take all the boxes inside."

**Solution:**

1. *Had take* uses the past-perfect helper *had* followed by the base verb *take*.
2. The past participle of *take* is **taken**.
3. Change *had take* to **had taken**: "Before the rain began, the volunteers had taken all the boxes inside."

**Check:** Test with *have*: "I have taken it" sounds right, while "I have take it" does not.

### #3: Two Past Actions in One Sentence

A question asks you to use past perfect for the earlier action: "The guests arrived at the hall, but the crew already repaired the sound system."

**Solution:**

1. Find the two past actions: *arrived* and *repaired*.
2. Ask which action was already complete when the other happened.

:::try
Finish the last step: which verb group must change, and what should replace it?
:::answer
Change **already repaired** to **had already repaired**: "The guests arrived at the hall, but the crew had already repaired the sound system." The repair was complete before the guests came, so it takes the past perfect. *Arrived* stays in simple past as the later point.

**Check:** Retell it in order: "The crew had already repaired the sound system, and then the guests arrived." The *had* sits on the earlier event.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Look for time clues (*yesterday, last Tuesday*) first, check every *had + verb* pair for a true past participle, and put *had* only on the earlier completed event.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A finished past clue like *yesterday*: use a past form, not present progressive. {#past-time-clue}
- A base verb after *had*: switch it to the past participle (*had reviewed*, *had taken*). {#participle-after-had}
- Two past events when the earlier one must be marked: put *had* on the earlier completed action, not the later one. {#which-event-gets-had}
:::
