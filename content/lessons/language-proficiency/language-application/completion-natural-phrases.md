## Core Concept

### Word Combinations

**Collocations** are words commonly used together, such as *strong evidence*, *make a decision*, and *reach an agreement*. Grammar alone may not decide between choices. The usual word partner also matters.

Let's fill the blank in "The storm brought ______ rain."

1. The blank describes *rain*.
2. Ask which word usually goes with *rain*. People say *heavy rain*, not *strong rain*.

The storm brought heavy rain.

:::pair #wrong-partner
Right: The committee must make a decision.
Wrong: The committee must do a decision.
*Do* fits some nouns, such as *homework*, but the usual partner of *decision* is *make*.
:::

:::check
Choose the best word: "The guide asked the group to ___ attention to the map." *(pay / make / do)*
:::answer
*pay*. *Attention* usually goes with *pay*, not *make* or *do*: "The guide asked the group to pay attention to the map."
:::

### Idioms

An **idiom** is an expression whose whole meaning may differ from the meanings of its parts. Read it as a whole expression, not word by word.

Let's read "The speaker told a joke to break the ice."

1. *Break the ice* is an idiom.
2. Its whole meaning is to help people relax at the start of a meeting.
3. No ice is broken.

The speaker told a joke to help the audience relax.

:::pair #idiom-by-parts
Right: Please keep an eye on the door. (watch it)
Wrong: Please turn a blind eye to the door. (ignore it)
Both idioms contain *eye*, but their whole meanings are opposites. Read the whole expression.
:::

:::check
In "We finished early, so let's call it a day," what does *call it a day* mean? *(stop work for now / phone a friend)*
:::answer
*Stop work for now*. The whole idiom means to end the day's work, not to make a phone call.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Word for Evidence

Choose the best completion: "The panel found ______ evidence that the records had been changed."

(A) strong

(B) heavy

(C) tall

(D) loud

**Solution:**

1. The blank describes *evidence*.
2. Ask which word usually goes with *evidence*. *Strong evidence* is the usual combination for convincing support.

Choose **(A)**: "The panel found strong evidence that the records had been changed."

**Check:** Say each choice with *evidence*. Only *strong evidence* sounds natural.

### #2: A Day at Home

Choose the best completion: "Mina stayed home because she felt ______ the weather."

(A) under

(B) over

(C) behind

(D) beside

**Solution:**

1. The idiom is *feel ______ the weather*.
2. It means feel slightly ill.

Choose **(A)**: "Mina stayed home because she felt under the weather."

**Check:** The sentence gives a reason for staying home. *Under the weather* means feeling unwell.

### #3: A Night Shift

Choose the best completion: "The guard promised to ______ a close watch on the gate all night."

(A) do

(B) keep

(C) make

(D) say

**Solution:**

1. The blank is a verb before *a close watch*.
2. Ask which verb usually goes with *watch* in this sense.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice is the usual partner of *a close watch*?
:::answer
The answer is **(B) keep**: "The guard promised to keep a close watch on the gate all night."

**Check:** Say each choice with *a close watch*. *Do*, *make* and *say* do not usually go with it.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Reread the completed sentence. Check meaning, grammar, and natural word use together.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A blank before a noun such as *decision*: choose its usual partner (*make a decision*). A general word such as *do* may not fit. {#wrong-partner}
- An idiom with a familiar word such as *eye*: read the whole expression for its meaning (*keep an eye on* means watch). {#idiom-by-parts}
:::
