## Core Concept

### What Transitions Show

**Transitions** show links between ideas. Common ones include *however*, which shows contrast, and *therefore*, which shows a result. *For example* introduces a case that helps explain an idea, and *in addition* adds a point.

What does *therefore* show here?

"The road was flooded. Therefore, the bus took the hill route."

1. The first sentence gives a cause: "The road was flooded."
2. *Therefore* tells us the next sentence is the result.
3. The result is "the bus took the hill route."

:::check
Which fits the blank: *however* or *therefore*? "The trail was steep. ___, we rested often."
:::answer
The answer is *therefore*. Resting often is a result of the steep trail, not a contrast: "The trail was steep. Therefore, we rested often."
:::

### Placing a Transition Sentence

A sentence that starts with *however* or *for example* goes against, or gives a case of, an idea before it. Put that idea first.

Put in order:

(A) "However, it lost the first game."

(B) "The team trained for months."

1. (A) says "However," so it goes against something.
2. (B) is what it goes against: the team trained.
The team trained for months. However, it lost the first game.

:::pair #contrast-before-claim
Right: "The old map showed a short road. However, that road had been closed for years."
Wrong: "However, that road had been closed for years. The old map showed a short road."
*However* needs a claim to go against. With nothing before it, the contrast has no first part.
:::

:::pair #example-before-idea
Right: "Many pets need daily exercise. For example, dogs need long walks."
Wrong: "For example, dogs need long walks. Many pets need daily exercise."
*For example* introduces a case of an idea. Put the general idea first.
:::

:::check
Which sentence comes first? (A) "For example, tomatoes grow well in pots." (B) "Many vegetables can grow on a balcony."
:::answer
The answer is (B). It states the general idea. (A) is a case of that idea: "Many vegetables can grow on a balcony. For example, tomatoes grow well in pots."
:::

### Where a Transition Goes

A transition does not give an exact position by itself. Read the meaning of the sentences it connects.

Where does *however* go?

(A) "The plant needs light."

(B) "It also needs water."

(C) "However, too much water rots the roots."

1. (C) goes against (B): the plant needs water, but not too much.
2. So *however* sits right after (B).
3. The order is A, B, C: "The plant needs light. It also needs water. However, too much water rots the roots."

:::check
Put in order: (A) "However, the new route cost more." (B) "The new route was faster." (C) "Many drivers chose it anyway."
:::answer
The answer is B, A, C. *However* goes against the speed in (B), and *anyway* in (C) answers the cost in (A): "The new route was faster. However, the new route cost more. Many drivers chose it anyway."
:::

## Examples

### #1: Documents for Readers

Arrange the sentences in the best order.

(A) For example, a large-print copy can help a reader who struggles with small text.

(B) This larger copy presents the same information in an easier-to-read form.

(C) Offices can offer documents in forms that meet different readers' needs.

**Solution:**

1. (C) gives the general idea.
2. (A) is a specific example of that idea.
3. *This larger copy* in (B) points to the large-print copy in (A).

Offices can offer documents in forms that meet different readers' needs. For example, a large-print copy can help a reader who struggles with small text. This larger copy presents the same information in an easier-to-read form.

**Check:** Idea, example, then a sentence about it.

### #2: A Staff Policy

Read: "Many staff members supported the new policy. **However**, not everyone agreed with it."

What does the word in bold tell you?

**Solution:**

1. The word is **however** in "However, not everyone agreed with it."
2. It follows "Many staff members supported the new policy," and the two lines go against each other.
3. So *however* shows contrast.

**Check:** Put *therefore* in its place: "Many supported the policy. Therefore, not everyone agreed." That makes no sense, so contrast fits.

### #3: Three Linked Sentences

Arrange the sentences in the best order.

(A) For example, the clinic's evening hours let workers visit after their shifts.

(B) Clinics can adjust their schedules to fit patients' needs.

(C) Therefore, these workers can still get care.

**Solution:**

1. (B) states a general idea and does not look back, so it opens.

:::try
Finish the last step: put all three in order.
:::answer
The answer is B, A, C. *For example* in (A) introduces a case, so it follows the idea in (B). *These workers* in (C) points to the workers in (A), and *therefore* shows a result of the evening hours: "Clinics can adjust their schedules to fit patients' needs. For example, the clinic's evening hours let workers visit after their shifts. Therefore, these workers can still get care."

**Check:** Read the order aloud. Idea, example, then the result of that example.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A sentence that starts with *for example*: put the general idea first (idea, then example). {#example-before-idea}
- A sentence that starts with *however*: put the claim it goes against first (claim, then contrast). {#contrast-before-claim}
- A transition word alone: it shows a link, not a fixed place, so read the two sentences it joins (meaning sets the place). {#where-a-transition-goes}
:::
