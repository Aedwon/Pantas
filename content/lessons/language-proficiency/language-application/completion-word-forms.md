## Core Concept

### The Job of the Blank

A **subject** is the person or thing the sentence says something about. A **verb** expresses an action or a state. In *The clerk works* and *The clerk is helpful*, *the clerk* is the subject. *Works* and *is* are the verbs. A verb may have several words, as in *was working* or *had finished*.

**Word form** means the kind of word and the shape it takes. A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea. An **adjective** describes a noun. An **adverb** often describes a verb or adjective. Use *a clear answer* but *answer clearly*.

Let's fill the blank in "The nurse spoke ______ to the patient." *(gentle / gently)*

1. *Spoke* is the verb. It is an action.
2. The blank tells how she spoke.
3. A word that describes a verb is an adverb.
4. Use *gently*.

The nurse spoke gently to the patient.

:::pair #wrong-job
Right: The mayor spoke calmly.
Wrong: The mayor spoke calm.
*Calm* describes a noun, and *spoke* is an action. An adverb tells how the mayor spoke.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "The report gave a ______ summary." *(brief / briefly / brevity)*
:::answer
*brief*. The blank describes the noun *summary*, so use an adjective: "The report gave a brief summary."
:::

### Matching the Verb to Its Subject

**Subject-verb agreement** means choosing a verb form that fits the subject. In the simple present, use *the clerk works* but *the clerks work*. With *be*, use *she is* and *they are*; in the past, use *she was* and *they were*.

Let's fill the blank in the two sentences below.

1. *The driver ______ the route each day.* One driver, so *checks*.
2. *The drivers ______ the route each day.* More than one driver, so *check*.

The driver checks the route each day. The drivers check the route each day.

:::pair #plural-subject-s
Right: The cooks taste the soup.
Wrong: The cooks tastes the soup.
*Cooks* means more than one person, so the verb takes no *-s*. Find the subject before choosing the verb.
:::

:::check
Choose the verb: "My cousins ______ in Davao." *(live / lives)*
:::answer
*live*. *Cousins* is plural, so the verb takes no *-s*: "My cousins live in Davao."
:::

### Choosing the Form After a Verb

Some verbs take a certain form after them. Use *want to go*, with *to* plus the base verb. Verbs about a plan or a choice, such as *hope*, usually work the same way. The **base form** is the plain form, such as *go*, *leave*, or *talk*.

Use *enjoy reading*, with an *-ing* form. Verbs about liking, avoiding or completing an action, such as *enjoy* and *avoid*, usually work this way. Learn each verb together with its pattern. A pattern that fits *want* may not fit *enjoy*.

Let's fill the blanks in "Mina wants ______ early, but she enjoys ______ with her coworkers." *(leave, talk)*

1. *Wants* takes *to* plus the base verb: *to leave*.
2. *Enjoys* takes an *-ing* form: *talking*.

Mina wants to leave early, but she enjoys talking with her coworkers.

:::pair #verb-pattern
Right: The interns want to join the team.
Wrong: The interns want joining the team.
*Want* meaning wish to do something takes *to* plus the base verb, not an *-ing* form.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "The committee plans ______ a new rule next month." *(to adopt / adopting)*
:::answer
*to adopt*. *Plan* is a verb about a plan, so it takes *to* plus the base verb: "The committee plans to adopt a new rule next month."
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Proposal

Choose the best completion: "Yesterday, the committee ______ the proposal after months of review."

(A) approve

(B) approving

(C) approved

(D) approval

**Solution:**

1. The sentence needs a verb to tell what *the committee* did.
2. *Yesterday* places the completed decision in the past.

Choose **(C) approved**: "Yesterday, the committee approved the proposal after months of review."

**Check:** Read it whole: *approved* matches *yesterday*, while *approve* is present and *approval* and *approving* cannot be the main verb.

### #2: A Supply Cabinet

Choose the best completion: "Each evening, the nurses ______ the supply cabinet."

(A) checks

(B) check

(C) to check

(D) has checked

**Solution:**

1. The subject *nurses* means more than one nurse.
2. *Each evening* describes a routine, so use the simple present.

Choose **(B)**: "Each evening, the nurses check the supply cabinet."

**Check:** Read it whole: "Each evening, the nurses check the supply cabinet." *Check* fits the plural *nurses*, and *has checked* does not fit the routine.

### #3: A Kitchen Plan

Choose the correct pair: "The cooks hope ______ the soup early, and they finish ______ the rice by noon."

(A) serving, to prepare

(B) to serve, to prepare

(C) serve, prepare

(D) to serve, preparing

**Solution:**

1. *Hope* is the first verb. It takes *to* plus the base verb: *to serve*.
2. *Finish* is the second verb. Find the form it takes.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice gives the form that follows *finish*?
:::answer
The answer is **(D) to serve, preparing**: "The cooks hope to serve the soup early, and they finish preparing the rice by noon." *Finish* is about completing an action, like *enjoy* and *avoid*, so it takes an *-ing* form, *preparing*.

**Check:** Read the wrong form in full: "The cooks hope to serve the soup early, and they finish to prepare the rice by noon" sounds wrong, so (D) stands.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A blank that describes a verb: use an adverb (*spoke calmly*), even when a noun or adjective has the right idea. {#wrong-job}
- A plural subject in the present: use the plain verb, with no *-s* (*the cooks taste*; with *be*, *are*). {#plural-subject-s}
- A verb followed by another verb: use the form that first verb takes (*want to join*). {#verb-pattern}
:::
