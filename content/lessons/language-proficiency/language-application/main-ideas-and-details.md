## Core Concept

### Finding the Main Idea

The **main idea** is the main point the text makes about its topic. Choose a main idea that covers the whole text and adds no claim the text does not make.

Notice the kind of text. A story may follow events, instructions may follow steps, and an opinion may give a claim with reasons. An explanation may show how or why something happens.

Read: "Many schools now open their libraries on Saturdays. Students use the quiet room to finish projects. Parents can borrow books for younger children. Some libraries also offer free tutoring."

What is the main idea?

1. The details are "finish projects," "borrow books for younger children" and "free tutoring."
2. Each detail is a way the Saturday opening helps people.
3. One point covers all three: Saturday library hours help students and families.

:::pair #widening-the-claim
Right: From "Rain sends shoppers home. A covered stall would keep some of them shopping," the main idea is to add a covered stall.
Wrong: Replace all the stalls with a covered building.
The wrong line turns a small addition into a full replacement. The main idea must stay as big as the text, no bigger.
:::

:::check
Read: "Fresh fish spoils quickly in the heat. A cold box at the dock keeps it fresh for days. Sellers who use one throw away less fish." What is the main idea?
:::answer
The answer is that cold boxes help sellers keep fish fresh. All three lines are about keeping fish fresh or wasting less of it.
:::

### Choosing Supporting Details

A **detail** supports the main idea when the text states it and it shows or explains that idea. A sentence can sound true and still not be in this text.

Read: "The town hall made its parking lot safer. It painted the walking paths yellow, added a light above the gate, and put up a sign that reads 'Slow down.'"

Which detail supports the main point: "Walking paths are painted yellow" or "Parking is now free"?

1. The main point is in the first line: "made its parking lot safer."
2. "Painted the walking paths yellow" is stated, and it is a safety change.
3. "Parking is now free" appears nowhere in the text.
4. The supporting detail is that the walking paths are painted yellow.

:::pair #claim-not-in-text
Right: "Put up a sign that reads 'Slow down.'"
Wrong: No accident has happened in the lot since.
It sounds like safety, but the text never says it. A supporting detail must be a line the text states.
:::

:::check
Read: "The bakery changed its counter so lines move faster. It opens a second register at noon and sets a basket by the door for pre-paid orders." Which detail supports the main point: "A second register opens at noon" or "Its bread costs less"?
:::answer
The answer is "A second register opens at noon." The text states it, and a second register helps lines move. Nothing in the text says the bread costs less.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Main Idea in an Explanation

Read: "Many offices use digital forms. These forms reduce repeated handwriting and make records easier to store. They can also be sent quickly between departments."

What is the main idea?

(A) Handwriting is always slow.
(B) Digital forms can make office work easier.
(C) Every department uses the same form.
(D) Paper records should never be used.

**Solution:**

1. The details are "reduce repeated handwriting," "easier to store" and "sent quickly between departments."
2. All three are benefits of digital forms.
3. (B) covers all three benefits. (A), (C) and (D) each add a claim no line makes.

**Check:** Say the point in your own words: digital forms help. That matches (B).

### #2: Main Point in an Opinion

Read: "The town should add benches along the riverside path. Many older walkers need to rest every few minutes. Benches would also give families a place to stop and watch the water."

What does the writer want, and why?

**Solution:**

1. The first line states the claim: "The town should add benches along the riverside path."
2. The next lines give reasons: "Many older walkers need to rest every few minutes" and "a place to stop and watch the water."
3. The writer wants benches added, because older walkers need rests and families need a place to stop.

**Check:** Ask "why benches?" after the claim. Both reasons answer it.

### #3: Main Idea in a Description

Read: "Many people think night markets only sell food. In fact, one market near the pier also sells handmade bags, lamps and toys. Visitors can watch the makers at work."

What is the main idea?

(A) Night markets never sell food.
(B) One pier market sells many kinds of goods and lets visitors see the makers.
(C) All night markets sell handmade goods.
(D) The pier market is the oldest in town.

**Solution:**

1. The first detail is "also sells handmade bags, lamps and toys."
2. The second detail is "Visitors can watch the makers at work."

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice covers both details and adds no claim?
:::answer
The answer is (B). It names the goods and the makers. (A) and (C) claim more than the text says, and (D) is not mentioned.

**Check:** Test (B) against each detail. Both fit it. Each other choice has a part with no line to support it.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Ask what every detail points to. Then check that your answer is not bigger than the text.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A main-idea choice with "all," "every" or "replace": pick the one as large as the text, not larger (stay inside the text). {#widening-the-claim}
- A detail that sounds true: use it only if the text states it (the stated line). {#claim-not-in-text}
:::
