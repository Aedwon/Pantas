## Core Concept

### Finding the Main Idea

The **main idea** is the main point the text makes about its topic. Choose a main idea that covers the whole text and adds no claim the text does not make.

Read: "Many schools now open their libraries on Saturdays. Students use the quiet room to finish projects. Parents can borrow books for younger children. Some libraries also offer free tutoring."

What is the main idea?

1. The details are "finish projects," "borrow books for younger children" and "free tutoring."
2. Each detail is a way the Saturday opening helps people.
3. One point covers all three: Saturday library hours help students and families.

:::pair #widening-the-claim
Right: From "Rain sends shoppers home. A covered stall would keep some of them shopping," the main idea is to add a covered stall.
Wrong: Replace all the stalls with a covered building.
The wrong line turns a small addition into a full replacement. The main idea must stay as big as the text, no bigger.
:::

:::check
Read: "Fresh fish spoils quickly in the heat. A cold box at the dock keeps it fresh for days. Sellers who use one throw away less fish." What is the main idea?
:::answer
The answer is that cold boxes help sellers keep fish fresh. All three lines are about keeping fish fresh or wasting less of it.
:::

### Choosing Supporting Details

A **detail** supports the main idea when the text states it and it shows or explains that idea. A sentence can sound true and still not be in this text.

Read: "The town hall made its parking lot safer. It painted the walking paths yellow, added a light above the gate, and put up a sign that reads 'Slow down.'"

Which detail supports the main point: "Walking paths are painted yellow" or "Parking is now free"?

1. The main point is in the first line: "made its parking lot safer."
2. "Parking is now free" appears nowhere in the text.
3. "Painted the walking paths yellow" is stated and is a safety change, so it is the supporting detail.

:::pair #claim-not-in-text
Right: "Put up a sign that reads 'Slow down.'"
Wrong: No accident has happened in the lot since.
It sounds like safety, but the text never says it. A supporting detail must be a line the text states.
:::

:::check
Read: "The corner bakery cut its waste this year. It bakes smaller batches in the afternoon and gives day-old bread to a shelter. The shop opens at six." Which detail supports the main point: "It gives day-old bread to a shelter" or "The shop opens at six"?
:::answer
The answer is "It gives day-old bread to a shelter." The text states it, and it cuts waste. "The shop opens at six" is also stated, but it says nothing about waste.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Main Idea in an Explanation

Read: "Many offices use digital forms. These forms reduce repeated handwriting and make records easier to store. They can also be sent quickly between departments."

What is the main idea?

(A) Many offices use digital forms.

(B) Digital forms can make office work easier.

(C) Digital forms can be sent quickly between departments.

(D) Digital forms reduce repeated handwriting.

**Solution:**

1. The details are "reduce repeated handwriting," "easier to store" and "sent quickly between departments."
2. All three are benefits of digital forms.
3. The main idea is (B): digital forms can make office work easier. (A) names only the topic, and (C) and (D) each give one benefit.

**Check:** Test (C) and (D) against the other details. Each leaves out two of the three benefits, and only (B) covers all of them.

### #2: Main Point in a Story

Read: "Mario's bike chain kept slipping on the hill. He cleaned the chain, added oil, and tightened the bolts. Now the bike climbs smoothly."

What is the main idea?

**Solution:**

1. The first line states the problem: the chain kept slipping.
2. The next line gives the fixes: "cleaned the chain," "added oil" and "tightened the bolts."
3. The last line gives the result: the bike climbs smoothly.
4. Mario fixed the slipping chain, and the bike now climbs smoothly.

**Check:** Each fix answers the problem in the first line.

### #3: Main Idea in a Description

Read: "The old ferry terminal is busy again. A bakery and a bike shop opened inside it this year, and a craft fair fills the hall on Saturdays. The terminal clock still shows the wrong time."

What is the main idea?

(A) A bakery opened in the old ferry terminal this year.

(B) New shops and a weekly fair have brought life back to the old ferry terminal.

(C) The terminal clock still shows the wrong time.

(D) A craft fair fills the old ferry terminal hall on Saturdays.

**Solution:**

1. The first details are the bakery and the bike shop.
2. The second detail is the Saturday craft fair.

:::try
Finish the last step: which choice covers the details that develop the main point?
:::answer
The answer is (B): new shops and a weekly fair have brought life back to the old ferry terminal. (A) and (D) each give one detail, and (C), the clock, supports neither.

**Check:** Test (B) against the shops and the fair. Both are new life in the terminal, so both fit. The wrong clock shows no new life, so it is off the point.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Ask what every detail points to. Then check that your answer is not bigger than the text.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A main-idea choice with "all," "every" or "replace": pick the one as large as the text, not larger, unless the text says so. {#widening-the-claim}
- A detail that sounds true: use it only if the text states it. {#claim-not-in-text}
:::
