## Core Concept

### Phrases, Clauses, and Punctuation

A **phrase** is a word group without its own subject-and-verb pair, such as *after the meeting*. A **clause** has a subject and a verb. An **independent clause** can stand alone; a **dependent clause** cannot: *because the meeting ended* leaves the thought unfinished.

A **simple sentence** has one independent clause. A **compound sentence** has at least two independent clauses and no dependent clause.

A **complex sentence** has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. A **compound-complex sentence** has at least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause.

## Examples

### #1: Sentence Types

Classify each sentence as simple, compound, complex, or compound-complex. Here, **classify** means name the sentence type.

(A) The guard opened the gate.

(B) The guard opened the gate, and the visitors entered.

(C) When the guard opened the gate, the visitors entered.

(D) When the guard opened the gate, the visitors entered, and the clerk greeted them.

**Solution:**

1. (A) is **simple**. It has one independent clause.
2. (B) is **compound**. Each side of *and* can stand alone.
3. (C) is **complex**. The *when* clause depends on *the visitors entered* to complete the thought.
4. (D) is **compound-complex**. It has the dependent *when* clause and two independent clauses: *the visitors entered* and *the clerk greeted them*.
