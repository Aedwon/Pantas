## Core Concept

### Using Stated Details

A **detail** is something the text says directly. To answer a detail question, find the line that states the answer and use that line, not what you expect.

Read: "The library closes early on Thursday for a staff meeting. Books returned after 3 p.m. will be counted on Friday. The book drop stays open all day."

On which day is a book returned at 4 p.m. on Thursday counted?

1. The line that fits is "Books returned after 3 p.m. will be counted on Friday."
2. 4 p.m. is after 3 p.m., so the book is in that group.
3. The line gives the day: Friday.

:::pair #wrong-day
Right: "The pool is closed on Saturday. Lockers will be emptied when it reopens on Sunday." The lockers are emptied on Sunday.
Wrong: The lockers are emptied on Saturday.
Saturday is in the text, but it belongs to the closing. The line about the lockers names the day.
:::

:::check
Read: "The clinic sees walk-in patients until 4 p.m. Patients with appointments are seen until 5 p.m." A patient without an appointment arrives at 4:30 p.m. Will the clinic see the patient?
:::answer
The answer is no. The line says walk-in patients are seen until 4 p.m., and 4:30 is later.
:::

### Supported Inferences

To **infer** means to reach an idea from clues in the text. An inference need not repeat a sentence, but the text must support it. A possible guess with no clue is not enough.

Read: "Mia packed a towel, goggles and a swim cap into her bag. When she reached the pool, her coach was waiting."

Why is Mia going to the pool?

1. The first clue is "a towel, goggles and a swim cap." People use these for swimming.
2. The second clue is "her coach was waiting." A coach fits a lesson or practice.
3. The clues support one idea: Mia is going to swim practice.

:::why
Two clues that point the same way support an idea. One clue that fits many ideas supports none of them.
:::

:::pair #adding-a-guess
Right: From "The clerk compared the label with the log, then handed the box to the courier": she checked the delivery details.
Wrong: She found a mistake in the log.
The text never says she found a mistake. Checking something does not mean finding a problem.
:::

:::check
Read: "Rita hung her dripping coat on the hook and shook out her umbrella. Drops ran down the window behind her." What can be inferred about the weather?
:::answer
The answer is that it is raining. The dripping coat, the shaken umbrella and the drops on the window all point to rain.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Important Detail in a Notice

Read: "The records desk will close at noon on Friday for equipment repairs. Requests received after noon will be processed on Monday. Online submission will remain open."

When will a request received on Friday afternoon be processed?

**Solution:**

1. The line that fits is "Requests received after noon will be processed on Monday."
2. Friday afternoon is after noon, so the request is in that group.
3. The line gives the day: Monday.

**Check:** "Online submission will remain open" says where requests can be sent. It does not say when they are processed.

### #2: Nina at the Office

Read: "Nina reached the office before it opened. She held a folder of forms and checked the posted list of required documents. When the guard opened the door, she joined the application line."

What can be inferred about Nina's purpose?

**Solution:**

1. The clues are "She held a folder of forms" and "checked the posted list of required documents."
2. "She joined the application line" shows what the visit is for.
3. The clues support one idea: Nina came to submit an application.

**Check:** The story never says whether she is approved. "She will be approved" is a guess, not an inference.

### #3: A Sign at an Entrance

Read: "Because the east entrance was blocked, the post office put up a sign that sent visitors to the side door. By 9 a.m., the line at the side door reached the corner."

Which statement is best supported?

(A) Many people used the side door that morning.
(B) The side door is wider than the east entrance.
(C) The post office will close early today.
(D) The east entrance will reopen at noon.

**Solution:**

1. The first clue is "put up a sign that sent visitors to the side door."
2. The second clue is "the line at the side door reached the corner."

:::try
Finish the last step: test each choice against these two clues. Which choice do they support?
:::answer
The answer is (A). A line that reaches the corner shows many people used the side door. No line mentions the width of the door, closing early, or noon.

**Check:** Cover the choices and say what the clues show: many people used the side door. That matches (A).
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Return to the text for every answer. Keep apart what the text states and what its clues support.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A question about a day, time or number: use the line that names it, not the nearest day in the text (the day in that line). {#wrong-day}
- An answer that adds a reason, result or fact: drop it unless a line supports it (no guess). {#adding-a-guess}
:::
