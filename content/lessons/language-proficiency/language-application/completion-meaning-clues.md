## Core Concept

### Signal Words

In **sentence completion**, you choose words for a blank. *Choose the best completion* means pick the choice that fits the blank in meaning and grammar. Read the whole sentence before looking at the choices.

Words such as *although, despite,* and *however* show contrast. *Because* introduces a reason. *Therefore* introduces a result, while *for example* introduces a case that explains a broader idea.

Do not choose from a signal word alone. Read the ideas on both sides of it. They must make sense together.

Let's fill the blank in "Although the clinic was crowded, the nurse stayed ______ with every patient."

1. *Although* shows contrast.
2. The first idea is a crowded, stressful clinic.
3. The second idea must go against it. Predict a word such as *calm*.
4. Check *calm* against the whole sentence.

Although the clinic was crowded, the nurse stayed calm with every patient.

:::pair #synonym-after-far-from
Right: Far from being anxious, the cashier stayed calm and polite.
Wrong: Far from being relaxed, the cashier stayed calm and polite.
*Far from being* denies the blank. The blank needs the opposite of *calm*, and *relaxed* only repeats it.
:::

:::check
Choose the best word: "Because the road was slick with ice, drivers moved ______." *(quickly / carefully)*
:::answer
*carefully*. *Because* gives the reason: ice makes a road dangerous, so drivers take care.
:::

### Check Every Blank

For two or more blanks, solve the blank with the clearest clue first. Then read the whole sentence with every blank filled.

Let's fill both blanks in "Because the room was ______, we opened the windows and felt ______ afterward."

1. *Because* gives a reason. Opening the windows is the result.
2. The clearest clue is the windows. The room was *stuffy*.
3. Fresh air in a stuffy room leaves people *relieved*.
4. Read both words in the sentence.

Because the room was stuffy, we opened the windows and felt relieved afterward.

:::pair #one-blank-only
Right: The manager's bold plan to scrap every old rule met widespread opposition.
Wrong: The manager's ordinary plan to scrap every old rule met widespread opposition.
*Opposition* fits, but scrapping every old rule is not ordinary. One good word does not make the pair correct.
:::

:::check
Choose the best pair: "Although the new clerk felt ______ on the first day, weeks of practice left her ______ by the end." *(uneasy, comfortable / comfortable, uneasy / uneasy, hesitant)*
:::answer
*uneasy, comfortable*. *Although* sets a contrast, and practice brings comfort. A clerk who starts uneasy becomes comfortable.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Forecast and an Event

Choose the best completion: "Although the forecast predicted heavy rain, the outdoor event was surprisingly ______."

(A) postponed

(B) successful

(C) canceled

(D) rescheduled

**Solution:**

1. *Although* links the bad forecast to an unexpected outcome.
2. The word must go against the forecast, so predict something good.

Choose **(B) successful**: "Although the forecast predicted heavy rain, the outdoor event was surprisingly successful."

**Check:** Test (C) *canceled*. A canceled event after a bad forecast is no surprise, so *surprisingly* does not fit.

### #2: A Damaged Bridge

Choose the best completion: "Because the bridge was severely damaged, engineers deemed it ______ for public use."

(A) suitable

(B) essential

(C) unsafe

(D) convenient

**Solution:**

1. *Because* gives the reason: the bridge was severely damaged.
2. The engineers' judgment must follow from that damage.

Choose **(C) unsafe**: "Because the bridge was severely damaged, engineers deemed it unsafe for public use."

**Check:** Test (A) *suitable*. Damage does not lead engineers to call a bridge suitable.

### #3: Reusing Supplies

Choose the best completion: "Some office supplies can be reused. ______, a folder in good condition can hold a new set of papers."

(A) For example

(B) In contrast

(C) Nevertheless

(D) Instead

**Solution:**

1. The first sentence states a general idea: some supplies can be reused.
2. The second sentence names one case of that idea: a folder holding new papers.

Choose **(A) For example**: "Some office supplies can be reused. For example, a folder in good condition can hold a new set of papers."

**Check:** *In contrast* and *Instead* would set the folder against the first sentence. Here the folder supports it.

### #4: A Presentation

Choose the best pair: "Despite her ______ preparation, she felt ______ before the presentation."

(A) thorough, relaxed

(B) thorough, nervous

(C) careless, nervous

(D) rushed, unprepared

**Solution:**

1. *Despite* calls for contrast.

:::try
Finish the last step: choose the pair.
:::answer
The answer is **(B) thorough, nervous**: "Despite her thorough preparation, she felt nervous before the presentation." Thorough preparation suggests readiness, and feeling nervous goes against that.

**Check:** Test (A). "Despite her thorough preparation, she felt relaxed" has no contrast, so *despite* does not fit.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Predict the meaning and word form before reading the choices. This helps you set aside a word that sounds good but does not fit.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- *Far from*, *although* or *despite* before a blank: choose a word that usually goes against the other idea (an opposite, not a synonym). {#synonym-after-far-from}
- Two or more blanks: fill every blank, then read the whole sentence. {#one-blank-only}
- A choice that sounds grammatical: still test it against the signal word and the ideas it connects. {#signal-words}
:::
