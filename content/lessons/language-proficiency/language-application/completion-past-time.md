## Core Concept

### Time Clues

Check time as well as meaning. Simple past describes a finished past action: *She sent it yesterday*.

Let's fill the blank in "Yesterday, Ana ______ (walk) to the bank."

1. *Yesterday* places the action in the past.
2. The walk is finished.
3. Use the simple past of *walk*: *walked*.

Yesterday, Ana walked to the bank.

:::pair #present-for-past
Right: Last Friday, the cashier paid the supplier.
Wrong: Last Friday, the cashier pays the supplier.
*Last Friday* puts the action in the past. A present form does not match it.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "The receptionist signed for the package that the driver ______ to the office an hour ago." *(delivers / delivered)*
:::answer
*delivered*. *An hour ago* is a finished time in the past, so use the simple past: "The receptionist signed for the package that the driver delivered to the office an hour ago."
:::

### Actions Around Another Event

Past progressive uses *was* or *were* plus an *-ing* form for an ongoing past action: *She was writing when I called*. The action was in progress when something else happened.

Let's fill the blank in "When the bell rang, the students ______ (take) a test and had not finished it."

1. The bell rang at one moment in the past.
2. The test was already going on at that moment, and it was not finished.
3. Use *were* plus the *-ing* form: *were taking*.

When the bell rang, the students were taking a test and had not finished it.

:::pair #had-for-unfinished
Right: When the manager walked in, the clerk was counting the cash. (still counting)
Wrong: When the manager walked in, the clerk had counted the cash. (still counting)
*Had counted* says the counting was already finished.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "While the technician ______ the printer, the lights went out and he had to stop halfway." *(repaired / was repairing)*
:::answer
*was repairing*. The repair was still going on when the lights went out: "While the technician was repairing the printer, the lights went out and he had to stop halfway."
:::

### Two Events in the Past

Past perfect uses *had* plus a **past participle** to make an earlier past action clear. A past participle is the form used after *have, has,* or *had*. Regular examples end in *-ed*, as in *had finished*; irregular examples include *had written* and *had gone*.

Let's fill the blank in "By the time the guests arrived, the cook ______ (prepare) the soup."

1. Two past events happened: the guests arrived, and the cook prepared the soup.
2. The soup came first.
3. Use *had* plus the past participle: *had prepared*.

By the time the guests arrived, the cook had prepared the soup.

Do not add *had* to every past verb. For one finished event, simple past is often enough.

:::check
Choose the form: "Rico handed out his slides at once because he ______ them before the meeting began." *(had printed / was printing / will print)*
:::answer
*had printed*. He handed the slides out at once, so the printing was finished before the meeting began. Use *had* plus the participle: "Rico handed out his slides at once because he had printed them before the meeting began."
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Form at the Desk

Choose the best forms: "By the time the desk opened, I ______ the form. When the clerk called my name, I ______ the instructions and stopped halfway through."

For the first blank: (had finished / had finishing / have finished / am finishing).

For the second blank: (had read / was reading / will read / read tomorrow).

**Solution:**

1. Finishing happened before the desk opened. Use **had finished**, which is *had* plus the participle *finished*.
2. The reading was still in progress when the clerk called. Use **was reading**.

"By the time the desk opened, I had finished the form. When the clerk called my name, I was reading the instructions and stopped halfway through."

**Check:** Read the timeline: I finish the form, the desk opens, then the clerk calls while I am halfway through the instructions. *Had finished* is the earliest event, and *was reading* is still going on at the call.

### #2: A Repair

Choose the best completion: "Last week, the technician ______ the printer and told us it was working again."

(A) fixes

(B) fixed

(C) had fix

(D) fixing

**Solution:**

1. *Last week* places the action in the past, and *told* is also past.
2. The repair is a finished action, so use the simple past.

Choose **(B)**: "Last week, the technician fixed the printer and told us it was working again."

**Check:** Read it whole: "the technician fixed the printer and told us it was working again." *Fixed* and *told* are both past, so the verbs match.

### #3: A Night Cleaner

Choose the best completion: "When the guard arrived at six, the cleaner ______ the hallway. She finished it at seven."

(A) was mopping

(B) had mopped

(C) mops

(D) will mop

**Solution:**

1. The guard arrived at one moment in the past.
2. Compare that moment with the time she finished.

:::try
Finish the last step: choose the form.
:::answer
The answer is **(A) was mopping**: "When the guard arrived at six, the cleaner was mopping the hallway. She finished it at seven." The mopping was still going on at six.

**Check:** *Had mopped* would put the finish before six, but she finished at seven.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Find the time clue first, then ask: finished or still going on?

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A time word such as *Last Friday*: use a past form, not a present one (*paid*). {#present-for-past}
- An action still in progress when another one happened: use *was* or *were* plus *-ing*, not *had* plus a participle (*was counting*). {#had-for-unfinished}
- A sentence with two past events: read the whole timeline (finished, ongoing, earlier) before choosing a form. {#two-events-in-the-past}
:::
