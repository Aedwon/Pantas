## Core Concept

### A Finished Past Action

Check time as well as meaning. Simple past describes a finished past action: *She sent it yesterday*. A time clue such as *yesterday* or *last week* places the action in the past. *Choose the best completion* means pick the choice that fits the blank in meaning and grammar.

Let's fill the blank in "Yesterday, Ana ______ (walk) to the bank."

1. *Yesterday* places the action in the past.
2. The walk is finished.
3. Use the simple past of *walk*: *walked*.

Yesterday, Ana walked to the bank.

:::pair #present-for-past
Right: Last Friday, the cashier paid the supplier.
Wrong: Last Friday, the cashier pays the supplier.
*Last Friday* puts the action in the past. A present form does not match it.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "On Monday, the driver ______ the package to the office." *(delivers / delivered)*
:::answer
*delivered*. *On Monday* is a finished time in the past, so use the simple past.
:::

### An Ongoing Past Action

Past progressive uses *was* or *were* plus an *-ing* form for an ongoing past action: *She was writing when I called*. The action was in progress when something else happened.

Let's fill the blank in "When the bell rang, the students ______ (take) a test."

1. The bell rang at one moment in the past.
2. The test was already going on at that moment.
3. Use *were* plus the *-ing* form: *were taking*.

When the bell rang, the students were taking a test.

:::pair #had-for-unfinished
Right: When the manager walked in, the clerk was counting the cash.
Wrong: When the manager walked in, the clerk had counted the cash.
*Had counted* says the counting was already finished. The clerk was still in the middle of it.
:::

:::check
Choose the form: "While the technician ______ the printer, the lights went out." *(repaired / was repairing)*
:::answer
*was repairing*. The repair was still going on when the lights went out.
:::

### An Earlier Past Action

Past perfect uses *had* plus a **past participle** to make an earlier past action clear. A past participle is the form used after *have, has,* or *had*. Regular examples end in *-ed*, as in *had finished*; irregular examples include *had written* and *had gone*.

Let's fill the blank in "By the time the guests arrived, the cook ______ (prepare) the soup."

1. Two past events happened: the guests arrived, and the cook prepared the soup.
2. The soup came first.
3. Use *had* plus the past participle: *had prepared*.

By the time the guests arrived, the cook had prepared the soup.

Do not add *had* to every past verb. If you only report a finished event, simple past may be enough.

:::check
Choose the form: "By the time the meeting began, Rico ______ the report to everyone." *(had sent / sends / will send)*
:::answer
*had sent*. Sending happened before the meeting began, so use *had* plus the participle.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Earlier Past and Ongoing Past

Choose the best forms: "By the time the desk opened, I ______ the form. When the clerk called my name, I ______ the instructions and stopped halfway through."

For the first blank: (had finished / had finishing / have finished / am finishing).

For the second blank: (had read / was reading / will read / read tomorrow).

**Solution:**

1. Finishing happened before the desk opened. Use **had finished**, which is *had* plus the participle *finished*.
2. The reading was still in progress when the clerk called. Use **was reading**.

"By the time the desk opened, I had finished the form. When the clerk called my name, I was reading the instructions and stopped halfway through."

**Check:** Read the timeline: finish the form, then the desk opens, then the clerk calls. Each form fits its place. Simple past is not offered for the first blank here.

### #2: A Repair

Choose the best completion: "Last week, the technician ______ the printer and told us it was working again."

(A) fixes

(B) fixed

(C) had fix

(D) fixing

**Solution:**

1. *Last week* places the action in the past, and *told* is also past.
2. The repair is a finished action, so use the simple past.

Choose **(B)**: "Last week, the technician fixed the printer and told us it was working again."

**Check:** *Fixes* is present, *had fix* has no participle, and *fixing* has no helping verb. Only *fixed* fits.

### #3: A Power Cut

Choose the best completion: "When the storm cut the power, the clerk ______ the day's sales and had entered only half of them."

(A) was recording

(B) had recorded

(C) records

(D) will record

**Solution:**

1. Two past events: the power was cut, and the clerk recorded the sales.
2. She had entered only half of them, so the recording was not finished.

:::try
Finish the last step: choose the form for an action that was in progress.
:::answer
The answer is **(A) was recording**: "When the storm cut the power, the clerk was recording the day's sales and had entered only half of them."

**Check:** *Had recorded* would mean the work was done, and "only half" says it was not.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Use the strongest clue first. A verb pattern, a reference to a known object, or a clear time clue can narrow the choices.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A time word such as *Last Friday*: use a past form, not a present one (*paid*). {#present-for-past}
- An action still in progress when another one happened: use *was* or *were* plus *-ing*, not *had* plus a participle (*was counting*). {#had-for-unfinished}
- One time word: read the whole timeline (finished, ongoing, earlier) before choosing a form. {#an-earlier-past-action}
:::
