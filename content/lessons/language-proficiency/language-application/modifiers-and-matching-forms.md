## Core Concept

### Placing Modifiers Near Their Target

A **modifier** is a word or phrase that describes another word. Place it right beside the word it describes so the meaning stays clear.

When a sentence opens with an *-ing* or *after*-action phrase, the noun right after the comma must be the person or thing doing that action. Put limiting words like *only* right beside the word they limit (*to Paolo only*, not *only Lina*, when only the receiver is limited). Place a *that*-clause directly after the noun it describes.

:::pair #dangling-opening
Right: "After inspecting the roof, the engineer approved repairs."
Wrong: "After inspecting the roof, repairs were approved by the engineer."
Repairs cannot inspect a roof. Name the person who did the inspecting right after the comma.
:::

:::check
Which sentence means Lina sent the draft to Paolo and to no one else: "Only Lina sent the draft to Paolo" or "Lina sent the draft to Paolo only"?
:::answer
The answer is **"Lina sent the draft to Paolo only."** Placing *only* beside *Paolo* limits who received the draft.
:::

### Parallel Lists and Comparisons

**Parallel structure** means items doing the same job share the same grammar form. Match base verbs with base verbs (*submit, update, and review*) and *-ing* forms with *-ing* forms (*reading and watching*).

Comparisons must also compare the same kind of thing. Compare a rating to another rating (*higher than those of the older models*), never a rating to the physical models themselves.

:::why
Why do exams reject "The rating of the new panel is higher than the older models"? A number (rating) cannot be higher than a piece of hardware (models). Adding *that of* or *those of* keeps both sides equal in kind.
:::

:::pair #parallel-list
Right: "The committee asked that heads submit reports, update logs, and review needs."
Wrong: Accept "The committee asked that heads submit reports, update logs, and to review needs" as No error.
All three requested actions follow *that heads* and need bare base verbs. Adding *to* on the last verb breaks the list.
:::

:::pair #like-comparison
Right: "The efficiency rating of the new panel is higher than those of the older models."
Wrong: Accept "The efficiency rating of the new panel is higher than the older models" as No error.
Compare a rating with ratings (*those of the older models*), not with the physical models.
:::

:::check
Choose the matching comparison: "The battery life of this phone is longer than *(last year's model / that of last year's model)*."
:::answer
The answer is **That of last year's model**. *That of* stands for *the battery life of*, so battery life compares with battery life.
:::

### Word Form, Word Choice, and Concise Wording

For **word form**, check the blank's job. Use an adjective before a noun (*a clear rule*) and an adverb to describe a verb (*explain clearly*).

Watch close pairs and repeated words too. *Affect* is usually a verb meaning influence an existing thing (*adversely affect performance*), while *effect* is usually a noun meaning result (as a verb, *effect* means cause or bring about). Also drop needless repeated words: *revert* already means go back, so cut *back* from *revert back*.

:::check
Choose the word that means influence: "Cold weather can *(affect / effect)* battery speed."
:::answer
The answer is **Affect**. It means influence how the existing battery performs.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Moving a Misplaced Description

The generator, not the wrench, stopped working. Move the misplaced part: "The technician repaired the generator using a wrench that had stopped working during the storm."

**Solution:**

1. Find the clause describing what failed: *that had stopped working during the storm*.
2. As written, it sits next to *wrench*, so it sounds like the wrench stopped working.
3. Move it right beside *generator*: **"The technician repaired the generator that had stopped working during the storm, using a wrench."**

**Check:** Ask "What sits right before *that had stopped working*?" Now *generator* is directly before it.

### #2: Parallel List and Word Choice

Correct two errors: "The team must finalize the budget, inspect the hall, and to check the lights, or delays will effect the event."

**Solution:**

1. The three verbs after *must* are *finalize*, *inspect*, and *to check*. Drop *to* so all three are bare base verbs: **check**.
2. The verb *will* needs a verb meaning influence the event: change *effect* to **affect**.

**Check:** Read the parallel list alone: "must finalize, inspect, and check." Read the result clause: "delays will affect the event." Both match form and meaning.

### #3: Fixing a Comparison and Redundancy

Revise this sentence so it compares like with like and removes repeated meaning: "The cost of the new printer is lower than the old printer, so the office will revert back to the older plan."

**Solution:**

1. The sentence compares *cost* to *the old printer* instead of to its cost. Change the comparison target to **that of the old printer**.
2. Check *revert back* for repeated meaning.

:::try
Finish the last step: how do you make *revert back* concise while keeping the same meaning?
:::answer
The answer is **Delete *back*** so the clause reads "the office will revert to the older plan." *Revert* already means return to a former state.

**Check:** Read the revised sentence: "The cost of the new printer is lower than that of the old printer, so the office will revert to the older plan." Cost compares with cost, and no meaning is lost without *back*.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Read lists side by side to spot mismatched forms, check the noun right after an opening comma, and ask whether both sides of *than* name the same kind of thing.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- An opening action phrase followed by a comma: put the person who did that action right after the comma. {#dangling-opening}
- A list of actions sharing one frame: give every item the exact same verb shape. {#parallel-list}
- A comparison with *than*: match a rating, cost, or size to *that of* or *those of*, not to a bare object. {#like-comparison}
:::
