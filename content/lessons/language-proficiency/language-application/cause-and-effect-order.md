## Core Concept

### Words That Point Back

A **cause** explains why something happens. An **effect** is the result. A word such as *as a result* or *consequently* points back to a cause, so it comes after the cause.

Let's order these two sentences:

- (A) Consequently, the shop closed for the day.
- (B) A fallen tree blocked the only road.

1. (A) starts with *Consequently*, which points back. It cannot come first.
2. (B) makes sense alone, so it goes first.

A fallen tree blocked the only road. Consequently, the shop closed for the day.

:::pair #resulting-before-cause
Right: The pipe froze overnight. The resulting leak soaked the floor.
Wrong: The resulting leak soaked the floor. The pipe froze overnight.
*The resulting leak* points back to a cause. Put first, it has nothing to point back to.
:::

:::check
Put these in order: (A) Because of this, the picnic moved indoors. (B) Dark clouds covered the sky by noon.
:::answer
The answer is **B → A**. *Because of this* points back to the clouds in (B), so (B) comes first: "Dark clouds covered the sky by noon. Because of this, the picnic moved indoors."
:::

### Phrases That Point Back

A writer can state the effect first and explain it after. A phrase such as *the reason for this* or *this was due to* points back to an effect. The effect comes first.

Let's order these two sentences:

- (A) The reason for this was a power failure at the station.
- (B) Several trains left late.

1. (A) starts with *The reason for this*, which points back to an effect. It cannot come first.
2. (B) is the effect and makes sense alone, so it goes first.

Several trains left late. The reason for this was a power failure at the station.

:::pair #cause-first-forced
Right: The match ended early. The reason for this was a flooded field.
Wrong: The reason for this was a flooded field. The match ended early.
*The reason for this* points back to the effect, so the effect must come first.
:::

:::check
Put these in order: (A) This delay was caused by a late delivery of supplies. (B) Work on the new library began two weeks late.
:::answer
The answer is **B → A**. *This delay* points back to the late start in (B), so (B) comes first: "Work on the new library began two weeks late. This delay was caused by a late delivery of supplies."
:::

## Examples

### #1: Rain and Streets

Which order makes the link clear?

(A) As a result, several streets were flooded.

(B) Heavy rain fell for hours without stopping.

**Solution:**

1. (A) starts with *As a result*, which points back. It cannot come first.
2. (B) gives the cause, the long rain.

Use **B → A**: "Heavy rain fell for hours without stopping. As a result, several streets were flooded."

**Check:** Read (A) first. *As a result* has nothing to point back to.

### #2: Canceled Classes

Which order makes the link clear?

(A) Several classes were canceled that morning.

(B) The reason for these cancellations was that floodwater had blocked the school road.

**Solution:**

1. (B) starts with *The reason for these cancellations*, which points back to (A).
2. (A) states the effect and stands alone.

Use **A → B**: "Several classes were canceled that morning. The reason for these cancellations was that floodwater had blocked the school road."

**Check:** *These cancellations* needs the cancellations to be named first, and (A) names them.

### #3: A Warehouse

Which order makes the link clear?

(A) As a result, the warehouse threw away most of its stock.

(B) Without it, the temperature inside the warehouse rose above the safe level.

(C) A refrigeration unit stopped working overnight.

**Solution:**

1. (A) starts with *As a result*, which points back. It cannot come first.

:::try
Put all three sentences in order.
:::answer
The answer is **C → B → A**. The unit stopped (C), so the temperature rose (B), so the stock was thrown away (A): "A refrigeration unit stopped working overnight. Without it, the temperature inside the warehouse rose above the safe level. As a result, the warehouse threw away most of its stock."

**Check:** Read C, B, A aloud. Each sentence is the reason for the next.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A sentence that starts with *As a result* or *Consequently*: put it after its cause (cause, then effect). {#resulting-before-cause}
- A sentence that starts with *The reason for this*: put the effect before it (effect, then cause). {#cause-first-forced}
:::
