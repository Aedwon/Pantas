## Core Concept

### Eye height

An observer's eyes are above the ground. The angle is measured from the eye, so the right triangle starts at eye level, not at the ground.

If the ground under the observer and the object's base are at the same height, use these rules. For an angle of elevation, add the rise above eye level to the eye height. For an angle of depression, subtract the drop below eye level from the eye height.

If you want the horizontal distance, do not add the eye height to it. Use the height above or below eye level in the ratio.

If the observer's eyes are at the same level as the object's base, no eye height adjustment is needed.

## Examples

### #1: Adding the Observer's Eye Height

A student whose eyes are $1.6$ meters above level ground stands $40$ meters from a flagpole. The angle of elevation of the top of the pole, measured from the student's eyes, is $35^\circ$. How tall is the flagpole? Give the answer to the nearest tenth of a meter.

The distance between the student and the pole is labeled 40 m. A line of sight from the student's eyes to the top of the pole makes a 35° angle of elevation. The pole height is labeled "Pole height = ?". -->

**Solution:**
The angle is measured from eye level. The triangle gives only the part of the pole above the student's eyes.

1. Let $h$ be the height above eye level. The horizontal distance is adjacent, so use tangent.

$$\tan35^\circ=\dfrac{h}{40}$$

2. Solve for $h$ with a calculator in degree mode.

$$h=40\tan35^\circ\approx40(0.7002)\approx28.0\text{ m}$$

3. Add the eye height to get the height above the ground.

$$28.0+1.6=29.6\text{ m}$$

4. The flagpole is about $29.6$ meters tall.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Forgetting the Observer's Height

> To find an object's height from an angle of elevation, add the eye height to the rise when both bases are at the same level.

If the object's base is at a different level from the ground under you, use the height of your eyes measured from the level of that base. Add the rise above eye level or subtract the drop below eye level.
