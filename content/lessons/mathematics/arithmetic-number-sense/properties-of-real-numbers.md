## Core Concept

**Real numbers** include whole numbers, fractions, decimals, and numbers such as $\sqrt{2}$. They can be shown as points on a number line. The letters below stand for real numbers.

### Commutative Property

The **commutative property** lets you swap the order of numbers in addition and multiplication. In a product, each number being multiplied is a **factor**. Writing $ab$ means $a\times b$.

$$a+b=b+a$$

$$ab=ba$$

### Associative Property

The **associative property** lets you change grouping in repeated addition or repeated multiplication. Parentheses show which part to do first. The order of the numbers stays the same.

$$(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$$

$$(ab)c=a(bc)$$

These two properties do not apply to subtraction or division in general.

### Distributive Property

The **distributive property** lets you multiply a sum or difference one term at a time. Multiply the outside factor by every term inside the parentheses.

$$a(b+c)=ab+ac$$

$$a(b-c)=ab-ac$$

You can also read this rule backward. A **common factor** is a factor that appears in each product. To **factor out** that factor, write it once outside parentheses and put the remaining parts inside.

$$ab+ac=a(b+c)$$

### Identity Property

An **identity** keeps a number unchanged under an operation. The additive identity is $0$ because adding $0$ leaves the same number.

$$a+0=a$$

The multiplicative identity is $1$ because multiplying by $1$ leaves the same number.

$$a\times1=a$$

### Inverse Property

An **additive inverse** is a number with the opposite sign. The additive inverse of $a$ is $-a$. Their sum is $0$.

$$a+(-a)=0$$

For $a\ne0$, the **multiplicative inverse** of $a$ is $\dfrac{1}{a}$. Their product is $1$. For a nonzero fraction, switch its numerator and denominator to find this inverse.

$$a\times\dfrac{1}{a}=1$$

Zero has no multiplicative inverse. Multiplying $0$ by any real number gives $0$, never $1$. Division by $0$ is undefined.

### Like Terms

A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number. An **exponent** tells how many times a factor is repeated: $x^2=x\times x$.

A **term** is a number, a variable, or a product of numbers and variables separated from other terms by addition or subtraction. Keep the sign with its term. In $8x-3x+5$, the terms are $8x$, $-3x$, and $5$.

**Like terms** have the same variable part with the same exponents. The number multiplying the variable part is the **coefficient**. A lone $x$ has coefficient $1$.

To combine like terms, add their signed coefficients and keep the variable part unchanged. This works because of reverse distribution.

$$8x-3x=(8-3)x=5x$$

The terms $8x$ and $3y$ are not like terms. Neither are $x$ and $x^2$. Plain numbers can be combined with other plain numbers.

In exam questions, **simplify** means write an equal expression in a simpler form. **Evaluate** means find the final number answer.

## Examples

### #1: Identifying the Basic Property

Which of the following illustrates the commutative property of multiplication?

- A. $3\times(4\times5)=(3\times4)\times5$
- B. $3\times4=4\times3$
- C. $3\times(4+5)=(3\times4)+(3\times5)$
- D. $3\times1=3$

**Solution:**

Choice B swaps the order of the factors without changing their product. The answer is B. Choice A changes grouping, C distributes, and D uses an identity.

### #2: Using Identity Properties

Simplify $(48+0)\times1+25$.

**Solution:**

1. Use the additive identity inside the parentheses.

$$48+0=48$$

2. Use the multiplicative identity.

$$48\times1=48$$

3. Add the remaining term.

$$48+25=73$$

### #3: Regrouping a Product

Evaluate $25\times(4\times17)$.

**Solution:**

1. Use the associative property to group the first two factors.

$$25\times(4\times17)=(25\times4)\times17$$

2. Multiply the easy pair, then the remaining factor.

$$100\times17=1,700$$

### #4: Two Kinds of Inverse

What is the additive inverse of the multiplicative inverse of $-\dfrac{2}{3}$?

- A. $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- B. $-\dfrac{3}{2}$
- C. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- D. $-\dfrac{2}{3}$

**Solution:**

1. Switch the numerator and denominator. Keep the negative sign. Check the product.

$$\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)=1$$

2. Find the additive inverse by changing the sign. Check the sum.

$$-\dfrac{3}{2}+\dfrac{3}{2}=0$$

3. The answer is A, $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

### #5: Collecting Like Terms

Simplify $7x^2-3x+4-2x^2+8x-9$.

**Solution:**

1. Group terms with matching variable parts. Keep each sign.

$$(7x^2-2x^2)+(-3x+8x)+(4-9)$$

2. Add the signed coefficients in each group.

$$(7-2)x^2+(-3+8)x+(4-9)$$

3. Keep the unlike groups separate.

$$5x^2+5x-5$$

### #6: Simplifying with the Distributive Property

Simplify $4(2x-3y)-2(x+5y)$.

- A. $6x-22y$
- B. $6x-2y$
- C. $8x-22y$
- D. $8x+2y$

**Solution:**

1. Distribute through both grouped expressions.

$$4(2x-3y)=8x-12y$$

$$-2(x+5y)=-2x-10y$$

2. Combine like terms by adding their signed coefficients.

$$(8-2)x+(-12-10)y$$

$$=6x-22y$$

3. The answer is A.

### #7: Reverse Distribution

Evaluate $847\times63+847\times37$.

- A. $84,700$
- B. $8,470$
- C. $847,000$
- D. $100,000$

**Solution:**

1. Both products have the common factor $847$. Factor it out.

$$847(63+37)$$

2. Add inside the parentheses.

$$63+37=100$$

3. Multiply.

$$847\times100=84,700$$

4. The answer is A.

### #8: Zero Has No Multiplicative Inverse

Which statement is correct?

- A. The multiplicative inverse of $0$ is $0$.
- B. The multiplicative inverse of $0$ is $1$.
- C. The number $0$ has no multiplicative inverse.
- D. Every real number has a multiplicative inverse.

**Solution:**

A multiplicative inverse must give a product of $1$. Multiplying any real number by $0$ gives $0$. The answer is C.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### Reverse Distribution

When products share a common factor, factor it out before doing the arithmetic. This helps when the remaining numbers have an easy sum.

$$847\times63+847\times37=847(63+37)$$

### Additive Inverses

In a sum, opposite terms such as $5$ and $-5$ combine to $0$. You may swap and regroup terms in a sum to bring these pairs together. Keep the sign attached to each term.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Confusing Commutative and Associative

> Commutative changes order. Associative changes grouping.

The equation $(1+2)+3=(2+1)+3$ uses commutativity. The equation $(1+2)+3=1+(2+3)$ uses associativity. Calling any change of parentheses commutative gives the wrong property name.

### #2: Applying the Rules to Subtraction and Division

> Subtraction and division are not commutative or associative in general.

For example, $5-3\ne3-5$ and $10\div2\ne2\div10$. Swapping these numbers changes the answer. Regrouping subtraction or division can change it too.

### #3: Dropping the Negative Sign

> A negative factor must multiply every term inside the parentheses.

For example, $-2(x-5)=-2x+10$. Writing $-2x-10$ misses the positive product of two negative numbers.

### #4: Combining Unlike Terms

> Matching letters must also have matching exponents.

The terms $3x$ and $2x^2$ cannot be combined into $5x$ or $5x^2$. Their variable parts are different.
