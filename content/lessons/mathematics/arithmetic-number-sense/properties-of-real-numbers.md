## Core Concept

Several useful properties describe addition and multiplication of real numbers: the **commutative**, **associative**, **distributive**, **identity**, and **inverse** properties.

**1. Commutative Property**
You can swap the order of terms or factors in addition and multiplication.

Addition: $a + b = b + a$

Multiplication: $ab = ba$

**2. Associative Property**
You can change grouping in repeated addition or repeated multiplication without changing the value.

Addition: $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$

Multiplication: $(ab)c = a(bc)$

**3. Distributive Property**
Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction.

$$a(b+c)=ab+ac$$

$$a(b-c)=ab-ac$$

**4. Identity Property**
The additive identity is $0$ because $a+0=a$. The multiplicative identity is $1$ because $a\times1=a$.

**5. Inverse Property**
The additive inverse of $a$ is $-a$ because:

$$a+(-a)=0$$

For $a\ne0$, the multiplicative inverse of $a$ is $\dfrac{1}{a}$ because:

$$a\times\dfrac{1}{a}=1$$

Zero has no multiplicative inverse because division by zero is undefined.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

> **C**ommutative = **C**ommute: the order changes.

> **A**ssociative = **A**ssociate: the grouping changes.

> **D**istributive = **D**istribute: multiply the outside factor through each term inside.

> **I**dentity: adding $0$ or multiplying by $1$ keeps the number unchanged.

> **Inverse**: combine a number with its additive or multiplicative inverse to reach the corresponding identity.

## Examples

### #1: Identifying the Basic Property

Which of the following illustrates the commutative property of multiplication?
- A. $3 \times (4 \times 5) = (3 \times 4) \times 5$
- B. $3 \times 4 = 4 \times 3$
- C. $3 \times (4 + 5) = (3 \times 4) + (3 \times 5)$
- D. $3 \times 1 = 3$

**Solution:**

1. Choice A changes grouping, so it is associative.
2. Choice B swaps the order of the factors, so it is commutative.
3. Choice C is distributive.
4. Choice D is the multiplicative identity property.
5. The answer is B.

### #2: Inverse Trap

What is the additive inverse of the multiplicative inverse of $-\dfrac{2}{3}$?
- A. $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- B. $-\dfrac{3}{2}$
- C. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- D. $-\dfrac{2}{3}$

**Solution:**

1. The multiplicative inverse of $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ is $-\dfrac{3}{2}$.
2. The additive inverse of $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
3. The answer is A.

### #3: Simplifying with the Distributive Property

Simplify $4(2x - 3y) - 2(x + 5y)$.
- A. $6x - 22y$
- B. $6x - 2y$
- C. $8x - 22y$
- D. $8x + 2y$

**Solution:**

1. Distribute through both grouped expressions.

$$4(2x-3y)=8x-12y$$

$$-2(x+5y)=-2x-10y$$

2. Combine like terms.

$$8x-12y-2x-10y=6x-22y$$

3. The answer is A.

### #4: Reverse Distribution

Evaluate $847 \times 63 + 847 \times 37$.
- A. $84,700$
- B. $8,470$
- C. $847,000$
- D. $100,000$

**Solution:**

1. Factor out the common factor $847$.

$$847(63+37)$$

2. Add inside the parentheses.

$$63+37=100$$

3. Multiply.

$$847\times100=84,700$$

4. The answer is A.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Reverse Distribution.**
When two products share a common factor, factor it out before doing the arithmetic. For example:

$$847\times63+847\times37=847(63+37).$$

**Cancel Additive Inverses.**
In a sum, terms such as $5$ and $-5$ combine to $0$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Confusing Commutative and Associative

If the order changes, think commutative. If only the grouping changes, think associative.

$$(1+2)+3=(2+1)+3$$

uses commutativity, while

$$(1+2)+3=1+(2+3)$$

uses associativity.

### #2: Applying Commutative or Associative Rules to Subtraction and Division

Subtraction and division are not commutative or associative in general. For example, $5-3\ne3-5$ and $10\div2\ne2\div10$.

### #3: Dropping the Negative Sign

When distributing a negative factor, multiply every term inside the parentheses by that factor.

$$-2(x-5)=-2x+10$$
