## Core Concept

Imagine building a house. You must build the walls before the roof. Math is the same. When a problem has many steps, you must follow a strict order. If you do not, you will get the wrong answer.

This strict order is called **PEMDAS** or **GEMDAS**. It stands for the steps you must take to solve the problem.

First, you do **G**rouping symbols or **P**arentheses. This means you solve the math inside the curved brackets $()$, square brackets $[]$, or curly braces $\{\}$ first.

Second, you do **E**xponents. Exponents are the tiny numbers on top, like the $2$ in $3^2$. You also do roots here, like square roots.

Third, you do **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision. You do not always multiply first! You must solve them from left to right, just like reading a book.

Last, you do **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction. Again, do these from left to right.

CETs (College Entrance Tests) love this topic. They will give you long problems to see if you know the exact order. They want to trick you into adding before multiplying. If you follow GEMDAS, you will not be tricked.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

To remember PEMDAS, use this famous sentence:

> Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally.

If you prefer GEMDAS, you can use this animal sentence:

> Giant Elephants March Down A Street.

The biggest mistake students make is always multiplying before dividing. To stop this, think of the M and D as twins. Think of the A and S as another set of twins.

> Twins hold hands and walk together from left to right.

## Examples

### #1: The Left-to-Right Rule

Evaluate the expression: $16 \div 4 \times 2 + 3$.

**Solution:**
"Evaluate" means find the final number answer.

1. There are no parentheses. There are no exponents.
2. We look for multiplication and division. We have both.
3. Because they are twins, we go from left to right. We divide first.

$$16 \div 4 = 4$$


4. Now our problem is $4 \times 2 + 3$. We multiply next.

$$4 \times 2 = 8$$


5. Now our problem is $8 + 3$. We add last.

$$8 + 3 = 11$$



### #2: Nested Grouping Symbols

Simplify the following expression completely: $2 [ 5 + 3 ( 4 - 2 )^2 ] - 10$.

**Solution:**
"Simplify" means make the math problem as short as possible. Nested symbols mean brackets inside brackets. Always start with the innermost brackets.

1. Solve inside the regular parentheses $( 4 - 2 )$.

$$4 - 2 = 2$$


2. Rewrite the problem with this new number.

$$2 [ 5 + 3 ( 2 )^2 ] - 10$$


3. Look inside the square brackets. We must do exponents next.

$$2^2 = 4$$


4. Rewrite the problem again.

$$2 [ 5 + 3 ( 4 ) ] - 10$$


5. Inside the brackets, we have addition and multiplication. Multiply first.

$$3 \times 4 = 12$$


6. Now add inside the brackets.

$$5 + 12 = 17$$


7. The brackets are done. A number right outside a bracket means multiply. So, $2$ times $17$.

$$2 \times 17 = 34$$


8. Finally, subtract $10$.

$$34 - 10 = 24$$



### #3: Fraction Bars as Grouping Symbols

What is the value of $\dfrac{3^2 + 4 \times 5}{6 - 2^3 + 4}$?

**Solution:**
A big fraction bar acts like a wall. It groups the top numbers and the bottom numbers. You must solve the whole top first. Then you solve the whole bottom. Then you divide them at the end.

1. Solve the top part: $3^2 + 4 \times 5$.
2. Do the exponent first.

$$3^2 = 9$$


3. Do the multiplication next.

$$4 \times 5 = 20$$


4. Add them together. The top part is $29$.

$$9 + 20 = 29$$


5. Now solve the bottom part: $6 - 2^3 + 4$.
6. Do the exponent first.

$$2^3 = 8$$


7. Rewrite the bottom: $6 - 8 + 4$.
8. Subtract and add from left to right. Subtract first.

$$6 - 8 = -2$$


9. Add next.

$$-2 + 4 = 2$$


10. The bottom part is $2$. Put the top over the bottom. The final answer is $\dfrac{29}{2}$.

### #4: Algebraic Evaluation

If $x = -2$ and $y = 3$, evaluate the expression: $x^2 - 3xy + \dfrac{12}{x}$.

**Solution:**
First, "substitute" the letters. This means swap the letters for the numbers. Always put negative numbers in parentheses when you swap them!

1. Swap the letters for numbers.

$$(-2)^2 - 3(-2)(3) + \dfrac{12}{-2}$$


2. Do the exponent first. A negative times a negative is a positive.

$$(-2)^2 = 4$$


3. The problem is now: $4 - 3(-2)(3) + \dfrac{12}{-2}$.
4. Do multiplication and division from left to right. Let's do the multiplication block: $3 \times -2 \times 3$.

$$3 \times -2 = -6$$


$$-6 \times 3 = -18$$


5. Now do the division block.

$$12 \div -2 = -6$$


6. Rewrite the problem. Be careful with the minus signs!

$$4 - (-18) + (-6)$$


7. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive.

$$4 + 18 - 6$$


8. Add and subtract from left to right.

$$22 - 6 = 16$$



## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Think of plus ($+$) and minus ($-$) signs as walls. These walls break a long problem into smaller "rooms" or chunks.

You can solve each chunk at the same time. You do not have to rewrite the whole problem every single step.

Look at this long problem: $5 \times 4 - 3^2 + 20 \div 5$.

The minus and plus signs are walls. We have three chunks:
- Chunk 1: $5 \times 4$
- Chunk 2: $3^2$
- Chunk 3: $20 \div 5$

Solve them all in your head quickly:
Chunk 1 is $20$.
Chunk 2 is $9$.
Chunk 3 is $4$.

Now just read them with the walls put back in:


$$20 - 9 + 4 = 15$$


This trick saves you a lot of time on the exam.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Multiplication Before Division

> Do not always multiply before you divide. Do whichever comes first from left to right.

Test makers love problems like $10 \div 2 \times 5$. Many students see PEMDAS and multiply $2 \times 5$ to get $10$. Then they do $10 \div 10 = 1$. **This is completely wrong**! Because division is on the left, you must do $10 \div 2 = 5$ first. Then $5 \times 5 = 25$. The correct answer is $25$.

### #2: Negative Exponents

> $-3^2$ is not the same as $(-3)^2$.

This is a very common trick. If the negative sign is inside parentheses, you square the whole thing. So, $(-3)^2$ means $-3 \times -3 = 9$. But if there are no parentheses, the exponent only talks to the number, not the minus sign. So, $-3^2$ means $-(3 \times 3) = -9$.

### #3: Addition Before Subtraction

> Do not always add before you subtract.

Just like multiplying, addition and subtraction go left to right. If a problem is $10 - 4 + 2$, do not add $4 + 2$ first. You must subtract first. $10 - 4 = 6$, and then $6 + 2 = 8$. If you added first, you would get $10 - 6 = 4$, which is the trick answer in the choices.
