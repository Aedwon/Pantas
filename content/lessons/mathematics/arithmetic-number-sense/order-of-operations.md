## Core Concept

When an expression has several operations, use the standard order of operations so the expression has one consistent value.

This order is often remembered as **PEMDAS** or **GEMDAS**.

First, evaluate **G**rouping symbols or **P**arentheses. This includes parentheses $()$, square brackets $[]$, curly braces $\{\}$, and other grouping devices such as fraction bars.

Second, evaluate **E**xponents and roots.

Third, evaluate **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision from left to right. Multiplication does not automatically come before division.

Last, evaluate **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction from left to right. Addition does not automatically come before subtraction.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

To remember PEMDAS, use:

> Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally.

If you prefer GEMDAS:

> Giant Elephants March Down A Street.

The important detail is that multiplication and division share the same priority, as do addition and subtraction. Within each pair, work from left to right.

## Examples

### #1: The Left-to-Right Rule

Evaluate the expression: $16 \div 4 \times 2 + 3$.

**Solution:**

1. There are no grouping symbols or exponents to evaluate first.
2. Work through division and multiplication from left to right.

$$16 \div 4 = 4$$

$$4 \times 2 = 8$$

3. Add last.

$$8 + 3 = 11$$

### #2: Nested Grouping Symbols

Simplify: $2 [ 5 + 3 ( 4 - 2 )^2 ] - 10$.

**Solution:**

1. Start with the innermost parentheses.

$$4 - 2 = 2$$

2. Evaluate the exponent.

$$2^2 = 4$$

3. Multiply inside the brackets.

$$3 \times 4 = 12$$

4. Add inside the brackets.

$$5 + 12 = 17$$

5. Multiply by the $2$ outside the brackets, then subtract $10$.

$$2(17)-10=34-10=24$$

### #3: Fraction Bars as Grouping Symbols

What is the value of $\dfrac{3^2 + 4 \times 5}{6 - 2^3 + 4}$?

**Solution:**

1. Treat the numerator and denominator as grouped expressions.
2. Numerator:

$$3^2 + 4 \times 5 = 9 + 20 = 29$$

3. Denominator:

$$6 - 2^3 + 4 = 6 - 8 + 4 = 2$$

4. Therefore:

$$\dfrac{29}{2}$$

### #4: Algebraic Evaluation

If $x = -2$ and $y = 3$, evaluate $x^2 - 3xy + \dfrac{12}{x}$.

**Solution:**

1. Substitute the values, using parentheses around the negative value of $x$.

$$(-2)^2 - 3(-2)(3) + \dfrac{12}{-2}$$

2. Evaluate powers, multiplication, and division.

$$4 - (-18) - 6$$

3. Finish the addition and subtraction.

$$4+18-6=16$$

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Addition and subtraction signs can help you see large additive terms. In an expression such as

$$5 \times 4 - 3^2 + 20 \div 5,$$

you can evaluate the higher-priority pieces first:

$$20 - 9 + 4 = 15.$$

This is safe as long as you do not break apart an operation that has higher priority or ignore grouping symbols.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Multiplication Before Division

> Do not always multiply before dividing. Work from left to right when multiplication and division have the same priority.

For $10 \div 2 \times 5$:

$$10 \div 2 \times 5 = 5 \times 5 = 25.$$

### #2: Negative Bases and Exponents

> $-3^2$ is not the same as $(-3)^2$.

In $(-3)^2$, the parentheses make $-3$ the base, so the value is $9$. In $-3^2$, the exponent applies to $3$ before the leading negative sign, so the value is $-9$.

### #3: Addition Before Subtraction

> Addition does not automatically come before subtraction.

For $10 - 4 + 2$, work from left to right:

$$10 - 4 + 2 = 6 + 2 = 8.$$
