## Core Concept

### Finding Elapsed Time

**Elapsed time** means the time that passes from a start to an end.

$$\text{elapsed time}=\text{end time}-\text{start time}$$

Before subtracting, put the times in the same form. Include the day if the end is after midnight.

You can also count forward. Stop when you reach the ending time. If the end is before the next exact hour, subtract the starting minutes from the ending minutes.

For example, find the time from $8{:}10$ am to $8{:}20$ am on the same day. Subtract $20-10=10$ minutes. The elapsed time is $10$ minutes.

Otherwise, count the minutes to the next exact hour. Then count whole hours without passing the ending time.

If minutes remain, count from the last exact hour to the ending time. Add these durations.

When subtracting hours and minutes, you may need to **borrow**. This means changing $1$ hour into $60$ minutes.

Reduce the hour by $1$ and add $60$ to the minutes. The total time stays the same.

## Examples

### #1: Elapsed Time Across Noon

A school garden team starts work at $8{:}46$ am and finishes at $1{:}18$ pm on the same day. How long does the team work?

**Solution:**

1. Count from $8{:}46$ am to the next exact hour, $9{:}00$ am.

$$60-46=14\text{ minutes}$$

2. Count whole hours from $9{:}00$ am to $1{:}00$ pm. From $9{:}00$ am to noon is $3$ hours. From noon to $1{:}00$ pm is another $1$ hour.

$$3+1=4\text{ hours}$$

3. Count from $1{:}00$ pm to $1{:}18$ pm. That is $18$ minutes. Combine the minute parts.

$$14+18=32\text{ minutes}$$

The elapsed time is $4$ hours $32$ minutes.

4. Check by subtraction. Change $1{:}18$ pm to $13{:}18$.

   The start is $08{:}46$. Since $18$ minutes is less than $46$ minutes, borrow $1$ hour from $13$ hours.

$$13\text{ h }18\text{ min}=12\text{ h }78\text{ min}$$

5. Subtract the minutes and then the hours.

$$78-46=32\text{ minutes}$$

$$12-8=4\text{ hours}$$

Both methods give $4$ hours $32$ minutes. Crossing noon did not begin a new day.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### Choose Counting or Subtraction

Count forward when the next exact hour is close. Subtract when both times are already in $24$-hour form. For either method, write down the day if midnight lies between the times.

### Check by Reversing the Work

Add an elapsed time back to the start. It should reach the end.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Borrowing the Wrong Amount

> Borrow $60$ minutes from an hour.

Borrowing $100$ minutes treats time like decimal numbers. In Example #1, borrowing changes $18$ minutes to $78$ minutes. It also reduces the hour by $1$.
