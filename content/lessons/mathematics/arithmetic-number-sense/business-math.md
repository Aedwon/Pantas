## Core Concept

Business math uses percentages and rates to describe changes in money. The same idea appears in discounts, markups, commission, sales tax, and interest.

### Discounts and Markups

A **discount** is an amount taken off a listed price. When the discount rate is based on the original price:

$$\text{Discount amount}=\text{Original price}\times\text{Discount rate}$$

The price after the discount is:

$$\text{Selling price}=\text{Original price}-\text{Discount amount}$$

A **markup** is an amount added to an item's cost to set or help set its selling price. A markup is not automatically the same as profit because a business may have other costs and expenses.

When the markup rate is based on cost:

$$\text{Markup amount}=\text{Cost}\times\text{Markup rate}$$

$$\text{Listed price}=\text{Cost}+\text{Markup amount}$$

### Commission and Sales Tax

A **commission** is pay based on a stated amount, such as sales. When a problem says the commission rate is based on sales:

$$\text{Commission}=\text{Sales}\times\text{Commission rate}$$

A **sales tax** problem gives a tax rate and a price on which the tax is based. That starting amount is the **taxable price**.

$$\text{Sales tax}=\text{Taxable price}\times\text{Tax rate}$$

If the tax is added to the price, the total is:

$$\text{Total price}=\text{Taxable price}+\text{Sales tax}$$

Always check which amount the percentage is based on. A discount, markup, commission, and tax can use different bases.

### Simple and Compound Interest

**Interest** is money paid for the use of money. A borrower may pay interest, while a saver or investor may earn interest.

The **principal** is the starting amount. The **rate** is the interest rate for a stated time period. **Per annum** means per year.

**Simple interest** is calculated only on the original principal. When the rate is annual, use:

$$I=PRT$$

Here $I$ is the interest, $P$ is the principal, $R$ is the annual interest rate written as a decimal, and $T$ is the time in years.

**Compound interest** adds interest to the balance. Later interest can then be earned or charged on earlier interest.

If $r$ is the rate per compounding period and $t$ is the number of compounding periods, the total amount $A$ is:

$$A=P(1+r)^t$$

When a problem gives an annual rate but compounds more than once a year, convert the rate to the compounding period. Then count the total number of periods.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

For simple interest, remember:

$$I=PRT$$

Interest equals Principal times Rate times Time.

For a discount, you can find the percentage that remains. If an item has a $20\%$ discount, then $80\%$ of the original price remains to be paid.

## Examples

### #1: Basic Discount

A pair of shoes originally priced at Php $1,500$ is on sale for $20\%$ off. What is the final selling price of the shoes?

**Solution:**

1. Find the percentage that remains.

$$100\%-20\%=80\%$$

2. Convert $80\%$ to $0.80$ and multiply by the original price.

$$1500\times0.80=1200$$

3. The final selling price is Php $1,200$.

### #2: Successive Discounts

A laptop listed at Php $30,000$ is given successive trade discounts of $20\%$ and $10\%$. What is the net price of the laptop?

**Solution:**

1. Apply the first $20\%$ discount.

$$30000\times0.80=24000$$

2. Apply the $10\%$ discount to the new price.

$$24000\times0.90=21600$$

3. The net price is Php $21,600$.

### #3: Markup Based on Cost

A retailer buys a gadget for Php $1,200$ and applies a markup of $25\%$ based on cost. At what price is the gadget listed for sale?

**Solution:**

1. Find the markup amount.

$$1200\times0.25=300$$

2. Add the markup to the cost.

$$1200+300=1500$$

3. The listed price is Php $1,500$.

### #4: Commission

A salesperson earns a $6\%$ commission on Php $85,000$ in sales. How much commission does the salesperson earn?

**Solution:**

1. Convert the commission rate to a decimal and multiply by sales.

$$85000\times0.06=5100$$

2. The commission is Php $5,100$.

### #5: Sales Tax

An item has a taxable price of Php $2,400$. A sales tax of $5\%$ is added. What is the total price?

**Solution:**

1. Find the sales tax.

$$2400\times0.05=120$$

2. Add the tax to the taxable price.

$$2400+120=2520$$

3. The total price is Php $2,520$.

### #6: Finding Simple Interest Time

How many years will it take for an investment of Php $15,000$ to earn Php $4,500$ in simple interest at $6\%$ per annum?

**Solution:**

1. Use the simple-interest formula.

$$I=PRT$$

2. Substitute the given values.

$$4500=15000\times0.06\times T$$

3. Simplify.

$$4500=900T$$

4. Divide by $900$.

$$T=5$$

5. It will take $5$ years.

### #7: Compound Interest

An amount of Php $10,000$ is deposited into a savings account that yields an annual interest rate of $4\%$ compounded semi-annually. What is the total amount after $2$ years? Assume $1.02^4\approx1.0824$.

**Solution:**

1. Semi-annual compounding means interest is applied twice a year. The rate per period is:

$$4\%\div2=2\%$$

2. Count the compounding periods.

$$2\times2=4$$

3. Substitute into the compound-interest formula.

$$A=10000(1+0.02)^4$$

4. Use the approximation given in the question.

$$A=10000\times1.0824$$

5. The total amount is approximately Php $10,824$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For **compound growth**, the **Rule of $72$** gives a rough doubling-time estimate. Divide $72$ by the annual percentage rate.

At an annual compound growth rate of about $8\%$, the estimated doubling time is:

$$72\div8=9$$

The result is about $9$ years. This is only an approximation, and it is not the simple-interest formula.

For successive discounts of $a\%$ and $b\%$, the equivalent single discount is:

$$\text{Total discount}=a+b-\dfrac{ab}{100}$$

For discounts of $20\%$ and $10\%$:

$$20+10-\dfrac{20\times10}{100}=28\%$$

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Adding Successive Discounts

> Do not add successive discount percentages directly.

A $20\%$ discount followed by another $20\%$ discount is not a $40\%$ discount. The second discount is applied to the already-reduced price.

### #2: Using the Wrong Percentage Base

> Check which amount the rate is based on.

A commission rate may be based on sales, while a markup rate may be based on cost. Using the wrong base gives the wrong amount.

### #3: Mismatching the Time Unit and Rate

> The time unit must match the period used by the interest rate.

If the simple-interest rate is per annum and the time is $6$ months, use $0.5$ year for $T$. If a rate is stated for another period, convert the time to match it.

### #4: Missing the Compounding Frequency

> Check how often compound interest is applied.

If interest is compounded quarterly, there are four compounding periods each year. Convert the annual rate to a quarterly rate and count four periods for each year.
