## Core Concept

Business math is all about how money changes in the real world. College entrance exams test this to see if you can handle daily life problems. You only need to learn two main ideas.

### Discounts and Markups

A **discount** is money taken off a price. It makes things cheaper during a sale. To find the discount, you multiply the original price by the percentage of the sale.

A **markup** is money added to a price. Stores buy items cheaply and add a markup before selling them. This extra money is their profit.

We use two important words here. The **Original Price** is the starting amount. The **Selling Price** is the final amount you pay at the register.

### Simple and Compound Interest

**Interest** is extra money. If you borrow money, you must pay back extra. If you save money in a bank, the bank pays you extra.

The **Principal** is the starting money. The **Rate** is the percentage of interest charged per year.

**Simple Interest** means the interest stays exactly the same every year. It is based only on your starting money.

**Compound Interest** means your money grows faster. You earn interest on your starting money, and you also earn interest on the interest you already got.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

Use this to remember the Simple Interest formula: 

$$I = PRT$$

Interest equals Principal times Rate times Time.

This helps you find sale prices faster. If a shirt has a $20\%$ discount, do not calculate the $20\%$. Instead, remember that $100\% - 20\% = 80\%$. You just pay the $80\%$ that stays.

## Examples

### #1: Basic Discount

A pair of shoes originally priced at Php $1,500$ is on sale for $20\%$ off. What is the final selling price of the shoes?

1. Find the percentage you actually have to pay. The whole price is $100\%$.
2. Subtract the discount:

$$100\% - 20\% = 80\%$$


3. Multiply the original price by this new percentage. Remember that $80\%$ is $0.80$ as a decimal.
4. Calculate the final answer:

$$1500 \times 0.80 = 1200$$


5. The final selling price is Php $1,200$.

### #2: Successive Discounts

A laptop listed at Php $30,000$ is given successive trade discounts of $20\%$ and $10\%$. What is the net price of the laptop?

1. Never add the two discounts together! You must do them one by one.
2. First, take off the $20\%$ discount. You will pay $80\%$ of the price.
3. Multiply to find the first new price:

$$30000 \times 0.80 = 24000$$


4. Now, apply the $10\%$ discount to this new price. You will pay $90\%$ of it.
5. Multiply to find the final price:

$$24000 \times 0.90 = 21600$$


6. The net price is Php $21,600$.

### #3: Markup Based on Cost

A retailer buys a gadget for Php $1,200$ and wants to realize a markup of $25\%$ based on cost. At what price should the gadget be sold?

1. Find out how much extra money the retailer wants to add.
2. Multiply the starting cost by the markup percentage. Remember that $25\%$ is $0.25$.

$$1200 \times 0.25 = 300$$


3. The retailer wants to add Php $300$ to the price.
4. Add this to the starting cost:

$$1200 + 300 = 1500$$


5. The gadget should be sold for Php $1,500$.

### #4: Finding Simple Interest Time

How many years will it take for an investment of Php $15,000$ to earn Php $4,500$ in interest if it is invested at a simple interest rate of $6\%$ per annum?

1. Write down the simple interest formula.

$$I = PRT$$


2. Put the numbers from the problem into the formula. The interest $I$ is $4500$. The principal $P$ is $15000$. The rate $R$ is $0.06$.

$$4500 = 15000 \times 0.06 \times T$$


3. Multiply the numbers on the right side.

$$15000 \times 0.06 = 900$$


4. Now your equation is simpler.

$$4500 = 900 \times T$$


5. Divide both sides by $900$ to find the time $T$.

$$T = 5$$


6. It will take $5$ years.

### #5: Compound Interest

An amount of Php $10,000$ is deposited into a savings account that yields an annual interest rate of $4\%$ compounded semi-annually. What is the total amount in the account after $2$ years? (Assume $1.02^4 \approx 1.0824$)

1. Notice the word "semi-annually". This means the bank gives you interest twice a year.
2. You must cut the annual rate in half. The rate per period is:

$$4\% \div 2 = 2\%$$


3. You must double the number of years. You get interest twice a year for $2$ years. The total number of periods is:

$$2 \times 2 = 4$$


4. Put these new numbers into the compound interest formula.

$$A = P(1 + r)^t$$


$$A = 10000(1 + 0.02)^4$$


5. Use the hint given in the question for $1.02^4$.

$$A = 10000 \times 1.0824$$


6. Multiply to get the final amount. The total is Php $10,824$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For simple interest problems, use the **Rule of $72$** to estimate time. If a question asks how long it takes for your money to double, just divide $72$ by the interest rate. If the rate is $8\%$, it takes $72 \div 8 = 9$ years to double.

For successive discounts, use the **A-B-Combo** trick. If an item is discounted by $a\%$ and then $b\%$, the total true discount is:


$$Total = a + b - \dfrac{a \times b}{100}$$

Let us try it with $20\%$ and $10\%$ discounts. Add them to get $30$. Then multiply them to get $200$, and divide by $100$ to get $2$. Subtract $2$ from $30$. The true total discount is exactly $28\%$.

## Traps to Watch For

### 1: Adding Successive Discounts

> Never add two discount percentages together!

If a store says "Take $20\%$ off, and then take another $20\%$ off at the register," the total discount is NOT $40\%$. The second discount is taken from a smaller, already-discounted price. The true discount is always smaller than adding them up.

### 2: Forgetting Time is in Years

> Time in the simple interest formula must always be in years.

If a question asks for the interest after $6$ months, do not use $6$ for the time. Six months is half of a year. You must use $0.5$ or $\dfrac{1}{2}$ for the time in your formula.

### 3: Missing the Compound Word

> Always check if the interest rate is divided into smaller pieces.

Exams love to use words like "quarterly" or "monthly." If the interest is compounded quarterly, you must divide the rate by $4$. You must also multiply the years by $4$. If you forget this step, your answer will always be one of the wrong choices on the test.
