## Core Concept

### Reading Clock Time

A **clock time** tells when something happens. A **duration** tells how long it lasts. There are $60$ minutes in an hour and $24$ hours in a day.

In **$12$-hour time**, the hour runs from $1$ through $12$. Use am for times from midnight up to noon. Use pm for times from noon up to midnight.

Midnight is $12{:}00$ am. Noon is $12{:}00$ pm.

The hour after $12{:}59$ am is $1{:}00$ am. The hour after $12{:}59$ pm is $1{:}00$ pm.

In **$24$-hour time**, the hours run from $00$ through $23$. Midnight at the start of a day is $00{:}00$.

Noon is $12{:}00$. Do not add am or pm to this form.

For an am time, keep the hour unless it is $12$. Change that hour to $00$.

For a pm time, add $12$ to the hour unless it is already $12$. Leave the minutes unchanged.

To change back, hours $00$ through $11$ are am. Change $00$ to $12$.

Hour $12$ is pm. For hours $13$ through $23$, subtract $12$ and use pm.

## Examples

### #1: Reading a Museum Schedule

A museum opens at $12{:}25$ pm and closes at $6{:}40$ pm. Write both times in $24$-hour form.

A delivery is scheduled for $00{:}15$ the next day. Write that time in $12$-hour form.

**Solution:**

1. The opening hour is $12$ pm. Keep that hour unchanged. The opening time is $12{:}25$.

2. The closing hour is after noon but is not $12$. Add $12$ to the hour.

$$6+12=18$$

The closing time is $18{:}40$.

3. The delivery hour is $00$. Change it to $12$ and use am. The delivery is at $12{:}15$ am the next day.

4. Check the labels. The opening is shortly after noon.

   The delivery is shortly after midnight. Both use $12$ in $12$-hour time, but their am and pm labels differ.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Mixing Up Noon and Midnight

> $12{:}00$ pm is noon. $12{:}00$ am is midnight.

Adding $12$ to the hour of a noon time would give a wrong conversion. Keep the hour $12$ for noon. Use $00$ for the midnight hour in $24$-hour time.
