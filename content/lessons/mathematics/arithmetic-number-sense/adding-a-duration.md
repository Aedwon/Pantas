## Core Concept

### Adding a Duration

$$\text{end time}=\text{start time}+\text{duration}$$

Add hours to hours and minutes to minutes. If the minutes reach $60$, change each complete group of $60$ minutes into $1$ hour.

If the hour reaches $24$, subtract $24$ and move to the next day. Repeat for a duration that passes more days.

## Examples

### #1: Adding a Duration to a Start Time

A science club starts recording the night sky at $9{:}45$ pm on Saturday. The recording lasts $3$ hours $35$ minutes. When does it end?

**Solution:**

1. Change the start to $24$-hour time.

$$9+12=21$$

The start is $21{:}45$ on Saturday.

2. Add the minutes.

$$45+35=80\text{ minutes}$$

$$80\text{ minutes}=1\text{ h }20\text{ min}$$

3. Add the given hours and the extra hour from the minutes.

$$21+3+1=25$$

The result for the calculation is $25$ hours $20$ minutes.

4. Remove $24$ hours for one complete day.

$$25-24=1$$

The recording ends at $01{:}20$, or $1{:}20$ am on Sunday.

5. Check by counting forward. From $9{:}45$ pm to midnight is $2$ hours $15$ minutes.

   From midnight to $1{:}20$ am is $1$ hour $20$ minutes. Together, these make the given $3$ hours $35$ minutes.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### Keep Hours and Minutes Separate

Clock minutes use groups of $60$. Do not treat a colon like a decimal point.

For a total number of minutes, divide by $60$. The whole-number quotient gives hours. The remainder gives minutes.
