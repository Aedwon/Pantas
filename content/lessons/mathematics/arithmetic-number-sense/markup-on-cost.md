## Core Concept

### Markup Based on Cost

A seller buys goods at a **cost** and adds a **markup** to set the **listed price** (the selling price before any discount). When the markup rate is based on cost:

$$\text{Markup amount}=\text{Cost}\times\text{Markup rate}$$

$$\text{Listed price}=\text{Cost}+\text{Markup amount}$$

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal bar labeled "Cost = Php 800 (100%)" joined to an added right section labeled "Markup = Php 200 (25% of cost)", with a full bottom bracket labeled "Listed price = Php 1,000 (125% of cost)" -->

Find the listed price of a box of canned goods that costs Php $800$ and gets a $25\%$ markup based on cost.

1. Change $25\%$ to a decimal: $25\%=0.25$.

2. Multiply the cost by $0.25$ to find the markup amount.

$$800\times0.25=200$$

3. Add the markup amount to the cost to find the listed price.

$$800+200=1,000$$

The listed price is Php $1,000$.

:::pair #markup-only-not-price
Right: $\text{Listed price}=800+200=1,000$
Wrong: $\text{Listed price}=200$
Php $200$ is only the markup added on top. Add the Php $800$ cost to get the selling price.
:::

:::why
Why does multiplying by $1.25$ give Php $1,000$ in one step? The cost is the whole $100\%$, and adding $25\%$ of cost makes $100\%+25\%=125\%$, or $1.25$ times the cost.
:::

To work backward, divide the listed price by $1$ plus the markup rate, because the listed price is that multiple of the cost. Find the cost of an item listed at Php $900$ after a $20\%$ markup based on cost.

$$1+0.20=1.20$$

$$900\div1.20=750$$

The cost is Php $750$.

:::check
A shirt is listed at Php $650$ after a $30\%$ markup based on cost. How many pesos is the markup?
:::answer
The answer is Php $150$. The cost is $650\div1.30=500$, so the markup is $650-500=150$.
:::

### Markup Versus Profit and Expenses

A **markup** is not automatically the same as **profit**. A seller often pays extra selling expenses such as transport, bags, or stall rent after buying the item.

$$\text{Profit}=\text{Selling price}-\text{Cost}-\text{Expenses}$$

Find the profit on an item that costs Php $640$, gets a $25\%$ markup on cost, and has Php $45$ in selling expenses.

1. Find the markup amount.

$$640\times0.25=160$$

2. Find the listed price, which is the selling price.

$$640+160=800$$

3. Subtract the cost from the selling price.

$$800-640=160$$

4. Subtract the selling expenses.

$$160-45=115$$

The profit is Php $115$.

:::pair #markup-is-not-profit
Right: $\text{Profit}=160-45=115$
Wrong: $\text{Profit}=160$
The Php $160$ markup leaves out the Php $45$ selling expenses. Subtract the expenses too to get profit.
:::

### Checking Which Base a Rate Uses

Check what amount a percent rate is based on. A markup rate **based on cost** divides the markup by cost. A markup rate **based on selling price** divides the markup by selling price.

Find the markup rate on cost and on selling price for a pack of noodles that costs Php $20$ and sells for Php $25$.

1. Subtract cost from selling price to get the markup amount.

$$25-20=5$$

2. Divide by cost to find the markup rate on cost.

$$\dfrac{5}{20}=0.25$$

$$0.25=25\%$$

3. Divide by selling price to find what percent of the selling price is the markup.

$$\dfrac{5}{25}=0.20$$

$$0.20=20\%$$

The same Php $5$ markup gives $25\%$ of cost, but $20\%$ of selling price.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: one bar for the selling price, Php 25, made of Cost Php 20 and Markup Php 5 on the right. A bracket under the cost reads "5 of 20 = 25% of cost". A longer bracket under the whole bar reads "5 of 25 = 20% of selling price". The same Php 5 markup is measured against two different bases. -->

:::pair #wrong-percentage-base
Right: $\dfrac{5}{25}=0.20=20\%$ of selling price
Wrong: $\dfrac{5}{20}=0.25=25\%$
Dividing the Php $5$ markup by cost gives the rate on cost, not on selling price. Read the question to match the denominator to the stated base.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Markup Based on Cost

A retailer buys a gadget for Php $1,200$ and applies a markup of $25\%$ based on cost. At what price is the gadget listed for sale?

**Solution:**

1. Change $25\%$ to a decimal: $0.25$. Multiply the cost by $0.25$ to find the markup amount.

$$1,200\times0.25=300$$

2. Add the markup to the cost.

$$1,200+300=1,500$$

The listed price is Php $1,500$.

### #2: Working Backward from a Listed Price

A shop lists a bag at Php $2,000$ after adding a $60\%$ markup based on its cost. The bag sells at that price with no other expenses. What percent of the selling price is the markup?

**Solution:**

1. Cost is $100\%$ and markup is $60\%$ of cost, so the listed price is $160\%$ of cost, or $1.6\times\text{Cost}$.

2. Divide the listed price by $1.6$ to find the cost.

$$\dfrac{2,000}{1.6}=1,250$$

3. Subtract the cost from the selling price to find the markup amount.

$$2,000-1,250=750$$

:::try
Finish the last step: divide the markup amount by the selling price and write it as a percent.
:::answer
The answer is $37.5\%$. Dividing the Php $750$ markup by the Php $2,000$ selling price gives $\dfrac{750}{2,000}=0.375=37.5\%$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Asked for the selling or listed price: add the markup amount to the cost ($800+200=1,000$, not $200$ alone). {#markup-only-not-price}
- Asked for profit at the listed price when selling expenses are given: subtract expenses from the markup ($160-45=115$). {#markup-is-not-profit}
- Asked for percent of selling price versus percent of cost: put the stated base in the denominator ($\dfrac{5}{25}=20\%$ of selling price). {#wrong-percentage-base}
:::
