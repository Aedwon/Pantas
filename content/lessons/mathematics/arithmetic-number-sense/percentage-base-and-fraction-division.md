## Core Concept

### Percentage Part, Rate, and Base

A percent problem connects three numbers. The **base** is the whole or reference amount.

The **part** is the share taken from that base. The **rate** tells what fraction or percent of the base you take.

<!-- FIGURE WANTED: horizontal bar labeled "Base = 200 (100%)" split into a shaded left part labeled "Part = 60 (Rate = 30%)" and an unshaded right part labeled "140 (70%)" -->

In "$30\%$ of $200$ is $60$," the base is $200$, the rate is $30\%$ (or $0.30$), and the part is $60$. In questions like this, the base sits right after the word "of."

If you know the rate as a percent, write it as a decimal or fraction before you multiply or divide by it:

$$\text{Part}=\text{Rate}\times\text{Base}$$

$$\text{Base}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Rate}}$$

$$\text{Rate}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Base}}$$

Try finding the base when $25\%$ of a number is $60$. Here the part is $60$, the rate is $0.25$, and the base is unknown. Divide the part by the rate, moving both decimal points $2$ places right so the divisor is a whole number:

$$\dfrac{60}{0.25}$$

$$=\dfrac{6,000}{25}$$

$$=240$$

You can only divide by a nonzero rate or a nonzero base.

:::why
Why does dividing the part by the rate give the base? Because $\text{Part}=\text{Rate}\times\text{Base}$. Dividing both sides by the rate undoes the multiplication and leaves the base alone.
:::

:::check
A shop sold $54$ shirts, which is $20\%$ of its stock. How many shirts were not sold?
:::answer
The answer is $216$ shirts. The stock is the base: $54\div0.20=5,400\div20=270$. Then $270-54=216$.
:::

### Dividing Fractions to Find a Rate

The **reciprocal** of a nonzero fraction swaps its top and bottom numbers. For example, the reciprocal of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is $\dfrac{8}{5}$. Any nonzero fraction times its reciprocal equals $1$.

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal:

$$\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}$$

Here $b$, $c$, and $d$ cannot be $0$.

Suppose a problem asks: what percent of $\dfrac{4}{5}$ is $\dfrac{3}{5}$? In this question, the number after "of" is the base, so the base is $\dfrac{4}{5}$ and the part is $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

1. Find the rate: divide the part $\dfrac{3}{5}$ by the base $\dfrac{4}{5}$.

$$\dfrac{3}{5}\div\dfrac{4}{5}$$

$$=\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{5}{4}$$

$$=\dfrac{15}{20}$$

$$=\dfrac{3}{4}$$

$$=0.75$$

2. Change the decimal to a percent by multiplying by $100\%$.

$$0.75\times100\%$$

$$=75\%$$

:::pair #percent-decimal-shift
Right: $0.75\times100\%=75\%$
Wrong: $0.75\times100\%=7.5\%$
Multiplying by $100\%$ moves the decimal point $2$ places right and adds the percent sign, so the value stays the same. A rate of $7.5\%$ would be $0.075$.
:::

:::why
Why does multiplying by the reciprocal work? Writing $\dfrac{3}{5}\div\dfrac{4}{5}$ as a big fraction and multiplying top and bottom by $\dfrac{5}{4}$ turns the bottom into $\dfrac{4}{5}\times\dfrac{5}{4}=1$. Only the top remains: $\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{5}{4}$.
:::

## Examples

In exam questions, **simplify** means write an equal fraction or expression in a simpler form.

### #1: Finding a Base Before Another Percent

After completing $40\%$ of a practice set, a student has answered $72$ questions. How many questions equal $15\%$ of the entire set?

**Solution:**

1. Find the base: the part $72$ equals $40\%$ of the full set, so divide $72$ by $0.40$.

:::pair #wrong-percentage-base
Right: find the base first, $72\div0.40$, then take $15\%$ of it
Wrong: $15\%$ of the set $=0.15\times72=10.8$
Here $72$ is only the $40\%$ part, not the whole set. Find the base before taking another percent.
:::

2. Divide to find the base:

$$\dfrac{72}{0.40}$$

$$=\dfrac{7,200}{40}$$

$$=180$$

:::pair #stop-at-the-base
Right: $15\%$ of $180$: $0.15\times180=27$
Wrong: stopping at $180$
The question asks for $15\%$ of the set, not the full set. Check what the final sentence asks.
:::

3. Multiply the base by $0.15$:

$$0.15\times180$$

$$=27$$

There are $27$ questions.

### #2: Fraction Division to Find a Rate

What percent of $\dfrac{2}{5}$ is $\dfrac{7}{20}$?

**Solution:**

1. Identify the base after "of." The base is $\dfrac{2}{5}$ and the part is $\dfrac{7}{20}$.
2. Find the rate: divide the part $\dfrac{7}{20}$ by the base $\dfrac{2}{5}$.

$$\dfrac{7}{20}\div\dfrac{2}{5}$$

$$=\dfrac{7}{20}\times\dfrac{5}{2}$$

$$=\dfrac{35}{40}$$

$$=\dfrac{7}{8}$$

$$=0.875$$

:::try
Finish the last step: change the decimal rate $0.875$ to a percent.
:::answer
The answer is $87.5\%$. Multiply $0.875\times100\%=87.5\%$.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### Switch the Percent and the Base

For any two numbers $x$ and $y$, $x\%$ of $y$ equals $y\%$ of $x$. Find $16\%$ of $25$ by switching to $25\%$ of $16$:

$$25\%\text{ of }16$$

$$=\dfrac{1}{4}\times16$$

$$=4$$

### Find $10\%$ First

To find $15\%$ mentally, find $10\%$, take half of it for $5\%$, and add them. Find $15\%$ of $240$. Take $10\%$ by dividing by $10$:

$$240\div10$$

$$=24$$

Take $5\%$ as half of that:

$$24\div2$$

$$=12$$

Add the two parts:

$$24+12$$

$$=36$$

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Finding a new percent from a given part: divide the part by its rate to get the base first, then take the new percent of that base. {#wrong-percentage-base}
- Stopping after finding the base: re-read what the question asks before choosing your final number. {#stop-at-the-base}
- Changing a decimal rate to a percent: move the decimal point $2$ places right, so $0.75$ becomes $75\%$. {#percent-decimal-shift}
:::
