## Core Concept

### Put a Negative Value in Parentheses

To **substitute** means to replace a letter with its number. When the number is negative, put it in parentheses first. The parentheses keep the minus sign attached to its number.

Let's substitute $x = -2$ into $3x$, $x^2$ and $\dfrac{12}{x}$:

$$3x = 3(-2) = -6$$

$$x^2 = (-2)^2 = 4$$

$$\dfrac{12}{x} = \dfrac{12}{(-2)} = -6$$

Write the parentheses even when they seem extra. They cost nothing, and they stop the mistakes below.

### What an Exponent Applies To

An exponent applies only to what sits right before it. If that is a closing parenthesis, the exponent applies to everything inside the parentheses. If it is a number or a letter, the exponent applies to that number or letter alone.

Compare $(-3)^2$ and $-3^2$:

$$(-3)^2=(-3)\times(-3)=9$$

Here the exponent applies to $-3$, so you multiply two negatives. Same signs give a positive answer.

$$-3^2=-(3\times3)=-9$$

Here the exponent applies to $3$ only. You square $3$ to get $9$, then the minus sign in front makes it $-9$.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: Two side-by-side panels. Left panel: (-3)^2 with an outline around all of "(-3)" joined to the exponent 2, so the exponent reaches the negative sign too, then = (-3) x (-3) = 9. Right panel: -3^2 with an outline around only the "3" joined to the exponent 2, and the minus sign outside the outline, then = -(3 x 3) = -9. -->

:::why
Why does the exponent go first in $-3^2$? Powers come before multiplying, and the minus sign in front works like multiplying by $-1$. So $-3^2$ means $-1\times3^2$, which is $-1\times9=-9$.
:::

:::pair #exponent-scope
Right: if $x=-3$, then $x^2=(-3)^2=9$
Wrong: if $x=-3$, then $x^2=-3^2=-9$
The wrong line left out the parentheses, so the exponent applied to $3$ alone.
:::

:::check
If $a=-4$, find $-a^2$.
:::answer
The answer is $-16$. Put $-4$ in parentheses to get $-(-4)^2$. Square first: $(-4)^2=16$. Then the minus sign in front gives $-16$.
:::

### Powers of Negative Numbers

The count of negative signs tells you the sign of a power, too. When a negative number sits in parentheses, an even power is positive and an odd power is negative.

Compare $(-2)^2$ and $(-2)^3$:

$$(-2)^2=(-2)\times(-2)=4$$

$$(-2)^3=(-2)\times(-2)\times(-2)=-8$$

## Examples

In exam questions, **evaluate** means find the final number answer.

### #1: Two Variables, One Negative

If $x=-2$ and $y=3$, evaluate $x^2-3xy+\dfrac{12}{x}$.

**Solution:**

1. Substitute $x=-2$ and $y=3$ into $x^2-3xy+\dfrac{12}{x}$, putting $-2$ in parentheses.

$$x^2-3xy+\dfrac{12}{x}=(-2)^2-3(-2)(3)+\dfrac{12}{(-2)}$$

2. Do the power. The exponent applies to $-2$, so multiply two negatives.

$$(-2)^2=4$$

3. Do the product. Work out the number first.

$$3\times2\times3=18$$

There is one negative sign, which is odd, so the product is negative.

$$3(-2)(3)=-18$$

4. Do the division. The signs are different, so the answer is negative.

$$\dfrac{12}{(-2)}=-6$$

5. Put each answer back into $x^2-3xy+\dfrac{12}{x}$. Keep the signs between the parts.

$$x^2-3xy+\dfrac{12}{x}=4-(-18)+(-6)$$

6. Subtracting $-18$ is the same as adding $18$.

$$4-(-18)+(-6)=4+18+(-6)$$

7. Add from left to right.

$$4+18=22$$

$$22+(-6)=16$$

The answer is $16$.

:::pair #minus-before-negative
Right: $4-(-18)+(-6)=4+18+(-6)$
Wrong: $4-(-18)+(-6)=4-18+(-6)$
The wrong line kept only one minus sign. A minus sign before $-18$ makes a plus, so the wrong line ends at $-20$ instead of $16$.
:::

### #2: Two Negative Values

If $a=-3$ and $b=-1$, evaluate $2a^2-ab+b^3$.

**Solution:**

1. Substitute $a=-3$ and $b=-1$ into $2a^2-ab+b^3$, putting both values in parentheses.

$$2a^2-ab+b^3=2(-3)^2-(-3)(-1)+(-1)^3$$

2. Do the power of $a$. An even power of a negative is positive.

$$(-3)^2=9$$

3. Do the products. In $(-3)(-1)$ the signs are the same, so the product is positive.

$$2(-3)^2=2\times9$$

$$2\times9=18$$

$$(-3)(-1)=3$$

4. Put each answer back into $2a^2-ab+b^3$. Keep the signs between the parts. The $b^3$ part stays as it is.

$$2a^2-ab+b^3=18-3+(-1)^3$$

:::try
Finish the last step: find the value of $2a^2-ab+b^3$.
:::answer
The answer is $14$. The power is odd, so $(-1)^3=-1$. Then $18-3=15$, and $15+(-1)=14$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- No parentheses on $-3^2$: square $3$ first, then keep the minus ($-9$). {#exponent-scope}
- Substituting a negative value: put it in parentheses first, so $x=-3$ gives $x^2=(-3)^2=9$. {#put-a-negative-value-in-parentheses}
- A minus sign before a negative product: the two signs make a plus, so $4-(-18)=22$. {#minus-before-negative}
:::
