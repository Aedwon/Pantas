## Core Concept

### Splitting a Total into Equal Parts

In a **partitive proportion**, a whole amount is split into shares using a ratio. Think of the ratio numbers as equal boxes.

A ratio of $3:1$ means $3+1=4$ equal boxes in all. One share gets $3$ boxes, and the other gets $1$ box.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal bar split into 4 equal boxes totaling "Php 800". First 3 boxes bracketed as "Share A = 3 parts = Php 600"; last 1 box bracketed as "Share B = 1 part = Php 200" -->

Suppose you split Php $800$ in the ratio $3:1$.

1. Add the ratio parts to count all the equal boxes.

$$3+1=4\text{ parts}$$

2. Divide the total by the total parts to find the size of $1$ box.

$$\dfrac{800}{4}=200$$

3. Multiply by the number of parts in the share you need.

$$3\times200=600$$

The larger share is Php $600$, and the smaller share is Php $200$.

Sometimes a problem gives the size of one share instead of the whole. Suppose a mix uses water and syrup in a ratio of $3:5$, and there are $20$ liters of syrup.

Here $5$ parts equal $20$ liters, so $1$ part is $20\div5=4$ liters. The total is $(3+5)\times4=32$ liters.

:::pair #add-parts-to-amount
Right: $5$ parts $=20$ liters, so $1$ part $=4$ liters and total $=8\times4=32$ liters
Wrong: total $=3+20=23$ liters
Ratio parts and liters are different units. Never add a raw ratio number directly to an amount in liters or pesos.
:::

:::check
A sum of Php $900$ is divided in the ratio $2:3:4$. What is the largest share?
:::answer
The answer is Php $400$. Total parts are $2+3+4=9$, so $1$ part is $900\div9=100$, and $4$ parts are $4\times100=400$.
:::

### Using a Difference Between Shares

When a problem tells you how much larger one share is than another, subtract their ratio parts. That difference in parts equals the given difference in amount.

Suppose two amounts are in the ratio $3:5$, and the larger amount is $18$ more than the smaller amount.

1. Subtract the ratio parts.

$$5-3=2\text{ parts}$$

2. Those $2$ parts equal $18$, so divide to find $1$ part.

$$\dfrac{18}{2}=9$$

3. Multiply $1$ part by the total parts ($3+5=8$) to find the whole.

$$8\times9=72$$

:::why
Why does subtracting the ratio parts work? Every box in the ratio has the exact same size. The larger share has $5$ boxes and the smaller has $3$ boxes, so the extra amount comes from the $5-3=2$ extra boxes.
:::

:::pair #one-share-not-total
Right: total length is $(4+7)\times7=77\text{ meters}$
Wrong: total length is $7\times7=49\text{ meters}$
Here $49\text{ meters}$ is only the longer piece. Re-read the question to see if it asks for one piece or the total.
:::

Watch the order of names in every ratio problem. A blue-to-yellow ratio of $9:11$ is not the same as $11:9$.

:::pair #reverse-ratio-order
Right: blue to yellow is $9:11$
Wrong: blue to yellow is $11:9$
Match the first number to the first word named, and the second number to the second word named.
:::

:::check
Two numbers are in the ratio $2:7$. Their difference is $35$. What is the smaller number?
:::answer
The answer is $14$. The difference is $7-2=5$ parts, so $1$ part is $35\div5=7$, and the smaller number is $2\times7=14$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Splitting a Total by Ratio Parts

Three business partners divide Php $120,000$ in the ratio $2:3:5$. How much is the share corresponding to $5$ parts?

**Solution:**

1. Add the ratio parts to get the total number of parts.

$$2+3+5=10$$

2. Divide the total money by $10$ to find the value of $1$ part.

$$\dfrac{120,000}{10}=12,000$$

3. Multiply $1$ part by $5$ for the largest share.

$$5\times12,000=60,000$$

The share is Php $60,000$.

**Check:** Find the other two shares: $2\times12,000=24,000$ and $3\times12,000=36,000$. Add all three: $24,000+36,000+60,000=120,000$, which matches the total.

### #2: Finding a Total from a Difference

A piece of copper wire is cut into two pieces in the ratio $4:7$. If the longer piece is $21$ meters longer than the shorter piece, what was the original length of the wire?

**Solution:**

1. Find the difference in ratio parts.

$$7-4=3$$

2. The $3$-part difference equals $21$ meters, so find $1$ part.

$$\dfrac{21}{3}=7\text{ meters}$$

3. Find the total number of ratio parts in the whole wire.

$$4+7=11$$

:::try
Finish the last step: multiply the total parts by $7$ meters to find the original length.
:::answer
The answer is $77$ meters. Multiply $11\times7=77$ meters.

**Check:** The shorter piece is $4\times7=28$ meters, and the longer piece is $7\times7=49$ meters. Their difference is $49-28=21$ meters, and their sum is $28+49=77$ meters.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

- **Partitive variable.** Write a ratio $2:3:4$ as $2x$, $3x$, and $4x$. Then you can write a quick equation for either a sum ($9x=\text{total}$) or a difference ($4x-2x=\text{difference}$).
- **Joining two ratios.** If $a:b=3:4$ and $b:c=5:6$, make the shared letter $b$ the same number ($20$). Scale the first ratio by $5$ to get $15:20$ and the second by $4$ to get $20:24$, giving $a:b:c=15:20:24$.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Mixing ratio parts with real units: convert $1$ ratio part into pesos or meters before adding or subtracting. {#add-parts-to-amount}
- Asked for the whole total: add all ratio parts, do not stop at the largest single share. {#one-share-not-total}
- Writing or reading a ratio: keep the numbers in the same word order the question names. {#reverse-ratio-order}
:::
