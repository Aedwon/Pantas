## Core Concept

Fractions, decimals, and percentages can show the same quantity in different forms.

### Fractions and Conversions

A **fraction** shows division. Its **numerator** is the top number. Its **denominator** is the bottom number, and it cannot be $0$.

When a fraction describes part of a whole, the denominator gives the number of equal parts in the whole. The numerator counts the parts taken.

A **decimal** uses place value based on $10$. The first places after the decimal point are tenths, hundredths, and thousandths.

A **percentage** means "per hundred" or "out of $100$." The symbol $\%$ means percent.

To turn a fraction into a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator. Some answers end. Others have digits that repeat forever.

A **terminating decimal** ends after a fixed number of decimal places. To turn it into a fraction, write its digits without the decimal point over the matching place value.

Use $10$ for $1$ decimal place, $100$ for $2$, or $1000$ for $3$. For more places, continue adding zeros to the denominator. This method applies to terminating decimals, not to decimals that repeat forever.

"Simplify" means write an equal fraction whose numerator and denominator share no factor greater than $1$. Divide both by the same nonzero common factor until no further reduction is possible.

To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by $100$. To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by $100$ and attach the percent sign.

To turn a percentage into a fraction, write the percent number over $100$ and simplify.

$$p\%=\dfrac{p}{100}$$

To turn a fraction into a percentage, divide the numerator by the denominator. Then multiply the decimal by $100$ and attach the percent sign.

### Percentage Amount, Rate, and Base

The **base** is the whole or reference amount from which a percentage is taken. The **part** is the amount found by taking that percentage. The **rate** tells how much of the base to take.

Use the decimal form of the rate in these equations:

$$\text{Part}=\text{Rate}\times\text{Base}$$

$$\text{Base}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Rate}}$$

$$\text{Rate}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Base}}$$

Finding the base by division needs a nonzero rate. Finding the rate needs a nonzero base.

Look for the amount after "of." In "$30\%$ of $200$," the base is $200$. In "what percent of $80$ is $20$," the base is $80$ and the part is $20$.

For a discount, multiply the rate by the price before that discount. Subtract the discount amount to find the new price. A later discount uses that new price as its base.

For the total discount rate, divide the total price drop by the original price. A starting price of $100$ can help when only percentage discounts are given. It stands for the full original price, not an actual price stated in the problem.

"$120\%$ of" means take $1.20$ times the base. "$120\%$ more than" means add that increase to the base, giving $2.20$ times the base.

### Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Fractions must have a common denominator before you add or subtract them. This makes the parts the same size.

Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by the same nonzero number to make the denominators match. Then add or subtract the numerators. Keep the common denominator and simplify.

### Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

$$\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ac}{bd}$$

The denominators $b$ and $d$ must not be $0$. Multiplying by a fraction of a whole finds that fraction of the amount. You may simplify common factors before or after multiplying.

### Dividing Fractions

The **reciprocal** of a nonzero fraction is found by swapping its numerator and denominator. A fraction times its reciprocal equals $1$.

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. Multiplying both numbers in the division by that reciprocal makes the divisor $1$ and keeps the answer unchanged.

$$\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}$$

Here, $b$ and $d$ must not be $0$. The divisor must also be nonzero, so $c$ must not be $0$.

### Multiplying and Dividing Decimals

To multiply decimals, first multiply as if the numbers were whole numbers. Count the total decimal places in both factors. Put that many decimal places in the product, adding zeros on the left if needed.

The **dividend** is the number being divided. The **divisor** is the number you divide by. The **quotient** is the division answer.

To divide by a nonzero decimal, move its decimal point right until it becomes a whole number. Move the dividend's decimal point the same number of places, then divide.

Moving both points the same distance multiplies both numbers by the same power of $10$. This keeps the quotient unchanged. Division by $0$ is undefined.

## Examples

### #1: Decimal to Fraction

"Express" means write the same value in the form named by the question.

Express $0.125$ as a fraction in its simplest form.

**Solution:**

1. There are $3$ decimal places. Write the digits over $1000$.

$$0.125=\dfrac{125}{1000}$$

2. Divide both numbers by $125$.

$$\dfrac{125}{1000}=\dfrac{1}{8}$$

### #2: Percent to Decimal and Fraction

Express $35\%$ as a decimal and as a fraction in simplest form.

**Solution:**

1. Divide by $100$ for the decimal.

$$35\div100=0.35$$

2. Write the percent number over $100$.

$$35\%=\dfrac{35}{100}$$

3. Divide both numbers by $5$.

$$\dfrac{35}{100}=\dfrac{7}{20}$$

### #3: Fraction to Decimal and Percent

Express $\dfrac{3}{8}$ as a decimal and a percentage.

**Solution:**

1. Divide the numerator by the denominator.

$$3\div8=0.375$$

2. Multiply by $100$ and attach the percent sign.

$$0.375\times100=37.5$$

3. The answers are $0.375$ and $37.5\%$.

### #4: Mixed Forms

"Evaluate" means find the final number answer.

Evaluate $\dfrac{3}{4}+0.50-25\%$ and express the answer as a decimal.

**Solution:**

1. Convert the fraction to a decimal.

$$3\div4=0.75$$

2. Convert the percentage to a decimal.

$$25\div100=0.25$$

3. Add and subtract from left to right.

$$0.75+0.50-0.25=1.00$$

### #5: Unlike Denominators

Find $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{2}{3}-\dfrac{1}{6}$.

**Solution:**

1. Use $12$ as a common denominator. Multiply the top and bottom by $3$, $4$, and $2$, respectively.

$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{12}$$

$$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{8}{12}$$

$$\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{2}{12}$$

2. Add and subtract the numerators.

$$\dfrac{9+8-2}{12}=\dfrac{15}{12}$$

3. Divide both numbers by $3$.

$$\dfrac{15}{12}=\dfrac{5}{4}$$

### #6: Fraction Multiplication

A recipe uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of an ingredient per batch. How much is needed for $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a batch?

**Solution:**

1. Find $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the full-batch amount.

$$\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{6}{12}$$

2. Simplify.

$$\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac{1}{2}$$

3. The recipe needs $\dfrac{1}{2}$ cup.

### #7: Decimal Multiplication

Find $2.4\times0.35$.

**Solution:**

1. Multiply without decimal points.

$$24\times35=840$$

2. The factors have $1+2=3$ decimal places in all. Place the decimal $3$ places from the right.

$$2.4\times0.35=0.840$$

3. The answer is $0.84$.

### #8: Decimal Division

Find $4.8\div0.6$.

**Solution:**

1. Move both decimal points $1$ place right.

$$4.8\div0.6=48\div6$$

2. Divide.

$$48\div6=8$$

### #9: Finding the Percentage Base

If $40\%$ of a number is $72$, what is $15\%$ of the same number?

**Solution:**

1. Let $x$ be the unknown base. The known part is $72$ and the rate is $0.40$.

$$72=0.40x$$

2. Divide the part by the rate.

$$x=72\div0.40=180$$

3. Find $15\%$ of that same base.

$$0.15\times180=27$$

### #10: Fraction Division to Find a Rate

What percent of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is $\dfrac{1}{4}$?

**Solution:**

1. The base is $\dfrac{5}{8}$. Divide the part by the base to find the decimal rate $P$.

$$P=\dfrac{1}{4}\div\dfrac{5}{8}$$

2. Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.

$$P=\dfrac{1}{4}\times\dfrac{8}{5}$$

3. Multiply and simplify.

$$P=\dfrac{8}{20}=\dfrac{2}{5}=0.4$$

4. Convert the decimal rate to a percentage.

$$0.4\times100=40$$

5. The answer is $40\%$.

### #11: Successive Percentages

A store applies a $20\%$ discount on a jacket. It then applies an additional $25\%$ discount to the discounted price. What is the equivalent total percentage discount from the original price?

**Solution:**

1. Use $100$ to stand for the original price. Find the first discount.

$$0.20\times100=20$$

2. Subtract it from the original price.

$$100-20=80$$

3. The second discount uses $80$ as its base.

$$0.25\times80=20$$

4. Find the final price.

$$80-20=60$$

5. Find the total drop and compare it with the original price.

$$100-60=40$$

$$\dfrac{40}{100}\times100\%=40\%$$

### #12: Percent Of and Percent More Than

An office processed $200$ forms last week. This week's target is $120\%$ of last week's total. Next week's target is $120\%$ more than last week's total. How many forms are in each target?

**Solution:**

1. Both targets use last week's $200$ forms as the base. For this week, multiply the base by $1.20$.

$$1.20\times200=240$$

2. For next week, find the increase of $120\%$ of the base.

$$1.20\times200=240$$

3. Add that increase to the base.

$$200+240=440$$

4. This week's target is $240$ forms. Next week's target is $440$ forms.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### Switch the Percent and the Base

For numbers $x$ and $y$, $x\%$ of $y$ equals $y\%$ of $x$. Both calculations multiply $x$ by $y$ and divide by $100$.

For example, $16\%$ of $25$ equals $25\%$ of $16$. The second form may be easier because $25\%$ is one-fourth.

$$16\div4=4$$

### Common Fraction Equivalents

- $\dfrac{1}{2}=50\%$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}=33\dfrac{1}{3}\%\approx33.3\%$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}=25\%$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}=20\%$
- $\dfrac{1}{8}=12.5\%$

The symbol $\approx$ means "is about equal to." A rounded decimal is not always the exact value.

### Find $10\%$ First

To find $15\%$ mentally, find $10\%$, then half of that amount for $5\%$. Add the two parts.

For $80$, $10\%$ is $8$ and $5\%$ is $4$. So $15\%$ of $80$ is $12$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Using the Wrong Base

> Find the reference amount before multiplying.

In "$40\%$ of $180$," the base is $180$. Using $40$ as the base changes what the question asks.

### #2: Adding Discounts Together

> Successive discounts use different bases.

A $20\%$ discount followed by a $30\%$ discount does not give a $50\%$ total discount. The second discount acts on the smaller price left after the first.

### #3: Moving the Decimal Point Only Once

> $5\%$ is $0.05$, not $0.50$.

Dividing by $100$ moves the decimal point $2$ places left. Moving it only $1$ place gives a rate that is too large.

### #4: Confusing Percent Of with Percent More

> "$120\%$ of" and "$120\%$ more than" give different amounts.

For a salary that is $120\%$ of an earlier salary, multiply by $1.20$. For a salary that is $120\%$ more, multiply by $2.20$. Multiplying by $1.20$ in the second case leaves out the earlier salary.

### #5: Adding Denominators

> Addition does not change the size of the equal parts.

For $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}$, the answer is $\dfrac{2}{3}$, not $\dfrac{2}{6}$. Keep the common denominator and add only the numerators.
