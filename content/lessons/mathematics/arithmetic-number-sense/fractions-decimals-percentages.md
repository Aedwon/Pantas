## Core Concept

Fractions, decimals, and percentages are just three ways to wear the same outfit. They all show a part of a whole.

A **fraction** compares a part to a total using a top number and a bottom number. A **decimal** uses a dot to show parts of 10, 100, or 1000. A **percentage** simply means "out of 100."

College entrance exams love to mix these up. A question might give you a fraction but put percentages in the choices. You must learn to switch between them quickly.

To turn a percentage into a decimal, just move the dot. To turn a fraction into a percentage, divide the top by the bottom, then move the dot. It is all about knowing how to translate one form into another.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

Use the Alphabet Rule to remember which way to move the decimal point. The letter **D** (Decimal) comes before the letter **P** (Percent) in the alphabet.

> Think: **D** to **P** means moving RIGHT in the alphabet. So, to change a Decimal to a Percent, move the dot 2 steps RIGHT.

> Think: **P** to **D** means moving LEFT in the alphabet. So, to change a Percent to a Decimal, move the dot 2 steps LEFT.

When working with fractions, remember what the percent sign looks like.

> The percent sign ($\%$) looks like the number $100$ mixed up. It has a $1$ (the slash) and two $0$s. This reminds you that percent always means "divide by 100."

## Examples

### #1: Basic Conversion

Express $0.125$ as a fraction in its simplest form.

**Solution:**

1. Read the decimal out loud. The last digit is in the "thousandths" place.
2. This means the number is $125$ over $1000$.

$$\dfrac{125}{1000}$$


3. Divide the top and bottom by their greatest common factor. Both divide by $25$, which gives $5$ over $40$.
4. Divide by $5$ again to get the final answer.

$$\dfrac{1}{8}$$



### #2: Mixed Operations

Evaluate $\dfrac{3}{4} + 0.50 - 25\%$ and express the answer as a decimal.

**Solution:**

1. You cannot do math when the numbers wear different outfits. Change them all to decimals/fractions/percentages first. For this example, let's use decimals since the final answer is also in decimal.
2. Change $\dfrac{3}{4}$ to a decimal. Think of $3$ quarters in money. That is $0.75$.
3. Change $25\%$ to a decimal. Move the dot two steps to the left (P to D rule). This gives $0.25$.
4. Rewrite the math problem.

$$0.75 + 0.50 - 0.25$$


5. Add and subtract from left to right.

$$1.25 - 0.25 = 1.00$$


6. The answer is $1.00$.

### #3: "Of" Translation

If $40\%$ of a number is $72$, what is $15\%$ of the same number?

**Solution:**

1. Translate the English into math. The word "of" means multiply. The word "is" means equals. Let $x$ be the missing number.
2. Change $40\%$ to a decimal ($0.40$). Write the equation.

$$0.40 \times x = 72$$


3. Solve for $x$ by dividing both sides by $0.40$.

$$x = \dfrac{72}{0.40}$$


4. Move the dot in the bottom and top by two spaces to make the math easier.

$$x = \dfrac{7200}{40} = 180$$


5. The number is $180$. Now, find $15\%$ of $180$.
6. Multiply $0.15$ by $180$.

$$0.15 \times 180 = 27$$


7. The answer is $27$.

### #4: Complex Fraction to Percent

What percent of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is $\dfrac{1}{4}$?

**Solution:**

1. Translate the words into math. Let $P$ be the missing percent.

$$P \times \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{1}{4}$$


2. To leave $P$ alone, multiply both sides by the flipped version of $\dfrac{5}{8}$, which is $\dfrac{8}{5}$.

$$P = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{8}{5}$$


3. Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms.

$$P = \dfrac{8}{20}$$


4. Change the fraction to a percent. First, make the bottom $100$. Multiply top and bottom by $5$.

$$P = \dfrac{40}{100}$$


5. Anything over $100$ is a percent. The answer is $40\%$.

### #5: Successive Percentages

A retail store owner applies a $20\%$ discount on a jacket. During a clearance sale, an additional $25\%$ discount is applied to the discounted price. What is the total equivalent percentage discount from the original price?

**Solution:**

1. When a problem does not give you a starting price, use $100$. It makes percent math very easy. Let the original jacket cost $100$.
2. Take away the first $20\%$ discount. $20\%$ of $100$ is $20$.

$$100 - 20 = 80$$


3. The new price is $80$. Now, take away the second discount. You must find $25\%$ of $80$, not the original $100$.
4. Change $25\%$ to a fraction. It is $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
5. Find $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of $80$.

$$\dfrac{1}{4} \times 80 = 20$$


6. Subtract this second discount from $80$.

$$80 - 20 = 60$$


7. The final price is $60$. The question asks for the *total discount* from the start.

$$100 - 60 = 40$$


8. Since we started with $100$, the total drop is $40$. The answer is a $40\%$ total discount.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**The Percent Switcheroo**.
Did you know that $x\%$ of $y$ is exactly the same as $y\%$ of $x$? If the exam asks for $16\%$ of $25$, do not panic. Switch it! Ask yourself for $25\%$ of $16$ instead. $25\%$ is just one-fourth. One-fourth of $16$ is $4$. You just solved it in two seconds.

**Memorize the Magic Fractions**.
Do not waste time dividing on the scratch paper. Memorize these common exam fractions:

* $\dfrac{1}{2} = 50\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{3} = 33.3\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{5} = 20\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{8} = 12.5\%$

**Find $10\%$ First**.
If you need to find $15\%$ of a number, do not multiply by $0.15$. Find $10\%$ first by moving the decimal one spot left. Then cut that number in half to get $5\%$. Add the two pieces together. For example, $15\%$ of $80$: $10\%$ is $8$. Half of that is $4$. Add $8$ and $4$ to get $12$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Adding Discounts Together

> Never just add successive discounts. A $20\%$ drop followed by a $30\%$ drop is NOT a $50\%$ drop.

Test-makers always put the added number in the choices as a trap. You must apply the first percent, get the new total, and then apply the second percent to that *new* total.

### #2: Moving the Dot Only Once

> $5\%$ is $0.05$, not $0.50$. Always move the dot exactly two spaces, even if you must add a zero.

Students rush and write $5\%$ as $0.5$. But $0.5$ is actually $50\%$. If there are not enough numbers when moving the dot left, put a zero in the empty space.

### #3: Confusing "Percent Of" with "Percent More"

> "$120\%$ of a number" is not the same as "$120\%$ more than a number." Read carefully!

If your salary is $120\%$ of last year's salary, you multiply by $1.20$. If your salary is $120\%$ MORE than last year's salary, you add it to the original $100\%$. You would multiply by $2.20$. Always circle the words "of" or "more than" in the exam booklet.
