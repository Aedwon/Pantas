## Core Concept

Fractions, decimals, and percentages are different ways to show the same quantity.

### Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages

In a fraction, the **numerator** is the top number. The **denominator** is the bottom number, and it cannot be $0$.

A **decimal** uses place value based on powers of $10$. A **percentage** means "per hundred" or "out of $100$."

To turn a fraction into a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.

To turn a decimal into a fraction, use the decimal's place value as the denominator. A decimal ending in tenths uses $10$, hundredths uses $100$, and thousandths uses $1000$.

"Simplify" means reduce a fraction to an equal fraction until the numerator and denominator share no factor greater than $1$.

To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by $100$. To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by $100$.

To turn a percentage into a fraction, write the percent number over $100$ and simplify.

$$p\%=\dfrac{p}{100}$$

To turn a fraction into a percentage, first divide the numerator by the denominator. Then multiply the decimal by $100$.

### Percentage Amount, Rate, and Base

The **base** is the whole or reference amount that a percentage is taken from. The **part** is the amount that the percentage gives.

Write the percentage rate as a decimal. Then:

$$\text{Part}=\text{Rate}\times\text{Base}$$

If the part and rate are known, solve the same equation for the base.

If a question asks what percent one amount is of another, use:

$$\text{Rate}=\dfrac{\text{Part}}{\text{Base}}$$

The base must not be $0$. Look for the amount after the word "of" or the whole amount being compared.

### Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Fractions need a common denominator before you add or subtract them. Rewrite the fractions with the same denominator, then add or subtract the numerators.

Keep the common denominator. Simplify the result when possible.

### Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

$$\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ac}{bd}$$

The denominators $b$ and $d$ must not be $0$. Simplify before or after multiplying when possible.

### Dividing Fractions

The **reciprocal** of a nonzero fraction is found by swapping its numerator and denominator. To divide by a fraction, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.

$$\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}$$

The divisor must not be $0$. This means $c$ must not be $0$.

### Multiplying and Dividing Decimals

To multiply decimals, multiply as if the numbers were whole numbers. In the product, place the decimal so the number of decimal places equals the total decimal places in the factors.

To divide by a decimal, move the decimal point in the divisor until the divisor is a whole number. Move the decimal point in the dividend the same number of places, then divide.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

The letter **D** in Decimal comes before **P** in Percent.

> **D** to **P**: multiply by $100$, so the decimal point moves $2$ places right.

> **P** to **D**: divide by $100$, so the decimal point moves $2$ places left.

The percent sign ($\%$) can remind you that percent means "per $100$."

## Examples

### #1: Decimal to Fraction

"Express" means write the same value in the form named by the question.

Express $0.125$ as a fraction in its simplest form.

**Solution:**

1. The last digit is in the thousandths place. Write the decimal over $1000$.

$$0.125=\dfrac{125}{1000}$$

2. Divide the numerator and denominator by $125$.

$$\dfrac{125}{1000}=\dfrac{1}{8}$$

3. The simplest fraction is $\dfrac{1}{8}$.

### #2: Percent to Decimal and Fraction

Express $35\%$ as a decimal and as a fraction in simplest form.

**Solution:**

1. Divide by $100$ to get the decimal.

$$35\div100=0.35$$

2. Write the percent number over $100$.

$$35\%=\dfrac{35}{100}$$

3. Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by $5$.

$$\dfrac{35}{100}=\dfrac{7}{20}$$

4. The answers are $0.35$ and $\dfrac{7}{20}$.

### #3: Fraction to Decimal and Percent

Express $\dfrac{3}{8}$ as a decimal and a percentage.

**Solution:**

1. Divide the numerator by the denominator.

$$3\div8=0.375$$

2. Multiply the decimal by $100$ to get the percentage.

$$0.375\times100=37.5$$

3. Add the percent sign. The percentage is $37.5\%$.

4. The answers are $0.375$ and $37.5\%$.

### #4: Mixed Operations

"Evaluate" means find the final number answer.

Evaluate $\dfrac{3}{4}+0.50-25\%$ and express the answer as a decimal.

**Solution:**

1. Convert the fraction to a decimal.

$$3\div4=0.75$$

2. Convert the percentage to a decimal.

$$25\div100=0.25$$

3. Calculate from left to right.

$$0.75+0.50-0.25=1.00$$

4. The answer is $1.00$.

### #5: Percentage Base

If $40\%$ of a number is $72$, what is $15\%$ of the same number?

**Solution:**

1. Let $x$ be the base. Use part equals rate times base.

$$72=0.40x$$

2. Solve for the base.

$$x=\dfrac{72}{0.40}=180$$

3. Find $15\%$ of $180$.

$$0.15\times180=27$$

4. The answer is $27$.

### #6: Complex Fraction to Percent

What percent of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is $\dfrac{1}{4}$?

**Solution:**

1. Let $P$ be the decimal form of the unknown rate.

$$P\times\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{1}{4}$$

2. Divide by $\dfrac{5}{8}$ by multiplying by its reciprocal.

$$P=\dfrac{1}{4}\times\dfrac{8}{5}$$

3. Simplify.

$$P=\dfrac{2}{5}=0.4$$

4. Convert the decimal to a percentage.

$$0.4\times100=40$$

5. Add the percent sign. The answer is $40\%$.

### #7: Unlike Denominators

Find $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{2}{3}-\dfrac{1}{6}$.

**Solution:**

1. Use $12$ as a common denominator.

$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{12}$$

$$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{8}{12}$$

$$\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{2}{12}$$

2. Add and subtract the numerators.

$$\dfrac{9}{12}+\dfrac{8}{12}-\dfrac{2}{12}=\dfrac{15}{12}$$

3. Simplify.

$$\dfrac{15}{12}=\dfrac{5}{4}$$

4. The answer is $\dfrac{5}{4}$.

### #8: Fraction Multiplication

A recipe uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of an ingredient for one batch. You make $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a batch. How much of the ingredient is needed?

**Solution:**

1. Multiply the fractions.

$$\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{6}{12}$$

2. Simplify.

$$\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac{1}{2}$$

3. The recipe needs $\dfrac{1}{2}$ cup.

### #9: Decimal Multiplication

Find $2.4\times0.35$.

**Solution:**

1. Multiply without decimal points.

$$24\times35=840$$

2. The factors have a total of three decimal places. Place the decimal three places from the right.

$$2.4\times0.35=0.840$$

3. The answer is $0.84$.

### #10: Decimal Division

Find $4.8\div0.6$.

**Solution:**

1. Move both decimal points one place right so the divisor becomes a whole number.

$$4.8\div0.6=48\div6$$

2. Divide.

$$48\div6=8$$

3. The answer is $8$.

### #11: Successive Percentages

A retail store applies a $20\%$ discount on a jacket. During a clearance sale, an additional $25\%$ discount is applied to the discounted price. What is the total equivalent percentage discount from the original price?

**Solution:**

1. Use $100$ as a convenient original price.
2. After the first discount, the price is:

$$100\times0.80=80$$

3. Find the second discount amount.

$$80\times0.25=20$$

4. Subtract that amount from the discounted price.

$$80-20=60$$

5. Find the total drop from the original price.

$$100-60=40$$

6. The equivalent discount is $40\%$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

### The Percent Switcheroo

For real numbers $x$ and $y$, $x\%$ of $y$ equals $y\%$ of $x$ because both equal:

$$\dfrac{xy}{100}$$

For example, $16\%$ of $25$ equals $25\%$ of $16$.

$$0.16\times25=4$$

### Common Fraction Equivalents

* $\dfrac{1}{2}=50\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{3}=33\dfrac{1}{3}\%\approx33.3\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{4}=25\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{5}=20\%$
* $\dfrac{1}{8}=12.5\%$

### Find $10\%$ First

To find $15\%$ of a number mentally, find $10\%$ first. Then find half of that amount for $5\%$ and add the two parts.

For $80$:

$$10\%\text{ of }80=8$$

$$5\%\text{ of }80=4$$

$$15\%\text{ of }80=12$$

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Using the Wrong Base

> A percentage is always taken from a particular base.

In "$40\%$ of $180$," the base is $180$. In a percent-change problem, check whether the starting amount or another stated amount is the base.

### #2: Adding Discounts Together

> Do not simply add successive discounts.

A $20\%$ discount followed by a $30\%$ discount is applied to two different base amounts. It is not a $50\%$ total discount.

### #3: Moving the Decimal Point Only Once

> $5\%$ is $0.05$, not $0.50$.

Dividing a percentage by $100$ moves the decimal point $2$ places left.

### #4: Confusing Percent Of with Percent More

> "$120\%$ of a number" is not the same as "$120\%$ more than a number."

If a salary is $120\%$ of an earlier salary, multiply by $1.20$. If it is $120\%$ more than the earlier salary, the new amount is $220\%$ of the original, so multiply by $2.20$.
