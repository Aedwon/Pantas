## Core Concept

### Reading Rows, Columns, and Units

A table organizes numbers into horizontal **rows** and vertical **columns**. A row label names one category, and a column label names another. To find one entry, trace across the named row and down the named column until they meet.

Always read the unit note next to the table title or column header before reporting an answer. A table in "thousands of pesos" scales every printed number by $1,000$. A table in "millions of pesos" scales every number by $1,000,000$.

Suppose a table labeled "Office Purchases, in thousands of pesos" lists chairs as $12$, desks as $8$, and lamps as $5$.

1. Add the printed entries first.

$$12+8+5=25$$

2. Multiply by $1,000$ for the stated unit.

$$25\times1,000=25,000$$

The combined expense is $25,000$ pesos.

:::why
Why convert the unit after adding? Adding small printed numbers like $12+8+5=25$ is faster and reduces zero errors. Multiplying by $1,000$ at the end scales every entry at once.
:::

:::pair #missing-unit-scale
Right: $25\times1,000=25,000\text{ pesos}$
Wrong: $25\text{ pesos}$
The wrong answer used the printed total without the "thousands of pesos" unit from the table note. Check the unit caption before picking your final number.
:::

:::check
A revenue table labeled "in millions of pesos" lists $3.2$ for Product A and $4.8$ for Product B. What is their combined revenue in pesos?
:::answer
The answer is $8,000,000$ pesos. Add $3.2+4.8=8$, then multiply by $1,000,000$ to get $8,000,000$.
:::

### Finding Totals and Differences

For a total, add only the rows or columns the question asks for. Check each heading carefully so an entry from a neighboring column does not slip into your sum.

Suppose an expense table has a Paper column listing $12,000$, $15,000$, and $11,000$, right next to an Ink column listing $18,000$, $22,000$, and $19,000$. To find the three-month Paper total, add only the three Paper numbers:

$$12,000+15,000+11,000=38,000$$

:::pair #wrong-column-entry
Right: $12,000+15,000+11,000=38,000$
Wrong: $12,000+15,000+19,000=46,000$
The wrong sum grabbed $19,000$ from the neighboring Ink column instead of $11,000$ from Paper. Trace the column heading down for every single entry.
:::

For a difference ("how many more" or "by how much does X exceed Y"), find each group total first if needed, then subtract the smaller value from the larger one unless a signed change is asked for.

Suppose three centers list Female applicants as $1,500$, $950$, and $750$, and Male applicants as $1,200$, $800$, and $650$.

$$1,500+950+750=3,200$$

$$1,200+800+650=2,650$$

$$3,200-2,650=550$$

There are $550$ more female applicants than male applicants.

:::pair #reverse-difference-order
Right: $3,200-2,650=550$
Wrong: $2,650-3,200=-550$
The wrong line reversed the subtraction order. When a question asks how many more, subtract the smaller count from the larger count to get a positive answer.
:::

:::check
In a crop table, Rice yields are $450$, $520$, and $380$ metric tons, and Corn yields are $300$, $280$, and $410$ metric tons. By how many metric tons does total Rice exceed total Corn?
:::answer
The answer is $360$ metric tons. Rice totals $450+520+380=1,350$, Corn totals $300+280+410=990$, and $1,350-990=360$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Finding a Row-Column Value

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: table titled "Quarterly Office Expenses, in thousands of pesos" with columns Quarter 1, Quarter 2, Quarter 3, Quarter 4 and rows Administration 12, 15, 18, 11; Operations 20, 24, 27, 23; Training 8, 10, 9, 12 -->

Use the table. What was the Operations expense in Quarter $3$, in pesos?

**Solution:**

1. Find the Operations row.
2. Trace across to the Quarter $3$ column. The cell where they meet reads $27$.
3. Multiply by $1,000$ because the table unit is thousands of pesos.

$$27\times1,000=27,000$$

The Operations expense was $27,000$ pesos.

**Check:** In the Operations row, Quarter $2$ is $24$ and Quarter $4$ is $23$, so $27$ thousand ($27,000$ pesos) fits right in line with the rest of that row.

### #2: Finding a Total from a Table

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: table titled "Quarterly Office Expenses, in thousands of pesos" with columns Quarter 1, Quarter 2, Quarter 3, Quarter 4 and rows Administration 12, 15, 18, 11; Operations 20, 24, 27, 23; Training 8, 10, 9, 12 -->

Use the table. What was the total Administration expense for the four quarters, in pesos?

**Solution:**

1. Read all four entries across the Administration row: $12$, $15$, $18$, and $11$.
2. Add the four numbers.

$$12+15+18+11=56$$

:::try
Finish the last step: convert $56$ into pesos using the table's unit.
:::answer
The answer is $56,000$ pesos. Multiply $56\times1,000=56,000$.

**Check:** Each quarter's Administration expense is near $14$ thousand ($11$ to $18$), and $4\times14,000=56,000$ pesos, which matches.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Read the title, row labels, column labels, and unit note before calculating anything. Mark or trace only the row and column named in the question so extra numbers do not pull your eye away.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Table unit in thousands or millions: multiply your sum or cell reading by the stated unit before answering. {#missing-unit-scale}
- Summing down a column or across a row: double-check the label on every entry so an adjacent column does not slip in. {#wrong-column-entry}
- "How many more" or "exceed" questions: subtract the smaller total from the larger total. {#reverse-difference-order}
:::
