## Core Concept

### Finding a Group's Percentage of the Matching Total

A **percentage of a total** tells what part of a whole group belongs to one subgroup. When the total is nonzero, use:

$$\text{Percentage}=\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}\times100\%$$

Read carefully to identify the **matching total** named in the question. Do not divide by just the other subgroup, and do not pick a total from a different row or group.

In Quarter $1$, Facility X uses $120$ megawatt-hours and Facility Y uses $180$ megawatt-hours. Find what percent of the *combined* Quarter $1$ total Facility Y used.

1. Add both facilities to get the matching combined total:

$$120+180=300$$

2. Divide Facility Y ($180$) by that combined total ($300$):

$$\dfrac{180}{300}=0.60$$

3. Multiply by $100$ to write it as a percent:

$$0.60\times100=60$$

Facility Y used $60\%$ of the combined Quarter $1$ total.

A bracket under Facility Y reads "180 / 300 = 0.60, 60%, out of the whole bar, not X alone", so the denominator is the combined total. -->

:::pair #other-part-as-total
Right: $\dfrac{180}{120+180}=\dfrac{180}{300}=0.6$, which is $60\%$
Wrong: $\dfrac{180}{120}=1.5$, which is $150\%$
The wrong line divided Facility Y by Facility X alone instead of their combined total. When asked for a share of a combined group, add all parts to form the denominator first.
:::

To combine two separate groups that do not overlap (such as two branches), add the parts and add the totals first, then divide. Do not add their two percentages, because each is a share of a different group.

At one branch, $6$ of $10$ ratings are excellent. At another, $9$ of $30$ are. Add the parts, then add the totals:

$$6+9=15\text{ excellent},\qquad 10+30=40\text{ ratings}$$

$$\dfrac{15}{40}=0.375$$

Together, $37.5\%$ of the ratings are excellent.

:::check
A camp has $60$ Grade 6 pupils and $40$ Grade 5 pupils. Of the Grade 6 pupils, $15$ wear glasses. What percent of the Grade 6 pupils wear glasses?
:::answer
The answer is $25\%$. The matching total is the $60$ Grade 6 pupils, so $\dfrac{15}{60}=0.25$, and $0.25\times100=25$.
:::

### Writing Ratios in Order and Lowest Terms

A **ratio** such as $A:B$ compares two amounts in that exact order. Put the quantity named first on the left and the quantity named second on the right.

If the amounts measure the same kind of thing, write them in the **same unit** before you reduce the ratio. To compare $1.5$ million pesos for Road Works with $750$ thousand pesos for Drainage, convert $1.5$ million into thousands first:

$$1.5\times1,000=1,500\text{ thousand pesos}$$

Now both amounts are in thousands: $1,500:750$.

To reduce a whole-number ratio to **lowest terms**, divide both sides by their **greatest common divisor** (the largest whole number that divides both numbers with no remainder). Reduce $1,500:750$ by dividing both sides by $750$:

$$1,500\div750=2$$

$$750\div750=1$$

The ratio in lowest terms is $2:1$.

:::why
Why does dividing both numbers by the same nonzero value keep the ratio true? Splitting both amounts into equal-sized groups keeps their relative size unchanged, just like reducing $\dfrac{50}{75}$ to $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::

Product A sells $50$ units and Product B sells $75$ units. Write the ratio of A to B.

:::pair #reverse-ratio-order
Right: Product A to Product B $=50:75=2:3$
Wrong: Product A to Product B $=75:50=3:2$
The wrong ratio swapped the order. Write the numbers in the exact order the question names the items.
:::

:::pair #mismatched-ratio-units
Right: $1.5\text{ million}:750\text{ thousand}=1,500:750=2:1$
Wrong: $1.5:750=1:500$
The wrong line mixed millions with thousands. Both amounts here are money, so write both in pesos or both in thousands of pesos before simplifying.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Percentage of a Total

In an office of $200$ employees, $50$ work in human resources. What percentage of the workforce works in human resources?

**Solution:**

1. Confirm the matching total: $200$ is the whole workforce, and $50$ is the human resources part. Divide part by total.

$$\dfrac{50}{200}=0.25$$

2. Multiply by $100$ to write it as a percent.

$$0.25\times100=25$$

Human resources makes up $25\%$ of the workforce.

### #2: Simplifying a Ratio

A survey records $40$ satisfied customers and $100$ dissatisfied customers. What is the ratio of satisfied to dissatisfied customers in lowest whole-number terms?

**Solution:**

1. Write the ratio in the requested order, satisfied first and dissatisfied second.

$$40:100$$

2. Both numbers end in $0$, so divide by $10$ first to see a smaller pair.

$$4:10$$

:::try
Finish the last step: divide $4:10$ by the largest number that goes into both $4$ and $10$.
:::answer
The answer is $2:5$. Both $4$ and $10$ divide by $2$, giving $2:5$ (which is dividing $40:100$ by $20$).
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Let question wording guide your setup: "out of" signals part over total, while "$A$ to $B$" fixes the ratio order. To reduce large ratios fast when both terms end in $0$, divide by $10$ first, then look for any remaining common divisor.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Finding a percentage of a combined group: put the combined total in the denominator, not just the other subgroup. {#other-part-as-total}
- Writing a ratio: keep the numbers in the exact order the question names them. {#reverse-ratio-order}
- Ratios with mixed units like millions and thousands: convert both amounts to the same unit before reducing. {#mismatched-ratio-units}
:::
