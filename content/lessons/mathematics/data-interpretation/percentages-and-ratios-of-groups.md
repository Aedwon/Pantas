## Core Concept

### Finding a Group's Percentage of the Matching Total

A **percentage of a total** tells what part of a whole group belongs to one subgroup. When the total is nonzero, use:

$$\text{Percentage}=\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}\times100\%$$

Read carefully to identify the **matching total** named in the question. Do not divide by just the other subgroup, and do not pick a total from a different row or group.

Suppose in Quarter $1$, Facility X uses $120$ megawatt-hours and Facility Y uses $180$ megawatt-hours. To find what percent of the *combined* Quarter $1$ total was used by Facility Y:

1. Add both facilities to get the matching combined total:

$$120+180=300$$

2. Divide Facility Y ($180$) by that combined total ($300$):

$$\dfrac{180}{300}=0.60$$

3. Multiply by $100\%$:

$$0.60\times100\%=60\%$$

Facility Y used $60\%$ of the combined Quarter $1$ total.

:::pair #other-part-as-total
Right: $\dfrac{180}{120+180}\times100\%=\dfrac{180}{300}\times100\%=60\%$
Wrong: $\dfrac{180}{120}\times100\%=150\%$
The wrong line divided Facility Y by Facility X alone instead of their combined total. When asked for a share of a combined group, add all parts to form the denominator first.
:::

When combining two separate subgroups (such as two branches), add the parts and add the totals first before dividing. Never add two separate group percentages together.

:::check
In a survey, $30$ of $50$ customers at Branch A and $10$ of $50$ customers at Branch B rate service excellent. What percentage of all surveyed customers gives this rating?
:::answer
The answer is $40\%$. Add the parts $30+10=40$ and add the totals $50+50=100$, then $\dfrac{40}{100}\times100\%=40\%$.
:::

### Writing Ratios in Order and Lowest Terms

A **ratio** such as $A:B$ compares two amounts in that exact order. Put the quantity named first on the left and the quantity named second on the right.

Make sure both quantities use the **same unit** before writing or reducing the ratio. For example, to compare $1.5$ million pesos for Road Works with $750$ thousand pesos for Drainage, convert $1.5$ million into thousands first:

$$1.5\times1,000=1,500\text{ thousand pesos}$$

Now both amounts are in thousands: $1,500:750$.

To reduce a whole-number ratio to **lowest terms**, divide both sides by their **greatest common divisor** (the largest whole number that divides both numbers with no remainder).

$$1,500\div750=2$$

$$750\div750=1$$

The ratio in lowest terms is $2:1$.

:::why
Why does dividing both numbers by the same value keep the ratio true? Splitting both amounts into equal-sized groups keeps their relative size unchanged, just like reducing $\dfrac{50}{75}$ to $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::

:::pair #reverse-ratio-order
Right: Product A to Product B $=50:75=2:3$
Wrong: Product A to Product B $=75:50=3:2$
The wrong ratio swapped the order. Write the numbers in the exact order the question names the items.
:::

:::pair #mismatched-ratio-units
Right: $1.5\text{ million}:750\text{ thousand}=1,500:750=2:1$
Wrong: $1.5:750=1:500$
The wrong line mixed millions with thousands. Always convert both numbers to the same unit before simplifying.
:::

:::check
In a class of $30$ students, $12$ are boys and the rest are girls. What is the ratio of boys to girls in lowest terms?
:::answer
The answer is $2:3$. There are $30-12=18$ girls, so boys to girls is $12:18$. Divide both by $6$ to get $2:3$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Percentage of a Total

In an office of $200$ employees, $50$ work in human resources. What percentage of the workforce works in human resources?

**Solution:**

1. Confirm the matching total: $200$ is the whole workforce, and $50$ is the human resources part. Divide part by total.

$$\dfrac{50}{200}=0.25$$

2. Multiply by $100\%$ to write as a percent.

$$0.25\times100\%=25\%$$

Human resources makes up $25\%$ of the workforce.

**Check:** Four groups of $50$ make $200$, so $50$ is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of $200$, and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ equals $25\%$.

### #2: Simplifying a Ratio

A survey records $60$ satisfied customers and $40$ dissatisfied customers. What is the ratio of satisfied to dissatisfied customers in lowest whole-number terms?

**Solution:**

1. Write the ratio in the requested order, satisfied first and dissatisfied second.

$$60:40$$

2. Both numbers end in $0$, so divide by $10$ first to see a smaller pair.

$$6:4$$

:::try
Finish the last step: divide $6:4$ by the largest number that goes into both $6$ and $4$.
:::answer
The answer is $3:2$. Both $6$ and $4$ divide by $2$, giving $3:2$ (which is dividing $60:40$ by $20$).

**Check:** Multiply $3:2$ back by $20$: $3\times20=60$ and $2\times20=40$. No whole number larger than $1$ divides both $3$ and $2$.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Let question wording guide your setup: "out of" signals part over total, while "$A$ to $B$" fixes the ratio order. To reduce large ratios fast when both terms end in $0$, divide by $10$ first, then look for any remaining common divisor.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Finding a percentage of a combined group: put the combined total in the denominator, never just the other subgroup. {#other-part-as-total}
- Writing a ratio: keep the numbers in the exact order the question names them. {#reverse-ratio-order}
- Ratios with mixed units like millions and thousands: convert both amounts to the same unit before reducing. {#mismatched-ratio-units}
:::
