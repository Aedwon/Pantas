## Core Concept

A polynomial is a math sentence. It has numbers and letters. The letters have powers like $2$ or $3$. The powers must be whole numbers. They cannot be negative. They cannot be fractions.

### Operations on Polynomials

Adding and subtracting is just grouping alike things. You can only combine parts that have the exact same letters and powers. Multiplying means every piece of the first group multiplies with every piece of the second group.

### Special Products

Sometimes polynomials multiply in a predictable pattern. If you memorize the pattern, you can skip the long math. The most common pattern is the difference of two squares. Another is the perfect square trinomial.

### Factoring Techniques

Factoring is multiplying in reverse. You break a big polynomial into smaller pieces. Always look for a common factor first. Then look for patterns. For polynomials with three parts, you usually find numbers that multiply to the last number and add to the middle number.

### Long Division and Synthetic Division

You can divide polynomials just like regular numbers. Long division works for every problem. Synthetic division is a fast shortcut. You only write the numbers, not the letters. But it only works when you divide by a simple group like $x - 1$.

### Remainder and Factor Theorems

These are magic tricks for time-pressured exams. The Remainder Theorem lets you find a remainder without actually dividing. You just put a number in place of the letter. The Factor Theorem says if the remainder is $0$, you found a perfect factor.

## Mnemonics & Memory Aids

> FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

Use this to multiply two groups of two terms. It reminds you exactly which pieces to multiply together so you do not miss any parts.

> Soap: Same, Opposite, Always Positive.

Use this when factoring the sum or difference of two cubes. It tells you exactly what math signs to put in your final answer.

## Examples

### #1: Simplifying Polynomial Operations

If $A = 3x^2 - 2x + 5$ and $B = -x^2 + 4x - 1$, what is the simplified form of $2A - B$?

**Solution:**

1. First, multiply all parts of $A$ by $2$.

$$2(3x^2 - 2x + 5)$$


$$6x^2 - 4x + 10$$


2. Next, write the subtraction problem.

$$(6x^2 - 4x + 10) - (-x^2 + 4x - 1)$$


3. Change the signs of every part in $B$ because you are subtracting.

$$6x^2 - 4x + 10 + x^2 - 4x + 1$$


4. Group the same letters and powers together.

$$7x^2 - 8x + 11$$



### #2: Using Special Products

What is the product of $(x - 3)(x + 3)(x^2 + 9)$?

**Solution:**

1. Look at the first two groups. They are a difference of two squares.
2. Multiply them using the shortcut pattern.

$$x^2 - 9$$


3. Bring down the last group.

$$(x^2 - 9)(x^2 + 9)$$


4. This is another difference of two squares. Use the shortcut again.

$$x^4 - 81$$



### #3: Factoring a Quadratic Expression

Factor the expression completely: $6x^2 - 7x - 3$.

**Solution:**

1. Multiply the first number and the last number. This is called the AC method.

$$6 \times (-3) = -18$$


2. Find two numbers that multiply to $-18$ and add to the middle number $-7$.
3. Those numbers are $-9$ and $2$.
4. Split the middle part using these two numbers.

$$6x^2 - 9x + 2x - 3$$


5. Group them into two pairs and pull out common factors.

$$3x(2x - 3) + 1(2x - 3)$$


6. Put the outside parts together. Keep the matching inside part.

$$(3x + 1)(2x - 3)$$



### #4: The Remainder Theorem Shortcut

What is the remainder when $P(x) = x^4 - 3x^3 + 2x - 5$ is divided by $x - 2$?

**Solution:**

1. Find the test number. Set $x - 2 = 0$, so $x = 2$.
2. Use the Remainder Theorem. Just put $2$ wherever you see an $x$.

$$P(2) = (2)^4 - 3(2)^3 + 2(2) - 5$$


3. Calculate the powers first.

$$16 - 3(8) + 4 - 5$$


4. Multiply and combine the numbers.

$$16 - 24 + 4 - 5 = -9$$


5. The remainder is $-9$.

### #5: The Factor Theorem with Unknowns

If $x - 3$ is a factor of $x^3 - kx^2 + 4x - 12$, what is the value of $k$?

**Solution:**

1. Since it is a factor, the Remainder Theorem says the answer must be $0$.
2. The test number is $3$.
3. Put $3$ in place of $x$ and set the whole expression to $0$.

$$(3)^3 - k(3)^2 + 4(3) - 12 = 0$$


4. Solve the math parts.

$$27 - 9k + 12 - 12 = 0$$


5. Combine the regular numbers.

$$27 - 9k = 0$$


6. Move the $k$ part to the other side.

$$27 = 9k$$


7. Divide by $9$.

$$k = 3$$



### #6: Finding Coefficients with Multiple Factors

If the polynomial $P(x) = 2x^3 + ax^2 + bx - 6$ is exactly divisible by $x^2 - x - 2$, find the value of $a + b$.

**Solution:**

1. Factor the divisor first.

$$x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1)$$


2. This gives two test numbers. The roots are $2$ and $-1$.
3. Both test numbers must give a remainder of $0$.
4. Test the number $2$. Put it into the equation.

$$2(2)^3 + a(2)^2 + b(2) - 6 = 0$$


$$16 + 4a + 2b - 6 = 0$$


$$4a + 2b = -10$$


5. Divide the whole line by $2$ to make it simpler.

$$2a + b = -5$$


6. Now test the number $-1$.

$$2(-1)^3 + a(-1)^2 + b(-1) - 6 = 0$$


$$-2 + a - b - 6 = 0$$


$$a - b = 8$$


7. You now have two simple equations. Add them together to cancel out $b$.

$$(2a + b) + (a - b) = -5 + 8$$


$$3a = 3$$


$$a = 1$$


8. Put $1$ in place of $a$ in the second equation to find $b$.

$$1 - b = 8$$


$$b = -7$$


9. The question asks for the sum of $a$ and $b$.

$$1 + (-7) = -6$$



## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

The plug-in trick is the fastest way to check your work. If an exam asks you to simplify a messy polynomial, pick a small number like $1$ or $2$. Put it into the question to get a numerical value. Then put the exact same number into the answer choices. The correct choice will give you the exact same value.

When you need to divide just to find the remainder, never use long division. Always use the Remainder Theorem. It saves you whole minutes of writing.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Subtraction Trap

> Forgetting to distribute the negative sign when subtracting polynomials will ruin your whole answer.

Always flip the sign of every single part inside the second group when you subtract.

### #2: Fake Perfect Square

> Assuming $(x + y)^2$ equals $x^2 + y^2$ is a fatal mistake.

You must include the middle term. The real answer is $x^2 + 2xy + y^2$. Test-makers always put the wrong, shorter version in the choices to catch you.

### #3: Synthetic Sign Error

> Using the wrong sign in synthetic division guarantees failure.

If you divide by $x - 5$, you must write a positive $5$ in the synthetic division box. You always flip the sign of the number from the factor.
