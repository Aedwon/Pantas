## Core Concept

Word problems give relationships in sentences. Turn those relationships into equations, then use the answers to solve the problem.

Use this plan:

1. Pick a letter for the unknown and say what it means.
2. Turn each useful fact into math.
3. Solve the equation.
4. Read the question again and answer what it asks.

When a question says **find**, give the value it asks for.

### Age Problems

Time passes for everyone. If Ana is $x$ years old now, she is $x+6$ in $6$ years. Every other person's age also increases by $6$ in that time.

Use $x-6$ for that person's age $6$ years ago. The difference between two people's ages stays the same as time passes.

### Motion Problems

**Rate** tells how much distance is covered in each unit of time. Distance equals rate times time:

$$d=rt$$

Here $d$ is distance, $r$ is rate, and $t$ is time. Keep the units matched: a rate in kilometers per hour needs time in hours. **Kph** means kilometers per hour, and **km** means kilometers.

When objects move at steady speeds along the same straight path, **relative speed** tells how fast the gap changes. Add the speeds when objects move apart in opposite directions or move toward each other.

For a same-direction chase, subtract the slower speed from the faster speed. The faster object must be behind for the gap to close. If the object behind is no faster, it cannot catch the one ahead at those speeds.

Use the starting gap as $d$ and the relative speed as $r$ in $d=rt$ to find when a gap closes. For objects starting together and moving apart, use their final separation as $d$.

### Work Problems

A **work rate** tells how much of a job is done per unit of time. If a painter finishes $1$ room in $6$ hours at a steady rate, the rate is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ room per hour.

For one whole job, the rate is $1$ divided by the time. When people work on the same job at the same time, add their rates:

$$r_{\text{together}}=r_1+r_2$$

If the combined rate is $r$ jobs per hour, divide $1$ by that rate to find the hours for $1$ job:

$$t=\dfrac{1}{r}$$

For two workers who finish the same whole job alone in $a$ hours and $b$ hours, this gives the product-over-sum shortcut:

$$t=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$$

Both workers must contribute for the whole time being counted. Each must keep a steady positive rate on the same job. If one starts later or stops early, calculate each work period separately.

### Mixture Problems

Track the pure part, such as alcohol, salt, or sugar. **Concentration** tells what fraction or percent of the mixture is that pure part.

**Percent** means per hundred. Divide a percent number by $100$ to write it as a decimal:

$$20\%=\dfrac{20}{100}=0.20$$

Use that decimal in the pure-amount formula:

$$\text{pure amount}=\text{concentration}\times\text{total amount}$$

When mixing without losing liquid or pure material, add the starting pure amounts to get the final pure amount. In the mixture example, assume the liquid amounts also add.

### Coin and Digit Problems

**Coin problems** use separate equations for count and value. A group of $10$ coins gives a count of $10$. Multiply each coin count by its peso value to find the group's total value.

A **digit** is a single symbol from $0$ through $9$. A two-digit number with tens digit $t$ and units digit $u$ has value:

$$10t+u$$

Reversing the digits gives:

$$10u+t$$

The original tens digit cannot be $0$. If the reversed number must also have two digits, the original units digit cannot be $0$ either.

## Examples

### #1: Age Problem

Ana is $3$ times as old as Ben. In $6$ years, Ana will be twice as old as Ben. How old is Ana now?

**Solution:**

1. Let $b$ be Ben's age now. Ana's age now is $3b$.
2. Add $6$ to both ages to write the future relationship.

$$3b+6=2(b+6)$$

3. Multiply both terms inside the parentheses by $2$.

$$3b+6=2b+12$$

4. Subtract $2b$ and then subtract $6$.

$$b=6$$

5. Find Ana's age.

$$3(6)=18$$

6. Ana is $18$ years old now. In $6$ years, their ages will be $24$ and $12$. Check the future relationship.

$$24=2(12)$$

### #2: Motion Problem, Moving Apart

Two cars leave the same gas station at the same time. They drive in opposite directions along a straight road at constant speeds of $60$ kph and $80$ kph. After how many hours will they be $350$ km apart?

**Solution:**

1. Add the speeds to find how fast the gap grows, in kph.

$$60+80=140$$

2. Let $t$ be the hours after departure. Use $d=rt$.

$$350=140t$$

3. Divide by $140$.

$$t=\dfrac{350}{140}=2.5$$

4. They will be $350$ km apart after $2.5$ hours.

### #3: Motion Problem, Moving Toward Each Other

Two towns are $270$ km apart on a straight road. Two buses leave the towns at the same time and travel toward each other at constant speeds of $50$ kph and $40$ kph. After how many hours will they meet?

**Solution:**

1. Add the speeds to find how fast the gap closes, in kph.

$$50+40=90$$

2. Let $t$ be the hours until they meet. They must close the full starting gap.

$$270=90t$$

3. Divide by $90$.

$$t=3$$

4. They meet after $3$ hours.

### #4: Motion Problem, Chase

A motorcycle travels at a constant $70$ kph. A car behind it travels in the same direction along the same straight road at a constant $90$ kph. If the motorcycle has a $40$ km lead when the chase begins, how many hours will the car take to catch it?

**Solution:**

1. Subtract the speeds to find how fast the gap closes, in kph.

$$90-70=20$$

2. Let $t$ be the hours after the chase begins. The car must close a $40$ km gap.

$$40=20t$$

3. Divide by $20$.

$$t=2$$

4. The car catches the motorcycle after $2$ hours.

### #5: Work Problem

Marco can paint a room in $6$ hours. Liza can paint the same room in $3$ hours. They work together from start to finish at steady rates. How long will they take?

**Solution:**

1. Write each rate in rooms per hour.

$$r_M=\dfrac{1}{6}$$

$$r_L=\dfrac{1}{3}$$

2. Add the rates.

$$r_{\text{together}}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{3}$$

$$r_{\text{together}}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}$$

$$r_{\text{together}}=\dfrac{1}{2}$$

3. Divide $1$ job by the combined rate to find the time in hours.

$$t=\dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{2}}=2$$

4. They finish in $2$ hours. This is less than either worker's solo time.

### #6: Mixture Problem

How many liters of a $20\%$ alcohol solution must be added to $8$ liters of a $50\%$ alcohol solution to make a $30\%$ alcohol solution? Assume the volumes add and no liquid or alcohol is lost.

**Solution:**

1. Let $x$ be the liters of the $20\%$ solution to add.
2. Divide each percent number by $100$.

$$20\%=0.20$$

$$50\%=0.50,\qquad30\%=0.30$$

3. The starting pure alcohol amounts are $0.20x$ liters and $0.50(8)$ liters. The final volume is $x+8$ liters.
4. Set the starting pure alcohol equal to the final pure alcohol.

$$0.20x+0.50(8)=0.30(x+8)$$

5. Multiply and open the parentheses.

$$0.20x+4=0.30x+2.4$$

6. Subtract $0.20x$ and $2.4$ from both sides.

$$1.6=0.10x$$

7. Divide by $0.10$.

$$x=16$$

8. Check the pure alcohol and total volume.

$$0.20(16)+4=7.2$$

$$16+8=24$$

$$\dfrac{7.2}{24}=0.30$$

9. Add $16$ liters of the $20\%$ solution.

### #7: Coin Problem

A jar contains $1$-peso and $5$-peso coins. There are $20$ coins in all, worth $52$ pesos. How many $5$-peso coins are there?

**Solution:**

1. Let $x$ be the number of $1$-peso coins and $y$ be the number of $5$-peso coins.
2. Write the count equation.

$$x+y=20$$

3. Write the value equation in pesos.

$$x+5y=52$$

4. Subtract the count equation from the value equation.

$$4y=32$$

5. Divide by $4$.

$$y=8$$

6. There are $8$ five-peso coins. The other $12$ coins are one-peso coins. Check their total value.

$$12+5(8)=52$$

### #8: Digit Problem

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is $9$. When the digits are reversed, the new number is $27$ more than the original. Find the original number.

**Solution:**

1. Let $t$ be the tens digit and $u$ be the units digit.
2. Write the digit-sum equation.

$$t+u=9$$

3. Subtract the original number from the reversed number.

$$(10u+t)-(10t+u)=27$$

4. Combine the $u$ terms and the $t$ terms.

$$9u-9t=27$$

5. Divide by $9$.

$$u-t=3$$

6. Add this equation to $t+u=9$.

$$2u=12$$

$$u=6$$

7. Use the digit sum to find $t$.

$$t=9-6=3$$

8. Find the original number.

$$10(3)+6=36$$

9. Check the reversal.

$$63-36=27$$

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For two workers on the same job, the product-over-sum formula can save time. Both must work together for the whole time at steady positive rates.

$$t=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$$

For the work example:

$$t=\dfrac{6(3)}{6+3}=2$$

For motion at steady speeds along the same straight path, use relative speed. Add speeds for opposite-direction separation or for moving toward each other. In a same-direction chase, subtract the slower speed from the faster speed.

The faster object must start behind to catch the slower one. Use the gap at the start of the chase, not a distance traveled before it starts.

Before solving a mixture problem, check which amount should be larger. Here $30\%$ is closer to $20\%$ than to $50\%$. The mixture therefore needs more of the weaker solution than of the stronger one.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Time Passes for Everyone

> Change every person's age by the same number of years.

If a problem says "in $5$ years," each person's age increases by $5$. Changing only one age gives the wrong relationship.

### #2: Answering the Wrong Question

> Read what the question asks for after solving the equation.

In the age example, $b=6$ gives Ben's age. The question asks for Ana's age, which is $18$. Reporting $6$ would answer a different question.

### #3: Averaging Work Times

> Add work rates, not solo work times.

For the work example, averaging gives $\dfrac{6+3}{2}=4.5$ hours. Together they must finish faster than Liza's solo time of $3$ hours. The answer $4.5$ cannot be right.

### #4: Mixing Up a Digit and Its Value

> A tens digit $t$ is worth $10t$ in the number.

The digits of $36$ add to $9$. The number's value is $10(3)+6=36$. Adding the digits alone does not give the number.
