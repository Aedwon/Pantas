## Core Concept

Word problems are just equations wearing a costume. Your job is to remove the costume. The plan is always the same:

1. Pick a letter for the unknown. Write down exactly what it stands for.
2. Turn each sentence into math, one fact at a time.
3. Solve the equation.
4. Re-read the question and answer what it ASKS.

Each classic type has one key relationship to remember.

**Age problems**: time moves for everyone. If Ana is $x$ years old now, she is $x + 6$ in six years, and so is everyone else six years older.

**Motion problems**: distance equals rate times time.

$$d = rt$$

**Work problems**: think in rates. If a painter finishes a job in $6$ hours, the rate is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of the job per hour. Rates working together add up.

**Mixture problems**: track the pure "stuff" (acid, salt, sugar). Amount of stuff equals concentration times volume, and stuff in plus stuff in equals stuff out.

**Coin problems**: count and value are different equations. Ten coins is a count; $52$ pesos is a value.

**Digit problems**: a two-digit number with tens digit $t$ and units digit $u$ has value $10t + u$. Reversing it gives $10u + t$.

CETs use these six costumes over and over. Learn the key relationship for each, and every "hard" word problem becomes a two-line equation.

## Examples

### #1: Age Problem

Ana is $3$ times as old as Ben. In $6$ years, Ana will be twice as old as Ben. How old is Ana now?

**Solution:**
Let $b$ be Ben's age now. Then Ana's age now is $3b$.

1. Write the future fact. In $6$ years, Ana is $3b + 6$ and Ben is $b + 6$.

$$3b + 6 = 2(b + 6)$$

2. Distribute the $2$.

$$3b + 6 = 2b + 12$$

3. Subtract $2b$ and subtract $6$ from both sides.

$$b = 6$$

4. Ben is $6$, so Ana is $3 \times 6 = 18$. The question asks for Ana, so the answer is $18$.
5. Check: in $6$ years they are $24$ and $12$, and $24$ is twice $12$. Correct.

### #2: Motion Problem (Opposite Directions)

Two cars leave the same gas station at the same time and drive in opposite directions. One travels at $60$ kph and the other at $80$ kph. After how many hours will they be $350$ km apart?

**Solution:**
Moving in opposite directions, their distances ADD.

1. In $t$ hours, one car covers $60t$ km and the other covers $80t$ km.

$$60t + 80t = 350$$

2. Combine the terms. Together they separate at $140$ kph.

$$140t = 350$$

3. Divide both sides by $140$.

$$t = \dfrac{350}{140} = 2.5$$

4. They will be $350$ km apart after $2.5$ hours.

### #3: Work Problem

Marco can paint a room in $6$ hours. Liza can paint the same room in $3$ hours. Working together, how long will they take?

**Solution:**
Convert each time into a rate, in rooms per hour.

1. Marco's rate is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ room per hour. Liza's rate is $\dfrac{1}{3}$ room per hour.
2. Together, their rates add.

$$\dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$$

3. Together they paint $\dfrac{1}{2}$ room per hour. Time is the reciprocal of the rate.

$$t = \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{1}{2}} = 2$$

4. Working together, they finish in $2$ hours. Notice the answer is SMALLER than both solo times, which makes sense: two workers are faster than one.

### #4: Mixture Problem

How many liters of a $20\%$ alcohol solution must be added to $8$ liters of a $50\%$ alcohol solution to make a $30\%$ alcohol solution?

**Solution:**
Track the pure alcohol. Let $x$ be the liters of the $20\%$ solution.

1. Pure alcohol in: $0.20x$ from the first solution and $0.50(8) = 4$ from the second.
2. The final mix has $x + 8$ liters at $30\%$. Write "alcohol in equals alcohol out".

$$0.20x + 4 = 0.30(x + 8)$$

3. Distribute on the right.

$$0.20x + 4 = 0.30x + 2.4$$

4. Subtract $0.20x$ and subtract $2.4$ from both sides.

$$1.6 = 0.10x$$

5. Divide by $0.10$.

$$x = 16$$

6. You need $16$ liters. Check: alcohol is $0.20(16) + 4 = 7.2$ liters, and the mix is $24$ liters, and $\dfrac{7.2}{24} = 0.30$. Correct.

### #5: Coin Problem

A jar contains $1$-peso and $5$-peso coins. There are $20$ coins in all, worth $52$ pesos. How many $5$-peso coins are there?

**Solution:**
Write one equation for the COUNT and one for the VALUE. Let $x$ be the number of $1$-peso coins and $y$ the number of $5$-peso coins.

1. The count equation.

$$x + y = 20$$

2. The value equation. Each $1$-peso coin is worth $1$ and each $5$-peso coin is worth $5$.

$$x + 5y = 52$$

3. Subtract the count equation from the value equation.

$$4y = 32$$

4. Divide by $4$.

$$y = 8$$

5. There are $8$ five-peso coins (and $12$ one-peso coins). Check the value: $12 + 5(8) = 12 + 40 = 52$ pesos. Correct.

### #6: Digit Problem

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is $9$. When the digits are reversed, the new number is $27$ more than the original. Find the original number.

**Solution:**
Let $t$ be the tens digit and $u$ be the units digit. The number's value is $10t + u$.

1. The digit sum gives the first equation.

$$t + u = 9$$

2. The reversed number is $10u + t$, and it is $27$ bigger.

$$(10u + t) - (10t + u) = 27$$

3. Simplify the left side.

$$9u - 9t = 27$$

4. Divide everything by $9$.

$$u - t = 3$$

5. Now add this to the first equation $t + u = 9$.

$$2u = 12$$

$$u = 6$$

6. Then $t = 9 - 6 = 3$. The original number is $36$.
7. Check: reversed is $63$, and $63 - 36 = 27$. Correct.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For work problems with two workers, use this shortcut formula: time together equals the product of the times over the sum of the times.

$$t = \dfrac{ab}{a + b}$$

For Example $3$: $\dfrac{6 \times 3}{6 + 3} = \dfrac{18}{9} = 2$ hours. Instant.

For motion problems, add the speeds when moving apart or toward each other, and subtract the speeds when one chases the other. Then use $d = rt$ once with that combined speed.

Before solving, estimate. In Example $4$, the target $30\%$ is much closer to $20\%$ than to $50\%$, so you need much more of the $20\%$ solution than the $8$ liters of the strong one. If your algebra gives a small answer like $2$, you know you slipped.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Time Passes for Everyone

> In age problems, add the years to BOTH people.

If the problem says "in $5$ years", every person in the problem becomes $5$ years older. Students often age only one person, writing $x + 5 = 2y$ instead of $x + 5 = 2(y + 5)$. That wrong equation produces one of the trap choices.

### #2: Answering the Wrong Question

> Solve for $x$, then re-read what the question asks.

In Example $1$, solving gives $b = 6$, but the question asks for ANA's age, which is $18$. In coin problems, they may ask for the number of $1$-peso coins after you solved for the $5$-peso coins. The unasked value is always in the choices.

### #3: Averaging the Work Times

> Never average the times of two workers.

For Example $3$, students compute $\dfrac{6 + 3}{2} = 4.5$ hours. Impossible! Working together can never be slower than the faster worker alone ($3$ hours). Always convert to rates or use the product-over-sum shortcut.

### #4: Mixing Up a Digit and Its Value

> The digit $t$ in the tens place is worth $10t$, not $t$.

In digit problems, writing the number as $t + u$ instead of $10t + u$ ruins the setup. The digits of $36$ add to $9$, but the number itself is $10(3) + 6$. Keep "digit sum" and "number value" as two different ideas.
