## Core Concept

Word problems give relationships in sentences. Your job is to turn those relationships into equations.

Use this plan:

1. Pick a letter for the unknown and say what it means.
2. Turn each useful fact into math.
3. Solve the equation.
4. Read the question again and answer what it asks.

Each type below has a key relationship.

**Age problems:** time passes for everyone. If Ana is $x$ years old now, she is $x+6$ in $6$ years. Every other person's age also increases by $6$ in that time.

**Motion problems:** distance equals rate times time.

$$d=rt$$

When objects move at steady speeds along the same straight path, **relative speed** tells how fast the gap changes. Add speeds when they move apart or toward each other. For a same-direction chase, subtract the slower speed from the faster speed.

In the examples, **kph** means kilometers per hour.

**Work problems:** think in job rates. If a painter finishes $1$ room in $6$ hours at a steady rate, the rate is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ room per hour.

When people work on the same job at the same time, their rates add:

$$r_{\text{together}}=r_1+r_2$$

If the combined rate is $r$ jobs per hour, the time for $1$ job is:

$$t=\dfrac{1}{r}$$

For two workers who each finish the same whole job alone in $a$ hours and $b$ hours, the product-over-sum shortcut is:

$$t=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$$

This shortcut assumes both workers work together on the same job for the whole modeled time and each keeps a steady positive rate.

**Mixture problems:** track the pure part, such as alcohol, salt, or sugar. **Concentration** tells what fraction or percent of the mixture is that pure part. Pure amount equals concentration times total amount.

$$\text{pure amount}=(\text{concentration})(\text{total amount})$$

**Coin problems:** count and value give different equations. A group of $10$ coins gives a count of $10$. Their peso values give a separate total.

**Digit problems:** a two-digit number with tens digit $t$ and units digit $u$ has value:

$$10t+u$$

Reversing the digits gives:

$$10u+t$$

These patterns help turn common exam word problems into equations.

## Examples

### #1: Age Problem

Ana is $3$ times as old as Ben. In $6$ years, Ana will be twice as old as Ben. How old is Ana now?

**Solution:**

Let $b$ be Ben's age now. Then Ana's age now is $3b$.

1. Write the future relationship.

$$3b+6=2(b+6)$$

2. Distribute the $2$.

$$3b+6=2b+12$$

3. Solve for $b$.

$$b=6$$

4. Ana is $3$ times Ben's age.

$$3(6)=18$$

Ana is $18$ years old now.

5. Check: in $6$ years, their ages are $24$ and $12$, and $24$ is twice $12$.

### #2: Motion Problem, Moving Apart

Two cars leave the same gas station at the same time and drive in opposite directions. One travels at $60$ kph and the other at $80$ kph. After how many hours will they be $350$ km apart?

**Solution:**

1. Their steady speeds add because they move apart.

$$60+80=140$$

2. Use $d=rt$.

$$350=140t$$

3. Solve for $t$.

$$t=\dfrac{350}{140}=2.5$$

They will be $350$ km apart after $2.5$ hours.

### #3: Motion Problem, Moving Toward Each Other

Two towns are $270$ km apart. A bus leaves one town at $50$ kph while another bus leaves the other town at $40$ kph at the same time. They travel toward each other on the same road. After how many hours will they meet?

**Solution:**

1. Their steady speeds add because they move toward each other.

$$50+40=90$$

2. Use the starting distance as the distance closed.

$$270=90t$$

3. Solve for $t$.

$$t=3$$

They meet after $3$ hours.

### #4: Motion Problem, Chase

A motorcycle travels at $70$ kph. A car behind it travels in the same direction at $90$ kph. If the motorcycle has a $40$ km lead when the car starts chasing, how many hours will the car take to catch it?

**Solution:**

1. Subtract the steady speeds because both move in the same direction.

$$90-70=20$$

2. The car must close a $40$ km gap.

$$40=20t$$

3. Solve for $t$.

$$t=2$$

The car catches the motorcycle after $2$ hours.

### #5: Work Problem

Marco can paint a room in $6$ hours. Liza can paint the same room in $3$ hours. They work together at steady rates. How long will they take?

**Solution:**

1. Write each rate.

$$r_M=\dfrac{1}{6}$$

$$r_L=\dfrac{1}{3}$$

2. Add the rates.

$$r_{\text{together}}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{3}$$

$$r_{\text{together}}=\dfrac{1}{2}$$

3. One divided by the rate gives the time for one room.

$$t=\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{1}{2}}=2$$

They finish in $2$ hours.

### #6: Mixture Problem

How many liters of a $20\%$ alcohol solution must be added to $8$ liters of a $50\%$ alcohol solution to make a $30\%$ alcohol solution?

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the liters of the $20\%$ solution.

1. Change each percent to a decimal: $20\%=0.20$, $50\%=0.50$, and $30\%=0.30$.
2. Write the pure alcohol from each starting solution.

$$0.20x+0.50(8)$$

3. The final mixture has $x+8$ liters at $30\%$ alcohol.

$$0.20x+4=0.30(x+8)$$

4. Distribute on the right.

$$0.20x+4=0.30x+2.4$$

5. Solve for $x$.

$$1.6=0.10x$$

$$x=16$$

You need $16$ liters of the $20\%$ solution.

6. Check the concentration.

$$\dfrac{0.20(16)+4}{24}=0.30$$

### #7: Coin Problem

A jar contains $1$-peso and $5$-peso coins. There are $20$ coins in all, worth $52$ pesos. How many $5$-peso coins are there?

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the number of $1$-peso coins and $y$ be the number of $5$-peso coins.

1. Write the count equation.

$$x+y=20$$

2. Write the value equation.

$$x+5y=52$$

3. Subtract the first equation from the second.

$$4y=32$$

4. Solve for $y$.

$$y=8$$

There are $8$ five-peso coins.

5. Check the total value. Then $x=12$.

$$12+5(8)=52$$

### #8: Digit Problem

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is $9$. When the digits are reversed, the new number is $27$ more than the original. Find the original number.

**Solution:**

Let $t$ be the tens digit and $u$ be the units digit.

1. Write the digit-sum equation.

$$t+u=9$$

2. Write the reversed-number relationship.

$$(10u+t)-(10t+u)=27$$

3. Simplify.

$$9u-9t=27$$

$$u-t=3$$

4. Add this equation to $t+u=9$.

$$2u=12$$

$$u=6$$

5. Find $t$.

$$t=3$$

The original number is $36$.

6. Check the reversal.

$$63-36=27$$

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For two workers on the same job with steady positive rates, the product-over-sum formula can save time:

$$t=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$$

For the work example:

$$t=\dfrac{6(3)}{6+3}=2$$

For motion at steady speeds along the same straight path, use relative speed. Add speeds for moving apart or toward each other. In a same-direction chase, subtract the slower speed from the faster speed.

Before solving a mixture problem, estimate which amount should be larger. In the mixture example, the target $30\%$ is closer to $20\%$ than to $50\%$, so the weaker solution should have the larger amount.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Time Passes for Everyone

> In age problems, change every person's age by the same number of years.

If a problem says "in $5$ years," each person's age increases by $5$. Changing only one person's age gives the wrong relationship.

### #2: Answering the Wrong Question

> Solve the equations, then read what the question asks for.

In the age example, $b=6$ is Ben's age, but the question asks for Ana's age, which is $18$. A wrong intermediate value may also appear as a choice, so use the requested value.

### #3: Averaging Work Times

> Do not average solo work times to get the together time.

For the work example, $\dfrac{6+3}{2}=4.5$ hours is not the together time. Add work rates, then turn the combined rate into time.

### #4: Mixing Up a Digit and Its Value

> A tens digit $t$ is worth $10t$ in the number.

The digits of $36$ add to $9$, but the number itself is $10(3)+6$. Keep digit sum and number value as different ideas.
