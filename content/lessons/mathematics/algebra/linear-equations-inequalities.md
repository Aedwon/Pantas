## Core Concept

An **equation** states that two expressions have equal values. A linear equation in one variable can be written as $ax+b=0$, where $a\ne0$. The letters $a$ and $b$ stand for fixed numbers.

**Solve** means find all values that make a statement true. An equation stays balanced when you do the same valid operation to both sides.

You may add or subtract the same amount on both sides. You may also multiply or divide both sides by the same nonzero amount.

For equal fractions, multiplying both sides by the denominators clears the fractions. This is why cross-multiplication works when the denominators are nonzero.

### One-Variable Inequalities

An **inequality** compares values. The signs $<$ and $>$ mean less than and greater than. The signs $\le$ and $\ge$ mean less than or equal to and greater than or equal to.

A linear inequality can have many solutions. On a number line, each point stands for a number, with larger numbers to the right.

Use an **open circle** for $<$ or $>$ because the endpoint is not included. Use a **closed circle** for $\le$ or $\ge$ because the endpoint is included.

Shade left for values less than the endpoint. Shade right for values greater than the endpoint.

If you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign. For example, $2<5$, but multiplying both numbers by $-1$ gives $-2>-5$.

### Lines in Two Variables

A coordinate grid has a horizontal **$x$-axis** and a vertical **$y$-axis**. They meet at the origin, $(0,0)$. A point $(x,y)$ gives its horizontal position first, then its vertical position.

A linear equation in two variables can be written as:

$$Ax+By=C$$

Here, $A$, $B$, and $C$ are fixed numbers, and $A$ and $B$ are not both $0$. Its graph is a straight line through all points that make the equation true.

**Slope** measures how much $y$ changes for each increase of $1$ in $x$. Between two points on a nonvertical line:

$$m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$$

The points must have different $x$-values. A positive slope rises to the right, and a negative slope falls to the right.

An **intercept** is where a graph meets an axis. At the $y$-intercept, $x=0$. At the $x$-intercept, $y=0$.

For a nonvertical line, **slope-intercept form** is:

$$y=mx+b$$

The slope is $m$, and the line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,b)$. To draw the line, plot that point, use the slope to find another point, and draw a straight line through them.

For $Ax+By=C$ with $B\ne0$:

$$m=-\dfrac{A}{B}$$

If $B=0$, the equation is a vertical line:

$$x=\dfrac{C}{A}$$

A vertical line has undefined slope because its horizontal change is $0$. A horizontal line has slope $0$ because its vertical change is $0$.

Distinct nonvertical parallel lines have the same slope. Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle.

The **reciprocal** of a nonzero number is $1$ divided by that number. For a nonzero fraction, swap its numerator and denominator to get its reciprocal. For example, the reciprocal of $-\dfrac{2}{5}$ is $-\dfrac{5}{2}$.

For perpendicular lines with nonzero slopes, take the reciprocal of one slope and change its sign to get the other slope:

$$m_2=-\dfrac{1}{m_1}$$

A vertical line is perpendicular to a horizontal line.

The **point-slope form** of a nonvertical line through $(x_1,y_1)$ with slope $m$ is:

$$y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$$

### Two-Variable Linear Inequalities

A two-variable linear inequality describes a region on one side of a **boundary line**. Replace the comparison sign with an equality sign to get the boundary equation.

Use a dashed boundary for $<$ or $>$ because its points are excluded. Use a solid boundary for $\le$ or $\ge$ because its points are included.

Test a point that is not on the boundary. If it makes the inequality true, shade the side containing that point. Otherwise, shade the other side.

## Examples

### #1: Equation with Fractions

If $\dfrac{3x-1}{2}=\dfrac{x+4}{3}$, what is $x$?

**Solution:**

1. Multiply both sides by $6$ to clear the denominators.

$$3(3x-1)=2(x+4)$$

2. Multiply through the parentheses.

$$9x-3=2x+8$$

3. Subtract $2x$ from both sides, then add $3$ to both sides.

$$7x=11$$

4. Divide both sides by $7$.

$$x=\dfrac{11}{7}$$

### #2: Negative Division and an Open Endpoint

Solve $-2(x-4)>3x+13$, then describe its graph on a number line.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: number line with an open circle at -1 and shading to the left, labeled x<-1 -->

**Solution:**

1. Multiply through the parentheses.

$$-2x+8>3x+13$$

2. Subtract $3x$ and $8$ from both sides.

$$-5x>5$$

3. Divide by $-5$ and reverse the sign.

$$x<-1$$

4. Place an open circle at $-1$ because it is excluded. Shade left for smaller values.

### #3: A Closed Endpoint

Solve $3x+2\ge8$, then describe its number-line graph.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: number line with a closed circle at 2 and shading to the right, labeled x>=2 -->

**Solution:**

1. Subtract $2$ from both sides.

$$3x\ge6$$

2. Divide by $3$.

$$x\ge2$$

3. Place a closed circle at $2$ because it is included. Shade right for larger values.

### #4: Drawing a Line from Slope and Intercept

Graph $y=2x-3$.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: coordinate grid with axes labeled x and y, a straight line y=2x-3 through labeled points (0,-3), (1,-1), and (2,1); show a rightward step of 1 and an upward step of 2 -->

**Solution:**

1. Read the slope and $y$-intercept.

$$m=2,\qquad b=-3$$

2. Plot $(0,-3)$. For each step of $1$ to the right, move $2$ up to reach $(1,-1)$, then $(2,1)$.
3. Draw a straight line through the points and extend it in both directions.

### #5: Equation of a Perpendicular Line

Find the equation of the line through $(3,-1)$ that is perpendicular to $2x+5y=10$.

**Solution:**

1. Read the given line's slope from standard form.

$$m=-\dfrac{2}{5}$$

2. Swap the numerator and denominator, then change the sign.

$$m_2=\dfrac{5}{2}$$

3. Use point-slope form with $(3,-1)$.

$$y+1=\dfrac{5}{2}(x-3)$$

4. Multiply both sides by $2$ and distribute.

$$2y+2=5x-15$$

5. Rearrange the terms.

$$5x-2y=17$$

### #6: Vertical and Horizontal Slopes

Find the slope of $3x=12$ and the slope of $2y=6$. State whether the two lines are perpendicular.

**Solution:**

1. Divide the first equation by $3$.

$$x=4$$

This line is vertical, so its slope is undefined.

2. Divide the second equation by $2$.

$$y=3$$

This line is horizontal, so its slope is $0$.

3. The lines are perpendicular because one is vertical and the other is horizontal.

### #7: Identifying a Shaded Region

A shaded region includes $(0,0)$. Its boundary is a dashed line through $(0,3)$ and $(3,0)$. Which inequality defines the region?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: coordinate grid with a dashed line through labeled points (0,3) and (3,0), and the side containing the origin (0,0) shaded -->

**Solution:**

1. Find the boundary's slope.

$$m=\dfrac{0-3}{3-0}=-1$$

2. Its $y$-intercept is $3$, so write the boundary equation.

$$y=-x+3$$

$$x+y=3$$

3. The dashed line excludes equality. Test the shaded point $(0,0)$.

$$0+0<3$$

4. This is true, so the region is:

$$x+y<3$$

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For $Ax+By=C$ with $B\ne0$, read the slope as $-\dfrac{A}{B}$.

To find an $x$-intercept, set $y=0$. To find a $y$-intercept, set $x=0$. Some lines do not meet an axis, so check that the resulting equation has a solution.

For an inequality, the origin is a handy test point only when it is not on the boundary.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Negative Multiplier or Divisor

> Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses the sign.

Dividing $-5x>5$ by $-5$ gives $x<-1$. Keeping $>$ would select values that do not satisfy the original inequality.

### #2: Perpendicular Edge Cases

> The negative-reciprocal shortcut needs nonzero slopes.

A vertical line has undefined slope. Its perpendicular line is horizontal with slope $0$. Do not try to divide by $0$ in the slope rule.

### #3: Solid vs. Dashed Boundary

> A dashed line excludes the boundary, while a solid line includes it.

Use dashed for $<$ or $>$. Use solid for $\le$ or $\ge$. A wrong boundary style changes whether points on the line are solutions.
