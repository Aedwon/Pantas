## Core Concept

**Classify** means name the group something belongs to. **Evaluate** means find the final number value. **Solve** means find all values that make an equation true.

**Linear:** $f(x)=mx+b$. Here $m$ and $b$ are fixed numbers. Its graph is a straight nonvertical line.

A **polynomial** is a sum of terms with nonnegative whole-number powers of $x$, such as $x^2+3x-1$. A **power function** has the form $kx^p$, where $k$ and $p$ are fixed numbers. For example, $x^5$ is both a polynomial and a power function.

### Exponential, Logarithmic, and Piecewise Functions

An **exponential function** has the form $f(x)=a^x$ for $a>0$ and $a\ne1$. The variable is in the exponent. Each increase of $1$ in $x$ multiplies the output by $a$.

For a nonzero base, a zero exponent gives $1$:

$$a^0=1\qquad(a\ne0)$$

To see why, divide a nonzero number by itself. Subtracting the matching exponents gives $a^1/a^1=a^{1-1}=a^0$, while the division gives $1$.

If $a>1$, the outputs grow as $x$ increases. If $0<a<1$, they shrink toward $0$. Equal powers of the same allowed base have equal exponents because different exponents give different outputs.

## Examples

### #1: Naming the Family

Classify $f(x)=3x-7$, $g(x)=5^x$, and $h(x)=x^5$.

**Solution:**

1. $f(x)=3x-7$ is linear because it has the form $mx+b$.
2. $g(x)=5^x$ is exponential because the variable is in the exponent.
3. $h(x)=x^5$ is a polynomial power function. Its exponent is fixed, and its base is the variable.

### #2: Exponential Growth and Decay

Classify $f(x)=1.2^x$ and $g(x)=0.4^x$ as growth or decay. Find each function's values at $x=0$ and $x=1$.

**Solution:**

1. The base $1.2>1$ gives growth. Use the zero-exponent rule for the first value.

$$f(0)=1,\qquad f(1)=1.2$$

2. The base satisfies $0<0.4<1$, so it gives decay. Its zero power also equals $1$.

$$g(0)=1,\qquad g(1)=0.4$$

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Growth and Decay

> Check whether the exponential base is above or below $1$.

A base greater than $1$ gives growth. A base between $0$ and $1$ gives decay as $x$ increases. A large-looking exponent alone does not mean the output grows.
