## Core Concept

When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, the **same-side interior angles** add to $180^\circ$. These angles lie between the lines on the same side of the transversal. They are also called co-interior angles.

The letters help your eye. Alternate interior angles sit in the corners of a Z or a backward Z. Corresponding angles sit in an F, turned any way.

Same-side interior angles sit inside a C or a U. Check the positions too, because a drawing can hide the letter.

Suppose $l \parallel m$ and the transversal is not perpendicular to them. Then the eight angles take two values: one angle $x$ and its supplement $180^\circ - x$. Around each crossing the values alternate.

## Examples

### #1: One Angle Unlocks All Eight

Lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel, cut by a transversal. At the top crossing, an interior angle measures $65^\circ$. Find its vertical angle, its linear pair neighbor, its corresponding angle, and its same-side interior angle.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: parallel lines l (top) and m (bottom) cut by a slanted transversal; the interior angle at the top crossing, below l and right of the transversal, is labeled 65°; question marks mark its vertical angle, its linear pair neighbor, its corresponding angle at the bottom crossing, and its same-side interior angle -->

**Solution:**
The angle is not $90^\circ$, so every angle in the figure is either $65^\circ$ or its supplement.

1. The vertical angle faces the $65^\circ$ angle across the crossing, so it is $65^\circ$.
2. The linear pair neighbor completes a straight line.

$$180 - 65 = 115$$

3. The linear pair neighbor is $115^\circ$.
4. The corresponding angle is in the same corner at the bottom crossing, so it is $65^\circ$.
5. The same-side interior angle must add with $65^\circ$ to make $180^\circ$, so it is $115^\circ$.

### #2: Same-Side Interior Angles

Lines $a$ and $b$ are parallel. Two same-side interior angles measure $(3x + 20)^\circ$ and $(2x - 15)^\circ$. Find both angles.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: parallel lines a and b cut by a transversal; two same-side interior angles, one at each crossing, between the lines and on the same side of the transversal, are labeled (3x + 20)° and (2x - 15)° -->

**Solution:**
Same-side interior angles are supplementary, so they add to $180^\circ$.

1. Write the sum equation.

$$(3x + 20) + (2x - 15) = 180$$

2. Combine like terms.

$$5x + 5 = 180$$

3. Subtract $5$, then divide by $5$.

$$5x = 175$$

$$x = 35$$

4. The angles are $3(35) + 20 = 125^\circ$ and $2(35) - 15 = 55^\circ$.
5. Check: $125 + 55 = 180$. Correct.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

For two parallel lines cut by a transversal, any two of the eight angles are either equal or add to $180^\circ$. Name the pair first. A Z or F shape means equal, and a C or U shape means they add to $180^\circ$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Equal or Supplementary?

> With parallel lines, same-side interior angles add to $180^\circ$. They are equal only when each is $90^\circ$.

Do not set two same-side interior angles equal to each other, unless the problem says each is $90^\circ$. If both expressions sit between the lines on the same side of the transversal, write a sum equation. That also works when both angles are $90^\circ$.
