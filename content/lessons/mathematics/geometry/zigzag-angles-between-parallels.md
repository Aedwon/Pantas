## Core Concept

For a zigzag like Example $7$, the angle at $P$ equals the sum of the two outer angles. This holds when $P$ is between the lines, both outer angles are measured on the side that faces $P$, and $A$ and $B$ are on the same side of $P$. If the figure bends the other way, or one angle is measured on the other side, draw the helper line instead.

In that same setup, if you know the angle at $P$ and one outer angle, subtract to find the other.

The shortcut calls the zigzag problem below Example 7.

## Examples

### #1: The Zigzag Between Parallel Lines

Lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel. Point $A$ is on $l$ and point $B$ is on $m$, and both lie to the left of a point $P$ between the lines. Point $C$ is on $l$ to the right of $A$, and point $D$ is on $m$ to the right of $B$. Angle $CAP$ is $30^\circ$ and angle $DBP$ is $40^\circ$. Find angle $APB$.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: parallel lines l (top) and m (bottom); point A on l and point B on m are both left of point P between the lines; segments AP and BP are drawn; point C is on l right of A and point D is on m right of B; angle CAP is labeled 30°, angle DBP is labeled 40°, angle APB is labeled ? -->

**Solution:**
Draw a helper line through $P$, parallel to both lines. Let $E$ be a point on it to the left of $P$.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: the same figure with a dashed helper line through P parallel to l and m; point E is on the helper line left of P; angle APE is labeled 30° and angle EPB is labeled 40° -->

1. The helper line splits angle $APB$ into angle $APE$ and angle $EPB$.
2. Angle $APE$ and angle $CAP$ are alternate interior angles for the parallel lines $l$ and the helper line. So angle $APE$ is $30^\circ$.
3. Angle $EPB$ and angle $DBP$ are alternate interior angles for the helper line and $m$. So angle $EPB$ is $40^\circ$.
4. Add the two parts.

$$30 + 40 = 70$$

5. Angle $APB$ is $70^\circ$.
