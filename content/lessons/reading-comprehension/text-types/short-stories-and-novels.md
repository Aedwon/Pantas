## Core Concept

**Short stories** and **novel excerpts** can be studied through plot, character, setting, conflict, narration, style, and theme. These are tools for reading, not rules every story must follow.

An **excerpt** is a part taken from a longer work, such as one scene from a novel. You see only that part, so claim only what it shows.

Every claim needs evidence: point to the words that show it. To **infer** means to work out something the text does not say directly, using clues.

### Plot

**Plot** is the order in which a story tells its events.

A common model has five parts: exposition (the start), rising action (events that build the problem), climax, falling action (events after the climax), and resolution (the end). Not every story fits it. A story can start in the middle, jump back in time, or end without an answer.

The **climax** is the main turning point, where the central problem is decided or changes direction. It is not just the most exciting scene.

### Character

The **protagonist** is the main character, whose goals and choices the story follows. The **antagonistic force** is whatever opposes the protagonist. It need not be a villain: it can be a storm, a custom, or the protagonist's own fear.

Two questions about a character are different:

- **Round or flat?** This is about complexity. A round character has several sides, which may clash. A flat character shows only one or two traits.
- **Dynamic or static?** This is about change. A dynamic character changes in a big way. A static character stays the same.

A flat character can change, and a round character can stay the same. Example 5 works both cases.

### Setting

**Setting** is the time, the place, and the social situation, such as customs and weather. It can cause problems or limit what a character can do.

### Conflict

**Conflict** is a struggle that drives the story. A story can have several. Common types:

- **Character vs. character:** two people oppose each other.
- **Character vs. nature:** a character faces weather, animals, or a place.
- **Character vs. society:** a character faces a rule, a custom, or a group.
- **Character vs. self:** the character struggles inside with a choice, a fear, or a feeling. Being tired or hurt is a hardship of the body, not an inner conflict.

### Narration and Point of View

The **narrator** is the voice that tells the story. The **point of view** is where the narrator stands.

- **First person:** a character tells the story with "I". You see events through that character.
- **Third person limited:** an outside voice shows the thoughts of only one character.
- **Third person omniscient:** an outside voice knows the thoughts of many characters.

A narrator may be wrong or unfair, so compare what the narrator says with what characters do.

### Theme and Style

**Theme** is an idea the story explores, such as pride, fear, or belonging. It is not always a moral. Find it by looking at what repeats (similar choices, images, and results), then state it in a full sentence.

**Style** is how the author writes: the words, the sentence length, and the images. Style helps create **mood**, the feeling the reader gets. Say what a style seems meant to do only from the words on the page.

The author's **motivation** is why the author wrote it, such as to entertain, to move the reader, or to show an idea. Infer it from the text's choices, not from guesses about the author's life.

## Examples

### #1: Conflict Inside and Outside

> Mang Ador's hands were raw from the line. The big fish had dragged his boat past the reef, and the sun was low. He wanted to cut the rope, but he remembered the empty pots at home. "Not yet," he whispered.

Who is the protagonist, and what is the antagonistic force? Which conflict does "Not yet" show?

**Solution:**

1. Mang Ador is the protagonist. The story follows his choices.
2. The fish, the reef, and the low sun oppose him. The antagonistic force is nature, not a villain.
3. Raw hands are a hardship of the body, not an inner conflict.
4. "Not yet" shows him choosing between giving up and feeding his family: character vs. self.

### #2: Telling the Types of Conflict Apart

Name the main conflict in each case.

- **A:** Lia and her cousin both claim the same boat and will not speak.
- **B:** A village rule says only men may speak at the meeting, but Tala wants to speak.

**Solution:**

1. In A, two people oppose each other: character vs. character.
2. In B, a village rule opposes Tala, not one person: character vs. society.

### #3: Finding the Climax

> The dam is cracking. Mira warns the mayor, who laughs. She runs upriver and opens the gate as the wall gives way. The water drains safely. The villagers thank her.

Which event is the climax?

**Solution:**

1. The warning is tense, but the problem is still open: rising action.
2. Opening the gate decides whether the village is saved: the climax.
3. The draining water and the thanks come after it: falling action and resolution.

### #4: A Story Told Out of Order

> Today, Ben sold the last of his father's tools. He remembered the summer he was ten, when he promised to open a shop.

Which happened first in time, the sale or the promise?

**Solution:**

The promise came first. The story starts with the sale, then jumps back. The order of telling can differ from the order of happening.

### #5: Round or Flat, Dynamic or Static

> Dino shouted at every clerk who made a mistake. Yet each Friday he quietly mailed half his pay to his sick sister. After the flood, he stacked sandbags all night beside the clerks and asked each for a name.

> Mrs. Reyes, the landlady, appears only on rent day. She says "Next month" and nothing else.

> Mr. Cruz, the guard, is only ever strict. In the last scene, he stops being strict and lets the late children in.

> Lola Pilar is proud, funny, and fearful. At the end of the story she is still all three.

Describe each character.

**Solution:**

1. Dino is harsh but secretly kind. He has several sides, so he is round.
2. He shouts at the clerks at first and asks their names later. That is a big change, so he is dynamic.
3. Mrs. Reyes shows one trait and one action, so she is flat. She does not change, so she is static.
4. Mr. Cruz shows one trait, so he is flat. His big change makes him dynamic. A flat character can change.
5. Lola Pilar has several sides, so she is round. She stays the same, so she is static. A round character can stay the same.

### #6: Setting

> The ferry had not run since the typhoon, so Ana walked the long dike at night with her little sister on her back.

How does the setting shape what Ana must do?

**Solution:**

The time (after a typhoon) and place (a dike at night) remove the easy way home. They force Ana into a risky walk.

### #7: Point of View

> **A:** I slammed the door. Nobody in this house ever listens to me.

> **B:** Rica slammed the door. She believed nobody listened to her.

> **C:** Rica slammed the door. She believed nobody listened to her. In the kitchen, her mother only wished the girl would stay.

All three tell the same moment. Name the point of view of each passage.

**Solution:**

1. A uses "I", so it is first person. We know only what the speaker says and thinks.
2. B shows the thoughts of Rica only, so it is third person limited.
3. C also shows her mother's wish, so it is third person omniscient.

### #8: Theme From a Pattern

> Scene one: Lina hides her grandmother's woven mat when friends visit, and she eats alone. Scene two: she skips the fiesta weaving, and again she sits alone. Scene three: she weaves beside her grandmother, and classmates gather to learn.

State a theme, and connect it to real life.

**Solution:**

1. Look for what repeats. Twice Lina hides her tradition, and twice she is alone. When she shares it, others join.
2. A supported theme is that sharing a family tradition brings people together, while hiding it leaves a person alone.
3. In real life, a student may feel shy about a home language until classmates show they value it.

### #9: Style, Mood, and the Author's Motivation

> The rain stopped. The bell rang. Nobody moved. Tomas stared at the letter. His hands shook.

What mood does the style create? What does the author seem to want?

**Solution:**

1. The sentences are short and plain, so they read slowly.
2. This creates a tense mood.
3. The author seems to want us to feel Tomas's fear. The text does not state the author's reason.

### #10: Reading a Filipino Excerpt

> Madilim na nang makauwi si Aling Rosa mula sa palengke. Nakita niyang bukas ang pinto at wala ang anak niyang si Ben. Sa mesa ay may isang sulat. Hindi niya ito agad binuksan dahil natatakot siyang malaman ang nakasulat.

Anong tunggalian ang makikita sa dulo? Alin ang may patunay sa teksto: "Tumakas si Ben" o "Natatakot si Aling Rosa sa nakasulat sa sulat"?

**Solution:**

1. Gusto ni Aling Rosa na malaman ang totoo, pero natatakot siya. Ito ay tao laban sa sarili (character vs. self).
2. Sinasabi ng teksto na natatakot siya sa nakasulat. Kaya ang ikalawang pahayag ang may patunay.
3. Hindi sinasabi ng teksto kung bakit wala si Ben o ano ang nasa sulat. Walang patunay na tumakas si Ben.

## Reading Strategies

**For an excerpt, separate setup from certainty.** An excerpt can prepare an event without guaranteeing its outcome. If a character reaches for a door, you may infer she is about to act. You cannot say what happens next unless the text shows it.

**Track goals, obstacles, and change.** They show the central conflict and the turning points better than the loudest scene does.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Equating Flat with Static and Round with Dynamic

> Complexity and change are different questions.

Ask the two questions one at a time.

### #2: Treating the Antagonist as Always a Villain

> The antagonistic force can be a storm, a custom, or a fear.

Ask what stands in the protagonist's way, not who is bad.

### #3: Calling the Loudest Scene the Climax

> The turning point matters more than the noise.

A chase can come before the real decision. Pick the event that decides the problem.
