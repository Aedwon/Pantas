## Core Concept

Texts can share features. To **classify** a text means to name the type it belongs to. Ask what it mainly does and whose view it gives.

An **essay** develops an idea or experience. In a personal essay, the writer reflects on a life event. An expository essay explains a subject.

Some essays study how parts of something work together. These are analytical essays. An argumentative essay supports a claim with reasons, and essays may combine these purposes.

An **editorial** gives a publication's view, often through its editorial board. A **column, op-ed, or commentary** gives an individual writer's view. Both can support a position with facts and reasons.

A **news report** mainly tells readers what happened or what is known about an issue. It names sources so readers can tell where claims came from. The writer's main task is to report, not to urge readers to take the writer's side.

Reporters still choose which facts and quotes to include. A report can include someone's strong opinion without making that opinion the reporter's own view.

**Attribution** tells who said something or supplied information. In “The mayor said the bridge is safe,” the claim comes from the mayor. Attribution does not prove that the claim is true.

A **byline** is the line naming a text's writer. Labels such as “Editorial,” “Opinion,” “Analysis,” and “News” also help. A name alone does not prove that a text is an opinion piece, since news reports can have bylines too.

Some texts mix facts, explanations, and judgments. Read the whole passage to find its main purpose. Use its label and writer information when those are given.

### Pagkilala sa Tekstong Filipino

Ang **tekstong nagbibigay-impormasyon** (informational text) ay naglalahad o nagpapaliwanag ng mga detalye. Ang **tekstong nagpapahayag ng opinyon** (opinion text) ay nagbibigay ng pananaw at mga dahilan para rito. Maaaring gumamit ng mga totoong detalye ang dalawang uri.

Tingnan kung kanino galing ang pahayag. Ang opinyong sinipi sa isang balita ay hindi agad opinyon ng sumulat ng balita. Basahin ang buong teksto upang malaman ang pangunahing layunin nito.

## Examples

### #1: A Report with a Named Source

“City officials said Tuesday that the bridge would open ahead of schedule. Their statement named the contractor and listed the remaining safety checks.”

Which text type best fits this excerpt?

**Solution:**

1. The passage reports an announcement and names its source.
2. It does not ask readers to support a position. It best fits a news report.

### #2: A Publication's View

A passage labeled “Editorial” reads: “The council should restore library funding. Students need a safe place to read after school.” It is signed “The Editorial Board.”

Whose position does the passage present?

**Solution:**

1. The passage argues for restoring funding.
2. The label and signature identify the publication's view. This is an editorial.

### #3: An Individual Opinion

A passage labeled “Opinion” has the byline “By Lina Reyes.” It reads: “The council should restore library funding. I have seen how much our students depend on the library.”

Is this best described as an editorial or an individual opinion piece?

**Solution:**

1. The writer supports a position and refers to her own experience.
2. The opinion label and named writer support an individual opinion piece. Nothing says she speaks for the publication.

### #4: A Personal Essay

“I fell each time I tried to ride my bicycle. My sister waited while I tried again. Looking back, I remember her patience more clearly than my first successful ride.”

What is the main purpose of this passage?

**Solution:**

1. The writer recalls a personal event.
2. The last sentence explains what that memory means now. The passage reflects on an experience, as a personal essay does.

### #5: Essays with Different Tasks

Passage A explains how a library lends books. Passage B examines how a story's ending changes the meaning of its opening. Passage C argues that public libraries should stay open on weekends.

Which essay type best fits each passage?

**Solution:**

1. Passage A explains a process. It is expository.
2. Passage B studies how parts of a story work together. It is analytical.
3. Passage C supports a proposed action. It is argumentative.

### #6: Facts Used for Different Purposes

Passage A: “Officials estimate that the policy will raise $2$ million pesos each year.”

Passage B: “Officials expect the policy to raise $2$ million pesos each year. Even so, the council should reject it because it adds costs for families who already struggle to pay their bills.”

How do the passages differ in their main purpose?

**Solution:**

1. Passage A reports an estimate and gives its source.
2. Passage B uses the estimate as part of an argument against the policy. Facts can support opinion writing.

### #7: Reporting Mixed with Explanation

A passage labeled “Analysis” reports that a town added a bus route. It then compares the old and new routes to explain why travel times may change. It does not urge readers to support or oppose the change.

What is the main function of the passage?

**Solution:**

1. The passage combines reported facts with an explanation of what they may mean.
2. Its main function is analysis. The label supports this reading, and the passage does not mainly argue for an action.

### #8: A Strong Opinion in a News Report

“The council approved the parking plan on Tuesday. ‘This unfair plan should be canceled,’ said resident Rosa Lim. The transport office said it would review complaints before the plan takes effect.”

Does the quoted statement make this excerpt an opinion piece? Explain using the passage.

**Solution:**

1. The words “said resident Rosa Lim” show whose opinion is quoted. The call to cancel the plan belongs to Lim.
2. The passage reports the council's action, Lim's response, and the office's reply. Its main purpose is news reporting. It does not ask readers to adopt Lim's view.

### #9: Balita o Opinyon?

Teksto A: “Ayon sa abiso ng munisipyo, sarado ang silid-aklatan sa Lunes dahil sa pagkukumpuni.”

Teksto B: “Dapat maglaan ang munisipyo ng ibang lugar na mababasahan habang sarado ang silid-aklatan. Kailangan pa rin ng mga mag-aaral ng tahimik na lugar.”

Ano ang pangunahing pagkakaiba ng layunin ng dalawang teksto?

**Solution:**

1. Nagbibigay ng impormasyon ang Teksto A. Nakasaad din kung saan galing ang detalye.
2. Nagmumungkahi ng gagawin ang Teksto B at nagbibigay ng dahilan. Nagpapahayag ito ng opinyon.

## Reading Strategies

Start with the main task. Does the passage report, explain, reflect, study, or argue? A single strong word cannot settle the answer.

Check who owns each claim. “Residents called the plan unfair” reports the residents' view. “The plan is unfair and should end” directly states a position.

Use labels and bylines with the text. A news byline names a reporter. An opinion label helps show that a named writer is giving a personal view.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating Every Opinion as an Editorial

> Check whether the view belongs to the publication or an individual.

A named columnist can argue strongly without speaking for the publication. Choose “editorial” only when the given evidence supports that role.

### #2: Treating a Quoted View as the Writer's View

> A report can quote an opinion without adopting it.

Look for words that name the speaker. A strong quote alone does not make the whole passage an opinion piece.

### #3: Calling Every Current-Events Passage News

> The topic does not decide the text type.

A bridge project can appear in a report, an essay, or an editorial. Find what the writer does with that topic.

### #4: Assuming Opinions Have No Facts

> Evidence can support an argument.

Numbers and sources do not automatically make a passage straight news. Ask whether they mainly explain an event or support a position.
