## Core Concept

**Specific detail** questions ask for a fact stated in the passage. The answer may be a name, date, number, place, reason, action, or other exact detail.

Two reading skills can help you find that detail faster.

**Skimming** means reading quickly to see what each part of a passage is about. You may read the first sentence of each paragraph and notice repeated words or ideas. This helps you know where to look later.

**Scanning** means looking through the passage for one needed detail. You do not read every line with the same care. You search the part that is most likely to hold the answer, then read that part closely.

Start with the question. Pick out the key idea it asks about, such as a person, event, time, place, or reason. Then scan for that idea in the passage.

The exact words may not match. A question can **paraphrase**, or say the same idea in different words. If the keyword is missing, look for a synonym or a sentence that means the same thing.

## Examples

### #1: Scanning for a Specific Number

A report states: "The community garden opened in $2018$. It now has $24$ planting beds for local families." What year did the community garden open?

**Solution:**
1. The question asks for the opening year.
2. Scan for a number linked to the word "opened."
3. The passage says it opened in $2018$. The answer is $2018$.

### #2: Scanning for the Correct Person

A passage states: "Dr. Elena Cruz proposed the plan. Dr. Ramon Vega later tested it in a larger study." Who proposed the plan?

**Solution:**
1. The question asks who proposed the plan, not who tested it later.
2. Scan for the phrase "proposed the plan."
3. That phrase is linked to Dr. Elena Cruz. She is the answer.

### #3: Skimming a Longer Passage, Then Scanning

Read the passage.

The town library first focused on adding more printed books and replacing worn shelves. Staff also placed clearer signs so visitors could find each section faster.

Later, the library added free computer use and basic digital-skills classes. These services were meant for people who needed internet access or help using online forms.

The library then changed its schedule. It stayed open on two evenings each week so people who worked during the day could still visit.

More recently, staff began holding small reading events for children and families. The events include story reading and simple activities connected to the books.

The question asks: Which change made the library easier to use for people with daytime jobs?

**Solution:**
1. Skim the paragraph topics: books and signs, digital services, schedule, then family events.
2. The question says "people with daytime jobs." The passage uses different words: "people who worked during the day."
3. Scan the schedule paragraph. The answer is that the library stayed open on two evenings each week.

### #4: Avoiding an Old Detail

A passage states: "The festival was once held in March. It moved to April in $2015$ and has stayed in April since then." In what month is the festival held now?

**Solution:**
1. The passage gives both an old month and a current month.
2. The word "now" tells you to find the current detail.
3. The passage says the festival has stayed in April. The answer is April.

Bakit tuwing Sabado nagbubukas ang reading center?

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Skim for location, then scan for the answer.** On a long passage, first notice what each paragraph is about. Go straight to the paragraph that matches the question.

**Search for meaning, not only exact words.** If the question says "daytime jobs," the passage may say "worked during the day." Treat those as a match in meaning.

**Read around the match.** Once you find the likely detail, read the whole sentence and the nearby sentence if needed. This keeps you from choosing a nearby but wrong detail.

**Watch words that show time or change.** Words such as *before, later, now, since,* and *currently* can tell you which of two similar details the question wants.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Stopping at the First Matching Word

> The first matching word may point to the wrong detail.

A passage may name several people, dates, or places. After scanning, check that the detail answers the exact role or time in the question.

### #2: Looking Only for Exact Keywords

> The question and passage may use different words for the same idea.

If you cannot find the exact phrase from the question, look for a synonym or a sentence with the same meaning. Do not assume the detail is missing.

### #3: Confusing Old and Current Facts

> A passage may state what used to be true and what is true now.

Notice time words before choosing the answer. If the question asks about the current situation, do not stop at an older detail that appears first.

### #4: Using Skimming as the Final Answer Step

> Skimming helps you find the right area, but it may miss an exact detail.

After you locate the right paragraph, slow down and scan it carefully. A name, number, or small wording change can decide the answer.
