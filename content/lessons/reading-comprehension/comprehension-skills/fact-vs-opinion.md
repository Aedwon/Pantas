## Core Concept

A **factual claim** is a statement you can check as true or false by using records, measurements, observation, or other evidence. A factual claim can still be wrong. The label tells you what kind of claim it is, not whether it is true.

An **opinion or value judgment** tells what someone thinks, prefers, recommends, or judges. Words such as *best, unfair, beautiful,* and *should* often point to an opinion because they depend on a person's values or standards.

A sentence can contain both kinds. "The hall opened last year and is the best building in town" has a checkable claim about when it opened and an opinion about its quality.

### Projections and forecasts

A **projection or forecast** says what may happen in the future based on information available now. It is not a settled fact about the future.

An **assumption** is something an estimate treats as true while making the forecast. If the assumption changes, the projection may also change.

These three statements are different:

- **Fact about a projection:** "The agency published a projection of $10$ million people by $2030$." You can check whether the agency published that projection.
- **Projection itself:** "The population will reach $10$ million by $2030$." This says what is expected to happen later.
- **Opinion:** "A population of $10$ million would be terrible." The word "terrible" gives a judgment.

Keep claim type separate from evidence. A factual claim can have strong evidence, weak evidence, or no evidence given in the passage. It is still a factual claim if it is the kind of statement that can be checked.

## Examples

### #1: Basic Fact and Opinion

Classify each statement as a factual claim or an opinion.

(a) "The school library closes in the afternoon."  
(b) "The school library is the nicest room on campus."

**Solution:**
1. (a) is a factual claim because the closing time can be checked from a schedule or by observation.
2. (b) is an opinion because "nicest" is a judgment.

### #2: A Factual Claim Without Given Evidence

A report says, "Workers in Team A completed more tasks per hour than workers in Team B last month." Is this a factual claim or an opinion?

**Solution:**
1. The statement compares something that can be counted and measured.
2. The passage does not show the records, but missing evidence does not change the claim type.
3. It is a factual claim. You would need evidence to decide whether it is true.

### #3: A Mixed Statement

Classify the two parts of this sentence: "The new hall cost two billion pesos to build and was a waste of public money."

**Solution:**
1. "Cost two billion pesos to build" is a factual claim because records can check the cost.
2. "Was a waste of public money" is an opinion because it gives a judgment about whether the spending was worthwhile.

### #4: A Projection

A planning report says, "Based on current assumptions, the population is projected to reach $10$ million by $2030$." What kind of claim is this?

**Solution:**
1. The statement is about a future population.
2. It depends on current assumptions, so the future result is not settled.
3. It is a projection.

### #5: A Factual Claim That Is False

A match record says the Blue Team won three games. A student says, "The Blue Team won five games." What kind of claim is the student's statement?

**Solution:**
1. The number of games won can be checked in the record, so the statement is a factual claim.
2. The record shows that the claim is false.
3. This shows why claim type and truth are separate questions.

### #6: A Fact About a Projection

A passage says that an agency published a forecast in June. The forecast predicts higher demand for electricity next year. Which statement is a factual claim about the forecast: "The forecast was published in June" or "Demand will definitely rise next year"?

**Solution:**
1. The publication date can be checked in the agency's records.
2. The future demand has not happened yet, so it is not a settled fact.
3. "The forecast was published in June" is the factual claim about the projection.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

**Ask what can be checked.** If records, counting, measurement, or observation could show the statement true or false, it is a factual claim.

**Look for judgment words.** Words such as *best, unfair, beautiful, should,* and *wasteful* often signal an opinion. Still read the whole sentence because one sentence can mix fact and opinion.

**Look for future language.** Words such as *projected, forecast, expected,* and *likely* often signal a projection when the statement is about what may happen later.

**Separate type from support.** Do not call a claim an opinion only because the passage gives no proof. First decide what kind of claim it is. Then ask whether the passage supports it.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Thinking a Factual Claim Must Be True

> A factual claim can be checked and still turn out to be false.

If a record proves a checkable statement wrong, the statement does not become an opinion. It remains a factual claim, but a false one.

### #2: Treating a Forecast as a Settled Future Fact

> A projection tells what may happen, not what has already been proved about the future.

You can verify that a forecast was published. You cannot treat its predicted result as already observed.

### #3: Treating Every Number as a Fact

> A number can appear inside a fact, an estimate, a projection, or even a false claim.

Ask what the number means and what kind of statement it is part of. The presence of a number alone does not decide the claim type.

### #4: Treating Opinion as the Same as Unsupported Claim

> Evidence and claim type are separate questions.

An opinion can be supported by reasons. A factual claim can be unsupported or false. Classify the claim first, then judge its support.
