## Core Concept

### Reading the Labels and Scale

Graphs, maps, diagrams and charts arrange information outside ordinary paragraphs. Read the title and labels first. A **legend** explains symbols, and a **unit** tells what is counted. A **scale** tells how much each unit stands for. To find a value, count the units and multiply by the scale.

Let's find how many more visitors came on Tuesday than on Wednesday. Each unit stands for 4 visitors. The numbers are sample data.

| Library Visitors | Units |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | 2 |
| Tuesday | 5 |
| Wednesday | 3 |

1. Read the scale: each unit stands for 4 visitors.
2. Convert Tuesday's 5 units.

$$5\times4=20$$

3. Convert Wednesday's 3 units.

$$3\times4=12$$

4. Subtract the visitor counts.

$$20-12=8$$

Tuesday had 8 more visitors than Wednesday.

**Check:** The difference is 2 units, and 2 units are $2\times4=8$ visitors.

:::pair #units-not-counts
Right: 8 visitors
Wrong: 2 visitors
The wrong answer is the difference in units. Multiply by the scale to get visitors.
:::

:::check
Each unit stands for 6 books. The numbers are sample data. How many more books are on shelf B than on shelf C?

- Shelf A: 3 units
- Shelf B: 5 units
- Shelf C: 2 units
:::answer
18 books. Shelf B has $5\times6=30$ books, shelf C has $2\times6=12$ books, and $30-12=18$.
:::

### Using a Written Note With the Data

A written note can give a limit or a rule. Convert the data to real values first. Then apply the note exactly as worded. "More than 50" does not include 50, and "the next morning" refers to the day after.

Let's find which mornings had a second serving line. Each unit stands for 10 trays. The note says a second line opens the next morning after any day with more than 50 trays sold.

| Trays Sold | Units |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | 6 |
| Tuesday | 7 |
| Wednesday | 3 |
| Thursday | 4 |

1. Convert each day to trays: Monday 60, Tuesday 70, Wednesday 30, Thursday 40.
2. Apply "more than 50". Monday (60) and Tuesday (70) qualify.
3. Apply "the next morning". Tuesday and Wednesday follow them.

The second line opened on Tuesday and Wednesday.

**Check:** Wednesday's 30 and Thursday's 40 are not more than 50, so Thursday has no second line.

:::pair #same-morning
Right: Tuesday and Wednesday
Wrong: Monday and Tuesday
The wrong answer names the days with high sales. The note opens the line on the next morning.
:::

:::check
Each unit stands for 5 pupils. The note says the nurse calls in a second helper on any morning when 30 or more pupils are served. Which mornings had a second helper?

- Monday: 4 units
- Tuesday: 7 units
- Wednesday: 5 units
- Thursday: 8 units

A) Tuesday and Thursday

B) Thursday only

C) Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday

D) Every morning
:::answer
A. Tuesday is $7\times5=35$ and Thursday is $8\times5=40$, so both are 30 or more. Monday is 20 and Wednesday is 25.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Help Desk Visitors

Which statement does the bar graph support? The numbers are sample data.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal bar graph titled "Help Desk Visitors"; horizontal axis "Visitors" from 0 to 30 in steps of 5; Monday bar 20, Tuesday bar 30, Wednesday bar 15; all bars start at zero -->

| Day | Visitors |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | 20 |
| Tuesday | 30 |
| Wednesday | 15 |

A) Tuesday had twice as many visitors as Wednesday.

B) More visitors came on Tuesday because it was a holiday.

C) Monday had the fewest visitors.

D) Wednesday had half as many visitors as Monday.

**Solution:**

Let's read each bar against the Visitors axis, then test the choices:

1. Read the axis: it counts visitors from 0 to 30 in steps of 5.
2. Read each bar: Monday 20, Tuesday 30, Wednesday 15.
3. Test A: $15\times2=30$, so Tuesday had twice Wednesday's visitors.
4. Test B: the graph gives no reason for any count.
5. Test C and D: Wednesday's 15 is the fewest, and half of Monday's 20 is 10, not 15.

Supported statement: Tuesday had twice as many visitors as Wednesday.

**Check:** Halve Tuesday's bar: $30\div2=15$, the length of Wednesday's bar.

### #2: An Extra Window

The same help desk opens an extra window the following morning after any day with more than 25 visitors. The line on the graph marks 25. Which morning had an extra window?

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: horizontal bar graph titled "Help Desk Visitors"; horizontal axis "Visitors" from 0 to 30 in steps of 5; Monday bar 20, Tuesday bar 30, Wednesday bar 15; vertical reference line at 25 labeled "Extra window opens above 25" -->

**Solution:**

1. Read each bar: Monday 20, Tuesday 30, Wednesday 15.
2. Apply "more than 25": only Tuesday's bar passes the line.

:::try
Finish the last step: which morning follows that day, and so had the extra window?
:::answer
Wednesday. The window opens the morning after Tuesday, the only day above 25.

**Check:** Monday (20) and Wednesday (15) stop before the line, so Tuesday and Thursday get no extra window.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Counting units as the real number: multiply by the scale first. {#units-not-counts}
- A note with "the next morning": apply it to the day after the high count, not the high day. {#same-morning}
- A chart of counts alone: it does not explain why the counts changed. {#reading-the-labels-and-scale}
:::
