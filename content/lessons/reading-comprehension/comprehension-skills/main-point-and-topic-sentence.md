## Core Concept

### Topic, Main Idea and Details

The **topic** is what a paragraph is about, in a few words. The **main idea** is the most important point the paragraph makes about that topic. A **supporting detail** is a fact or example that helps explain the main idea.

Let's find the topic and main idea of the paragraph about the market:

> The public market gets ready for rainy days in several ways. Vendors move their goods under the covered walkway, the guard hands out plastic sheets, and the cleaners dry the floor every hour.

1. Ask what the paragraph is about. The clue is "gets ready for rainy days". Topic: how the market prepares for rain.
2. Ask what the paragraph says about it. The clues are "move their goods", "hands out plastic sheets" and "dry the floor every hour". Each names one action, so each is a supporting detail.
3. The first sentence is the point that all three actions explain.

Main idea: The public market gets ready for rainy days in several ways.

**Check:** Each detail answers "How does the market get ready?" The topic is only a phrase. The main idea is a full statement.

:::pair #detail-or-main
Right: Supporting detail: The guard hands out plastic sheets.
Wrong: Supporting detail: Community trust and financial discipline help stores survive.
The wrong choice sums up a whole passage, so it is a main idea. A supporting detail names one specific action or fact.
:::

:::check
Read the paragraph. Which choice states the main idea, not just the topic?

> Every Friday the school canteen sells only snacks made with local fruit. Students can buy banana chips, mango bars and guava jelly. The staff say these snacks are cheaper and fresher than packaged ones.

A) Local fruit snacks at the school canteen

B) On Fridays, the school canteen sells only snacks made with local fruit.

C) Mango bars are one of the snacks students can buy.

D) Students should eat fruit every day.
:::answer
B. It is a full statement and the other sentences explain it. A only names the topic, C is one example, and D is not stated.
:::

### Where the Topic Sentence Sits

A **topic sentence** states the main idea of a paragraph in one sentence. It is often first, but it can also come in the middle or at the end. Some paragraphs have no topic sentence at all. The next part covers those.

Let's find the topic sentence of the paragraph about the jeepney:

> Passengers at the corner stop waited more than an hour. Two jeepneys broke down on the route that morning. Many people walked to work instead. The morning ride to town was slow and hard for everyone.

1. "waited more than an hour" is one fact about the delay.
2. "Two jeepneys broke down" is a cause of the delay.
3. "Many people walked to work" is a result of the delay.
4. "slow and hard for everyone" covers all three facts above it.

Topic sentence: The morning ride to town was slow and hard for everyone.

**Check:** Each of the first three sentences explains why the ride was slow and hard.

:::pair #problem-only
Right: Topic sentence: The morning ride to town was slow and hard for everyone.
Wrong: Topic sentence: "Kapag hindi siya nakadalo, halos walang nakagagawa ng ibang tungkulin."
The wrong sentence is Filipino for "when he is absent, almost nobody does the other duties." It names only the problem. The topic sentence covers all the others.
:::

:::check
Which sentence is the topic sentence?

> Few families came to the barangay clinic on weekdays. Opening on Saturdays gave working parents a chance to bring their children. Three more families arrived the first weekend. The next Saturday, five came.

A) Few families came to the barangay clinic on weekdays.

B) Opening on Saturdays gave working parents a chance to bring their children.

C) Three more families arrived the first weekend.

D) The next Saturday, five came.
:::answer
B. It gives the point, and the sentences after it show how Saturday opening helped. A gives the starting problem. C and D are results.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Notice From the Barangay Hall

Which sentence states the main idea of the paragraph?

> The barangay hall now opens an hour earlier. The guard unlocks the gate at seven. Two clerks are at the counter by seven fifteen.

A) The barangay hall now opens an hour earlier.

B) The guard unlocks the gate at seven.

C) Two clerks are at the counter by seven fifteen.

D) Workers should visit the hall every day.

**Solution:**

1. "unlocks the gate at seven" is one action.
2. "at the counter by seven fifteen" is another action.
3. Both actions show the hall opening earlier. Choice A states that point.

Main idea: The barangay hall now opens an hour earlier.

**Check:** If the hall opens earlier, the guard and clerks must start earlier. D is not stated anywhere.

### #2: The Tricycle Repairs

Which sentence states the main idea of the paragraph?

> Mang Ben replaced the brakes on his tricycle last month. He takes good care of the tricycle he drives every day. He also fixed a cracked mirror and repainted the side. Passengers say the ride feels safe and smooth.

**Solution:**

1. "replaced the brakes" names one repair.
2. "fixed a cracked mirror and repainted the side" names two more repairs.

:::try
Finish the last step: which sentence covers all the repairs, "He takes good care of the tricycle he drives every day." or "Passengers say the ride feels safe and smooth."?
:::answer
"He takes good care of the tricycle he drives every day." Brakes, mirror and paint are all ways of taking care. The other sentence reports what passengers say, not the repairs.

**Check:** Place the topic sentence first. Each repair still explains it.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Asked for a supporting detail: choose the sentence that names one action, not the one that sums up the paragraph. {#detail-or-main}
- Looking for the topic sentence: test every sentence, because it is often first but can sit in the middle or at the end. {#where-the-topic-sentence-sits}
- A sentence that names only the problem: choose the sentence that covers all the others. {#problem-only}
:::
