## Core Concept

### Details That Suggest a Point

An **implied main idea** is a point the details suggest without stating it. Ask what the important details have in common. Then say that idea in your own words. Do not add a claim the details do not support.

Let's find the main idea suggested by the passage about Lani:

> Lani wakes at four each morning to bake pandesal. She sells it from a cart at the corner, then walks her younger brothers to school. In the evening she sorts the day's coins into jars marked rent, rice and school fees.

1. "wakes at four each morning to bake" shows hard work.
2. "walks her younger brothers to school" shows care for her family.
3. "jars marked rent, rice and school fees" shows planning for her family's needs.

Main idea: Lani works hard and plans carefully to support her family.

**Check:** All three details support it, and no sentence says it directly.

:::pair #unsupported-motive
Right: Lani works hard and plans carefully to support her family.
Wrong: She rejected the original proposals because of severe budget limitations.
The wrong choice adds a reason that no detail gives. State only what the details support.
:::

:::pair #one-detail-preference
Right: Lani puts her family's needs first.
Wrong: She favors youth athletic activities over local agricultural projects.
The wrong choice stretches one detail into a preference. Test the idea against all the details, not one.
:::

:::check
Which statement do the details suggest?

> Each afternoon, Ate Grace waters the plants along the barangay road. She picks up candy wrappers near the basketball court. Last Sunday she asked neighbors to help her paint the old fence.

A) Ate Grace works to keep her neighborhood clean and pleasant.

B) Ate Grace dislikes her neighbors.

C) Ate Grace is the barangay captain.

D) The basketball court needs repair.
:::answer
A. Watering plants, picking up wrappers and painting a fence all improve the neighborhood. No detail supports B, C or D.
:::

### Sentences That Belong

A paragraph has **unity** when every sentence helps explain its main point. A sentence about something else does not belong. Test each sentence: does it help explain the point?

Let's find the sentence that does not belong in the paragraph about the school bus:

> The school bus leaves the depot at six thirty. It stops at the same four corners in the same order. The principal's office was painted green last year. It reaches the school gate by seven fifteen.

1. "leaves the depot at six thirty", "the same four corners in the same order" and "reaches the school gate by seven fifteen" all show a fixed routine.
2. "The principal's office was painted green last year" says nothing about the routine.

Sentence that does not belong: The principal's office was painted green last year.

**Check:** Without it, the other three sentences all describe one routine.

:::check
Which sentence does not belong in the paragraph?

> The reading corner is quiet in the afternoon. Children share picture books on the mat. A tricycle is parked outside the gate. An older boy reads a story to the younger ones.

A) The reading corner is quiet in the afternoon.

B) Children share picture books on the mat.

C) A tricycle is parked outside the gate.

D) An older boy reads a story to the younger ones.
:::answer
C. The other three sentences are about reading in the corner. The parked tricycle has nothing to do with it.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Family at the Start of Typhoon Season

Which statement best expresses the main idea the passage suggests?

> Before the typhoon season, Mang Tonyo tied down the roof of his house and stored extra water. His wife packed a bag with flashlights and dry clothes. The family agreed on which school hall they would walk to if the water rose.

A) The family prepared early for a possible typhoon.

B) Mang Tonyo is the only person who works at home.

C) Typhoons always flood the school hall.

D) Roofs must be tied down in every house.

**Solution:**

1. "tied down the roof" and "stored extra water" show Mang Tonyo preparing.
2. "packed a bag with flashlights and dry clothes" shows his wife preparing.
3. "agreed on which school hall they would walk to" shows the whole family planning ahead.

Main idea: The family prepared early for a possible typhoon.

**Check:** B ignores the wife's work. C and D make claims about all typhoons and all houses, which the passage never makes.

### #2: A Visit to the Clinic

What main idea do the details suggest?

> Nena arrives at the clinic ten minutes before it opens. She brings her papers in a folder, already arranged in order. When her number is called, she answers each question briefly and thanks the nurse.

**Solution:**

1. "ten minutes before it opens" shows she is early.
2. "papers in a folder, already arranged in order" shows she is ready.

:::try
Finish the last step: which statement covers all three details, "Nena is prepared and polite at the clinic." or "Nena is always the first in line."?
:::answer
"Nena is prepared and polite at the clinic." Early arrival and ordered papers show she is prepared. Answering briefly and thanking the nurse show she is polite. No detail says she is always first.

**Check:** Each of the three details supports the first statement.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- No sentence states the point: connect the details, then say their shared idea yourself. {#details-that-suggest-a-point}
- A choice that adds a reason or motive: reject it unless a detail supports it. {#unsupported-motive}
- A choice built from one detail: test it against every detail. {#one-detail-preference}
:::
