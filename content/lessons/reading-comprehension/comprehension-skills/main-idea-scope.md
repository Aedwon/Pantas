## Core Concept

### Testing a Statement Against the Details

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: A box labeled "Main idea" connected to three boxes labeled "Supporting detail", showing that all three details explain the same main idea. -->

A main idea has the right **scope**, or range of ideas covered. It includes the important details of the text. It does not cover only one detail, and it does not make a claim about a much larger subject.

Let's test two statements against the passage about the reading hour:

> The Saturday reading hour at the barangay hall lets children borrow picture books. Volunteers read stories aloud. Parents sit at the back and learn songs to sing at home.

1. The important details are "borrow picture books", "Volunteers read stories aloud" and "Parents ... learn songs".
2. Test "Volunteers read stories aloud." It covers one detail and leaves out the other two. It is too narrow.
3. Test "The Saturday reading hour offers activities for children and their parents." It covers all three details.

Main idea: The Saturday reading hour at the barangay hall offers activities for children and their parents.

**Check:** Each detail fits inside the main idea, and the main idea says nothing beyond them.

:::pair #too-narrow
Right: Title: A Saturday Reading Hour for Children and Parents
Wrong: Title: Cleaning Plastic: The Project's Only Environmental Goal
The wrong title names one detail and calls it the only goal. A main idea has to cover all the important details.
:::

:::check
Which statement covers the important details without going past them?

> The fishing cooperative gives members boat repairs at low cost. It holds a monthly meeting to share fish prices. It also keeps a small fund for members who fall ill.

A) The cooperative holds a monthly meeting.

B) The cooperative offers its members several kinds of help.

C) The cooperative's fund is its most important service.

D) The cooperative pays for members' boat fuel.
:::answer
B. It covers repairs, meetings and the fund. A covers one detail. C ranks one service above the others, and D is not stated.
:::

### Testing a Statement Against the Passage

A statement is too broad when it makes a claim about a larger group or subject than the passage discusses. Check whether the passage says it.

Let's test two statements against the passage about the bakery:

> Mang Dado's bakery sells pandesal at five in the morning. He lets school pupils buy bread on credit until Friday. When the power went out, he baked in a wood oven so neighbors still had bread.

1. The important details are early pandesal, credit for pupils and the wood oven.
2. Test "Bakeries are important to every town." The passage is about one bakery and says nothing about every town. It is too broad.
3. Test "Mang Dado's bakery serves its neighbors in practical ways." It covers all three details and stays on one bakery.

Main idea: Mang Dado's bakery serves its neighbors in practical ways.

**Check:** Each detail is one practical way of serving neighbors.

:::pair #too-broad
Right: Title: Mang Dado's Bakery Serves Its Neighbors
Wrong: Title: The Complete History of Quezon Fishpond Construction
The wrong title is about a much larger subject than the passage discusses. Choose the statement that stays inside the passage.
:::

:::check
Which statement has the right scope?

> A school's bulletin board posts the week's lunch menu, the sports schedule and notices about lost items. Teachers also pin the names of pupils with perfect attendance.

A) A school's bulletin board shares different kinds of school news.

B) Schools should share all news publicly.

C) The bulletin board lists the lunch menu.

D) Pupils with perfect attendance receive prizes.
:::answer
A. It covers the menu, schedule, notices and names. B is too broad, C is too narrow, and D is not stated.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Weekend Market

Which statement best expresses the main idea of the passage?

> The town's night market opens only on weekends. Vendors sell grilled corn, secondhand books and handmade slippers. Families come to eat, shop and listen to a small band.

A) Families enjoy listening to the band at the night market.

B) Weekend markets are the best way to spend free time.

C) The weekend night market offers food, goods and entertainment for families.

D) Vendors earn more money at night than during the day.

**Solution:**

1. The important details are food ("grilled corn"), goods ("secondhand books and handmade slippers") and music ("a small band").
2. Test A. It covers only the band, so it is too narrow.
3. Test B. The passage does not compare weekend markets with other pastimes, so it is too broad.
4. Test D. The passage never mentions earnings, so it is not stated.
5. Test C. It covers all three kinds of detail.

Main idea: The weekend night market offers food, goods and entertainment for families.

**Check:** C is the only choice that includes the food, the goods and the band.

### #2: A Classroom Chart

Which title best fits the passage?

> Pupils in Grade 5 took turns sweeping the room, watering the plants and arranging the chairs. The teacher listed each chore on a chart. By the end of the month, nearly everyone had tried every job.

A) "Sweeping Is Hard Work"

B) "A Chart for Sharing Classroom Chores"

C) "How Schools Teach Responsibility Everywhere"

D) "Plants in the Classroom"

**Solution:**

1. The important details are three chores, the chart and everyone trying every job.
2. A covers one chore, and D covers only the plants. Both are too narrow.

:::try
Finish the last step: which title covers all the details without going past the classroom, "A Chart for Sharing Classroom Chores" or "How Schools Teach Responsibility Everywhere"?
:::answer
"A Chart for Sharing Classroom Chores." It covers the chores, the chart and the turns. The other title makes a claim about all schools.

**Check:** Read the chosen title back against each sentence of the passage. Each sentence fits it.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A statement that names one detail, often with "only": test it against all the details. {#too-narrow}
- A statement about a much larger group or subject: check whether the passage says it. {#too-broad}
- Two choices that both sound right: pick the one that covers every important detail and nothing more. {#testing-a-statement-against-the-details}
:::
