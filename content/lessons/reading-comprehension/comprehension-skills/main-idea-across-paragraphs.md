## Core Concept

### Joining the Points of Each Paragraph

The **main idea** of a passage is its most important point. In a longer passage, first state each paragraph's point in a few words. Then choose the idea that covers all of those points.

Let's find the main idea of the passage about the canal:

> Last year the barangay's drainage canal flooded the lower street each time it rained. Residents lost furniture, and children could not walk to school.
>
> This year the council widened the canal and trained volunteers to clear it every week. After the first heavy rain, the street stayed dry.

1. Paragraph 1: "flooded the lower street each time it rained" is a problem.
2. Paragraph 2: "widened the canal" and "clear it every week" are the response, and "the street stayed dry" is the result.
3. The main idea joins the problem and the response.

Main idea: The barangay solved a flooding problem by widening the canal and clearing it regularly.

**Check:** Both paragraphs fit inside this statement, and neither is left out.

:::pair #one-side-only
Right: The barangay solved a flooding problem by widening the canal and clearing it regularly.
Wrong: Safe nearby recreation was a need identified by the youth.
The wrong choice is one group's point from one part of its passage. The main idea must cover all the paragraphs.
:::

:::check
Which statement is the main idea of the passage?

> Each month, the school's Grade 6 pupils collect used paper in boxes outside every room. The boxes are weighed on the last Friday.
>
> The paper is sold to a recycler, and the money pays for new books in the reading corner.

A) Pupils weigh boxes of paper on Fridays.

B) Pupils collect and sell used paper to pay for new books.

C) Recycling is the best way to protect the planet.

D) The school buys its books from a recycler.
:::answer
B. Paragraph 1 is about collecting paper and paragraph 2 is about selling it for books. A is one detail, C is not stated, and D reverses who buys.
:::

### Joining Points That Differ

When a passage gives two sides or a limit, the main idea keeps both. A statement that keeps only one side leaves out half of the passage.

Let's find the main idea of the passage about buying fish:

> Some families in the village prefer to buy fish at the morning market because it is fresh.
>
> Others buy frozen fish from the store because it keeps for a week and costs less.

1. Paragraph 1: "fresh" is the reason for buying at the market.
2. Paragraph 2: "keeps for a week and costs less" is the reason for buying frozen fish.
3. The passage describes two choices, so the main idea keeps both.

Main idea: Families buy fresh fish at the market or cheaper frozen fish from the store.

**Check:** A statement about fresh fish alone fits only paragraph 1.

:::pair #detail-as-rule
Right: Families buy fresh fish at the market or cheaper frozen fish from the store.
Wrong: Weekly wholesale buying is the only requirement for survival.
The wrong choice turns one detail into the only rule. Keep the limits and the other sides the passage gives.
:::

:::check
Which statement is the main idea of the passage?

> Online classes let pupils study from home without paying for travel.
>
> But pupils with weak internet often miss parts of the lesson, so some schools still hold face-to-face classes.

A) Online classes save travel costs but do not work equally well for every pupil.

B) Online classes should replace face-to-face classes.

C) Pupils without internet should not go to school.

D) Travel costs are the main problem in education.
:::answer
A. It keeps both the benefit and the limit. B and C go past the passage, and D is not stated.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A New Route

Which statement best expresses the main idea of the passage?

> A new bus route now links the hillside barangay to the town center. Pupils who once walked two hours reach class in twenty minutes.
>
> The route also helps farmers, who now carry vegetables to the town market before noon.

A) A new bus route helps both pupils and farmers reach the town center.

B) Pupils used to walk two hours to class.

C) Farmers should sell vegetables only before noon.

D) The town market has the best prices in the province.

**Solution:**

1. Paragraph 1: "reach class in twenty minutes" is a benefit for pupils.
2. Paragraph 2: "carry vegetables to the town market" is a benefit for farmers.
3. Choice A covers both groups. B is one detail, and C and D are not stated.

Main idea: A new bus route helps both pupils and farmers reach the town center.

**Check:** Remove farmers from A and paragraph 2 is left out.

### #2: Trash at the Market

Which statement best expresses the main idea of the passage?

> At first, the market's trash piled up behind the fish stalls. Vendors complained about the smell.
>
> The council placed covered bins at every row and hired two cleaners in the morning. Vendors now say the market smells fresh by eight.

**Solution:**

1. Paragraph 1: "trash piled up" and "complained about the smell" are the problem.
2. Paragraph 2: "covered bins" and "two cleaners" are the response.

:::try
Finish the last step: which statement covers both paragraphs, "The council fixed the market's trash problem with bins and cleaners." or "Vendors complained about the smell."?
:::answer
"The council fixed the market's trash problem with bins and cleaners." It covers the problem and the response. The other sentence covers only paragraph 1.

**Check:** The last sentence of the passage, "smells fresh by eight", shows the problem was fixed.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- A choice that matches only one paragraph: state each paragraph's point first, then pick the choice that covers all of them. {#one-side-only}
- A choice that makes one detail the only rule: keep the limits and other sides the passage gives. {#detail-as-rule}
- A passage with two sides: the main idea keeps both. {#joining-points-that-differ}
:::
