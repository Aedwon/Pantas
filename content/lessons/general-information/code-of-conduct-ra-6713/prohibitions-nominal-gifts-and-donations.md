## Core Concept

### Unsolicited Nominal Gifts

RA $6713$ sets separate rules for each gift exception. Check the giver and purpose first, then test every condition of that exception.

Under Section $3(c)$, a prohibited gift excludes an **unsolicited** gift of **nominal or insignificant value**. It must not be given in anticipation of, or in exchange for, a favor. Giving a cheap token afterward never excuses a favor reward.

There is no fixed peso cutoff. Under Rule X, Section $1(f)(1)$, nominal value depends on the case circumstances:

1. The **salary** of the official or employee.
2. **How often** such gifts are given.
3. The **expectation of benefits**, plus similar factors.

Evaluate a clerk receiving an unrequested cheap keychain from a community workshop host with no expected or exchanged favor:

1. Check request and purpose: the clerk never asked for it, and no official favor is expected or rewarded.
2. Check nominal value: under the circumstances, one inexpensive keychain is nominal and fits Section $3(c)$.

:::pair #small-gift-no-favor
Right: An unsolicited nominal gift fits Section $3(c)$ only when no favor is expected or exchanged.
Wrong: Accepting a requested small token for quick permit approval, or treating low price alone as a complete exemption.
Check three points together: unsolicited, nominal under the circumstances, and unrelated to any expected or exchanged favor.
:::

:::pair #post-service-or-courtesy-note
Right: Polite wording or giving money as early thanks never cures a gift tied to an expected official favor.
Wrong: Relying on polite wording alone regardless of a gift's value or official connection.
Look past polite cards and timing to test actual value, frequency, and expected official action.
:::

:::check
A courier leaves a modest fruit basket every Friday for a clerk clearing port manifests, expecting smoother processing. Why does that fail the nominal-gift rule?
:::answer
The answer is **Weekly baskets tied to smoother processing fail both nominal-value factors and the no-favor rule.** Rule X weighs salary, frequency, and expected benefits, and Section $3(c)$ bars expected favors.
:::

### Nominal Donations From Persons

Rule X, Section $1(f)(3)$ sets a separate test for **nominal donations from any person**. Keep this test separate from personal gifts and organization donations.

Two conditions must both hold for a nominal donation from a person:

1. The donor has **no regular, pending, or expected transaction** with the department, office, or agency.
2. **No pecuniary (money or financial) gain or benefit** is expected.

Even with zero pending files today, planning to file an application soon creates an **expected** agency transaction and defeats this exception.

Test a retired nurse donating $2$ small clinic wall fans without expecting money back, with no past, active, or planned agency dealings:

1. Check dealings: she has no regular, pending, or expected transaction with the clinic or agency.
2. Check financial benefit: no money or financial gain is expected, so the personal-donation exception applies.

:::why
Why bar expected transactions on personal donations? Someone planning a bid or permit next month could use small early donations to buy goodwill before filing.
:::

:::pair #expected-transaction-bars-donation
Right: Personal nominal donations require no regular, pending, and no expected transaction with the agency.
Wrong: Checking only already-pending transactions, or thinking nominal value removes all conditions about agency dealings.
Check all three time frames for the donor's dealings: regular, pending, and expected transactions.
:::

:::check
A store owner has no open engineering file today, yet plans to bid on a street-lighting contract next week. He offers $3$ cheap clipboards expecting no cash back. Does this qualify?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Planning to bid next week creates an expected office transaction, defeating Rule X, Section $1(f)(3)$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Frequency and Salary Against Price

A low-paid clerk gets an unrequested $100$-peso meal voucher every Monday from a regular courier filer. Each voucher costs little, yet the habit builds goodwill. Does Section $3(c)$ allow it?

**Solution:**

1. Check frequency and salary: Rule X, Section $1(f)(1)$ weighs salary, gift frequency, and expected benefits.
2. Check purpose: weekly vouchers from a regular filer fail frequency and the no-favor rule.

Answer: No, weekly vouchers fail the nominal-gift exception.

**Check:** Low price never cures frequent gifts or expected favors.

### #2: Polite Reward After Action

After a zoning clerk rushes a warehouse clearance, the owner sends a cheap desk calendar thanking her politely for special help. Does polite wording and low price save it?

**Solution:**

1. Check the favor: the calendar rewards priority in an official action.
2. Apply Section $3(c)$: giving a cheap gift afterward for a favor fails the no-favor condition.

Answer: No, polite wording and low value never excuse a favor-linked gift.

**Check:** Test whether the gift anticipates or rewards an official favor, even afterward.

### #3: Two Citizens' Nominal Donations

Two citizens offer pens to a licensing bureau, expecting no money back. Citizen A has no regular, pending, or planned dealings there. Citizen B plans a commercial permit next month. Rule on both offers.

**Solution:**

1. Check Citizen A: no gain is expected, and there is no regular, pending, or expected transaction.
2. Check Citizen B: no gain is expected, yet next month's planned permit must pass the transaction rule.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on Citizen A and Citizen B under Rule X, Section $1(f)(3)$.
:::answer
The answer is **Citizen A meets the personal-donation exception, while Citizen B fails it.** Citizen B expects to file a permit next month, and an expected agency transaction defeats the exception despite small value.

**Check:** Check regular, pending, and expected transactions together before approving a nominal personal donation.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Solicited or favor-linked small gifts: reject any requested or favor-tied gift regardless of low price. {#small-gift-no-favor}
- Polite notes or early thanks: check expected official favors beyond wording and timing. {#post-service-or-courtesy-note}
- Nominal personal donations: bar the donation whenever a regular, pending, or expected agency transaction exists. {#expected-transaction-bars-donation}
:::
