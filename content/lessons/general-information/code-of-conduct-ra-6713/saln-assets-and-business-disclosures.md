## Core Concept

### Assets and Property Values

Section $8$ of RA $6713$ requires two sworn financial declarations. **Under oath** means sworn to be true.

The documents are the **Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Net Worth (SALN)** and the **Disclosure of Business Interests and Financial Connections**.

**Assets** are things you own, like land, buildings, vehicles, cash, savings, and investments.

For **real property**, report improvements, **acquisition cost**, **assessed value**, and current **fair market value**:

- **Improvements**: buildings added to land.
- **Acquisition cost**: price paid to acquire the property.
- **Assessed value**: value used for property tax.
- **Fair market value**: market value in the latest tax declaration.

If the tax declaration is missing or lists no market value, use the local Assessor's Office or Bureau of Internal Revenue zonal value. If no tax declaration exists, enter "N/A" for assessed and fair market values.

For personal property like vehicles, report acquisition cost.

Enter a clerk's farmland bought for ₱$650{,}000$, assessed at ₱$310{,}000$, with a ₱$580{,}000$ tax-declaration market value, plus a ₱$90{,}000$ motorcycle:

1. List the farmland under real property at ₱$650{,}000$ acquisition cost, ₱$310{,}000$ assessed value, and ₱$580{,}000$ fair market value.
2. List the motorcycle under personal property at ₱$90{,}000$ acquisition cost.

:::why
Why report acquisition cost alongside fair market value? Comparing purchase price with current tax values helps reviewers spot unexplained gains.
:::

:::check
A clerk bought a city apartment for ₱$1{,}400{,}000$ with a ₱$1{,}600{,}000$ tax-declaration market value, plus an untaxed lot lacking any tax declaration. What fair market value goes on each?
:::answer
The answer is **₱$1{,}600{,}000$ for the apartment and "N/A" for the lot.** Use the latest tax declaration when one exists, and enter "N/A" when none exists.
:::

### Liabilities and Business Links

**Liabilities** are all debts owed, whether secured or unsecured.

A **business interest** is an ownership stake in a private firm. **Shareholdings** are company shares owned. A **financial connection** is a paid link to a business, like paid consulting.

Sort an engineer's items: a ₱$140{,}000$ car loan, ₱$35{,}000$ in credit-card debt, and paid advisory work without owning shares:

1. Report both the ₱$140{,}000$ car loan and ₱$35{,}000$ card balance as liabilities.
2. Report the paid advisory work as a financial connection despite zero shares.

:::pair #unsecured-debts-count
Right: Report all liabilities, including unsecured personal loans and credit balances.
Wrong: Reporting only debts secured by real property and omitting unsecured personal loans.
Every debt lowers net worth, so unsecured balances belong on the SALN.
:::

:::pair #consultancy-without-shares
Right: Disclose paid consulting or advising as a financial connection even with zero shares owned.
Wrong: Omitting a paid consultancy because you own no shares in the private firm.
A financial connection covers paid advising even without an ownership stake.
:::

:::check
An accountant owes ₱$50{,}000$ on a salary loan and earns monthly fees as an outside bookkeeper for a bakery she does not own. Which items must she declare?
:::answer
The answer is **Both the salary loan and the bookkeeping work.** Report the unsecured loan under liabilities and the paid role under financial connections.
:::

### Net Worth Formula

**Net worth** is total assets minus total liabilities:

$$
\text{Net worth} = \text{assets} - \text{liabilities}
$$

Find the net worth of an auditor with ₱$1{,}200{,}000$ in assets and ₱$320{,}000$ in liabilities:

1. Apply the formula: subtract total liabilities from total assets.
2. Compute the difference:

$$
1{,}200{,}000 - 320{,}000 = 880{,}000
$$

The auditor's net worth is ₱$880{,}000$.

:::pair #subtract-debts-for-net-worth
Right: Find net worth by subtracting total liabilities from total assets.
Wrong: Adding total liabilities to total assets when computing net worth.
Debts are amounts owed to others, so subtract them from assets.
:::

:::check
A teacher reports ₱$460{,}000$ in total assets and ₱$190{,}000$ in total liabilities. What is her net worth?
:::answer
The answer is **₱$270{,}000$.** Subtracting ₱$190{,}000$ in liabilities from ₱$460{,}000$ in assets leaves ₱$270{,}000$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Real Property Valuation Sources

A revenue officer bought a house and lot for ₱$2{,}100{,}000$. Her latest tax declaration is unavailable, though the Assessor's Office records show ₱$2{,}400{,}000$. Which fair market value should she report?

**Solution:**

1. Check the main rule: use the latest available tax declaration first.
2. Apply the backup rule: when unavailable, use the local Assessor's Office or zonal value.

Answer: Report ₱$2{,}400{,}000$ from the local Assessor's Office.

**Check:** Enter "N/A" only when no tax declaration exists.

### #2: Loans and Non-Owner Links

A city planner holds ₱$500{,}000$ in bank savings and owes ₱$85{,}000$ on an unsecured loan. He also earns a retainer advising a design firm where he owns zero shares. What must he report?

**Solution:**

1. List the ₱$500{,}000$ savings under assets.
2. List the ₱$85{,}000$ unsecured loan under liabilities.
3. List the design-adviser retainer under financial connections.

Answer: Report the savings, the unsecured loan, and the advisory retainer.

**Check:** Assets, all debts, and financial connections are all required under Section $8$.

### #3: Net Worth From Mixed Entries

A customs examiner reports a house worth ₱$950{,}000$ and bank savings of ₱$210{,}000$. She owes ₱$180{,}000$ on a car loan and ₱$40{,}000$ on a personal loan. Compute her net worth.

**Solution:**

1. Add both asset amounts together:

$$
950{,}000 + 210{,}000 = 1{,}160{,}000
$$

2. Add both liability amounts together:

$$
180{,}000 + 40{,}000 = 220{,}000
$$

:::try
Finish the last step: subtract total liabilities from total assets to find the final net worth.
:::answer
The answer is **₱$940{,}000$.** Subtracting ₱$220{,}000$ from ₱$1{,}160{,}000$ leaves ₱$940{,}000$.

**Check:** Net worth (₱$940{,}000$) plus liabilities (₱$220{,}000$) equals total assets (₱$1{,}160{,}000$).
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Unsecured debts: include unsecured loans and credit balances alongside land-secured debts. {#unsecured-debts-count}
- Paid advising or consulting: report it as a financial connection even with zero shares owned. {#consultancy-without-shares}
- Net worth math: subtract total liabilities from total assets instead of adding them. {#subtract-debts-for-net-worth}
:::
