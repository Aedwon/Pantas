## Core Concept

### The Two Tests for Private Practice

**Private professional practice** means serving private clients in a profession like law, medicine, accounting, or engineering.

Under Section $7(b)(2)$ of RA $6713$, public workers must not practice privately unless two conditions both hold:

1. Legal authority: authorized by the Constitution or law (Rule X, Section $1(c)$ also refers to regulation).
2. Zero conflict: the practice will not **conflict**, or **tend to conflict**, with official functions.

**Tend to conflict** means the private work risks clashing with public duties, even before an actual dispute happens.

Private practice is not banned completely, yet passing one test alone never suffices.

Apply Section $7(b)(2)$ to a state university accounting professor with statutory practice authority hired to audit a current college laptop bidder:

1. Check legal authority: she holds statutory authority to practice accounting privately.
2. Check conflict: auditing a bidder before her college tends to conflict with her official duties.

Result: The professor must refuse the bidder's audit under Section $7(b)(2)$.

:::why
Why bar work that merely tends to conflict? Waiting for an actual clash erodes public trust while an official serves both sides.
:::

:::pair #authority-does-not-cure-conflict
Right: Both valid legal authority and freedom from conflict must hold before private practice is allowed.
Wrong: Thinking statutory or constitutional authority removes the conflict-of-interest test.
Authority allows unrelated private work only, never work that conflicts with official duties.
:::

:::pair #tendency-to-conflict-barred
Right: Section $7(b)(2)$ bars private practice whenever it conflicts or tends to conflict with official functions.
Wrong: Assuming private client work is lawful until an actual clash occurs in an active case.
Even a potential tendency to conflict fails Section $7(b)(2)$'s second condition.
:::

:::check
A town doctor holds valid authority for an evening clinic. While her office inspects city bus safety, an injured rider asks her to prepare an expert medical report for his claim against the city. May she accept?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Despite legal authority, preparing an expert report for a claim against her city's safety inquiry tends to conflict with official duties.
:::

### Job Recommendations and Mandated Referrals

Under Section $7(b)(3)$, workers must not recommend anyone to a private firm with a **regular** or **pending** transaction with their office.

**Pending** means awaiting action or completion. Both regular ongoing dealings and single pending dealings trigger this ban.

Under Rule X, Section $1(d)$, referrals required by **law**, **international commitments**, or **assigned public duties** are excepted.

Apply Rule X when a labor-placement specialist whose job requires matching welders refers one to a shipyard awaiting a labor permit:

1. Check the shipyard's link: the pending permit triggers Section $7(b)(3)$'s general rule.
2. Check the exception: matching welders belongs to her assigned public duties under Rule X.

Result: The referral fits the assigned-duty exception and is allowed.

:::pair #assigned-duty-exception-stands
Right: Referrals required by law, international commitments, or assigned public duties remain lawful despite a pending deal.
Wrong: Thinking a pending office transaction automatically blocks a referral required by assigned public duties.
Rule X expressly keeps mandated public-duty referrals lawful even when the employer has a pending transaction.
:::

:::check
A customs examiner has no placement duty, yet asks a brokerage with weekly port filings to hire his cousin. Does any exception apply?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Weekly filings are regular transactions, and no law, international commitment, or assigned duty required the referral.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Licensed Engineer Reviewing a Future Inspection

A public-works engineer holds statutory authority for weekend consulting. A contractor asks him to review a footbridge design his own district will inspect next month. No dispute exists yet. May he accept?

**Solution:**

1. Check authority: the engineer holds statutory authority for private practice.
2. Check conflict: reviewing a design his district will soon inspect tends to conflict with his inspection duty.

Answer: No, Section $7(b)(2)$ bars the review because it tends to conflict with official functions.

**Check:** Both legal authority and zero actual or potential conflict must hold together.

### #2: Unrelated Practice Without Authority

A bureau chemist lacks constitutional, statutory, or regulatory authority for private practice. On Saturdays, she runs paid soil tests for distant farms with zero matters before her bureau. Does Section $7(b)(2)$ allow it?

**Solution:**

1. Check conflict: testing distant farm soil creates no conflict with her bureau work.
2. Check legal authority: Section $7(b)(2)$ requires legal authority independently, so zero conflict cannot cure missing authority.

Answer: No, private practice without legal authority violates Section $7(b)(2)$.

**Check:** Reject private practice whenever either legal authority or freedom from conflict is missing.

### #3: Mandated Placement Versus Personal Favor

A vocational center's placement officer is assigned to refer graduating machinists to employers. She refers two graduates to a cannery awaiting center accreditation, and also asks the cannery to hire her brother-in-law. Rule on both referrals.

**Solution:**

1. Check the graduates' referral: test whether assigned placement duties trigger Rule X despite pending accreditation.
2. Check the brother-in-law's referral: test whether law, treaty, or assigned duty covers a relative's hiring request.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both referrals under Section $7(b)(3)$ and Rule X.
:::answer
The answer is **Referring the graduates is allowed, while recommending her brother-in-law is prohibited.** Assigned placement duties excuse the graduates' referral despite pending accreditation, whereas no exception covers a relative's referral.

**Check:** Separate referrals mandated by assigned duties from personal recommendations to transacting firms.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Legal authority for practice: check both legal authority and zero conflict before accepting clients. {#authority-does-not-cure-conflict}
- Potential clashes: reject private work that even tends to conflict with official functions. {#tendency-to-conflict-barred}
- Mandated job placement: keep Rule X's exception active for referrals required by assigned public duties. {#assigned-duty-exception-stands}
:::
