## Core Concept

### Resignation and Divestment Deadlines

When a conflict exists on assuming office, Section $9$ of RA $6713$ sets two deadlines in **calendar days**:

1. **Resign** from any conflicting private business position within **$30$ days** from assumption of office.
2. **Divest** conflicting shareholdings or interests within **$60$ days** from assumption of office.

The needed step depends on whether the conflict involves a position, ownership, or both. Partners in a partnership follow the same rules.

Resigning from a board never divests shares alone, and keeping control defeats divestment.

Find both deadlines for an officer entering on March $1$ ($31$ days in March) as a bus company officer owning $35\%$ stock:

1. Resignation ($30$ days from March $1$): start March $2$, giving $30$ days in March, due March $31$.
2. Divestment ($60$ days from March $1$): after $30$ days in March, add $30$ days in April, due April $30$.

:::why
Why allow $60$ days to divest shares versus $30$ days to resign? Stepping down takes a letter, while selling shares fairly takes longer.
:::

:::pair #resign-before-divest
Right: Resign from a conflicting private position within $30$ days and divest conflicting shares within $60$ days.
Wrong: Switching the two deadlines to allow $60$ days for resignation and only $30$ days for divestment.
Keep the shorter $30$-day window for resignation and the longer $60$-day window for share divestment.
:::

:::pair #calendar-days-section-nine
Right: Count Section $9$'s $30$-day resignation and $60$-day divestment limits in ordinary calendar days.
Wrong: Converting the $30$-day and $60$-day Section $9$ deadlines into working days.
Unlike the $10$-working-day SALN copying rule, Section $9$ uses calendar days, not working days.
:::

:::check
A water-board commissioner takes office on June $1$ ($30$ days in June) while serving as corporate treasurer of a pipeline firm, owning zero shares. What is due, and by what date?
:::answer
The answer is **Resign as corporate treasurer by July $1$.** Starting June $2$ leaves $29$ days in June plus $1$ day in July for the $30$-day resignation rule.
:::

### Conflicts Arising After Entry and Exemptions

Under Rule IX, Section $2$(a) of the implementing rules, a mid-service conflict starts both clocks **when the conflict arises**.

Trace an agency lawyer hired in $2022$ who inherits controlling shares in a regulated courier on October $5$ ($31$ days in October):

1. Start the $60$-day divestment clock on October $5$, the date the conflict arose.
2. Count $31 - 5 = 26$ days in October, $30$ in November ($56$ total), plus $4$ in December, giving December $4$.

Section $9$ exempts three groups from divestment: **honorary capacity**, **laborers**, and **casual or temporary workers**.

:::pair #later-conflict-trigger
Right: When a conflict arises after an employee is already serving, start both periods on the date the conflict arose.
Wrong: Waiting until the next annual SALN filing date to start counting the conflict deadlines.
A mid-service conflict starts the $30$-day and $60$-day clocks on the exact day it arises.
:::

:::check
A senior inspector hired in $2021$ becomes a managing partner in a regulated firm on July $1$ ($31$ days in July). When does her $30$-day resignation clock start and end?
:::answer
The answer is **Starts July $1$ and ends July $31$.** Under Rule IX, Section $2$(a), a later conflict starts the $30$-day clock on July $1$, ending $30$ days later on July $31$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Partnership Conflict on Assumption

On April $10$ ($30$ days in April), an official takes office while serving as managing partner and part-owner of a regulated auditing firm. What deadlines apply under Section $9$?

**Solution:**

1. Resignation ($30$ days from April $10$): $30 - 10 = 20$ days in April plus $10$ days in May, due May $10$.
2. Divestment ($60$ days from April $10$): $20$ days in April, $31$ in May ($51$ total), plus $9$ in June:

$$
60 - (20 + 31) = 9 \text{ days in June}
$$

Answer: Resign by May $10$, and divest the partnership stake by June $9$.

**Check:** Section $9$ gives partners $30$ days to resign and $60$ days to divest.

### #2: Excluded Casual Worker Versus Regular Chief

A casual road laborer owns shares in a hardware shop bidding for city supplies. Separately, a regular procurement chief sits on a bidder's board. Who must comply with Section $9$?

**Solution:**

1. Casual laborer: Section $9$ exempts laborers and casual or temporary workers from divestment.
2. Procurement chief: regular officials are covered and must resign from the board within $30$ days.

Answer: Only the regular procurement chief is bound by Section $9$.

**Check:** Honorary workers, laborers, and casual or temporary workers are exempt under Section $9$.

### #3: Inherited Shares During Service

A career regional director entered service years ago. On May $15$ ($31$ days in May), she inherits voting shares enough to elect a director in a regulated mining firm. Find her divestment deadline.

**Solution:**

1. Start date: Rule IX, Section $2$(a) starts the $60$-day clock on May $15$, when the conflict arose.
2. Count remaining days in May plus all of June:

$$
31 - 15 = 16 \text{ days in May}
$$

$$
16 + 30 = 46 \text{ days through June } 30
$$

:::try
Finish the last step: compute the remaining days in July and state the exact divestment deadline.
:::answer
The answer is **July $14$.** Subtracting $46$ from $60$ leaves $14$ days in July, making July $14$ the $60$-day divestment deadline.

**Check:** $16$ days in May plus $30$ in June plus $14$ in July equals $60$ calendar days.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Resignation versus divestment windows: allow $30$ days to resign from a private post and $60$ days to divest shares. {#resign-before-divest}
- Day unit under Section $9$: count calendar days, never working days, for both conflict deadlines. {#calendar-days-section-nine}
- Later conflict start date: start both clocks when the conflict arose, not at annual SALN time. {#later-conflict-trigger}
:::
