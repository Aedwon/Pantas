## Core Concept

### Covered Workers and Agencies

Section $5$ of RA $6713$ sets service duties, and Section $7$ lists prohibited acts. Both protect citizens from poor service and abuse of office.

Under Section $3(b)$, **public officials and employees** include everyone elected or appointed to a government post, **permanent** or **temporary**.

Coverage includes **career service** (merit-based posts with advancement paths), **non-career service** (elected, term, or project posts), and **military and police personnel**.

Under Section $3(a)$, **government** covers national offices, local offices, and **government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs)** plus their **subsidiaries** (companies they control).

Test Sections $3(a)$ and $3(b)$ on a $6$-month contractual clerk at a state power corporation's subsidiary:

1. Check worker status: temporary and non-career workers are covered under Section $3(b)$.
2. Check agency scope: a GOCC subsidiary sits inside Section $3(a)$'s definition of government.

Result: RA $6713$ covers the contractual subsidiary clerk.

:::why
Why cover temporary staff and GOCC subsidiaries too? Both use public funds and serve citizens, so leaving them out would weaken accountability.
:::

:::pair #temporary-workers-covered
Right: RA $6713$ covers permanent and temporary public workers from day one.
Wrong: Treating temporary appointees as excluded until they gain a permanent post.
Section $3(b)$ covers temporary workers alongside permanent, career, and non-career staff.
:::

:::pair #subsidiaries-are-government
Right: GOCCs and their subsidiaries fall inside RA $6713$'s definition of government.
Wrong: Treating GOCC subsidiaries as private companies outside the Code's coverage.
Under Section $3(a)$, a subsidiary controlled by a GOCC counts as government.
:::

:::check
A newly enlisted Air Force sergeant argues that RA $6713$ covers only civilian desk clerks. Is he right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $3(b)$ explicitly includes military and police personnel alongside civilian workers.
:::

### Unpaid Volunteers and SALN Exemptions

Pay never decides coverage under Section $3(b)$. The Code applies whether or not a worker receives compensation, regardless of amount.

**Free voluntary service** means government service given without pay under a written assignment by a fit person. Rules against hiring relatives improperly also apply.

The **SALN** (Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Net Worth) tracks property owned (**assets**), debts (**liabilities**), and assets minus debts (**net worth**).

Under Rule XII of the **implementing rules** (rules carrying out the Act), unpaid volunteers need not file a SALN. That exemption never lifts conduct rules.

Apply Rule XII to an unpaid clinic volunteer doctor offered a resort trip by a drug supplier:

1. Check disclosure rules: Rule XII exempts the unpaid volunteer doctor from filing a SALN.
2. Check conduct rules: Section $3(b)$ and Rule XII keep all duties and gift bans active.

Result: The volunteer doctor must refuse the supplier's gift.

:::pair #saln-exemption-keeps-duties
Right: Unpaid volunteers skip SALN filing under Rule XII, yet stay bound by all duties and gift bans.
Wrong: Assuming a volunteer's SALN filing exemption also lifts the Code's gift restrictions.
Rule XII removes financial disclosure paperwork only, leaving ethical rules intact.
:::

:::check
An unpaid barangay disaster volunteer assigned in writing asks if zero pay lets him take a contractor's cash tip. What applies?
:::answer
The answer is **The gift ban still applies**. Section $3(b)$ covers unpaid workers, and Rule XII waives only SALN filing.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Elected Mayor and Project Engineer

Voters elect a city mayor, while an engineer holds a $1$-year project contract in city hall. Which one does RA $6713$ cover?

**Solution:**

1. Check the mayor: elected local officials belong to non-career service covered by Section $3(b)$.
2. Check the engineer: project-based non-career workers are covered under Section $3(b)$ like permanent staff.

Answer: RA $6713$ covers both the elected mayor and the project engineer.

**Check:** Section $3(b)$ covers elective and appointive workers across career and non-career posts.

### #2: Clerk at a State Rail Subsidiary

A contractual clerk at a state rail subsidiary claims corporate status exempts the subsidiary from RA $6713$. Does RA $6713$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Check agency scope: Section $3(a)$ places GOCC subsidiaries inside the definition of government.
2. Check worker status: Section $3(b)$ covers contractual workers just like permanent staff.

Answer: Yes, RA $6713$ covers the contractual clerk at the state rail subsidiary.

**Check:** Test both agency scope under Section $3(a)$ and worker status under Section $3(b)$.

### #3: Volunteer Librarian and Gift Rules

A retired teacher serves unpaid at a provincial library under a written order. She files no SALN and later takes a book vendor's gift card. Rule on both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check SALN filing: test whether a written unpaid volunteer order qualifies for Rule XII's filing exemption.
2. Check the gift card: test whether Rule XII's filing exemption changes Section $3(b)$ gift rules.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the unfiled SALN and the gift card under Rule XII and Section $3(b)$.
:::answer
The answer is **Skipping the SALN is allowed, while taking the gift card breaks the Code.** Rule XII waives financial disclosures for volunteers, yet keeps all gift bans active.

**Check:** Separate disclosure paperwork under Rule XII from conduct duties under Section $3(b)$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Temporary or non-career posts: include temporary workers from day one instead of waiting for permanent status. {#temporary-workers-covered}
- GOCC subsidiaries: treat subsidiaries controlled by a GOCC as covered government offices. {#subsidiaries-are-government}
- Volunteer SALN exemption: keep duties and gift bans active even when SALN filing is waived. {#saln-exemption-keeps-duties}
:::
