## Core Concept

### Open Inspection During Reasonable Hours

Under Section $5(e)$ of RA $6713$, public documents must be accessible and readily available for public inspection within **reasonable working hours**.

Without a specific lawful limit, citizens face no blanket waiting period, and access extends to the public rather than just staff.

Refusing access simply to avoid embarrassment or bad press violates Section $5(e)$.

Apply Section $5(e)$ when a homeowner visits a town office at $10$:$30$ a.m. on Thursday to read an approved drainage contract:

1. Check timing: Thursday at $10$:$30$ a.m. falls inside reasonable working hours.
2. Check limits: an approved drainage contract triggers no lawful secrecy rule.

Result: The office must make the contract open for public inspection.

:::pair #no-blanket-waiting-period
Right: Public documents must be open for inspection during reasonable working hours unless restricted by law.
Wrong: Imposing a general $30$-day waiting period before the public may inspect any public document.
Section $5(e)$ requires prompt access during reasonable working hours, not a blanket waiting period.
:::

:::check
A city clerk tells a shopkeeper that only city hall staff may read approved market-stall lease schedules. Does that follow Section $5(e)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $5(e)$ opens non-exempt public documents to the general public during reasonable working hours.
:::

### Lawful Grounds to Withhold Records

Under Rule IV, Section $3$ of the implementing rules, specific records carry lawful protection from disclosure:

1. Defense, security, or foreign-affairs secrets, or records endangering a person's life.
2. **Privileged information** (legally protected records like lawyer-client talks) and unwarranted invasions of personal **privacy**.
3. Draft decisions, orders, and similar pre-decisional papers.
4. Investigation records if release would harm enforcement, deny a fair trial, expose a **confidential source**, or reveal secret methods.
5. Early release that would seriously harm financial markets or defeat planned official action.

Unfinished status alone never makes every record secret, and hiding mistakes is never a lawful ground.

Apply Rule IV when a resident requests an active probe folder naming an informant alongside approved clinic repair totals:

1. Check the informant's name: Rule IV protects records that would expose a confidential source.
2. Check the repair totals: public spending totals without a Rule IV ground stay open.

Result: The office withholds the confidential source while leaving public spending totals open.

:::why
Why list exact exceptions in Rule IV? Narrow grounds protect safety, privacy, and active probes without letting officials hide routine records.
:::

:::pair #unfinished-not-automatically-secret
Right: Withholding a record needs a specific lawful ground such as protecting a confidential source.
Wrong: Treating every record on an unfinished matter as automatically secret under Rule IV.
Ongoing files are withheld only when release triggers a specific Rule IV exception.
:::

:::pair #embarrassment-not-lawful-ground
Right: Only a recognized statutory or Rule IV ground justifies refusing public record inspection.
Wrong: Refusing inspection to shield an agency from unfavorable public attention or criticism.
Avoiding embarrassment is never a lawful exception to Section $5(e)$.
:::

:::check
A citizen asks to read an applicant's private medical diagnosis inside a disability-grant file. May the office withhold those medical details?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Rule IV allows withholding details whose release would invade personal privacy without justification.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Hiding Approved Contracts From Bad Press

A provincial road office approves its final paving audit report. The director bars public inspection during office hours because reporters might criticize cost overruns. Is the refusal lawful?

**Solution:**

1. Check timing: visitors requested inspection during regular working hours under Section $5(e)$.
2. Check Rule IV grounds: avoiding criticism or unfavorable public attention is not a lawful limit.

Answer: No, the office must open the approved report for public inspection.

**Check:** Test every refusal against explicit Rule IV grounds rather than image concerns.

### #2: Tipster Identity in an Ongoing Probe

A neighborhood group asks for an agency probe file that names an informant who reported illegal dumping. The group claims Section $5(e)$ opens every agency folder. May the agency withhold the informant's name?

**Solution:**

1. Check the general rule: Section $5(e)$ opens public documents during reasonable working hours.
2. Check Rule IV limits: Rule IV protects investigation records when disclosure would expose a confidential source.

Answer: Yes, the agency may withhold information that would expose the confidential source.

**Check:** Match source protection to the confidential-source exception, not unfinished status alone.

### #3: Draft Order and Market-Sensitive Rule

At $2$:$00$ p.m. on Tuesday, a citizen asks a financial board for an unsigned draft order. She also requests a planned currency rule whose leak would disrupt financial markets. The clerk refuses both under Rule IV. Evaluate both refusals.

**Solution:**

1. Check the draft order: test whether an unsigned pre-decisional draft matches a listed Rule IV ground.
2. Check the currency rule: test whether early release triggers Rule IV's financial-market harm exception.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the draft order and the planned currency rule under Rule IV.
:::answer
The answer is **Both refusals are lawful under Rule IV.** Draft decisions are protected, and early release that would seriously harm financial markets or defeat planned official action may be withheld.

**Check:** Confirm each withheld item matches an explicit Rule IV ground rather than agency preference.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Inspection timing: open public documents during reasonable working hours with no $30$-day delay. {#no-blanket-waiting-period}
- Ongoing files: withhold only when a specific Rule IV ground applies, not because work is unfinished. {#unfinished-not-automatically-secret}
- Agency image: never deny inspection just to avoid unfavorable public attention. {#embarrassment-not-lawful-ground}
:::
