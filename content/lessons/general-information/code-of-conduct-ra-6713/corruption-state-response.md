## Core Concept

### Ombudsman, Sandiganbayan, and Impeachment

The State counters corruption through complaint bodies, courts, and impeachment:

- **Office of the Ombudsman**: under **RA $6770$, Section $13$**, acts on complaints against government workers. A complaint starts review without proving guilt.
- **Sandiganbayan**: the anti-graft court retained by **Article XI, Section $4$** to try cases within its legal authority.
- **Impeachment**: under **Article XI, Sections $2$ and $3$**, removes only specified high officials. These include the President, Vice President, Supreme Court justices, Constitutional Commissioners, and the Ombudsman.

Section $2$ lists **bribery** and **graft and corruption** as grounds, without needing plunder. Under Section $3$, the **House** brings charges and the **Senate** tries them. Ordinary workers follow statutory removal laws.

Trace two cases to separate them:

1. City engineer rigging bids: complaints go to the Ombudsman, and trials go to the Sandiganbayan.
2. Constitutional Commissioner: the House brings charges, and the Senate tries the case.

Result: Distinct bodies handle complaints, trials, and impeachment.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: "Complaint" points to "Ombudsman: acts on complaints"; separate box "Sandiganbayan: tries cases within its legal authority". -->

:::pair #impeachment-high-officials-only
Right: Impeachment applies only to specified high officials under Article XI, Section $2$.
Wrong: Assuming every public employee must be removed through impeachment for corruption charges.
Other workers face ordinary administrative or court removal set by law.
:::

:::check
A city treasurer is accused of diverting market fees, while a Supreme Court Justice faces a graft allegation. Which official is removed through impeachment rather than ordinary law?
:::answer
The answer is **The Supreme Court Justice**. Article XI, Section $2$ limits impeachment to specified high officials, while other public workers follow removal procedures set by law.
:::

### Sworn SALN Records

Under **Article XI, Section $17$**, public workers file a sworn **SALN** (Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth).

The SALN lists **assets** (owned) and **liabilities** (owed), opening wealth and debts to checks. Filing never proves assets lawful, and complaints never prove guilt alone.

Apply SALN checks when auditors inspect $4$ years of filings:

1. Entries: auditors compare reported wealth and debts against known income.
2. Limits: unexplained gaps prompt inquiry without proving guilt automatically.

Result: The SALN opens wealth and debts to checks without replacing inquiry.

:::pair #saln-not-proof-of-lawful-wealth
Right: A filed SALN opens wealth and debts to checks rather than proving assets lawful.
Wrong: Treating a filed SALN as proving every reported asset is lawful so investigation is unnecessary.
Sworn statements assist checks and must be verified against records.
:::

:::check
A clerk omits personal bank loans from her SALN, claiming checks cover owned property only. Is she right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A SALN requires reporting both assets and liabilities so reviewers can verify net worth and debts.
:::

### Gift Restrictions and Section $3(c)$

**RA $6713$, Section $7(d)$** bars soliciting or accepting gifts or benefits tied to official duties or regulated work.

**RA $6713$, Section $3(c)$** excludes an **unsolicited** gift of **insignificant value** when **not given for an expected or past favor**. All three conditions must hold.

Test Section $3(c)$ when a retiree leaves an unsolicited paper bookmark after a routine check:

1. Request and value: unasked-for and insignificant.
2. Favor link: the check finished with no favor sought.

Result: The bookmark meets all three conditions.

:::why
Why does a small gift fail Section $3(c)$ when tied to a favor? Even a cheap item turns public service into a paid exchange once it rewards special treatment.
:::

:::pair #small-price-never-excuses-favor
Right: An excluded gift under Section $3(c)$ must be unsolicited, insignificant, and free of any favor.
Wrong: Treating a small gift as allowed under Section $3(c)$ even when sent to move an application ahead of others.
Low price alone never excuses a favor-linked gift.
:::

:::check
A contractor sends an unsolicited ₱$30{,}000$ watch to a city engineer while awaiting road-bill approval. Does Section $3(c)$ exclude it?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A ₱$30{,}000$ watch from a pending contractor fails both the insignificant-value rule and the no-favor condition.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Impeachment Ground and Chambers

A speaker says a Constitutional Commissioner cannot be impeached for graft without bribery, and claims the Senate files charges. Check both claims.

**Solution:**

1. Ground: Section $2$ lists bribery and graft and corruption separately for specified officials.
2. Chambers: under Section $3$, the House brings charges and the Senate tries them.

Answer: Both claims are wrong because graft is a listed ground and the House files charges.

**Check:** Separate Section $2$'s listed grounds from Section $3$'s House-charge, Senate-trial roles.

### #2: Checking Two Small Tokens

A clerk finds a cheap pen inside a tax appeal asking to waive penalties. Another gets an unsolicited desk calendar from a neighbor with no office business. Which meets Section $3(c)$?

**Solution:**

1. Pen: despite low price, it comes with a waiver request, failing the no-favor test.
2. Calendar: the neighbor's calendar is unsolicited, insignificant, and free of any favor.

Answer: Only the neighbor's calendar meets Section $3(c)$.

**Check:** Require all three Section $3(c)$ conditions together before treating any gift as excluded.

### #3: SALN Defense and Court Authority

Citizens send records to the Ombudsman showing a director owns cars missing from his SALN. He claims his SALN proves his assets lawful and tells the Sandiganbayan to dismiss the complaint. Rule on both.

**Solution:**

1. SALN claim: check whether filing a SALN proves assets lawful or blocks an inquiry.
2. Roles: separate the Ombudsman's complaint role from the Sandiganbayan's trial role.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the SALN defense and the demand to bypass the Ombudsman under RA $6770$ and Article XI.
:::answer
The answer is **Both arguments fail.** A SALN never proves assets lawful or blocks inquiry. Under RA $6770$, the Ombudsman acts on complaints before any Sandiganbayan trial.

**Check:** Match complaints to the Ombudsman, trials within authority to the Sandiganbayan, and wealth checks to the SALN.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Impeachment scope: limit impeachment to specified high officials while others follow ordinary laws. {#impeachment-high-officials-only}
- Filed SALN records: verify assets and debts against records instead of treating filing as proof. {#saln-not-proof-of-lawful-wealth}
- Small favor gifts: reject any gift sent to speed an application regardless of price. {#small-price-never-excuses-favor}
:::
