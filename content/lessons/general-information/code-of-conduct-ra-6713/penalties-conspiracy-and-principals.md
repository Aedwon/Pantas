## Core Concept

### Section $11(c)$ Coverage and Joint Trials

RA $6713$ governs public workers, yet **Section $11(c)$** reaches private individuals who conspire with them.

Private **co-principals**, **accomplices**, or **accessories** in a conspiracy face the same **penal liabilities**. **Penal liabilities** means criminal responsibility and penalties. For Sections $7$, $8$, or $9$, that means up to $5$ years imprisonment, ₱$5{,}000$ fine, or both.

Under Section $11(c)$, private conspirators are tried **jointly** with the public workers.

Check a private broker and customs officer who conspire in a Section $7(d)$ payoff:

1. Private status: holding no public office never shields the broker from criminal penalties.
2. Trial setup: both face trial together in one joint proceeding.

Result: If proved, the broker faces the same $5$-year prison ceiling in a joint trial.

:::why
Why impose equal penalties and joint trials on private conspirators? Otherwise, officials could hide behind private partners to commit Code crimes without shared accountability.
:::

:::pair #private-conspirator-faces-same-ceiling
Right: Private conspirators under Section $11(c)$ face the same imprisonment ceiling as public officers.
Wrong: Excluding private conspirators from imprisonment because only public officials hold office.
Section $11(c)$ expressly extends the Code's penal liabilities to private people who conspire with public workers.
:::

:::pair #joint-trial-not-separate
Right: Private conspirators under Section $11(c)$ are tried jointly with the public officials or employees.
Wrong: Requiring private participants to be tried separately from public officers in every case.
Section $11(c)$ directs a joint trial rather than splitting public and private co-conspirators apart.
:::

:::check
A private supplier conspires as a co-principal with a city buyer under Section $7(a)$. The supplier claims private citizens face at most $1$ year and a separate trial. Is that right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Section $11(c)$, a private co-principal faces the same $5$-year Section $7$ ceiling in a joint trial.
:::

### Conspiracy and Co-Principal Roles

Under **Revised Penal Code Article $8$**, **conspiracy** means $2$ or more people agree and decide to commit a crime. Knowing about a plan alone is never conspiracy.

**Articles $10$ and $17$** give supplementary role definitions. These examples illustrate those statutory roles rather than independent findings of RA $6713$ conspiracy.

A **co-principal** fits one of three Article $17$ paths:

1. Direct participation: directly committing the crime with another offender.
2. Inducement: directly forcing or inducing someone else to commit it.
3. Indispensable cooperation: giving help without which the crime could not happen.

Compare two outsiders around a confidential-records sale:

1. A courier hears two clerks discuss the sale and walks away: mere knowledge, no conspiracy.
2. A consultant agrees to the plan and hands buyers stolen files directly: agreement plus direct participation.

Result: Only the consultant fits conspiracy and a co-principal role.

:::pair #agreement-plus-essential-help
Right: Agreeing, deciding to act, and supplying indispensable help can make a private worker a co-principal.
Wrong: Treating agreement plus indispensable help as mere passive knowledge of a plan.
Knowledge lacks agreement and aid, whereas deciding to act with essential help shows co-principal participation.
:::

:::check
A messenger sees an officer copying secret appraisal sheets for a buyer. The messenger never agrees, never helps, and walks out. Does that show conspiracy under Section $11(c)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Mere knowledge without agreeing and deciding to commit the crime never shows conspiracy.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Direct Sale by a Private Consultant

An agency clerk and a private consultant agree and decide to sell restricted land-title records. Both act on the plan, and the consultant hands buyers the files for cash. Assess the consultant's role and trial.

**Solution:**

1. Conspiracy: agreement plus decision by $2$ people meets Article $8$.
2. Role: handing over records for cash is direct co-principal participation under Article $17$.
3. Section $11(c)$: if proved, the consultant faces up to $5$ years prison and joint trial.

Answer: The consultant is a co-principal, faces the same Section $7$ liabilities, and is tried jointly.

**Check:** Verify agreement, decision, and one Article $17$ principal act before applying Section $11(c)$.

### #2: Overheard Plan Without Joining

A print-shop owner hears two city engineers planning to leak confidential road-bid estimates. The owner disagrees, refuses to print anything, and takes no part. Does Section $11(c)$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Agreement: the owner never agreed or decided to commit the violation.
2. Participation: refusing to print means no principal, accomplice, or accessory act occurred.

Answer: No, passive knowledge without agreement creates no conspiracy liability under Section $11(c)$.

**Check:** Separate passive awareness from an actual agreement and decision to commit the crime.

### #3: Master Key Access to Sealed Bids

A private software vendor and a procurement chief agree and decide to leak sealed bids under Section $7(c)$. Only the vendor holds the database master key. The vendor extracts the bids so the chief can share them. Check role, liability, and trial.

**Solution:**

1. Check conspiracy: confirm whether agreeing and deciding to leak the bids meets Article $8$.
2. Check help: test whether using the sole master key is indispensable cooperation under Article $17$.
3. Check Section $11(c)$: apply the Section $7$ penalty ceiling and trial rule once conspiracy is proved.

:::try
Finish the last step: state the vendor's role, the Section $7$ prison ceiling if proved, and the trial setup.
:::answer
The answer is **Co-principal, up to $5$ years prison, and tried jointly**. Agreeing, deciding, and giving essential help fit Article $17$. Section $11(c)$ imposes equal Section $7$ liabilities in a joint trial.

**Check:** Confirm indispensable aid under Article $17$ brings the same $5$-year ceiling and joint trial.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Private conspirator liability: apply the same Section $7$ penal ceilings to private conspirators as to public officers. {#private-conspirator-faces-same-ceiling}
- Trial forum: try private co-conspirators jointly with the public workers under Section $11(c)$. {#joint-trial-not-separate}
- Essential aid vs. knowledge: treat agreement, decision, and indispensable help as co-principal participation, not mere knowledge. {#agreement-plus-essential-help}
:::
