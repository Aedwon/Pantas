## Core Concept

### Protected Work Information

**Confidential or classified information** is protected work information not made open to the public.

Under Section $7(c)$ of RA $6713$, workers must not use or disclose protected work information for three banned purposes:

1. To further their own private interests.
2. To give **undue advantage** (an unfair edge) to anyone.
3. To prejudice the public interest.

Zero payment never excuses a leak. Sharing protected work files with an ordinary citizen for an unfair edge still breaks Section $7(c)$.

Apply Section $7(c)$ when a bidding clerk emails sealed competitor prices to his cousin's firm for free before bids open:

1. Check file status: sealed competitor prices learned at work are confidential.
2. Check purpose and pay: giving his cousin an unfair edge breaks Section $7(c)$ even without cash.

Result: Free disclosure of the sealed bids violates Section $7(c)$.

:::pair #unpaid-leak-still-barred
Right: Section $7(c)$ bars leaking confidential work files for undue advantage, even without payment.
Wrong: Treating confidential-file leaks as allowed whenever the employee takes no cash in return.
The violation turns on private interest, undue advantage, or public harm, never on cash payment.
:::

:::check
A tax clerk reads sealed audit files at work and tells a neighbor, for free, which indebted owner plans to sell land cheap. Does Section $7(c)$ apply?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Sharing confidential work records to give a neighbor an undue advantage breaks Section $7(c)$, even without payment.
:::

### Gifts and Favors Tied to Office

Under Section $7(d)$ of RA $6713$, workers must not **solicit** (ask for) or **accept** any gift, favor, entertainment, loan, or valuable benefit:

1. In the course of official duties.
2. Concerning any operation regulated by their office.
3. Concerning any transaction their office's functions can affect.

Section $3(c)$ excludes an **unsolicited nominal gift** only if **not given in anticipation of, or in exchange for, a favor**.

Low price alone never excuses a favor-linked gift. Late delivery after the decision, or delivery outside office hours, never breaks that link either.

Apply Section $7(d)$ when a warehouse owner hands an inspector a small fruit basket while asking him to overlook a blocked exit:

1. Check office link: the inspector's safety check regulates the warehouse.
2. Check favor link: small value never shields a gift offered for overlooking a violation.

Result: The inspector must refuse the fruit basket under Section $7(d)$.

:::why
Why bar even small gifts tied to official favors? Small perks normalize trading public duty for private gifts and open the door to larger bribes.
:::

:::pair #low-value-not-favor-shield
Right: Even a low-value gift must be refused when offered in anticipation of or exchange for an official favor.
Wrong: Accepting a favor-linked gift simply because its cash value looks small or insignificant.
Under Sections $3(c)$ and $7(d)$, nominal value never excuses a favor-linked gift.
:::

:::pair #timing-never-cures-favor-gift
Right: A gift tied to an official favor stays barred even when delivered after the decision or outside office hours.
Wrong: Thinking a favor-linked gift becomes lawful once delivered after the official action finishes.
Later delivery never breaks the prohibited link between the gift and the official favor.
:::

:::check
A builder invites a clerk to Saturday mall dinner to thank her for pushing his permit ahead of earlier applicants. May she accept?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Weekend timing and post-favor dinner never cure a gift tied to official action.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Tipping Off an Importer Without Pay

A port analyst sees raid logs showing customs agents will inspect a warehouse tomorrow. Without pay, she texts the manager so he can move goods tonight. Does Section $7(c)$ bar her text?

**Solution:**

1. Check file status: raid logs learned at work are protected information.
2. Check effect: tipping off the manager harms enforcement and gives an unfair edge without pay.

Answer: Yes, texting the classified raid plan violates Section $7(c)$.

**Check:** Confirm the information came from work and served private gain, undue advantage, or public harm.

### #2: Inexpensive Gift Delivered After Approval

A quarry owner promises a clerk pastries if she speeds up a permit. Two days after she signs, he leaves the pastries at her house. May she keep them?

**Solution:**

1. Check favor link: the pastries were promised for speeding up a pending permit.
2. Check price and timing: low value and late delivery never meet Section $3(c)$'s no-favor rule.

Answer: No, she must refuse the pastries because they tie to an official favor.

**Check:** Reject favor-linked gifts regardless of low price, late delivery, or home drop-off.

### #3: Solicited Lunch and Leaked Competitor Bid

An officer asks a bidder with a pending contract for free lunch vouchers. Separately, she gives a rival's confidential cost sheet to a friend's shop for free so he can underbid. Rule on both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check the vouchers: test whether asking a pending bidder for lunch vouchers triggers Section $7(d)$.
2. Check the cost sheet: test if giving confidential cost figures for free breaks Section $7(c)$.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the lunch vouchers and the shared cost sheet under Sections $7(c)$ and $7(d)$.
:::answer
The answer is **Both acts violate Section $7$.** Soliciting lunch vouchers breaks Section $7(d)$, and leaking confidential cost sheets for free breaks Section $7(c)$ by giving an unfair edge.

**Check:** Match unpaid confidential leaks to Section $7(c)$ and solicited or favor-linked gifts to Section $7(d)$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Unpaid leaks: bar sharing confidential work files for private gain even with zero cash paid. {#unpaid-leak-still-barred}
- Inexpensive favor gifts: refuse any gift offered for an official favor regardless of low price. {#low-value-not-favor-shield}
- Delayed or off-site gifts: reject favor-linked gifts delivered after the action or outside office hours. {#timing-never-cures-favor-gift}
:::
