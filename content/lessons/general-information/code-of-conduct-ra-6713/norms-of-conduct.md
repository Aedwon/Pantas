## Core Concept


Republic Act No. $6713$ is the **Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees**. It was approved on February $20$, $1989$ and took effect on March $25$, $1989$.

Section $4$ gives eight norms of conduct. These are standards for public officials and employees while they carry out official duties.

### The Eight Norms of Conduct

1. **Commitment to public interest:** Put the public good before personal gain. Use government money, property, power, and time carefully, honestly, and without waste.
2. **Professionalism:** Do public work with skill, care, and dedication. Officials should also avoid making public service look like a source of special favors.
3. **Justness and sincerity:** Be fair and truthful. Do not unfairly discriminate, respect the rights of others, and do not use office to give improper favors to relatives.
4. **Political neutrality:** Give fair service no matter which political party a person supports or prefers.
5. **Responsiveness to the public:** Give prompt, polite, and enough service. Explain policies and procedures clearly, keep information open when the law and public interest allow it, hold public consultations or hearings when appropriate, accept suggestions, simplify procedures, and avoid red tape.
6. **Nationalism and patriotism:** Be loyal to the Republic and the Filipino people. Support locally produced goods, resources, and technology, build pride in the country, and help defend Philippine sovereignty from foreign intrusion.
7. **Commitment to democracy:** Follow democratic values and uphold the Constitution. **Public accountability** means officials must answer to the people for how they use public power and resources. Loyalty to the country must come before loyalty to a person or political party.
8. **Simple living:** Officials, employees, and their families should live modestly in a way that fits their position and income. They should not make extravagant displays of wealth.

The norms do not all describe the same kind of conduct. On an exam, focus on the action in the situation and match it to the norm that most directly covers that action.

### Incentives and Rewards for Ethical Service

Section $6$ creates an annual system of incentives and rewards for outstanding public officials and employees. A Committee on Awards reviews performance and gives recognition based on the standards in the law.

Possible rewards include bonuses, citations, scholarships, paid vacations, directorships in government-owned or controlled corporations, and automatic promotion when the legal conditions for promotion are met. The system is meant to encourage high ethical standards in public service.

## Examples

### #1: Counting the Norms

How many norms of conduct are listed in Section $4$ of RA $6713$?

**Solution:**

1. Section $4$ lists the norms from Commitment to public interest through Simple living.
2. There are exactly $8$ norms.
3. The answer is $8$.

### #2: Public Interest Over Personal Interest

A government officer can choose a supplier owned by a close friend, but another supplier offers the same needed quality at a much lower cost to the government. Which norm most directly requires the officer to put the government's interest first?

**Solution:**

1. The main issue is choosing the public good over a personal relationship.
2. Commitment to public interest requires public resources and powers to be used honestly and without waste.
3. The best answer is **Commitment to public interest**.

### #3: Professional Service Without Patronage

A public employee does careful, skilled work but tells applicants that they need a personal connection to receive help faster. Which norm is most directly violated?

**Solution:**

1. The employee is making public service look like a source of special favors.
2. Professionalism requires skill and dedication and discourages the idea that officials give improper patronage.
3. The best answer is **Professionalism**.

### #4: Rights Versus an Improper Order

A supervisor tells an employee to deny a lawful service to a poor applicant because the applicant criticized the office. The employee knows the applicant has the same right to the service as everyone else. Which norm most directly requires fair treatment?

**Solution:**

1. The key facts are unfair discrimination and the applicant's rights.
2. Justness and sincerity requires fair treatment and respect for the rights of others.
3. The best answer is **Justness and sincerity**.

### #5: Political Neutrality

A permit officer gives the same service to supporters of the administration and to supporters of the opposition. Which norm is shown?

**Solution:**

1. The service does not depend on political party or preference.
2. That is the rule of political neutrality.
3. The answer is **Political neutrality**.

### #6: Clear Information and Public Consultation

An agency plans a policy that will strongly affect local communities. It explains the proposed rules in clear language, opens information to the public, and holds a public consultation when appropriate. Which norm is most directly shown?

**Solution:**

1. The agency is giving clear information and allowing the public to take part.
2. Responsiveness to the public includes clear communication, openness, and consultation when appropriate.
3. The answer is **Responsiveness to the public**.

### #7: Local Resources and Philippine Sovereignty

An agency can use suitable locally produced technology for a project and chooses it while meeting the project's needs. The same agency also refuses a foreign demand that would weaken Philippine control over an official matter. Which norm best connects these actions?

**Solution:**

1. The actions support local resources and defend the country's sovereignty.
2. Those duties belong to nationalism and patriotism.
3. The answer is **Nationalism and patriotism**.

### #8: Accountability Above Party Loyalty

An official is asked by party leaders to hide a lawful public record that shows how government funds were used. The official keeps the record open as required and puts duty to the country above party loyalty. Which norm is most directly shown?

**Solution:**

1. The key ideas are public accountability and loyalty to country over party.
2. Both are part of commitment to democracy.
3. The answer is **Commitment to democracy**.

### #9: Simple Living

A public officer earns a modest official income but repeatedly displays unexplained luxury as part of an extravagant public lifestyle. Which norm directly addresses this conduct?

**Solution:**

1. The issue is an extravagant display of wealth.
2. Simple living calls for a modest life that fits the official's position and income.
3. The answer is **Simple living**.

### #10: Reward for Outstanding Ethical Service

A public employee has years of strong and consistent ethical service and is considered for an annual award under RA $6713$. Which part of the law provides this reward system?

**Solution:**

1. Section $6$ creates the annual incentives and rewards system.
2. It recognizes outstanding public officials and employees based on the law's standards.
3. The answer is **Section $6$, System of Incentives and Rewards**.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: "At All Times" Is Too Broad for the Eight Norms

> Do not say that Section $4$ makes all eight norms a blanket rule for every moment of private life.

Section $4$ frames the eight norms as standards of personal conduct in carrying out official duties. Some individual norms also use broader wording, but the Section $4$ list itself has this official-duty setting.

### #2: Political Neutrality Is About Fair Service

> Do not confuse political neutrality with having no personal political view.

For this norm, the tested action is fair public service regardless of party affiliation or preference.

### #3: Responsiveness Is More Than Being Polite

> Clear communication alone is not the whole norm.

Responsiveness also covers prompt and adequate service, openness of information when allowed, consultation when appropriate, suggestions, simpler procedures, and avoiding red tape.

### #4: Nationalism Is More Than a Loyalty Statement

> Look for local goods, resources, technology, national pride, or sovereignty.

A situation about these actions can point to nationalism and patriotism even if the word "loyalty" never appears.
