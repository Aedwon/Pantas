## Core Concept

Republic Act No. 6713, also known as the Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees, was approved on February 20, 1989 and took effect on March 25, 1989.

Section 4 of RA 6713 sets forth eight specific norms of conduct that every public official and employee must observe at all times.

### The Eight Norms of Conduct

1. **Commitment to public interest:** Public officials and employees shall always uphold the public interest over and above personal interest. All government resources and powers of their offices must be used efficiently, effectively, honestly, and economically, particularly to avoid wastage of public funds and revenues.
2. **Professionalism:** Public officials and employees shall perform and discharge their duties with the highest degree of excellence, professionalism, intelligence, and skill. They shall enter public service with utmost devotion and dedication to duty.
3. **Justness and sincerity:** Public officials and employees shall remain true to the people at all times. They must act with justness and sincerity, without discrimination, and respect the rights of others.
4. **Political neutrality:** Public officials and employees shall provide service to everyone without unfair discrimination, regardless of party affiliation or preference.
5. **Responsiveness to the public:** Public officials and employees shall extend prompt, courteous, and adequate service to the public, and communicate clearly.
6. **Nationalism and patriotism:** Public officials and employees shall at all times be loyal to the Republic and to the Filipino people.
7. **Commitment to democracy:** Public officials and employees shall commit themselves to the democratic way of life and values, maintain the principle of public accountability, uphold the Constitution, and put loyalty to country above loyalty to persons or party.
8. **Simple living:** Public officials and employees and their families shall lead modest lives appropriate to their position and income. They shall not indulge in extravagant or ostentatious display of wealth in any form.

## Examples

### #1: Total Count of Statutory Norms of Conduct

A civil service review question asks how many statutory norms of conduct are defined in Section 4 of RA 6713. What is the total count?

**Solution:**
Recall the official list of norms in Section 4 of RA 6713.

1. Section 4 enumerates the norms of conduct in statutory order.
2. The list contains: Commitment to public interest, Professionalism, Justness and sincerity, Political neutrality, Responsiveness to the public, Nationalism and patriotism, Commitment to democracy, and Simple living.
3. Count the listed norms: exactly $8$ norms.

### #2: Identifying Political Neutrality

A government employee processes applications for public permits without bias, giving equal service to supporters of all political parties. Which statutory norm of conduct is the employee demonstrating?

**Solution:**
Match the behavior to the appropriate norm under Section 4.

1. Providing service to everyone without unfair discrimination regardless of party affiliation is the definition of political neutrality.
2. Political neutrality is the fourth norm of conduct under Section 4.
3. Therefore, the employee is demonstrating political neutrality.

### #3: Identifying Simple Living

A public officer avoids ostentatious displays of wealth and leads a modest lifestyle appropriate to their official income. Which norm of conduct requires this lifestyle?

**Solution:**
Identify the norm governing personal lifestyle and display of wealth.

1. The eighth norm under Section 4 requires public officials to lead modest lives appropriate to their position and income.
2. It prohibits indulging in extravagant or ostentatious displays of wealth.
3. This norm is titled Simple living.
