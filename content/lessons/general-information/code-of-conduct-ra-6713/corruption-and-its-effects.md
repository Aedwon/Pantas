## Core Concept

### What corruption is

**Corruption** means misusing public office or public trust for private gain. Article XI, Section $1$ of the Constitution says public office is a public trust.

The Norms of Conduct lesson explains public interest and simple living. Corruption breaks these standards. An honest mistake alone does not prove corruption.

### Forms

**Bribery** involves gifts or benefits tied to public office. Under Revised Penal Code Article $210$, direct bribery includes accepting a gift to skip an official duty. Article $211$ covers accepting gifts offered because of one's office, even without a specific bargain.

**Graft and corrupt practices** include the acts listed in RA $3019$, Section $3$. Important clauses cover:

- Section $3(b)$: requesting or receiving a benefit tied to a government transaction the officer must officially handle.
- Section $3(e)$: causing undue injury or giving unwarranted private benefits through manifest partiality, evident bad faith, or gross inexcusable negligence. These mean clear favoritism, clearly dishonest intent, or extreme carelessness with no excuse.
- Section $3(h)$: a financial interest in a transaction the officer officially handles, or an interest forbidden by law.
- Section $3(k)$: giving valuable confidential office information to unauthorized people, or releasing it early.

Section $3(g)$ also covers clearly and greatly harmful government contracts, even without private profit.

**Plunder** involves a public officer gathering ill-gotten wealth through a combination or series of the criminal acts covered by RA $7080$. This means unlawfully gained wealth. Section $2$, amended by RA $7659$, Section $12$, sets the total threshold at at least ₱$50$ million.

**Red tape** means ineffective rules or procedures that cause poor or slow service. A **fixer** uses access to government workers to speed transactions for money or another benefit. RA $11032$, Section $4$, supplies these definitions.

Section $9$ generally limits processing to $3$ working days for simple transactions, $7$ for complex ones, and $20$ for highly technical ones. Complete requirements and fees are needed. Lawful extensions, disaster-related suspensions, and special rules can apply.

### Effects

**Social effects** concern people's lives and fair access to services. Aid given to friends can leave needy families without help. Poor households may have no other service, and unfair treatment reduces trust.

**Economic effects** concern money, work, and resources. Inflated prices waste funds. Poor work needs repairs, while bribes and red tape raise costs and discourage honest businesses and investors.

**Political effects** concern public power and accountability, which means answering for one's actions. Officials may serve payers instead of voters. This weakens public bodies and trust in government and elections.

Vote buying and vote selling are election offenses under Omnibus Election Code Section $261(a)$. These involve benefits intended to influence voting.

### How the State responds

The **Ombudsman** acts on complaints against government officers and employees under RA $6770$, Section $13$. The **Sandiganbayan** is the anti-graft court retained by Article XI, Section $4$. It tries cases within its legal authority.

Article XI, Section $2$ names bribery and graft and corruption as impeachment grounds for specified high officials. Other public workers follow removal procedures under law.

A **SALN**, or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, discloses wealth and debts. Article XI, Section $17$ requires sworn declarations. The Statements of Assets and Divestment lesson explains these checks.

The Duties and Prohibited Acts lesson explains RA $6713$ gift rules. Section $3(c)$ excludes unsolicited gifts of insignificant value that are not tied to a favor. Section $7(d)$ prohibits seeking or accepting benefits connected with official duties or affected transactions, with stated exceptions.

### Weighing the effects

“Evaluate” means judge a claim using reasons. Separate acts from results. One act can cause all three kinds of harm.

## Examples

### #1: Mistake or Misuse?

A clerk corrects a typo. Another demands money to move an application ahead. Which shows corruption?

**Solution:**
The demand uses office for private gain. The typo alone proves no corruption.

### #2: Naming Bribery

An inspector accepts money to skip a required inspection. What form does this show?

**Solution:**
Direct bribery under Article $210$: payment to skip a duty.

### #3: Contract Gift

An officer receives a supplier's gift for handling a government contract. Which RA $3019$ clause applies?

**Solution:**
Section $3(b)$ covers this benefit tied to an official transaction.

### #4: Plunder Threshold

An officer gathers exactly ₱$50$ million through a series of criminal acts covered by RA $7080$. Is the amount below the plunder threshold?

**Solution:**
No. The threshold includes exactly ₱$50$ million. The required acts must also be proved.

### #5: Social Harm

Aid goes to an official's friends while eligible families go without food. What main effect is shown?

**Solution:**
Social harm: unfair access to help.

### #6: Economic Harm

Inflated construction bills leave funds for fewer classrooms. Explain the economic effect.

**Solution:**
1. Excess payments use up the building budget.
2. The remaining funds buy fewer classrooms.

### #7: Political Harm

An official follows a donor's demands instead of residents' needs. What main effect is shown?

**Solution:**
Political accountability weakens. Payers influence public decisions.

### #8: Mixed Effects

Classify these results: families lose aid, repairs waste funds, and voters lose faith in government.

**Solution:**
1. Lost aid shows social harm.
2. Wasted repair funds show economic harm.
3. Lost faith in government shows political harm.

### #9: Handling a Complaint

A citizen wants to report an officer's demand for a payoff. Which office can act on this complaint?

**Solution:**
The Ombudsman. A complaint alone does not prove guilt.

### #10: Preventing Payoffs

An office posts its requirements, fees, and processing times. How can this reduce corruption?

**Solution:**
People can check the process and spot hidden charges or invented delays.

### #11: Evaluating a Claim

A speaker says corruption harms only the government budget. Is this claim sound?

**Solution:**
1. Lost funds cause economic harm.
2. Denied services also harm families, while unfair decisions weaken accountability. The claim leaves out social and political harm.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Every Gift Is a Bribe

> Check the gift's purpose and the legal conditions.

Small gifts must still meet the exception, including no link to a favor.

### #2: Only One Effect

> Choose by the main harm named.

Lost classrooms affect resources and education. Use the question's focus.

### #3: Every Delay Proves Corruption

> Slow service alone does not prove private gain.

Red tape concerns bad procedures. Demanding a payoff adds corruption. Lawful extensions are different.

### #4: Only Cash Counts

> Benefits and misuse can take other forms.

Favoritism and information leaks can violate RA $3019$. Check its conditions.

### #5: Nobody Loses

> Ask who loses the service or fair treatment.

Payoffs can deny others their proper place or needed help.
