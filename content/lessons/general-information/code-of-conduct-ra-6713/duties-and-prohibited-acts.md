## Core Concept

Section 5 and Section 7 of RA 6713 establish statutory duties, response deadlines, prohibited acts, and gift rules for public officials and employees.

### Duties and Response Deadlines

- **Act on letters and requests:** Respond within 15 working days from receipt of any request or letter sent by the public. Failure to act within 15 working days is a ground for administrative action.
- **Acknowledge written requests:** Written requests must be acknowledged within 15 working days.
- **Limit signatures:** Written actions must contain no more than three initials or signatures.
- **Annual performance report:** Public officers must submit an annual performance report to their superiors within 45 working days from the end of the year.
- **Public access:** Public documents must be accessible and readily available for inspection during office hours, except those officially classified as confidential.

### Prohibited Acts and Transactions

Section 7 of RA 6713 prohibits public officials and employees from engaging in specific transactions:

- **Financial or material interest:** Having a direct or indirect financial interest in any transaction requiring official approval from their office.
- **Outside employment:** Owning, controlling, managing, or accepting employment in a private enterprise regulated or supervised by their office, unless authorized by law.
- **Private practice:** Engaging in private practice that conflicts with official functions. Private practice is prohibited for 1 year after separation from service regarding any matter before their former office.
- **Recommending persons:** Recommending any person to a position in a private enterprise that has regular or pending transactions with their office.
- **Misuse of information:** Disclosing or misusing confidential or classified information for private interest or undue advantage.
- **Soliciting or accepting gifts:** Soliciting or accepting gifts, favors, loans, or anything of monetary value in connection with official duties.

### Exceptions to the Gift Rule

Recognized exceptions to the gift rule include:
- Unsolicited gifts of nominal or insignificant value not given in anticipation of a favor.
- Gifts from family members on special occasions with no pecuniary expectation.
- Nominal donations from persons with no pending transaction.
- Humanitarian or altruistic private donations.
- Government-to-government donations.
- Foreign government souvenirs of nominal value.
- Scholarships, fellowships, and medical treatment.
- Travel grants entirely outside the country where appropriate.

## Examples

### #1: Deadline to Act on Public Requests

A citizen submits a written request for official information to a government office on May 2. Under Section 5 of RA 6713, within how many working days must the office act on or respond to the request?

**Solution:**
Identify the statutory deadline for acting on public requests under Section 5.

1. Section 5 requires public officials and employees to act on letters and requests from the public.
2. The statutory deadline is within 15 working days from receipt of the request.
3. Failure to act within 15 working days is a ground for administrative disciplinary action.
4. Therefore, the office must act on the request within 15 working days.

### #2: Post-Employment Private Practice Restriction

A former bureau chief resigns from government service to enter private law practice. For how long after separation from service is the former official prohibited from practicing before their former office on pending matters?

**Solution:**
Apply the post-employment conflict-of-interest restriction under Section 7.

1. Section 7 prohibits private practice that conflicts with official functions.
2. After separation from service, private practice regarding any matter before the former office is prohibited for 1 year.
3. Therefore, the restriction lasts for exactly 1 year after separation.

### #3: Maximum Number of Signatures on Official Documents

To reduce bureaucratic delay, Section 5 sets a maximum limit on signatures or initials for written actions. What is the maximum allowed number of signatures or initials?

**Solution:**
Recall the efficiency rule under Section 5.

1. Section 5 specifies rules to simplify administrative procedures.
2. Written actions must contain not more than three initials or signatures.
3. Therefore, the maximum limit is three initials or signatures.
