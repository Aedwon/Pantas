## Core Concept

### Public Access and Use Limits

Filed statements are open for inspection during reasonable hours upon a valid and lawful request under the repository's rules. RA 6713 makes copies available after $10$ working days from filing. The Ombudsman makes SALNs in its repositories available for inspection and copying once they are processed and recorded, at least $10$ working days after the final agency submission deadline. A reasonable fee covers copying, mailing, and certification.

Statements stay available to the public for $10$ years after receipt. After that, they may be destroyed unless needed in an ongoing investigation. Destruction is allowed, not required. The Ombudsman keeps and digitizes the SALNs already in its files.

Section $8(D)$ bans obtaining or using statements for purposes contrary to morals or public policy. This means purposes that go against accepted standards of right conduct or the public good.

Commercial use is also banned, except use by news and communications media to inform the general public. Access to a statement does not allow someone to sell its data for marketing.

## Examples

### #1: Copying an Older Statement

A journalist requests a SALN received $8$ years ago. When does a statement become available for copying, and is this one still within the public-access period?

**Solution:**

1. Under RA 6713, the copying period is $10$ working days from filing. For Ombudsman repositories, the earliest release is $10$ working days after the final agency submission deadline, once the SALN has been processed and recorded.
2. Statements stay available for $10$ years after receipt. An $8$-year-old statement is within that period.
3. The journalist can request a copy from the repository. Getting a copy depends on a lawful request, the repository's access rules, and payment of the applicable fee.

### #2: A Statement Still Needed as Evidence

A statement was received more than $10$ years ago. An ongoing investigation still needs it. May it be destroyed under the records rule?

**Solution:**

1. Destruction is allowed after $10$ years only if the statement is not needed in an ongoing investigation.
2. This statement is still needed. It must not be destroyed under that rule.

### #3: Marketing and News Uses

A marketing company sells SALN information in a customer list. A news organization uses a SALN in a report informing the general public. How does the commercial-use rule treat these uses?

**Solution:**

1. Selling the marketing list is commercial use outside the media exception. It is prohibited.
2. The news use fits the stated exception for news and communications media informing the general public.
3. The exception does not allow every commercial user to claim a public-information purpose.


## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating Public Access as Unlimited Use

> Access and permitted use are different rules.

Statements may be inspected and copied under the access rules. Section $8(D)$ still bars prohibited purposes, including commercial use outside the stated media exception.
