## Core Concept

### Copying Window and Ten-Year Retention

Under Section $8$(C), anyone may request to inspect a filed SALN during reasonable working hours under repository rules.

Statements open for copying **$10$ working days** after filing. At Ombudsman repositories, copies open once recorded and not before $10$ working days after the final agency deadline.

Requesters pay a reasonable fee for copying, mailing, and certification.

Repositories keep statements publicly available for **$10$ years** after receipt. After $10$ years, they **may** destroy a statement unless needed in an **ongoing investigation**. Destruction is optional, and the Ombudsman keeps and digitizes its files.

Check two June $2$ requests: copying a SALN filed $6$ working days ago, and inspecting a $7$-year-old SALN from $2019$:

1. The $6$-working-day-old SALN stays closed for copying until $10$ working days pass.
2. The $7$-year-old SALN sits inside the $10$-year retention window and stays publicly available.

:::why
Why keep SALNs past $10$ years for active probes? Shredding them at $10$ years would erase evidence needed for ongoing graft or unexplained-wealth inquiries.
:::

:::pair #ten-working-days-copying
Right: Statements become available for copying after $10$ working days from filing.
Wrong: Requiring a $15$-working-day wait after filing before copies become available.
Remember the twin $10$s in Section $8$(C): $10$ working days for copying, and $10$ years for retention.
:::

:::pair #investigation-blocks-destruction
Right: After $10$ years, a statement may be destroyed only if no ongoing investigation still needs it.
Wrong: Automatically destroying every SALN at the $10$-year mark even during an active investigation.
The $10$-year destruction permission stops whenever an active inquiry still needs the statement.
:::

:::check
A civic researcher requests a mayor's SALN $4$ working days after filing. Meanwhile, an Ombudsman probe still needs a $13$-year-old SALN in the same vault. May the researcher copy the new file now, and may the vault shred the $13$-year-old file?
:::answer
The answer is **No to both.** Copying opens after $10$ working days, and an ongoing investigation bars destruction of the $13$-year-old file.
:::

### Prohibited Purposes and the Media Exception

Section $8$(D) bars obtaining or using any SALN for two prohibited purposes:

1. Any purpose **contrary to morals or public policy** (against right conduct or the public good).
2. Any **commercial purpose**, except use by **news and communications media** for dissemination to the general public.

Paying the copying fee grants access to a copy, never a license to sell financial data.

Compare a radio newsroom and a credit-scoring firm that both pay fees for officials' SALNs:

1. The radio newsroom broadcasts wealth reports to voters, fitting the news-media exception.
2. The credit-scoring firm sells paid borrower profiles, which is a prohibited commercial use.

:::pair #no-commercial-resale
Right: Commercial use is barred unless done by news and communications media to inform the general public.
Wrong: Treating payment of the copying fee as allowing a private firm to resell SALN data.
Paying for copies meets Section $8$(C) access rules, while Section $8$(D) separately bars commercial resale.
:::

:::check
A real-estate brokerage pays the certification fee for city officials' SALNs so its agents can pitch luxury condos to wealthy filers. Does paying the fee make that use lawful?
:::answer
The answer is **No.** Pitching sales leads is a commercial purpose outside Section $8$(D)'s news-media exception.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Working Days and Retention Years

A reporter asks on May $8$ for a SALN filed $12$ working days earlier at a local agency. She also requests a $9$-year-old SALN received in $2017$. May she copy both?

**Solution:**

1. Check the new SALN: $12$ working days exceeds the $10$-working-day threshold from filing.
2. Check the older SALN: $9$ years falls inside the $10$-year public availability window after receipt.

Answer: Yes, both may be copied upon paying the reasonable reproduction fee.

**Check:** Copying requires $10$ working days, and availability lasts $10$ years after receipt.

### #2: Older Record Needed as Evidence

A SALN received $14$ years ago is subpoenaed in a pending administrative case. The former official demands its destruction because $10$ years passed. Must the repository shred it?

**Solution:**

1. Read Section $8$(C): destruction after $10$ years is permitted only when no ongoing investigation needs the file.
2. Apply the rule: the pending case needs the record, and destruction is optional anyway.

Answer: No, retain the SALN for the ongoing investigation.

**Check:** Never destroy a record past $10$ years while an investigation still needs it.

### #3: Television Report Versus Wealth Directory

A television network uses SALNs in an evening broadcast informing voters. Separately, a private data broker pays the copying fee and sells printed wealth directories to luxury car dealers. Rule on both uses under Section $8$(D).

**Solution:**

1. Television network: check whether broadcasting public accountability reports to voters fits Section $8$(D)'s news-media exception.
2. Data broker: test whether paying copying fees permits selling printed wealth directories under Section $8$(D).

:::try
Finish the last step: state the final ruling on both the television network and the private data broker.
:::answer
The answer is **The television network's broadcast is permitted, while the data broker's sale is prohibited.** News media informing the public are exempt, whereas selling wealth directories is unlawful commercial use.

**Check:** Separate news reporting to the general public from commercial data sales.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Copying wait period: wait $10$ working days from filing, not $15$ working days, before copies open. {#ten-working-days-copying}
- Ten-year retention limit: keep any SALN past $10$ years whenever an ongoing investigation needs it. {#investigation-blocks-destruction}
- Commercial use ban: paying copying fees never allows private firms outside news media to sell SALN data. {#no-commercial-resale}
:::
