## Core Concept

### One-Year Bans on Business Roles and Referrals

Under Section $7(b)$ of RA $6713$, leaving service through **resignation**, **retirement**, or **separation** does not end conflict limits right away.

Section $7(b)$ restrictions continue for **$1$ year** after separation, even after government pay stops.

Two bans continue in full for that $1$-year period:

1. Section $7(b)(1)$: holding covered roles (owner, manager, consultant, counsel, broker, agent, trustee, or nominee) in firms regulated, supervised, or licensed by the **former office**. Only express law allows an exception.
2. Section $7(b)(3)$: recommending anyone to private firms with **regular** or **pending** transactions before the **former office**, unless law or assigned duty requires it.

Apply Section $7(b)$ to an aviation officer who left $7$ months ago and is offered consulting by a licensed airline:

1. Check time: $7$ months sits inside the $1$-year post-separation window.
2. Check the ban: Section $7(b)(1)$ bars consultant roles at former-office-licensed firms for $1$ year.

Result: Leaving office did not lift the ban, so the contract stays prohibited.

:::why
Why keep Section $7(b)$ running for $1$ year after leaving? A $1$-year cooling-off window stops insiders from trading agency ties for immediate private jobs.
:::

:::pair #departure-starts-one-year
Right: Resignation, retirement, or separation starts a $1$-year continuation of Section $7(b)$ restrictions.
Wrong: Assuming resignation or retirement ends Section $7(b)$ limits as soon as government pay stops.
Stopping government pay starts the $1$-year cooling-off clock rather than ending former-office bans.
:::

:::pair #recommendation-ban-continues
Right: Both outside-business roles and job recommendations to former-office-transacting firms stay barred for $1$ year.
Wrong: Treating the $1$-year post-separation rule as covering business ownership only while allowing job recommendations.
Section $7(b)$ carries the recommendation ban forward for the full $1$-year period after departure.
:::

:::check
Eight months after retiring, a former port collector recommends her nephew to a shipping firm with a pending tariff refund before her former port. Is that allowed?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. At $8$ months, the $1$-year Section $7(b)(3)$ recommendation ban still covers firms with pending former-office matters.
:::

### Former-Office Practice Versus All Private Work

Section $7(b)(2)$ sets a targeted $1$-year limit on **private professional practice** after leaving office.

For **$1$ year** after resignation, retirement, or separation, a former worker cannot practice privately **on any matter before the former office**.

This limit does not ban every private job everywhere. Unrelated clients whose matters never reach the former office remain allowed during that year.

Test Section $7(b)(2)$ on a tax lawyer who retired $9$ months ago and gets two offers:

1. Arguing a factory's tax appeal before her former revenue office: barred for $1$ year.
2. Drafting a private school's teacher contract with zero former-office filings: allowed right away.

Result: Only the matter before her former office is barred during the $1$-year window.

:::pair #former-office-matters-only
Right: For $1$ year after leaving, private practice is barred only on matters before the former office.
Wrong: Assuming the $1$-year former-office limit bans all private professional work with any client anywhere.
Check where the matter sits: former-office matters are barred for $1$ year, while unrelated clients stay open.
:::

:::check
Five months after leaving the energy board, an accountant audits a bakery's books with zero energy-board filings. Does Section $7(b)(2)$ bar that audit?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. During the $1$-year window, Section $7(b)(2)$ bars private practice only on matters before the former office.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Regulated Role Five Months After Resignation

Five months after leaving a telecom agency, an engineer becomes a **trustee** at a cable firm licensed by that agency. No law permits it. He claims resignation ended RA $6713$. Is he right?

**Solution:**

1. Check departure: resignation starts the $1$-year window instead of ending Section $7(b)$.
2. Check the role: serving as trustee in a former-office-licensed firm stays barred at $5$ months.

Answer: No, the trustee role stays prohibited until $1$ year after resignation.

**Check:** Confirm the $1$-year window and whether the former office regulates or licenses the firm.

### #2: Recommendation for a Friend After Retirement

Ten months after retiring from a highway bureau, a former chief recommends his neighbor to an asphalt firm selling gravel to the bureau monthly. No exception applies. Does retirement clear it?

**Solution:**

1. Check the firm: monthly gravel sales are a regular transaction with the former bureau.
2. Check time: $10$ months sits inside the $1$-year ban, and helping a neighbor is no exception.

Answer: No, recommending a friend to a regular former-office supplier stays barred for $1$ year.

**Check:** Apply the $1$-year rule to job recommendations as well as business roles.

### #3: Former-Office Appeal and Unrelated Teaching

Eight months after leaving a labor department, a former arbiter accepts a factory's wage appeal before that department. She also teaches evening law classes at a private college. Rule on both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check the wage appeal: arguing before her former department involves a matter before her former office inside the $1$-year window.
2. Check evening teaching: test whether teaching at a private college involves a regulated firm or former-office matter.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the wage appeal and the evening teaching job under Section $7(b)$.
:::answer
The answer is **The wage appeal is barred, while evening teaching is allowed.** At $8$ months, Section $7(b)$ bars practice before the former office without banning unrelated work like college teaching.

**Check:** Identify the exact activity first, then test regulated-firm links, transacting employers, and former-office matters.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Leaving office and salary end: count $1$ year from resignation or retirement before former-office limits expire. {#departure-starts-one-year}
- Recommendation ban after retirement: apply the $1$-year rule to job recommendations as well as business roles. {#recommendation-ban-continues}
- Post-separation practice scope: bar matters before the former office for $1$ year without banning unrelated clients. {#former-office-matters-only}
:::
