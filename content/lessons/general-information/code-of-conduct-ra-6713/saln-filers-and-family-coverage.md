## Core Concept

### Who Files and Who Is Exempt

Section $8$(A) of RA $6713$ requires every public official and employee to file a SALN, except three groups:

1. People serving in an **honorary capacity**
2. **Laborers**
3. **Casual or temporary workers**

Under Rule VII, Section $1$ of the implementing rules, **honorary capacity** means government service **without pay** and **without service credit**. **Service credit** means the work counts toward length-of-service benefits.

Unpaid work alone is not honorary if it earns service credit.

Section $3$(b) covers permanent and temporary appointees as public officials. Check the worker's actual category instead of relying on an appointment label.

Classify a retired doctor on a city board with zero pay and zero service credit, alongside an unpaid museum adviser earning service credit:

1. The retired doctor has zero pay and zero service credit, so honorary exemption applies.
2. The unpaid adviser earns service credit, so the honorary exemption fails and a SALN is required.

:::why
Why check both pay and service credit? Workers earning service credit gain future government benefits, so the law requires their SALN.
:::

:::pair #honorary-needs-no-credit
Right: Honorary capacity requires both zero pay and zero service credit under the implementing rules.
Wrong: Treating unpaid government service as honorary even when the worker earns service credit.
Verify both conditions together, because service credit removes the honorary exemption.
:::

:::check
A road laborer in the excluded category and an unpaid barangay consultant earning service credit ask if they must file a SALN. Who must file?
:::answer
The answer is **The unpaid consultant must file, while the laborer is exempt.** Earning service credit defeats honorary status, and laborers are excluded.
:::

### Spouse and Child Coverage

A SALN includes the filer, the **spouse**, and **unmarried children under $18$ living in their households**.

Every child needs all three tests to be included:

1. **Unmarried**
2. **Under $18$ years old**
3. **Living in the household**

Parents, adult children, siblings, and business partners stay off the filer's asset schedule.

Married government employees may file **jointly or separately**.

Under *Abid-Babano v. Executive Secretary*, a spouse's separate property is excluded when **complete separation of property** governs by law or premarital agreement.

Check a clerk's household under ordinary conjugal property: a spouse, an unmarried $14$-year-old son at home, and a married $17$-year-old daughter at home:

1. Include the spouse since complete separation of property does not apply.
2. Include the $14$-year-old son because he is unmarried, under $18$, and lives at home.
3. Exclude the $17$-year-old daughter because she is married.

:::pair #all-three-child-tests
Right: Include a child's property only when the child is unmarried, under $18$, and living in the household.
Wrong: Including an unmarried child age $20$ who lives at home despite being over $18$.
All three tests must pass together, so turning $18$ ends household coverage.
:::

:::pair #no-extended-family-finances
Right: Limit SALN asset and debt coverage to the filer, the spouse, and qualifying minor children.
Wrong: Adding parents, adult children, and business partners to the filer's own asset and debt schedule.
Extended family and business partners sit outside Section $8$(A)'s household coverage rule.
:::

:::check
A treasury clerk married under complete separation of property lives with an unmarried $15$-year-old daughter and an unmarried $18$-year-old son. Whose finances must she report?
:::answer
The answer is **Only her own and her $15$-year-old daughter's finances.** Complete separation excludes the spouse's separate property, and the $18$-year-old son fails the under-$18$ test.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Honorary Status Versus Temporary Label

A board member works without pay and earns zero service credit. Separately, a full-time clerk holds a temporary appointment in a regular agency post. Who is exempt from filing?

**Solution:**

1. Check the board member: zero pay and zero service credit satisfy Rule VII, Section $1$.
2. Check the clerk: Section $3$(b) covers temporary appointees, and an appointment label alone never makes a worker casual.

Answer: Only the unpaid board member without service credit is exempt.

**Check:** Both pay and service credit must be absent for honorary capacity.

### #2: Testing Household Members

A municipal engineer lives with his spouse, a married $17$-year-old son, an unmarried $12$-year-old daughter, and his retired mother. Ordinary property rules govern the marriage. Whose finances must be included?

**Solution:**

1. Include the spouse because complete separation of property does not apply.
2. Exclude the $17$-year-old son because marriage fails the unmarried test.
3. Include the $12$-year-old daughter because she meets all three child tests.
4. Exclude the retired mother because parents sit outside household coverage.

Answer: Include the engineer, the spouse, and the $12$-year-old daughter.

**Check:** Test every family member against spouse rules and all three child tests.

### #3: Joint Filing and Separate Property

Two public teachers are married under complete separation of property by premarital agreement. They have a $13$-year-old unmarried son at home and a $15$-year-old unmarried daughter living full-time in a relative's household. How does Section $8$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Spousal filing: check joint versus separate filing rules and how complete separation affects separate property.
2. Daughter age $15$: test whether living full-time in a relative's household meets all three child tests.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the $13$-year-old son and state whose finances each teacher reports if filing separately.
:::answer
The answer is **Each teacher includes their own finances and their $13$-year-old son's finances, excluding the spouse's separate property and the daughter.** The $13$-year-old passes all three child tests, while complete separation excludes spousal separate property.

**Check:** Verify all three child tests and check for complete separation of property.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Unpaid government service: verify zero service credit alongside zero pay before claiming honorary status. {#honorary-needs-no-credit}
- Child age and status: require unmarried status, age under $18$, and living in the household together. {#all-three-child-tests}
- Household asset scope: exclude parents, adult children, and business partners from the SALN schedule. {#no-extended-family-finances}
:::
