## Core Concept

### Humanitarian and Altruistic Organization Donations

Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$ allows donations from local or foreign private organizations. They must be considered and accepted as **humanitarian** (helping people in need) and **altruistic** (unselfish) in purpose and mission.

Check who receives the aid and how it is used. Food for disaster evacuees fits that mission. A personal resort trip for an approving officer never qualifies just because a donor calls it humanitarian.

Unlike nominal personal donations under Rule X, Section $1(f)(3)$, this rule omits the no-office-transaction bar. A donor's pending license never defeats the exception alone, yet the gift still cannot buy favorable license action.

Examine a foundation awaiting permit renewal that sends $50$ cases of clean water to an agency's landslide shelter, with zero staff perks:

1. Check use: all water goes to landslide evacuees, fitting a humanitarian and altruistic mission.
2. Check pending permit: the renewal alone does not defeat Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$ when the permit is decided fairly.

:::pair #org-vs-personal-donation
Right: Organization donations under Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$ follow their humanitarian purpose test without the personal-donation transaction bar.
Wrong: Treating a donor's pending agency application as automatically defeating a genuine humanitarian organization donation.
Keep the two rules separate: personal nominal donations check regular, pending, or expected dealings, while organization donations check humanitarian mission and acceptance.
:::

:::pair #donation-never-buys-favor
Right: An accepted relief donation still leaves the agency bound to decide any pending donor application fairly.
Wrong: Thinking agency acceptance of a humanitarian donation permits favorable treatment on the donor's pending application.
Genuine relief aid helps disaster victims and never buys special treatment or personal perks.
:::

:::check
A clinic awaiting accreditation renewal donates $20$ medical tents to a public hospital's typhoon ward, seeking no staff favor. Must the hospital reject the tents?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$ carries no automatic transaction bar, though the accreditation renewal must still be decided strictly on merit.
:::

### Government-to-Government Donations

Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$ separately allows donations from **one government entity to another government entity**. Keep these transfers separate from personal donations and foreign courtesy souvenirs.

Two limits keep this exception clear:

1. The donated property belongs to the **receiving government entity**, never to the accepting officer personally.
2. Unlike foreign courtesy souvenirs, government-to-government donations have no nominal-value ceiling.

Evaluate a provincial disaster office receiving $2$ rescue vans donated officially by a foreign ministry:

1. Check parties: both are government entities, fitting Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$ despite the high value.
2. Check ownership: the vans belong to the provincial office, never to the provincial director personally.

:::why
Why treat government-to-government transfers separately? Equipment moving between public agencies supports public operations instead of enriching an individual official.
:::

:::pair #gov-to-gov-not-personal
Right: Government-to-government donations go to the receiving agency without a nominal-value cap and never become personal officer property.
Wrong: Treating agency-to-agency donated equipment as the accepting officer's personal courtesy gift.
Agency-to-agency transfers belong to the public office and follow Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$ without a nominal-value cap.
:::

:::check
A foreign ministry donates $5$ solar grain dryers to a town agriculture office for farmers. May the town agriculturist keep one dryer at home?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$, the dryers belong to the receiving town office, not to the accepting officer personally.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Relief Purpose Versus Disguised Personal Perk

A mining group awaiting an environmental review offers "community outreach aid" consisting of $4$ paid resort vouchers for the reviewing directors. Does the humanitarian label satisfy Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$?

**Solution:**

1. Check who benefits: resort vouchers reward officials personally instead of helping people in need.
2. Apply Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$: a humanitarian label never turns a personal favor into altruistic relief aid.

Answer: No, the resort vouchers fail the humanitarian and altruistic purpose test.

**Check:** Trace the actual recipients and use of the donation, never the donor's label alone.

### #2: Intergovernmental Transfer Above Nominal Value

A foreign science ministry donates a costly laboratory spectrometer directly to a Philippine state university for student research. Does the high value disqualify the transfer under RA $6713$?

**Solution:**

1. Check the parties: the transfer moves directly from one government entity to another.
2. Apply Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$: the nominal-value cap applies to personal courtesy souvenirs, not government-to-government transfers.

Answer: No, high value does not disqualify the university under Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$.

**Check:** Distinguish agency-owned intergovernmental transfers from personal courtesy souvenirs given to an official.

### #3: Evaluating Both Donation Types Together

An international NGO awaiting registration donates $100$ solar lanterns for typhoon evacuees, seeking no favor. The same week, a sister city abroad donates $2$ rescue boats to the city disaster office. Classify both transfers and state the remaining limits.

**Solution:**

1. Check solar lanterns: evacuee aid meets Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$'s humanitarian mission despite pending registration.
2. Check rescue boats: a transfer between public entities fits Rule X, Section $1(f)(5)$ without a nominal-value limit.

:::try
Finish the last step: name both exceptions and state the limits still governing the registration and the boats.
:::answer
The answer is **The lanterns fit Rule X, Section $1(f)(4)$ (organization donations) and the boats fit Section $1(f)(5)$ (government-to-government donations).** The agency must decide the NGO's registration strictly on merit. The rescue boats belong to the city disaster office, not any officer.

**Check:** Match private relief aid to Section $1(f)(4)$ with fair licensing, and public transfers to Section $1(f)(5)$ with agency ownership.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Organization donations with pending files: apply the humanitarian-purpose test instead of importing the personal-donation transaction bar. {#org-vs-personal-donation}
- Pending donor decisions: keep licensing and regulatory decisions fair because relief aid never buys approval. {#donation-never-buys-favor}
- Government-to-government equipment: keep donated assets in agency ownership without applying a nominal-value cap. {#gov-to-gov-not-personal}
:::
