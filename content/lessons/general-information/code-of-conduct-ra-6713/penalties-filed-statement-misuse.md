## Core Concept

### Prohibited Uses Under Section $8(D)$

A **SALN** is a Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth. Assets are things owned, liabilities are debts, and net worth is assets minus debts.

Public inspection rules let citizens read and copy filed statements, yet public access never permits every use.

Under **Section $8(D)$**, obtaining or using a filed statement is banned for two purposes:

1. Any purpose **contrary to morals or public policy**, meaning against right conduct or the public good.
2. Any **commercial purpose**, such as selling marketing lists. An exception allows **news and communications media** to inform the general public.

Compare two requesters who copy a governor's filed SALN:

1. A radio newsroom sharing net-worth facts with voters: allowed under the news-media exception.
2. A loan broker harvesting home addresses to pitch credit cards: banned commercial use.

Result: Media reporting to the public is lawful, whereas commercial marketing is prohibited.

:::why
Why allow public inspection while barring commercial resale? Disclosure lets citizens check unexplained wealth, while Section $8(D)$ stops brokers from selling personal records for profit.
:::

:::check
An insurance agency pays copying fees for officials' SALNs to mail brochures to wealthy filers. Is that use allowed?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Mailing sales brochures is a commercial purpose outside Section $8(D)$'s news-media exception, even after paying fees.
:::

### The Section $11(d)$ Court Remedy

Under **Section $11(d)$**, **the official or employee concerned** may sue over prohibited use of a filed statement.

Only that concerned worker holds the right to sue, never a business buying statements for resale.

The court may assess up to **₱$25{,}000$** against the wrongdoer. If another applicable sanction is heavier, that heavier sanction applies.

Keep Section $11$'s three money limits separate:

1. General administrative fine: up to $6$ months' salary under Section $11(a)$.
2. Sections $7$, $8$, or $9$ criminal fine: up to ₱$5{,}000$ under Section $11(a)$.
3. Filed-statement misuse penalty: up to ₱$25{,}000$ under Section $11(d)$.

Apply Section $11(d)$ when a firm sells a teacher's SALN data and the teacher earns ₱$28{,}000$ monthly:

1. Standing: the teacher concerned brings the Section $11(d)$ court action.
2. Ceiling: the court assesses up to ₱$25{,}000$, not ₱$5{,}000$ and not $6 \times 28{,}000$.

Result: The teacher sues for up to ₱$25{,}000$, unless a heavier sanction applies.

:::pair #twenty-five-thousand-ceiling
Right: Cap a Section $11(d)$ court penalty for statement misuse at ₱$25{,}000$, unless a heavier sanction applies.
Wrong: Using Section $11(a)$'s ₱$5{,}000$ criminal fine as the Section $11(d)$ statement-misuse ceiling.
Separate the ₱$5{,}000$ criminal fine in Section $11(a)$ from the ₱$25{,}000$ penalty in Section $11(d)$.
:::

:::pair #not-salary-based-fine
Right: Section $11(d)$ penalizes the misuser up to ₱$25{,}000$, independent of the worker's salary.
Wrong: Measuring the Section $11(d)$ statement-misuse penalty as $6$ months of the employee's salary.
Section $11(d)$ targets the person misusing the statement, so the worker's salary scale never applies.
:::

:::pair #official-concerned-sues
Right: Section $11(d)$ gives standing to bring the action to the official or employee concerned.
Wrong: Letting a business that wants to buy statements for resale bring a Section $11(d)$ suit.
The law protects the filer's record from misuse, so only the concerned official or employee may sue.
:::

:::check
A credit firm uses a clerk's SALN to sell wealth scores to landlords. The clerk earns ₱$22{,}000$ monthly. Who may sue under Section $11(d)$, and what maximum penalty applies?
:::answer
The answer is **The clerk concerned, for up to ₱$25{,}000$**. Section $11(d)$ gives the clerk standing and sets a ₱$25{,}000$ cap unless a heavier law applies.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Newspaper Story Versus Commercial Mailing List

A daily newspaper prints a factual comparison of governors' disclosed debts to inform voters. Meanwhile, a marketing firm uses the same SALNs to sell mailing lists to resorts. Rule on both uses under Section $8(D)$.

**Solution:**

1. Newspaper: informing the public through news media fits Section $8(D)$'s exception.
2. Marketing firm: selling mailing lists is commercial use outside news media and violates Section $8(D)$.

Answer: The newspaper story is allowed, whereas the marketing firm's mailing list is prohibited.

**Check:** Separate public news reporting from commercial data sales under Section $8(D)$.

### #2: Standing and Ceiling for Statement Resale

A startup pays copying fees and sells a paid online wealth directory of bureau directors. One director sues under Section $11(d)$. The startup claims only statement buyers can sue and damages stop at ₱$5{,}000$. Is that right?

**Solution:**

1. Standing: Section $11(d)$ grants the action to the official or employee concerned, meaning the director.
2. Ceiling: Section $11(d)$ authorizes up to ₱$25{,}000$, subject to any heavier sanction, not ₱$5{,}000$.

Answer: No, the director concerned may sue for up to ₱$25{,}000$ under Section $11(d)$.

**Check:** Verify the concerned filer as claimant and ₱$25{,}000$ as the Section $11(d)$ limit.

### #3: Commercial Harassment and Competing Sanctions

A car dealer copies an engineer's SALN to send ads and extort money over listed debts. The engineer earns ₱$60{,}000$ monthly. A separate extortion law carries a heavier penalty than Section $11(d)$. Check the purpose, claimant, and penalty rule.

**Solution:**

1. Check Section $8(D)$: test whether mailing ads and extortion violate the commercial and public-policy bans.
2. Check standing and ceiling: identify who sues under Section $11(d)$. Compare ₱$25{,}000$, $6$ months' salary, and the heavier extortion sanction.

:::try
Finish the last step: name who may sue under Section $11(d)$ and state which penalty rule controls.
:::answer
The answer is **The engineer concerned may sue, and the heavier extortion sanction applies**. Section $11(d)$ grants up to ₱$25{,}000$ to the official concerned, yet yields when another sanction is heavier.

**Check:** Confirm Section $11(d)$'s ₱$25{,}000$ cap applies unless another statute sets a heavier sanction.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Statement-misuse ceiling: assess up to ₱$25{,}000$ under Section $11(d)$, never Section $11(a)$'s ₱$5{,}000$ fine. {#twenty-five-thousand-ceiling}
- Fixed peso limit: apply ₱$25{,}000$ against the misuser instead of $6$ months of the employee's salary. {#not-salary-based-fine}
- Right to sue: file the Section $11(d)$ action through the concerned official or employee, never a resale firm. {#official-concerned-sues}
:::
