## Core Concept

### Recommending Persons to Transacting Firms

Under Section $7(b)(3)$, an official or employee must not recommend anyone for a job in a private business with **regular** or **pending** office dealings. Regular dealings are ongoing, while pending deals still await action.

Rule X, Section $1(d)$ allows referrals only when required by **law**, **international commitments**, or **assigned public duties**. Using personal email, acting off-duty, taking no pay, or citing strong credentials never creates an exception.

Evaluate a city engineering chief emailing an endorsement from home for a surveyor applying at a contractor with a pending road bid:

1. Check the firm's link: the contractor has a pending transaction before her office.
2. Check the exception: home email and strong credentials never replace a legal mandate or assigned duty.

:::pair #referral-merit-not-exception
Right: Only referrals required by law, international commitments, or assigned duties escape the Section $7(b)(3)$ ban.
Wrong: Thinking strong job qualifications remove Section $7(b)(3)$'s ban on recommending to a transacting firm.
Check the firm's transactions with the office and whether a legal mandate or assigned duty requires the referral.
:::

:::check
A customs clerk sends a Sunday email from home recommending an honors graduate to a brokerage clearing cargo weekly at her port. Is that allowed?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Weekly cargo clearing is a regular office dealing, and Sunday timing, private email, or honors grades create no exception.
:::

### Restrictions for One Year After Separation

**Separation** means leaving government service through resignation, retirement, or other departure. Under the last paragraph of Section $7(b)$, leaving office does not end conflict limits at once.

Two bans continue in full for $1$ year after separation:

1. Section $7(b)(1)$ roles in private firms regulated, supervised, or licensed by the former office.
2. Section $7(b)(3)$ referrals to private firms with regular or pending dealings with the former office.

Section $7(b)(2)$ sets a narrower $1$-year practice rule. For $1$ year after leaving, the former worker cannot practice privately on **any matter before the former office**. Other unrelated work stays open.

Apply the $1$-year rule to a securities examiner who resigned $8$ months ago and receives two offers:

1. Consulting for a brokerage regulated by her former agency violates the $1$-year continuation of Section $7(b)(1)$.
2. Auditing a rural cooperative with zero matters before her former agency stays lawful.

:::why
Why keep these limits for $1$ year after separation? A $1$-year cooling-off window stops former insiders from trading immediate office ties for private jobs.
:::

:::pair #one-year-cooling-off
Right: The post-separation limits under Section $7(b)$ last exactly $1$ year after resignation, retirement, or separation.
Wrong: Shortening the Section $7(b)$ post-separation cooling-off period to $6$ months.
Remember one rule across all three Section $7(b)$ paragraphs: the statutory cooling-off period is $1$ year.
:::

:::pair #former-office-matter-scope
Right: For $1$ year after separation, private practice is barred only on matters before the former office.
Wrong: Expanding the $1$-year former-office limit into a total ban on all professional practice everywhere.
Check where the matter sits: any matter before the former office is barred for $1$ year, while unrelated clients stay open.
:::

:::check
Ten months after retiring from a land agency, a lawyer handles a school's trial court lease case unrelated to her former agency. Is that barred?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. During the $1$-year cooling-off period, Section $7(b)$ bars private practice only on matters before the former office.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Mandated Duty Versus Personal Referral

A maritime academy officer's official duty under regulation requires nominating cadets for sea-time berths on merchant vessels. She nominates a cadet to a shipping line awaiting her agency's safety certificate. Is that referral barred?

**Solution:**

1. Spot the pending deal: the shipping line awaits agency certification, triggering Section $7(b)(3)$.
2. Apply Rule X, Section $1(d)$: official duties under regulation require cadet nominations, so the pending certificate never defeats the exception.

Answer: No, the nomination fits the assigned-duty exception.

**Check:** Law, international commitments, and assigned duties excuse a referral, while personal favors never do.

### #2: Business Roles Versus Textbook Writing

Seven months after retiring, an aviation inspector wants to consult for an airline licensed by his former bureau and write a safety textbook. Which activity is barred?

**Solution:**

1. Check consulting: Section $7(b)(1)$ bars consultant roles in former-office-licensed firms for $1$ year.
2. Check textbook writing: writing a book involves no regulated firm role and no matter before his former bureau.

Answer: Airline consulting is barred for $1$ year, while textbook writing is allowed.

**Check:** Name the activity first, then apply the $1$-year rule to regulated roles, referrals, and former-office matters.

### #3: Two Acts at Nine Months

Nine months after leaving a power board, an engineer recommends her cousin to a supplier with regular board filings. She also writes software for a foreign retailer with no energy filings. Rule on both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check the referral: test the supplier's regular board dealings and the $9$-month gap against Section $7(b)(3)$.
2. Check software work: test whether it involves a board-regulated firm or a matter before her former board.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the cousin's referral and the software consulting under Section $7(b)$.
:::answer
The answer is **The referral violates Section $7(b)$, while the software consulting is allowed.** At $9$ months, the $1$-year referral ban still covers firms with regular former-board dealings. Unrelated retail software work involves no matter before the former board.

**Check:** Apply the $1$-year rule separately to regulated roles, referrals, and matters before the former office.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Qualified applicants: require law, treaty, or assigned duty for referrals instead of applicant qualifications. {#referral-merit-not-exception}
- Cooling-off duration: apply $1$ full year after separation, never shortening it to $6$ months. {#one-year-cooling-off}
- Former-office practice scope: bar any matter before the former office for $1$ year without banning unrelated practice. {#former-office-matter-scope}
:::
