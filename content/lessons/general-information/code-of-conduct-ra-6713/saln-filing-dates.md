## Core Concept

### Three Filing Deadlines

Section $8$(A) of RA $6713$ sets three deadlines for filing your sworn SALN:

1. **Initial filing**: within **$30$ days** after assumption of office.
2. **Annual filing**: on or before **April $30$** every year thereafter, showing assets and debts as of December $31$ of the prior year.
3. **Separation filing**: within **$30$ days** after separation from government service.

Count $30$ days starting on the **next day** after entry or exit. All three deadlines use **calendar days**, never working days. Only the copying rule uses working days.

A separation filing never replaces an annual filing due earlier that year.

Find the initial deadline for an officer assuming office on March $5$ ($31$ days in March):

1. Start counting on March $6$.
2. Count days left in March, then days needed in April:

$$
31 - 5 = 26 \text{ days in March}
$$

$$
30 - 26 = 4 \text{ days in April}
$$

The initial SALN is due on or before April $4$.

:::why
Why is the annual SALN due on April $30$ instead of December $31$? Filers need time after year-end to gather bank, tax, and loan records.
:::

:::pair #thirty-days-not-sixty
Right: File both the initial SALN and the separation SALN within $30$ calendar days after the trigger date.
Wrong: Using Section $9$'s $60$-day divestment period for initial or separation SALN filing instead of $30$ days.
Section $8$(A) gives $30$ days for entry and exit; $60$ days belongs to share divestment.
:::

:::pair #annual-deadline-april
Right: File every annual SALN on or before April $30$, covering the prior year ending December $31$.
Wrong: Treating December $31$ as the annual SALN filing deadline instead of the valuation date.
December $31$ is the year-end snapshot date, while April $30$ is the filing deadline.
:::

:::check
A public health nurse separates from government service on November $10$ ($30$ days in November). By what exact date must she file her separation SALN?
:::answer
The answer is **December $10$.** Starting on November $11$ leaves $20$ days in November plus $10$ days in December.
:::

### Authority for Ombudsman Access

Under Section $8$(A), covered filers must give the **Ombudsman** written authority to obtain government financial records. These include Bureau of Internal Revenue tax files.

This authority is due within **$30$ days** after assumption of office, matching the initial SALN clock.

Trace a customs appraiser assuming office on May $4$ ($31$ days in May):

1. Start on May $5$, giving $31 - 4 = 27$ days in May.
2. Add $30 - 27 = 3$ days in June.
3. Submit both the initial SALN and the Ombudsman access authority by June $3$.

:::pair #authority-within-thirty-days
Right: Grant the Ombudsman authority to obtain government financial records within $30$ days after assuming office.
Wrong: Waiting until the next annual SALN filing date to grant the Ombudsman record-access authority.
Do not wait for April $30$ or a criminal case; the authority is due within $30$ days of entry.
:::

:::check
A budget analyst takes office on August $12$ ($31$ days in August). She files her initial SALN on August $28$ without signing the Ombudsman record-access authority. By what date is that authority due?
:::answer
The answer is **September $11$.** From August $13$, $19$ days remain in August plus $11$ days in September.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Initial Filing Across a $31$-Day Month

A district engineer assumes office on July $10$ ($31$ days in July). By what date must he file his initial SALN?

**Solution:**

1. Start counting on July $11$, the day after assumption of office.
2. Count days left in July, then days needed in August:

$$
31 - 10 = 21 \text{ days in July}
$$

$$
30 - 21 = 9 \text{ days in August}
$$

Answer: August $9$.

**Check:** $21$ days in July plus $9$ days in August equals $30$ calendar days.

### #2: Annual and Separation Filings in One Year

A state auditor stays in service through May and retires on October $20$ ($31$ days in October). What annual and separation deadlines apply that year?

**Solution:**

1. Annual filing: because she served through May, her annual SALN was due on or before April $30$.
2. Separation filing: start on October $21$ and count days in October plus November:

$$
31 - 20 = 11 \text{ days in October}
$$

$$
30 - 11 = 19 \text{ days in November}
$$

Answer: April $30$ for the annual SALN, and November $19$ for the separation SALN.

**Check:** Retiring in October never erases the April $30$ annual filing deadline.

### #3: Entry SALN and Ombudsman Authority

A legal officer assumes office on February $10$ in a non-leap year ($28$ days in February). By what date are her initial SALN and Ombudsman access authority due?

**Solution:**

1. Start counting on February $11$, the day after assumption of office.
2. Count days left in February:

$$
28 - 10 = 18 \text{ days in February}
$$

:::try
Finish the last step: compute the remaining days in March to find the exact deadline for both filings.
:::answer
The answer is **March $12$.** Subtracting $18$ February days from $30$ leaves $12$ days in March, and both filings share that $30$-day deadline.

**Check:** $18$ days in February plus $12$ days in March equals $30$ calendar days.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Initial and separation filings: count $30$ calendar days from the day after entry or exit, not $60$ days. {#thirty-days-not-sixty}
- Annual filing deadline: file on or before April $30$, using December $31$ only as the valuation date. {#annual-deadline-april}
- Ombudsman access authority: submit it within $30$ days after assuming office, never later. {#authority-within-thirty-days}
:::
