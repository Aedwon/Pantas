## Core Concept

### Three-Part Review and Corrective Notice

Under Section $10$(a) and $10$(c) of RA $6713$, reviewers examine filed statements for three things:

1. **Timeliness**: filed on or before the deadline.
2. **Completeness**: no required entries left blank.
3. **Proper form**: signed, sworn, and formatted properly.

If a statement fails any check, the reviewer must **inform the reporting individual and direct corrective action**. Defects bring notice and correction, never automatic guilt.

Heads of other offices perform this review for their own staff.

Check an April $25$ SALN filed before April $30$ that leaves a vehicle value blank and lacks an oath signature:

1. Timeliness passes, yet completeness and proper form fail.
2. The reviewer notifies the filer and directs correction instead of accepting it or imposing punishment.

:::pair #timely-but-incomplete-needs-correction
Right: When a timely SALN has missing entries or wrong form, notify the filer and direct correction.
Wrong: Accepting an incomplete SALN without correction simply because it was filed on time.
Meeting the filing date never excuses blank entries or missing signatures under Section $10$.
:::

:::check
A regional accountant files her SALN on April $20$ with all figures filled in, yet omits the signed oath page. May the reviewer mark it passed, or what must happen?
:::answer
The answer is **Notify the accountant and direct correction.** Timely filing never replaces proper form under Section $10$.
:::

### Approved Written Opinions and Good-Faith Reliance

Section $10$(b) and $10$(d) allow designated bodies to issue **written opinions** interpreting RA $6713$, subject to strict approval rules:

1. **Senate or House committees**: issue opinions within their authority, approved by a **majority vote** of that chamber.
2. **Executive Department offices**: office-head opinions require approval by the **Secretary of Justice**.
3. **Judicial Department offices**: office-head opinions require approval by the **Chief Justice**.

Anyone acting in **good faith** (honestly) in reliance on an approved opinion **after issuance** is protected from RA $6713$ sanctions.

Protection covers both the requester and **anyone else with similar facts**. It never shields acts before issuance or acts with different facts.

Compare two officials citing a Senate majority-approved opinion on unpaid teaching: one follows it two weeks after issuance, while another claims it for paid consulting:

1. The official with matching facts acting after issuance is protected from RA $6713$ sanctions.
2. The official with different paid consulting facts gets no protection.

:::why
Why require majority or top-level approval before protecting reliance? Central approval keeps agencies from issuing conflicting ethics loopholes.
:::

:::pair #protection-extends-to-similar-facts
Right: Good-faith reliance protects both the requester and anyone else with similar facts after issuance.
Wrong: Limiting penalty protection only to the exact official who requested the written opinion.
Section $10$(b) protects any person with similar facts who relies honestly after issuance.
:::

:::pair #reliance-must-follow-issuance
Right: Approved opinions protect honest acts performed after the opinion is issued, never earlier conduct.
Wrong: Applying a newly issued opinion backward to excuse acts done before it was issued.
An official cannot rely in good faith on an opinion before it existed.
:::

:::check
An executive chief drafts an opinion not yet approved by the Secretary of Justice. A clerk cites it to excuse an act committed last year. Does Section $10$ protect the clerk?
:::answer
The answer is **No.** Executive opinions need Secretary of Justice approval, and protection never covers acts committed before issuance.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Approving Authorities Across Branches

An Executive Department agency head and a Judicial Department office head each write opinions interpreting RA $6713$. Whose approval must each receive?

**Solution:**

1. Executive Department opinion: Section $10$(d) requires approval by the Secretary of Justice.
2. Judicial Department opinion: Section $10$(d) requires approval by the Chief Justice.

Answer: Secretary of Justice for Executive offices, and Chief Justice for Judicial offices.

**Check:** Congress needs chamber majority votes, Executive needs the Secretary of Justice, and Judicial needs the Chief Justice.

### #2: Unapproved Draft and Differing Facts

An Executive office head writes an ethics opinion without Secretary of Justice approval, and Worker A relies on it. Meanwhile, Worker B relies on a Chief Justice-approved Judicial opinion for materially different business facts. Is either worker protected?

**Solution:**

1. Worker A: Executive office-head opinions carry zero protection until approved by the Secretary of Justice.
2. Worker B: an approved opinion protects only similar facts, never materially different conduct.

Answer: Neither Worker A nor Worker B is protected from RA $6713$ sanctions.

**Check:** Protection requires branch-head or chamber approval, matching facts, and honest reliance after issuance.

### #3: Review Defect and Co-Worker Reliance

A bureau reviewer finds an April $22$ SALN missing required business links. Separately, a co-worker with identical facts honestly follows a Secretary of Justice-approved opinion issued two weeks earlier. What rules apply?

**Solution:**

1. SALN defect: omitting business links fails completeness despite timely filing, so the reviewer directs correction.
2. Co-worker's facts: test approving authority, issuance timing, and fact match under Section $10$.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on whether the co-worker is protected from RA $6713$ sanctions and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **Yes, the co-worker is protected from RA $6713$ sanctions.** Section $10$ extends good-faith protection after issuance to anyone with similar facts following an approved opinion.

**Check:** Incomplete timely SALNs require notice and correction; approved opinions protect similar-fact reliance after issuance.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Timely but defective SALN: notify the filer and direct correction instead of passing it on time alone. {#timely-but-incomplete-needs-correction}
- Who an opinion protects: protect both the requester and anyone else with similar facts. {#protection-extends-to-similar-facts}
- Timing of reliance: apply approved opinions only to honest acts done after issuance, never backward. {#reliance-must-follow-issuance}
:::
