## Core Concept

### Public Trust and Corruption

**Corruption** means misusing public office or public trust for private gain.

Article XI, Section $1$ of the Constitution states that **public office is a public trust**. Officials must serve people with responsibility, integrity, loyalty, and efficiency.

Under RA $6713$, **public interest** puts people's needs above personal gain. **Simple living** means living modestly within one's position and income.

An honest error, lawful delay, or unpopular decision alone does not prove corruption. Misuse for private gain is required.

Test Article XI, Section $1$ when a mayor's aide hands city laptops to campaign helpers as a victory prize:

1. Check the property: public laptops belong to the city, not election winners.
2. Check the rule: rewarding campaign helpers puts personal loyalty above public needs.

Result: Handing out city laptops as a political prize breaks public trust.

:::why
Why does the Constitution call public office a public trust? Public power and funds belong to citizens, so officials hold them only to serve public needs.
:::

:::pair #honest-error-not-corruption
Right: Corruption requires misusing public office for private gain, not an honest work error.
Wrong: Counting any honest error made while carrying out a public duty as corruption.
An accidental mistake lacks private gain, while corruption turns public power into personal benefit.
:::

:::pair #office-not-election-reward
Right: Office funds and equipment remain public tools meant to serve citizens fairly after an election.
Wrong: Treating office resources as a personal reward earned by winning an election.
Winning an election creates a duty to serve, never personal ownership of public property.
:::

:::pair #supporters-never-first
Right: Public offices must serve applicants fairly without putting political supporters ahead of others.
Wrong: Giving political supporters first access to a public service ahead of the line.
Public trust demands equal treatment for every citizen, regardless of party ties.
:::

:::check
A civil registrar types the wrong birth year on a record and fixes it free the same day. Does that show corruption?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. An accidental typing error fixed without seeking private gain is an honest mistake, not corruption.
:::

### Direct Bribery Under Article $210$

**Direct bribery** is a corrupt exchange tied to official duties.

Under Revised Penal Code Article $210$, direct bribery happens when an officer agrees to receive, or receives, a gift or promise for three bargains:

1. Agreeing to commit a crime tied to official duties.
2. Doing an unjust act tied to official duties that is not a crime.
3. Refraining from performing a required official duty.

A bribe need not be cash. Free trips, goods, loans, or favors count when tied to that bargain.

An officer who refuses an offered gift and completes the duty has not accepted a bribe.

Apply Article $210$ when a customs appraiser accepts a paid hotel stay to skip a required cargo check:

1. Check the duty: checking imported cargo is a required official task.
2. Check the bargain: the hotel stay rewards skipping that duty.

Result: Taking the hotel stay to omit the check shows direct bribery under Article $210$.

:::check
A road engineer refuses a bidder's cash envelope to certify an unbuilt drain and reports the bidder. Did the engineer accept a bribe?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Refusing the cash and declining the wrongful act shows no accepted bribe under Article $210$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Mistake or Private Gain?

A clerk misfiles a tax clearance and delays a renewal by $2$ days before fixing it. Another asks for meal money to sign a clear permit. Which shows corruption?

**Solution:**

1. First clerk: misfiling a folder without seeking private gain is an honest mistake.
2. Second clerk: demanding meal money to sign a permit uses office for private gain.

Answer: Only the second clerk shows corruption.

**Check:** Look for private gain from official power, not an unpaid mistake.

### #2: Unjust Act for a Non-Cash Gift

A zoning officer accepts a builder's free car lease in exchange for approving a variance that breaks road-safety rules. Does Article $210$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Benefit: a free car lease carries clear value even without cash.
2. Act: approving an unlawful safety variance is an unjust official act.

Answer: Yes, taking the car lease for an unjust act fits direct bribery under Article $210$.

**Check:** Confirm a valued gift or promise tied to a crime, unjust act, or skipped duty.

### #3: Gift Basket Versus Concert Tickets

A permit reviewer rejects a cafe owner's gift basket and finishes the check on time. Another reviewer takes concert tickets and omits a failed kitchen from the report. Rule on both.

**Solution:**

1. First reviewer: test whether refusing the gift and finishing the check creates bribery.
2. Second reviewer: test whether taking tickets to hide a failed kitchen fits Article $210$.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both reviewers under Article $210$'s acceptance and duty-omission rules.
:::answer
The answer is **Only the second reviewer shows direct bribery.** The first refused the gift and did her duty. The second took valued tickets to skip reporting a violation.

**Check:** Direct bribery requires accepting or agreeing to a benefit for a wrongful act or skipped duty.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Honest work errors: require proof of private gain before calling an error corruption. {#honest-error-not-corruption}
- Election wins: keep office funds and equipment for public service, never personal rewards. {#office-not-election-reward}
- Line priority: serve everyone equally instead of putting political supporters first. {#supporters-never-first}
:::
