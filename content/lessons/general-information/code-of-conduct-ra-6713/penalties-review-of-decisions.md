## Core Concept

### Motion for Reconsideration Versus Rule $43$ Appeal

A **motion for reconsideration** asks the deciding body to review its own ruling. An **appeal** asks a higher body to review a decision.

Under the $2025$ Civil Service rules (RACCS), judicial review of a **CSC** disciplinary ruling goes to the **Court of Appeals** under **Rule $43$**.

The filing must be a **verified petition for review**, meaning confirmed under oath. An unverified letter or wrong-court filing fails Rule $43$.

Follow required administrative review first. This rule governs CSC rulings, not every agency ruling straight to court. Criminal and Ombudsman cases keep separate review rules.

Compare two ways a dismissed officer challenges a CSC decision:

1. Filing a criminal case in the Sandiganbayan to reverse the CSC order: wrong court and wrong remedy.
2. Filing a verified Rule $43$ petition in the Court of Appeals: proper review route.

Result: Only the verified Rule $43$ petition in the Court of Appeals properly seeks review.

:::why
Why require a verified Rule $43$ petition instead of an informal letter? Oath verification confirms the facts, and Rule $43$ brings a proper appellate record to the higher court.
:::

:::pair #verified-rule-forty-three-petition
Right: Review a CSC disciplinary decision in the Court of Appeals with a verified Rule $43$ petition.
Wrong: Sending an unverified request directly to the Court of Appeals without the required petition.
Rule $43$ requires a formal petition confirmed under oath rather than an unverified letter.
:::

:::pair #appeal-not-criminal-complaint
Right: Challenge a CSC disciplinary ruling through a Rule $43$ petition in the Court of Appeals.
Wrong: Filing a new criminal complaint in the Sandiganbayan to reverse a CSC disciplinary ruling.
The Sandiganbayan hears criminal cases, whereas Rule $43$ routes CSC administrative appeals to the Court of Appeals.
:::

:::check
A suspended assessor mails an unsworn one-page memo asking the Court of Appeals to reconsider a CSC ruling. Name two defects.
:::answer
The answer is **The memo is unverified and uses the wrong remedy**. Rule $43$ requires a verified petition under oath, not an unsworn memo or a reconsideration motion.
:::

### The $15$-Day Period and Notice to Counsel

Under Rule $43$, Section $4$, the ordinary filing period is **$15$ days**. It runs from notice of the decision or of denial of timely reconsideration.

When represented by counsel, **notice to counsel** ordinarily starts the $15$-day clock, as in G.R. No. $143538$. Check applicable filing and extension rules.

Track dates when a lawyer receives a CSC denial on June $1$, and the client reads it on June $6$:

1. Trigger date: counsel received notice on June $1$, starting the clock.
2. Add $15$ days to June $1$ for the ordinary deadline.

$$
1 + 15 = 16
$$

Result: The ordinary $15$-day period ends on June $16$, not June $21$.

:::pair #notice-to-counsel-starts-clock
Right: For a represented party, notice to counsel starts the $15$-day Rule $43$ clock.
Wrong: Counting the $15$ days from when the employee personally reads the denial after counsel got notice.
Service on counsel of record starts the review period rather than later personal reading by the client.
:::

:::check
A clerk's lawyer receives a CSC denial of reconsideration on March $3$. The clerk opens it on March $10$ and files a verified Rule $43$ petition on March $22$. Is it timely?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Fifteen days from March $3$ notice to counsel ended on March $18$, making March $22$ late without an extension.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Crossing a Month Boundary on an Initial Decision

An unrepresented clerk files no reconsideration motion and receives a final CSC decision on November $21$ ($30$ days in November). Find the ordinary Rule $43$ deadline.

**Solution:**

1. Trigger date: with no counsel and no reconsideration motion, direct notice on November $21$ starts the $15$-day count.
2. Count across November $30$ into December.

$$
30 - 21 = 9 \text{ days in November}
$$

$$
15 - 9 = 6 \text{ days in December}
$$

Answer: The ordinary Rule $43$ deadline is December $6$, subject to filing and extension rules.

**Check:** $9$ days left in November plus $6$ days in December equals $15$ days from direct notice.

### #2: Choosing the Proper Review Forum

A dismissed inspector receives a final CSC ruling after reconsideration fails. One friend advises filing a criminal complaint in the Sandiganbayan to cancel it. Another suggests a motion asking the Court of Appeals to reconsider it. Why are both wrong?

**Solution:**

1. Sandiganbayan: a criminal complaint cannot review a CSC administrative ruling.
2. Reconsideration motion: reconsideration asks the deciding body to review itself, whereas a higher court hears appeals.

Answer: Both fail; file a verified Rule $43$ petition for review in the Court of Appeals.

**Check:** Match CSC judicial review to a verified Rule $43$ petition in the Court of Appeals.

### #3: Unverified Filing and Missed Clock

An engineer's counsel receives a CSC denial of reconsideration on October $2$. The engineer reads it on October $8$ and mails an unverified request to the Court of Appeals on October $20$. Check the form and timing.

**Solution:**

1. Check form: test whether an unverified request meets Rule $43$, Sections $1$ and $5$.
2. Check timing: count $15$ days from October $2$ notice to counsel versus October $8$ personal receipt.

:::try
Finish the last step: state the two defects in the engineer's October $20$ filing under Rule $43$.
:::answer
The answer is **The request is unverified and filed past the $15$-day deadline**. Rule $43$ requires a verified petition under oath. Fifteen days from October $2$ ended on October $17$.

**Check:** Confirm oath verification and the $15$-day count from counsel's October $2$ notice.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Petition form: file a verified Rule $43$ petition under oath in the Court of Appeals, never an unverified request. {#verified-rule-forty-three-petition}
- Review court: challenge CSC rulings under Rule $43$ in the Court of Appeals, not via a Sandiganbayan complaint. {#appeal-not-criminal-complaint}
- Filing clock trigger: count the ordinary $15$-day period from notice to counsel when represented, not later client reading. {#notice-to-counsel-starts-clock}
:::
