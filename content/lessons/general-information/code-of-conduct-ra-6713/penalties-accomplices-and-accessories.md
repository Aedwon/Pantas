## Core Concept

### Accomplices Under Article $18$ and Section $11(c)$

Under **Article $18$**, an **accomplice** knowingly concurs with a crime and gives effective help before or during it.

The help must relate to the principal's act without being essential. Check knowledge, timing, and essentiality. A title like "consultant" never settles the role.

Once Section $11(c)$'s conspiracy condition is proved, a private accomplice faces the **same penal liabilities** in a **joint trial**. Never assume every helper joined a conspiracy; courts apply Section $11(c)$ to proved facts.

Apply Article $18$ when a private driver lends an official a van during a leak while public taxis run nearby:

1. Timing and essentiality: the van helps during the act without being indispensable.
2. Section $11(c)$: if conspiracy is proved, full liability and joint trial apply.

Result: The driver fits an accomplice role rather than a principal role.

:::why
Why separate accomplices from accessories? An accomplice aids the crime before or during execution, whereas an accessory acts only after the crime ends.
:::

:::pair #accomplice-same-liability
Right: Under Section $11(c)$, a private accomplice who conspires with an official faces the same penal liabilities.
Wrong: Cutting a private accomplice's penalty in half because the accomplice is private.
Section $11(c)$ imposes identical penal liabilities on private co-principals, accomplices, and accessories.
:::

:::pair #accomplice-joint-trial
Right: Private accomplices covered by Section $11(c)$ are tried jointly with the public workers.
Wrong: Requiring a separate trial in every case for a private accomplice under Section $11(c)$.
Both equal liability and joint trial apply together under Section $11(c)$ once conspiracy is proved.
:::

:::check
A clerk lends a portable scanner so an officer can copy tax dockets faster, though office copiers work fine. The clerk shares the purpose. What role fits, and how does Section $11(c)$ try it?
:::answer
The answer is **Accomplice, tried jointly with equal liability**. The scanner gave nonessential help during the act, triggering equal liability in a joint trial under Section $11(c)$.
:::

### Accessories, Article $19$, and Article $20$ Exceptions

Under **Article $19$**, an **accessory** knows the crime occurred and joins afterward without having been a principal or accomplice.

Covered post-crime acts include:

1. Profiting from proceeds or helping offenders profit.
2. Hiding or destroying evidence or instruments to prevent discovery.
3. Helping the principal escape under conditions involving abuse of public functions or listed crimes.

Section $11(c)$ expressly lists accessories. Lawful customers, uninvolved relatives, and lawful trial reporters fall outside Section $11(c)$.

Under **Article $20$**, certain close relatives are exempt from ordinary accessory penalties, except when profiting. Check all exceptions and Section $11(c)$ conditions first.

Compare two outsiders after a Section $7(d)$ payoff:

1. A sister who knew her brother worked there and took no part: zero liability.
2. A friend who burns bribe receipts later to hide the crime: accessory act under Article $19$.

Result: Only post-crime participation fits Article $19$, while uninvolved relatives face no charge.

:::pair #accessory-included-lawful-outsiders-not
Right: Section $11(c)$ covers accessories who act afterward, while excluding relatives who took no part.
Wrong: Treating a relative who knew the official without taking part as covered by Section $11(c)$.
Participation requires an actual criminal act such as hiding evidence, never mere family ties.
:::

:::check
A vendor pays a normal permit fee at a window where inspectors take bribes from others. Later, a retired guard hides bribe cash for a share. Who fits an accessory?
:::answer
The answer is **Only the retired guard**. Lawful customers commit no crime. Hiding proceeds afterward for profit fits Article $19$ and cannot use Article $20$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Useful Equipment Before the Offense

A private analyst learns of a decided plan to share procurement scores. The analyst agrees and lends a spare phone before the leak. The phone helps timing without being essential. Which role fits?

**Solution:**

1. Knowledge and timing: the analyst concurred and helped before the offense.
2. Essentiality: the phone was helpful, not indispensable, ruling out principal status.
3. Section $11(c)$: conspiracy still needs proof for joint penal liability.

Answer: The facts fit an accomplice role, subject to proving Section $11(c)$ conspiracy.

**Check:** Separate useful nonessential help before the crime from indispensable co-principal aid.

### #2: Burning Records After the Violation

A manager had zero part before or during a Section $7$ offense. After learning of it, the manager shreds signed receipts to stop investigators from discovering the crime. Which role applies?

**Solution:**

1. Timing: the manager acted only after the offense ended.
2. Article $19$: destroying instruments to prevent discovery is an accessory act.
3. Limits: check Article $20$ relative rules and Section $11(c)$ conspiracy proof.

Answer: The conduct fits an accessory under Article $19$, while Section $11(c)$ requirements still apply.

**Check:** Verify knowledge of the completed crime and post-crime action for accessory status.

### #3: Sorting Lawful Clients From Post-Crime Helpers

Investigators examine two outsiders tied to a Section $7(d)$ gift scheme. First, a courier paid normal counter fees for a permit. Second, a cousin took no prior part, then hid gifted watches later to block discovery. Check both under Section $11(c)$.

**Solution:**

1. Check the courier: test whether lawful counter dealings show participation under Section $11(c)$.
2. Check the cousin: check timing, Article $19$ evidence hiding, Article $20$ limits, and Section $11(c)$'s list.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the courier, name the cousin's Article $19$ role, and state whether Section $11(c)$ lists it.
:::answer
The answer is **The courier has no liability, and the cousin is an accessory listed in Section $11(c)$**. Lawful clients commit no crime. Hiding evidence afterward fits Article $19$ and Section $11(c)$, subject to conspiracy and Article $20$ rules.

**Check:** Exclude lawful outsiders first, then test timing, Article $19$, and Article $20$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Accomplice penalty ceiling: apply equal penal liabilities to private accomplices under Section $11(c)$ instead of halving them. {#accomplice-same-liability}
- Joint trial rule: try private accomplices jointly with public officials under Section $11(c)$. {#accomplice-joint-trial}
- Covered participants: include accessories who act afterward while excluding uninvolved relatives or lawful clients. {#accessory-included-lawful-outsiders-not}
:::
