## Core Concept

### Disclosure or Misuse of Confidential Information

Section $7(c)$ bars any official or employee from using or divulging **confidential or classified information** learned through office and not made public.

The ban applies whenever that use or sharing serves either of two ends:

1. To further private interests or give **undue advantage** to anyone.
2. To cause **prejudice to the public interest**.

Using nonpublic records yourself breaks Section $7(c)$ even without sharing them. Giving an undue advantage breaks the rule with zero pay, while lawfully public records sit outside it.

Review a port inspector checking an unannounced raid schedule and texting his brother-in-law's warehouse manager without pay:

1. Check status: the raid plan came from official duty and is nonpublic.
2. Apply Section $7(c)$: leaking it gives an undue advantage, violating Section $7(c)$ with no fee.

:::pair #confidential-info-section
Right: Place the ban on misusing or disclosing confidential official information in Section $7$ (specifically Section $7(c)$).
Wrong: Assigning the confidential-information ban to Section $2$ of RA $6713$.
Section $2$ states policy, Section $8$ covers financial statements, Section $9$ covers divestment, and Section $7$ covers prohibited acts.
:::

:::pair #confidential-not-public-release
Right: Confidential nonpublic information learned at work cannot be shared for undue private advantage under Section $7(c)$.
Wrong: Treating nonpublic work records as covered by a duty to release all official information at once.
Public-inspection duties cover public records, while Section $7(c)$ protects nonpublic confidential or classified information.
:::

:::check
A tax examiner uses an unreleased audit list from her desk to warn a cousin to hide stock, taking no fee. Which section and rule does she break?
:::answer
The answer is **Section $7(c)$ of RA $6713$, the ban on misusing or disclosing confidential information.** Taking no fee never excuses giving an undue advantage.
:::

### Solicitation or Acceptance of Valuable Benefits

Section $7(d)$ bars officials and employees from **soliciting or accepting**, directly or indirectly, any gift, favor, entertainment, loan, or anything of monetary value. The ban reaches benefits tied to official duties, office-regulated work, or office-affected deals.

**Solicitation** means asking for a benefit. Asking alone violates Section $7(d)$ even when the other party refuses and delivers nothing.

Calling a benefit a service, ticket, or small token never excuses a favor-linked trade. Taking leave or using a middleman never removes liability.

Test a customs clerk on approved leave who asks a port-regulated broker to pay for his family's hotel weekend, and the broker refuses:

1. Check solicitation and type: asking for hotel rooms seeks a valuable non-cash benefit from a regulated broker.
2. Apply Section $7(d)$: asking violates the rule despite the broker's refusal and the clerk's leave.

:::why
Why punish a refused request? Asking a regulated firm for favors abuses office authority and pressures private parties even when they say no.
:::

:::pair #solicitation-needs-no-delivery
Right: Soliciting a valuable benefit from a regulated business violates Section $7(d)$ even if refused, non-cash, or done on leave.
Wrong: Assuming Section $7(d)$ applies only if the requested benefit is actually delivered.
Both asking and accepting are banned, and valuable services, loans, or entertainment count alongside cash.
:::

:::check
On approved sick leave, a building inspector asks an office-supervised contractor for a zero-interest loan, and the contractor refuses. Did he violate Section $7(d)$?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Soliciting a loan from an office-supervised contractor violates Section $7(d)$ even when refused and during leave.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Nonpublic Files Versus Public Notices

A clerk shares an agency webpage listing approved zoning maps. Next day, she leaks an unreleased land-zoning inspection report so a developer buys lots early. Which act violates Section $7(c)$?

**Solution:**

1. Check the webpage link: records already lawfully public fall outside Section $7(c)$.
2. Check the inspection report: nonpublic findings learned at work give the developer an undue advantage.

Answer: Only leaking the unreleased land-zoning inspection report violates Section $7(c)$.

**Check:** Confirm the data came through office, stayed nonpublic, and furthered private gain or harmed public interest.

### #2: Indirect Delivery of Perks

A transport clerk asks an applicant bus company for $4$ VIP concert tickets for his sister, who picks them up. Does indirect receipt violate Section $7(d)$?

**Solution:**

1. Check benefit and link: concert tickets carry value and come from a firm needing office action.
2. Check indirect receipt: Section $7(d)$ bars soliciting or accepting benefits directly or indirectly.

Answer: Yes, asking for and taking the tickets through his sister violates Section $7(d)$.

**Check:** Non-cash favors and middleman pickups are barred like direct cash.

### #3: Draft Leaks and Refused Requests

An officer copies an unreleased penalty draft to warn a relative's plant manager, taking zero pay. While on leave, he asks a licensed hauler to cater his party, and it refuses. Rule on both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check the draft leak: test whether sharing an unreleased penalty draft for zero pay triggers Section $7(c)$.
2. Check catering: test whether asking a licensed hauler while on leave triggers Section $7(d)$ even if refused.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both acts under Sections $7(c)$ and $7(d)$, and explain why zero pay, leave, and refusal fail.
:::answer
The answer is **Both acts violate Section $7$: Section $7(c)$ for the penalty leak and Section $7(d)$ for soliciting catering.** Zero pay never excuses leaking nonpublic records for undue advantage. Leave status and refusal never excuse asking a licensed firm for a benefit.

**Check:** Match nonpublic leaks to Section $7(c)$ and asking for or taking valuable benefits to Section $7(d)$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Statutory section for leaks: assign confidential-information misuse to Section $7$, never Section $2$. {#confidential-info-section}
- Protected records versus public access: keep nonpublic work records confidential instead of releasing them for private advantage. {#confidential-not-public-release}
- Unfulfilled or non-cash requests: treat soliciting any valuable service, loan, or perk as a Section $7(d)$ violation even when refused. {#solicitation-needs-no-delivery}
:::
