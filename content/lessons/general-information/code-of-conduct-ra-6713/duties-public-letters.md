## Core Concept

### The Fifteen-Working-Day Reply Rule

Under Section $5(a)$ of RA $6713$, officials and employees must act promptly on letters and requests from the public.

The office must reply within **$15$ working days** from receipt. **Working days** count regular workdays only, excluding weekends and holidays.

The reply must state the **action taken** on the request. Simple requests finish right away. Complex requests still need a reply within $15$ working days explaining the action started and why more time is needed.

Trace Section $5(a)$ when a resident emails a permit inquiry on Monday, May $4$, with two holidays that month:

1. Start the clock: begin counting workdays after receipt on May $4$.
2. Count working days: skip weekends and holidays to reach $15$ working days.
3. State action: report the review started, never a bare receipt number.

Result: A substantive reply sent within $15$ working days satisfies Section $5(a)$.

:::why
Why require the action taken inside the $15$-working-day reply? A bare "received" note leaves citizens unsure whether anyone read or acted on their request.
:::

:::pair #working-days-not-calendar
Right: Section $5(a)$ gives $15$ working days from receipt to answer public letters.
Wrong: Counting $15$ calendar days, including weekends and holidays, as the Section $5(a)$ deadline.
Check the unit every time: letters take $15$ working days, not calendar days.
:::

:::pair #reply-needs-action-taken
Right: A Section $5(a)$ reply must state the specific action taken on the request.
Wrong: Sending only a receipt notice instead of stating what action the office took.
Logging receipt starts tracking, while Section $5(a)$ requires reporting the office's action.
:::

:::check
A records desk emails, "Your letter was logged today," and sends nothing else for $30$ working days. Did it satisfy Section $5(a)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A bare log notice fails Section $5(a)$ because it omits the action taken.
:::

### Incomplete Requests and Referrals

Under Rule VI, Section $3$ of the implementing rules, missing papers never excuse silence. Within $15$ working days, the reply must state four items:

1. The action taken so far.
2. The exact missing requirements.
3. A reasonable period to submit them.
4. The worker in charge.

Sometimes a citizen sends a letter to the wrong office. When the receiving office knows who has authority, Rule VI requires two steps: refer it there and inform the sender.

Apply Rule VI when a farmer mails a crop-insurance claim to the town treasurer instead of the agriculture office:

1. Check authority: the treasurer cannot act, yet knows the agriculture office handles crop claims.
2. Refer and notify: forward the claim to agriculture and notify the farmer of the transfer.

Result: Referring the claim and notifying the farmer meets Rule VI.

:::pair #refer-and-notify-sender
Right: Refer a misdirected request to the proper office and notify the sender.
Wrong: Forwarding a misdirected request to the proper office without telling the sender.
Rule VI requires both steps so the citizen knows where the request went.
:::

:::check
A homeowner submits a drainage permit request without the required lot plan. The clerk waits silently for $20$ working days. Does that follow Rule VI?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Within $15$ working days, the clerk must state the action taken, missing plan, filing period, and assigned worker.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Counting Days Across Public Holidays

A citizen files a written inquiry on August $3$. Two public holidays fall that month. Under Section $5(a)$, what is the deadline and required content?

**Solution:**

1. Apply the unit: Section $5(a)$ counts $15$ working days from receipt, skipping weekends and holidays.
2. Check content: the reply must state the action taken on the inquiry.

Answer: The office gets $15$ working days and must state the action taken.

**Check:** Confirm both the working-day unit and the action-taken requirement.

### #2: Missing Attachment on a Scholarship Request

A student sends a scholarship request without her grade sheet. The clerk mails a card reading only, "Letter received." What failed under Rule VI?

**Solution:**

1. Check receipt vs. action: a bare receipt omits what action the office took.
2. Check Rule VI items: the card omitted action taken, missing grade sheet, filing period, and worker in charge.

Answer: The card failed by omitting action taken, missing papers, reasonable period, and worker in charge.

**Check:** Incomplete requests require all four details in the reply, never a bare receipt.

### #3: Letter Sent to the Wrong Bureau

A retired nurse mails a pension adjustment request to the labor department instead of the pension system. The clerk forwards the file to the pension system quietly without telling the nurse. Evaluate the clerk's action.

**Solution:**

1. Check the transfer: forwarding the file to the proper agency completes half of Rule VI.
2. Check sender notice: test whether silent forwarding satisfies the receiving office's referral duty.

:::try
Finish the last step: state what duty the clerk missed and what Rule VI requires instead.
:::answer
The answer is **The clerk failed to notify the sender.** Rule VI requires referring a misdirected request to the proper office and informing the sender.

**Check:** Every referral requires both transfer to the proper office and notice to the sender.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Fifteen-day unit: count $15$ working days from receipt, never $15$ calendar days. {#working-days-not-calendar}
- Reply content: state the action taken (and missing-paper details if incomplete), never a bare receipt. {#reply-needs-action-taken}
- Misdirected letters: refer to the proper office and inform the sender. {#refer-and-notify-sender}
:::
