## Core Concept

### Constitutional, National Elective, and Legislative Filers

Submit your SALN to your own agency's human resource office or equivalent. That office forwards the original to the **statutory repository** named by RA $6713$. **Statutory** means stated in the law.

Section $8(A)(1)$ and $8(A)(2)$ assign top national and legislative officials to three distinct offices:

1. **National Office of the Ombudsman**: the President, Vice President, and constitutional officials (such as heads and members of independent commissions).
2. **Secretary of the Senate**: Senators.
3. **Secretary of the House of Representatives**: Members of the House of Representatives.

Match a newly appointed Elections Commissioner and a newly elected district Representative:

1. The Elections Commissioner is a constitutional official, so the SALN goes to the National Office of the Ombudsman.
2. The district Representative belongs to the House, so the SALN goes to the Secretary of the House of Representatives.

:::pair #each-chamber-own-secretary
Right: Senators file with the Secretary of the Senate, while House members file with the Secretary of the House of Representatives.
Wrong: Sending a senator's SALN to the Secretary of the House instead of the Secretary of the Senate.
Match each legislator strictly to their own chamber's secretary.
:::

:::check
The Chairperson of the Commission on Audit and a newly elected Senator submit their SALNs through their personnel offices. Which statutory repository receives each?
:::answer
The answer is **The National Office of the Ombudsman for the Chairperson, and the Secretary of the Senate for the Senator.** The Chairperson is a constitutional official, while Senators file with the Senate Secretary.
:::

### Justices Versus Judges

Section $8(A)(2)$ splits judicial filers into two separate repositories by judicial level:

1. **Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court**: Justices (Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, Sandiganbayan, and Court of Tax Appeals).
2. **Court Administrator**: Judges (Regional Trial Court, Municipal Trial Court, and other trial-court judges).

Route a Court of Appeals justice and a Municipal Trial Court judge:

1. The Court of Appeals justice is a justice, so the Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court receives the SALN.
2. The Municipal Trial Court judge is a judge, so the Court Administrator receives the SALN.

:::why
Why does the law separate justices from trial-court judges? The Supreme Court Clerk tracks appellate justices, while the Office of the Court Administrator supervises lower-court judges across the country.
:::

:::pair #justices-clerk-not-ombudsman
Right: Justices file with the Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court, not with the Ombudsman or the CSC.
Wrong: Sending a Supreme Court justice's SALN to the Office of the Ombudsman as a national constitutional official.
Although justices hold constitutional posts, Section $8(A)(2)$ gives them a specific judicial repository.
:::

:::pair #judges-court-administrator
Right: Trial-court judges file with the Court Administrator, separate from the justices' Clerk of Court.
Wrong: Sending a trial-court judge's SALN to the Office of the Ombudsman instead of the Court Administrator.
Trial-court judges have their own named repository under Section $8(A)(2)$, the Court Administrator.
:::

:::check
A Municipal Trial Court judge submits his annual SALN, and his clerk mails it to the Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court. Did the clerk pick the right statutory repository?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $8(A)(2)$ routes justices to the Supreme Court Clerk, while trial-court judges file with the Court Administrator.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Independent Constitutional Bodies

The Ombudsman and the Chairperson of the Civil Service Commission both submit annual SALNs. Which statutory repository keeps both filings?

**Solution:**

1. Identify their category: both are constitutional officials heading independent constitutional offices.
2. Match Section $8(A)(1)$: constitutional officials file with the National Office of the Ombudsman.

Answer: Both file with the National Office of the Ombudsman.

**Check:** Constitutional officials use the Ombudsman unless a specific legislative or justice rule applies.

### #2: Congress and Appellate Versus Trial Courts

A party-list House member, a Court of Tax Appeals justice, and a Metropolitan Trial Court judge submit their SALNs. Name the repository for each filer.

**Solution:**

1. Party-list House member: members of the House file with the Secretary of the House of Representatives.
2. Court of Tax Appeals justice: justices file with the Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court.
3. Metropolitan Trial Court judge: trial-court judges file with the Court Administrator.

Answer: Secretary of the House of Representatives, Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court, and Court Administrator.

**Check:** Separate House from Senate, and separate justices from trial-court judges.

### #3: Three High National Filers

The President, a Senator, and a Supreme Court associate justice file their sworn financial declarations. Which statutory repository receives each SALN?

**Solution:**

1. Check the President: as a national elective and constitutional officer, Section $8(A)(1)$ assigns the President to the National Office of the Ombudsman.
2. Check the Senator: as a member of the upper chamber, Section $8(A)(2)$ assigns the Senator to the Secretary of the Senate.

:::try
Finish the last step: name the statutory repository for the Supreme Court associate justice and explain why it differs from the President's repository.
:::answer
The answer is **The Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court.** Section $8(A)(2)$ creates a specific judicial repository for justices instead of routing them to the Ombudsman.

**Check:** National elective officials go to the Ombudsman, Senators to the Senate Secretary, and justices to the Supreme Court Clerk.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Legislative chambers: route Senators to the Secretary of the Senate and House members to the House Secretary. {#each-chamber-own-secretary}
- Supreme Court and appellate justices: route them to the Clerk of Court of the Supreme Court, not the Ombudsman. {#justices-clerk-not-ombudsman}
- Trial-court judges: route them to the Court Administrator, not the Ombudsman. {#judges-court-administrator}
:::
