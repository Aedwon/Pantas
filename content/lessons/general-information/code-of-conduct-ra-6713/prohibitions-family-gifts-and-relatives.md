## Core Concept

### Family-Celebration Gifts and Civil Degrees

Rule X, Section $1(f)(2)$ allows gifts from family members or relatives on a **family celebration**. No **pecuniary gain or benefit** (money or financial gain) may be expected.

Section $3(k)$ defines **family** as the spouse and unmarried children under $18$. It defines **relatives** as anyone within the **fourth civil degree** of **consanguinity** (blood) or **affinity** (marriage), plus three named ties.

Count blood civil degrees by stepping up to the shared ancestor, then down:

1. First degree: parents and children.
2. Second degree: grandparents, grandchildren, and siblings ($1$ up, $1$ down).
3. Third degree: aunts, uncles, nieces, and nephews ($2$ up, $1$ down).
4. Fourth degree: first cousins ($2$ up, $2$ down).

Marriage connects an official to the spouse's blood relatives in matching degrees, such as a spouse's parent ($1$st-degree affinity).

Count the degree for an employee's maternal aunt giving a wedding gift with no gain expected:

1. Step up $2$ births to the grandparent and down $1$ to the aunt ($2 + 1 = 3$rd degree).
2. Confirm conditions: $3$rd degree is within the fourth degree, with no financial gain expected.

:::pair #fourth-degree-count
Right: First cousins sit in the fourth civil degree ($2$ steps up, $2$ steps down) and qualify as covered relatives.
Wrong: Excluding a first cousin from the family-celebration exception as outside the fourth civil degree.
Count every birth step up to the shared ancestor and down; fourth degree includes first cousins.
:::

:::check
At a graduation dinner, an official gets a watch from her sister's daughter (her niece), expecting no money. Which degree is a niece, and does it qualify?
:::answer
The answer is **Third degree, and the gift qualifies.** Counting $1$ up to parent, $1$ down to sister, and $1$ down to niece equals $3$.
:::

### Named In-Law Ties Versus Friends

Section $3(k)$ expressly adds three Filipino in-law ties:

1. **Bilas**: a brother-in-law's wife or a sister-in-law's husband.
2. **Inso**: the wife of an elder brother or male cousin.
3. **Balae**: the parent of one's son-in-law or daughter-in-law.

A friend, neighbor, or godparent without a blood degree, marriage degree, or named tie is **not** a relative.

Classify two birthday guests expecting no gain: a sister-in-law's husband and a childhood best friend:

1. Sister-in-law's husband: he is a **bilas**, covered under Section $3(k)$.
2. Childhood friend: friendship carries no statutory degree or named tie, so the exception excludes him.

:::why
Why name bilas, inso, and balae in Section $3(k)$? Degree counting alone would omit these close Filipino extended-family links across two marriages.
:::

:::pair #in-law-named-terms
Right: Section $3(k)$ covers affinity degrees plus bilas, inso (elder brother's or male cousin's wife), and balae (child-in-law's parent).
Wrong: Excluding marriage ties and named in-laws by treating only blood relatives as covered.
Keep all three named in-law terms straight too: balae (child-in-law's parent), inso (elder brother's or male cousin's wife), and bilas (sibling-in-law's spouse).
:::

:::pair #friends-not-relatives
Right: Only statutory family, relatives within the fourth civil degree, and named in-laws qualify under Rule X, Section $1(f)(2)$.
Wrong: Treating family friends and relatives on the same basis simply because the event is a family celebration.
A celebration occasion alone never qualifies a donor; verify the legal relationship first.
:::

:::check
At an anniversary, an employee gets gifts from her elder brother's wife and her college classmate, with no favor expected. Which gift qualifies?
:::answer
The answer is **Only the gift from her elder brother's wife.** She is an inso under Section $3(k)$, while a classmate is not a relative.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Counting Marriage Affinity Degrees

At a baptism, an employee gets a gift from her husband's uncle, expecting no money or favor. Is he a covered relative under Section $3(k)$?

**Solution:**

1. Trace the spouse's blood steps: from husband up $2$ steps to his grandparent and down $1$ to his uncle equals $3$.
2. Apply affinity: marriage puts the husband's uncle in third-degree affinity, inside the fourth degree.

Answer: Yes, he is a third-degree relative by affinity, so the gift qualifies.

**Check:** Marriage mirrors the spouse's blood degrees, and $3$ is within the fourth degree.

### #2: Named In-Law Terms

At a reunion dinner, an employee gets gifts from her brother-in-law's wife and her daughter-in-law's father, expecting no gain. Name each Section $3(k)$ term.

**Solution:**

1. Brother-in-law's wife: Section $3(k)$ names her **bilas**.
2. Daughter-in-law's father: Section $3(k)$ names him **balae**.

Answer: The brother-in-law's wife is a bilas, and the daughter-in-law's father is a balae.

**Check:** Both sit in Section $3(k)$ alongside inso (elder brother's or male cousin's wife).

### #3: Three Anniversary Guests

Three anniversary guests give gifts with no gain expected: the employee's father's first cousin, her husband's sister, and her neighbor. How does Section $3(k)$ treat each guest?

**Solution:**

1. Count steps: compute the civil degree for her father's first cousin and for her husband's sister.
2. Check relationship status: test whether a neighbor has a blood degree, marriage degree, or named tie.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on all three gifts under Section $3(k)$ and Rule X, Section $1(f)(2)$.
:::answer
The answer is **Only the gift from her husband's sister ($2$nd-degree affinity) fits the family-celebration exception.** A spouse's sibling sits $2$ steps away by affinity inside the fourth degree. Her father's first cousin is fifth degree past the limit, and a neighbor is not a relative.

**Check:** Verify fourth civil degree of blood or marriage, or named ties bilas, inso, and balae.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Counting first cousins: count $2$ up and $2$ down to place first cousins inside the fourth degree. {#fourth-degree-count}
- Named in-law links: distinguish balae, inso, and bilas, and include marriage ties beyond blood relatives. {#in-law-named-terms}
- Friends at celebrations: exclude friends and neighbors who lack a fourth-degree or named tie. {#friends-not-relatives}
:::
