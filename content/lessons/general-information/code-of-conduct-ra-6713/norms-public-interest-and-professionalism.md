## Core Concept

### The Eight Norms and Official-Duty Setting

Republic Act No. $6713$ is the **Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees**. It was approved on February $20$, $1989$, and took effect on March $25$, $1989$.

Section $4$(A) names $8$ norms of conduct in statutory order as personal standards for carrying out official duties:

1. Commitment to public interest
2. Professionalism
3. Justness and sincerity
4. Political neutrality
5. Responsiveness to the public
6. Nationalism and patriotism
7. Commitment to democracy
8. Simple living

Section $4$ sets this list for official duties, though some norms add broader wording. Sub-duties like efficiency never add a ninth norm.

Sort three review items against Section $4$(A):

1. Professionalism is the second named norm.
2. Avoiding revenue waste sits inside Commitment to public interest, not as an extra norm.
3. Gardening on Sunday is private life outside official-duty conduct.

:::why
Why memorize the exact count and order? Exams often test how many norms Section $4$(A) names, which norm sits first or second, and which word is only a sub-duty.
:::

:::pair #eight-named-norms
Right: Section $4$(A) names $8$ norms in order, and duties like efficiency sit inside those norms.
Wrong: Counting sub-duties inside Section $4$(A) as extra norms, giving $10$ or $12$ norms.
Count only the $8$ lettered headings from Commitment to public interest through Simple living.
:::

:::check
A reviewer lists "Accountability" and "Economy" as the ninth and tenth norms of Section $4$(A). Are those separate named norms?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $4$(A) names $8$ norms, and accountability and economy are duties inside named norms.
:::

### Commitment to Public Interest

**Commitment to public interest** is the first norm under Section $4$(A)(a). Officials and employees must uphold the public interest over personal interest.

They must use government resources and powers efficiently, effectively, honestly, and economically, especially to avoid wasting public funds and revenues.

Evaluate a division chief comparing two equal supply quotes, where Quote A costs more and comes from her college roommate:

1. Check personal ties: picking Quote A favors a roommate at higher cost to taxpayers.
2. Apply Section $4$(A)(a): choosing the lower-cost bid avoids waste and puts public interest first.

:::pair #public-interest-first
Right: Match public needs over personal gain and waste-free resource use to Commitment to public interest, the first norm.
Wrong: Assigning waste of public funds or resources to Responsiveness to the public instead of Commitment to public interest.
Commitment to public interest opens Section $4$(A) and governs public funds, property, and personal gain.
:::

:::check
A regional director cancels an unnecessary lounge remodel so the agency can buy rural clinic water tanks. Which norm guides that choice?
:::answer
The answer is **Commitment to public interest**. Section $4$(A)(a) directs officials to place public needs ahead of personal convenience and avoid waste.
:::

### Professionalism

**Professionalism** is the second norm under Section $4$(A)(b). Public workers must discharge duties with the highest degree of excellence, professionalism, intelligence, and skill.

They must enter service with utmost devotion and dedication. They must also discourage wrong views that officials dispense **undue patronage** (special favors).

Trace how a records analyst handles a permit backlog when an acquaintance asks for a secret shortcut:

1. Apply technical skill: the analyst verifies every file accurately and clears requests in order.
2. Reject patronage: the analyst refuses the shortcut so public office never looks like a favor counter.

:::pair #professionalism-vs-patronage
Right: Match skill, dedication, and rejecting undue patronage to Professionalism, the second norm.
Wrong: Assigning technical skill and rejecting undue patronage to Political neutrality instead of Professionalism.
Political neutrality bars party bias, while Professionalism pairs technical skill with refusing special favors.
:::

:::check
A hydrologist runs water tests carefully and warns staff never to hint that referrals speed up approvals. Which norm does she model?
:::answer
The answer is **Professionalism**. Section $4$(A)(b) requires skill and dedication while discouraging any perception of undue patronage.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Norm Count and Scope

A reviewer claims Section $4$(A) lists $10$ norms governing every private hobby. Which two corrections fix that claim?

**Solution:**

1. Check the list: Section $4$(A) names $8$ norms from Commitment to public interest through Simple living.
2. Check scope: Section $4$ frames those norms around official duties, though some single norms use broader wording.

Answer: Section $4$(A) names $8$ norms framed around official-duty conduct.

**Check:** Sub-duties like efficiency sit inside the $8$ named norms.

### #2: Public Savings Over Personal Ties

A city engineer needs road gravel. He rejects a classmate's overpriced quote for an equally strong, cheaper bid. Name the norm and its position.

**Solution:**

1. Spot the conflict: the engineer picks taxpayer savings over helping a classmate.
2. Match the norm: putting public interest above personal gain and avoiding waste is Commitment to public interest.

Answer: Commitment to public interest, first in Section $4$(A).

**Check:** Public funds and needs override personal ties.

### #3: Skill and Refusing Favors

A clerk finishes safety audits accurately and tells walk-in friends that personal ties never move files ahead. Which norm covers both acts?

**Solution:**

1. Match both acts: accurate audits show skill and devotion, while refusing shortcuts rejects undue patronage.
2. Rule out Commitment to public interest: these facts do not center on saving public funds.

:::try
Finish the last step: name the norm, state its position in Section $4$(A), and link both acts to it.
:::answer
The answer is **Professionalism, the second norm in Section $4$(A).** Accurate audits show excellence and skill, while refusing shortcuts rejects undue patronage.

**Check:** Match skill, dedication, and rejecting special favors to Professionalism under Section $4$(A)(b).
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Sub-duties like efficiency: count only the $8$ named Section $4$(A) norms. {#eight-named-norms}
- Public funds and personal gain: assign them to Commitment to public interest, the first norm. {#public-interest-first}
- Skill plus rejecting favors: match both to Professionalism, the second norm. {#professionalism-vs-patronage}
:::
