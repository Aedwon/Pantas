## Core Concept

### Justness and Sincerity

**Justness and sincerity** is the third norm under Section $4$(A)(c). Officials and employees must remain true to the people at all times.

They must act fairly, never discriminate against anyone, especially the poor and underprivileged, and respect others' rights. They must also avoid giving undue office favors to relatives.

Evaluate a clerk who speaks politely to wealthy landowners yet ignores an elderly market vendor waiting in line:

1. Check fairness and rights: treating a poor applicant worse than wealthy ones is unfair discrimination.
2. Apply Section $4$(A)(c): remaining true to the people and respecting everyone's rights requires Justness and sincerity.

:::pair #justness-vs-party-bias
Right: Match truth to the people, respect for rights, and fairness toward the poor to Justness and sincerity.
Wrong: Picking Political neutrality when an employee discriminates against poor applicants.
Political neutrality specifically covers party affiliation, while Justness and sincerity governs fairness, rights, and protecting the poor.
:::

:::check
A barangay treasurer turns away an indigent widow's valid burial-aid claim while rushing a wealthy donor's papers. Which norm is violated?
:::answer
The answer is **Justness and sincerity**. Section $4$(A)(c) bans discrimination, especially against the poor and underprivileged, and protects others' rights.
:::

### Political Neutrality

**Political neutrality** is the fourth norm under Section $4$(A)(d). Officials and employees must serve everyone without unfair discrimination, regardless of party affiliation or preference.

Having personal political views outside official duties breaks no rule. What matters is equal public service for supporters of every party or candidate.

Test a municipal permit officer who votes quietly on election day and serves both the mayor's allies and critics equally:

1. Check private belief: voting and holding personal views do not breach Section $4$(A)(d).
2. Check official service: serving allies and critics alike satisfies Political neutrality.

:::why
Why separate private views from official service? Public employees remain citizens with opinions, yet government services belong to every taxpayer regardless of party.
:::

:::pair #political-neutrality-party
Right: Match equal service regardless of political party or preference to Political neutrality.
Wrong: Labeling equal service across party lines as Justness and sincerity instead of Political neutrality.
Look for party affiliation or political preference as the trigger for Political neutrality.
:::

:::check
A licensing clerk rejects an opposition councilor's complete packet while approving an ally's identical packet that morning. Which norm is broken?
:::answer
The answer is **Political neutrality**. Section $4$(A)(d) requires public service without unfair discrimination based on party affiliation or preference.
:::

### Responsiveness to the Public

**Responsiveness to the public** is the fifth norm under Section $4$(A)(e). It requires prompt, courteous, and adequate service to the public.

This norm covers far more than politeness. Officials must explain rules clearly, keep allowed information open, hold consultations when appropriate, invite suggestions, simplify steps, and avoid **red tape** (unnecessary delays).

Review a city housing office posting plain-language checklists online and cutting three duplicate forms down to one:

1. Check clarity: plain-language checklists explain policies and procedures in clear terms.
2. Check speed: dropping duplicate forms simplifies steps and removes red tape under Section $4$(A)(e).

:::pair #responsiveness-beyond-courtesy
Right: Match prompt service, clear rules, open information, public hearings, and cutting red tape to Responsiveness to the public.
Wrong: Assigning prompt, courteous, and adequate public service to Professionalism.
Responsiveness goes beyond politeness to cover service speed, clear rules, open information, hearings, and simpler steps.
:::

:::check
An irrigation agency hosts a farmer hearing on water schedules and posts fee tables in plain Filipino. Which norm is shown?
:::answer
The answer is **Responsiveness to the public**. Section $4$(A)(e) includes clear policy information and public consultations when appropriate.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Poverty Bias Versus Party Bias

A welfare clerk guides well-dressed applicants calmly yet snaps at poor applicants and hides claim rights. Which norm applies, and why not Political neutrality?

**Solution:**

1. Check facts: the clerk shows poverty bias and disrespects rights, with no political party involved.
2. Match norm: Section $4$(A)(c) protects the poor and underprivileged and requires respect for rights.

Answer: Justness and sincerity.

**Check:** Political neutrality governs party affiliation, while Justness and sincerity governs fairness, poverty bias, and rights.

### #2: Private Views Versus Fair Service

A civil clerk wears no campaign colors at work. She processes birth records in order for both the governor's allies and rivals. Does this satisfy Political neutrality?

**Solution:**

1. Check conduct: she serves every applicant in order without favoring or delaying any political group.
2. Apply Section $4$(A)(d): Political neutrality requires fair service regardless of party affiliation or preference.

Answer: Yes, equal service across party lines fulfills Political neutrality.

**Check:** Private political views stay lawful when official service stays fair.

### #3: Clear Steps, Hearings, and Cutting Red Tape

A transport board plans new jeepney rules. It hosts a public consultation, posts a plain-language guide, and drops two redundant stamps so permits issue faster. Which norm fits?

**Solution:**

1. Separate the board's actions: list the steps it took on rules, public input, and permit stamps.
2. Test scope: check whether those actions fit politeness alone or broader duties under Section $4$(A).

:::try
Finish the last step: name the Section $4$(A) norm and state two duties beyond courtesy shown by the board.
:::answer
The answer is **Responsiveness to the public.** Beyond courtesy, the board gives clear information, holds an appropriate consultation, and simplifies procedures to avoid red tape.

**Check:** Section $4$(A)(e) pairs prompt, courteous service with clear rules, open information, hearings, and simpler steps.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Poverty discrimination: match unfair treatment of the poor to Justness and sincerity instead of Political neutrality. {#justness-vs-party-bias}
- Party-based service: assign equal service across party lines to Political neutrality. {#political-neutrality-party}
- Politeness versus full responsiveness: include clear rules, open records, hearings, and cutting red tape under Responsiveness to the public. {#responsiveness-beyond-courtesy}
:::
