## Core Concept

Section $11$ of RA $6713$ gives penalties for Code violations. Section $12$ assigns enforcement duties. First check what happened and which rule applies.

### Salary-Based Fine and Suspension Ceilings

**Administrative discipline** decides whether a public worker broke a rule and deserves a penalty.

Under Section $11(a)$, a general violation carries a fine up to $6$ months' salary or suspension up to $1$ year. **Suspension** keeps the worker off duty and pay.

To find the fine ceiling, multiply monthly salary by $6$:

$$
\text{Fine ceiling} = 6 \times \text{monthly salary}
$$

Apply Section $11(a)$ to a licensing clerk earning ₱$24{,}000$ monthly:

1. Multiply monthly salary by $6$ for the fine limit.

$$
6 \times 24{,}000 = 144{,}000
$$

2. Match suspension to time: the suspension ceiling is $1$ year.

Result: The clerk faces up to ₱$144{,}000$ in fines or $1$ year of suspension.

:::why
Why base the fine on months of salary instead of one flat peso amount? A salary multiple scales fairness across junior clerks and senior officials.
:::

:::pair #salary-fine-ceiling
Right: Cap the general administrative fine under Section $11(a)$ at $6$ months' salary.
Wrong: Setting the general fine ceiling at $12$ months' salary by copying the $1$-year suspension period.
Check each penalty's unit: $6$ months measures the salary fine, whereas $1$ year measures suspension.
:::

:::pair #suspension-ceiling-one-year
Right: Cap general administrative suspension under Section $11(a)$ at $1$ year.
Wrong: Limiting suspension to $6$ months by applying the salary-fine number to time off work.
Keep the two limits separate: $6$ months measures salary fines, and $1$ year measures suspension.
:::

:::check
A budget analyst earns ₱$18{,}500$ a month and breaks a general Code rule. What are the Section $11(a)$ fine and suspension ceilings?
:::answer
The answer is **₱$111{,}000$ fine ceiling and $1$ year suspension**. Multiply ₱$18{,}500$ by $6$ for ₱$111{,}000$, and keep suspension capped at $1$ year.
:::

### Gravity, Ceilings, and Removal

Section $11(a)$ also permits **removal** from office depending on the offense's gravity.

A maximum is a ceiling, not an automatic sanction. The proper body weighs how serious the violation is.

**Due notice and hearing** must precede any fine, suspension, or removal. Notice states the charge, and a hearing lets the worker present evidence.

Check a minor first-time discourtesy lapse by an officer earning ₱$40{,}000$ monthly:

1. Check process: serve notice and hold a fair hearing before picking any sanction.
2. Weigh gravity: a minor lapse need not draw removal or the full ₱$240{,}000$ ceiling.

Result: The agency follows fair process and selects a proportional penalty.

:::pair #removal-depends-on-gravity
Right: Removal under Section $11(a)$ depends on the offense's gravity after due notice and hearing.
Wrong: Ordering automatic removal upon any complaint without examining the evidence.
A complaint starts an inquiry; it never justifies removal without proof, notice, and hearing.
:::

:::check
A supervisor reads an unverified complaint against a tax examiner earning ₱$45{,}000$ monthly. Can the supervisor remove the examiner immediately without a hearing?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $11(a)$ requires due notice, a hearing, and an assessment of gravity before removal.
:::

## Examples

### #1: General Violation and Salary-Based Fine

After notice and a fair chance to answer, an employee is found liable for a general Code violation. Monthly salary is ₱$30{,}000$. Find the fine ceiling, suspension ceiling, and removal rule.

**Solution:**

1. Multiply monthly salary by $6$ for the fine ceiling.

$$
6 \times 30{,}000 = 180{,}000
$$

2. State suspension: suspension may reach $1$ year.
3. Check removal: removal depends on gravity after required process.

Answer: The fine ceiling is ₱$180{,}000$, suspension may reach $1$ year, and removal depends on gravity.

**Check:** Divide ₱$180{,}000$ by $6$ to confirm $1$ month's salary of ₱$30{,}000$.

### #2: Choosing a Penalty Below the Ceiling

A records clerk earning ₱$22{,}000$ misses an internal filing step once with no bad faith. After notice and hearing, a board member demands a $1$-year suspension and ₱$132{,}000$ fine as mandatory. Is that required?

**Solution:**

1. Check ceilings: $6$ months' salary (₱$132{,}000$) and $1$ year are maximums, not fixed minimums.
2. Apply gravity: the body selects a sanction matching the minor lapse.

Answer: No, ceilings are maximums, so the board may impose a lighter sanction.

**Check:** Match the sanction to gravity rather than treating ceilings as automatic.

### #3: Heavy Fine Claim Before a Hearing

A municipal assessor earning ₱$36{,}000$ monthly faces a new conflict complaint. Before notice or hearing, a unit head demands immediate removal and a $12$-month fine of ₱$432{,}000$. Check the process and Section $11(a)$ limits.

**Solution:**

1. Check due process: test whether a complaint allows removal before notice and hearing.
2. Check the fine: multiply ₱$36{,}000$ by the Section $11(a)$ multiplier to test ₱$432{,}000$.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether immediate removal is lawful and give the true Section $11(a)$ fine ceiling.
:::answer
The answer is **Immediate removal is unlawful, and the fine ceiling is ₱$216{,}000$**. Notice and hearing must come first, and $6 \times 36{,}000 = 216{,}000$.

**Check:** Confirm $6$ months' salary equals half of $12$ months' pay ($432{,}000 \div 2 = 216{,}000$).
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- General fine ceiling: multiply monthly salary by $6$, never $12$. {#salary-fine-ceiling}
- Suspension limit: cap suspension at $1$ year, separate from the $6$-month salary fine. {#suspension-ceiling-one-year}
- Immediate removal: weigh gravity after due notice and hearing instead of acting on a bare complaint. {#removal-depends-on-gravity}
:::
