## Core Concept

### Two Required Conditions for Private Practice

**Private professional practice** means serving private clients in a profession like law, medicine, accounting, or engineering. Under Section $7(b)(2)$, public workers may not practice privately during service unless two conditions both hold.

First, the practice needs legal authority. RA $6713$ requires authority under the Constitution or law. The **implementing rules** (Rule X, Section $1(c)$, explaining how to apply the Act) also refer to regulation.

Second, the practice must not **conflict**, or **tend to conflict**, with official functions. A conflict arises when private client work pulls against official duties.

Test a university accounting professor with statutory practice authority who is hired by a textbook vendor bidding before her university:

1. Check legal authority: the professor holds statutory authority to practice privately.
2. Check official conflict: advising a bidder before her university conflicts with her official role, barring the client.

:::why
Why require both authority and zero conflict? Legal authority permits outside practice only for unrelated clients, never for work that compromises public duty.
:::

:::pair #permission-alone-insufficient
Right: Legal authority and absence of conflict (or tendency to conflict) are both required under Section $7(b)(2)$.
Wrong: Thinking legal authorization removes every conflict restriction and allows conflicting private clients.
Even clear constitutional or statutory permission never allows private work that conflicts or tends to conflict with official duties.
:::

:::check
A municipal doctor holds legal authority for an evening clinic and treats patients only from a distant town with no local cases. Does that meet Section $7(b)(2)$?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. She has legal authority, and treating outside patients neither conflicts nor tends to conflict with municipal duties.
:::

### Timing and Nonpayment Never Cure a Breach

Two common excuses fail Section $7(b)(2)$ completely on exams. Check both carefully.

Working **after office hours** avoids schedule overlap. Still, evening or weekend timing never supplies missing legal authority and never erases a duty conflict.

Serving **without cash payment** avoids a money fee. Still, unpaid or pro bono work violates Section $7(b)(2)$ whenever legal authority is missing or duties conflict.

Examine an agency lawyer without practice authority who drafts a contract free on Saturday for a firm seeking an agency license:

1. Check scheduling and pay: Saturday work without a fee saves office time and involves no cash.
2. Apply Section $7(b)(2)$: lacking legal authority and aiding a license applicant both bar the work anyway.

:::pair #after-hours-still-barred
Right: Off-duty timing never cures missing legal authority or an official conflict under Section $7(b)(2)$.
Wrong: Treating work done outside government hours as automatically allowed without checking authority or conflicts.
Always test legal authority and duty conflicts even when the private work happens at night or on weekends.
:::

:::pair #unpaid-still-barred
Right: Free private work still requires legal authority and freedom from official conflict under Section $7(b)(2)$.
Wrong: Treating absence of cash payment as legal permission for unauthorized or conflicting private practice.
Unpaid work can still pull against official duties and never replaces constitutional, statutory, or regulatory authorization.
:::

:::check
A city engineer without legal authority for private practice draws free house plans on Sunday for a neighbor. Is that allowed under Section $7(b)(2)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Sunday scheduling and zero pay never substitute for required legal authorization.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Authorized Practice That Tends to Conflict

A government auditor holds legal permission for private bookkeeping. A builder currently audited by her regional team asks her to prepare its year-end statements. May she accept?

**Solution:**

1. Check authority: the auditor holds legal permission for private practice.
2. Check conflict: preparing statements for a firm her team audits creates an official conflict, failing the second condition.

Answer: No, the auditor may not accept the engagement.

**Check:** Confirm both conditions pass separately; failing either one makes the practice prohibited.

### #2: Unpaid Evening Work Without Authority

A state prosecutor lacks constitutional, statutory, or regulatory permission to practice privately. On evenings, he writes free pleadings for a cousin's civil land dispute outside his jurisdiction. Does Section $7(b)(2)$ allow it?

**Solution:**

1. Check conflict: the cousin's civil land dispute has no link to prosecutions in his office.
2. Check authority: neither evening timing nor free service supplies the missing legal authorization.

Answer: No, Section $7(b)(2)$ prohibits private practice without legal authority.

**Check:** Evening hours and zero fee never cure a lack of legal authority.

### #3: Authority, Scheduling, and Pay Together

A state university professor has statutory permission to consult privately. A bridge contractor asks him for an unpaid Saturday review of a bid his own university committee will grade next week. How does Section $7(b)(2)$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Check legal authority: the professor holds statutory permission to consult privately.
2. Check timing and pay: Saturday work for free never shields a conflict with his committee duty.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the Saturday review under Section $7(b)(2)$ and explain why each excuse fails.
:::answer
The answer is **The Saturday review is prohibited.** The professor has permission, yet grading-committee conflict bars the bid review. Saturday hours and zero pay never cure that conflict.

**Check:** Require both legal authorization and zero actual or potential conflict regardless of hours or pay.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Permission with conflict: require both legal authority and zero actual or potential conflict. {#permission-alone-insufficient}
- After-hours practice: test authority and conflict even for evening or weekend work. {#after-hours-still-barred}
- Unpaid private service: never treat free work as replacing legal authority or curing a conflict. {#unpaid-still-barred}
:::
