## Core Concept

### Fourth Civil Degree by Blood

Section $8$(B) of RA $6713$ requires officials and employees to disclose relatives in government **to the best of their knowledge and information**. The Civil Service Commission (CSC) sets the form, method, and frequency.

Disclosure covers relatives within the **fourth civil degree** of **consanguinity** (blood) or **affinity** (marriage).

Count $1$ degree per parent-child birth step. For side branches, count up to the shared ancestor, then count down:

1. **First degree**: parents and children ($1$ step).
2. **Second degree**: grandparents, grandchildren, and siblings ($1$ up, $1$ down).
3. **Third degree**: aunts, uncles, nieces, and nephews ($2$ up, $1$ down).
4. **Fourth degree**: first cousins ($2$ up, $2$ down), great-aunts, great-uncles, grandnephews, and grandnieces ($3$ up, $1$ down).

Trace a customs examiner's aunt at the health department and her mother's first cousin at town hall:

1. Aunt (mother's sister): $2$ steps up to grandparent plus $1$ down equals $3$rd degree, so disclose her.
2. Mother's first cousin: $3$ up to great-grandparent plus $2$ down equals $5$th degree, past the limit.

:::pair #fourth-degree-cap
Right: Disclose known relatives in government up through the fourth civil degree, including first cousins.
Wrong: Stopping disclosure at the third civil degree and leaving out first cousins.
First cousins sit $4$ steps away ($2$ up, $2$ down), inside the fourth degree.
:::

:::check
A teacher knows his sister's son works as a clerk at the Land Transportation Office. Count the degree and state whether he must disclose him.
:::answer
The answer is **Third degree, and yes, disclose him.** Count $1$ up to parent, $1$ down to sister, and $1$ down to the nephew for $3$ steps.
:::

### Affinity and Named In-Law Ties

**Affinity** means relationship by marriage. Take your spouse's blood degree directly without adding a step for marriage.

So a spouse's parent is **first-degree** affinity, and a spouse's sibling is **second-degree** affinity.

Section $3$(k) also expressly names three Filipino in-law ties:

- **Bilas**: brother-in-law's wife or sister-in-law's husband.
- **Inso**: wife of an elder brother or male cousin.
- **Balae**: parent of one's son-in-law or daughter-in-law.

Friends, neighbors, and godparents lacking a statutory degree or named tie are excluded.

Check a revenue officer's husband's sister (town treasurer) and her daughter-in-law's mother (barangay secretary):

1. Husband's sister: $1$ up and $1$ down on the husband's side equals $2$nd-degree affinity, so disclose her.
2. Daughter-in-law's mother: Section $3$(k) names her **balae**, so disclose her too.

:::why
Why does marriage add zero extra steps? In civil law, marriage places you in your spouse's exact step position.
:::

:::pair #affinity-uses-spouse-degree
Right: Count affinity by taking your spouse's blood degree directly without adding a step for marriage.
Wrong: Adding an extra degree for marriage so a spouse's parent becomes second-degree affinity.
Your spouse's parent is $1$ step from your spouse, making them first-degree affinity.
:::

:::pair #marriage-relatives-included
Right: Disclose relatives in government by both blood and marriage, plus bilas, inso, and balae.
Wrong: Leaving out a spouse's relatives on the ground that only blood relatives count.
Section $8$(B) covers both consanguinity and affinity up to the fourth degree.
:::

:::check
A city engineer's elder brother's wife teaches at a public high school, and her husband's first cousin works at a provincial hospital. Must the engineer disclose both?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes, disclose both.** Her elder brother's wife is an inso, and her husband's first cousin is fourth-degree affinity.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Side Branches Beyond First Cousins

A budget officer knows his grandniece works at the treasury and his second cousin works at a regional court. Which one falls inside the fourth degree?

**Solution:**

1. Grandniece (sister's granddaughter): $1$ up to parent, $1$ down to sister, $1$ down to niece, and $1$ down to grandniece equals $4$ steps.
2. Second cousin: $3$ up to great-grandparents plus $3$ down equals $6$ steps, beyond the limit.

Answer: Disclose the grandniece ($4$th degree), and exclude the second cousin ($6$th degree).

**Check:** Count every birth step up to the shared ancestor and back down.

### #2: Counting Affinity and Bilas

An auditor knows her husband's uncle sits on a town council and her sister-in-law's husband works at a public port. Must she disclose both?

**Solution:**

1. Husband's uncle: $2$ steps up to his grandparent plus $1$ down equals $3$rd-degree affinity.
2. Sister-in-law's husband: Section $3$(k) names him **bilas**.

Answer: Yes, disclose both on the SALN.

**Check:** Affinity mirrors the spouse's blood steps, and bilas is covered by Section $3$(k).

### #3: Sorting Four Government Contacts

A clerk knows four government workers: her elder brother's wife, her husband's grandmother, her father's first cousin, and her childhood godfather. Which ones must she disclose?

**Solution:**

1. Father's first cousin: $3$ up to great-grandparents plus $2$ down equals $5$th degree, past the fourth degree.
2. Childhood godfather: sponsorship creates no blood, affinity, or named tie.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the remaining two contacts and state the full list to disclose.
:::answer
The answer is **Disclose only her elder brother's wife (inso) and her husband's grandmother ($2$nd-degree affinity).** An elder brother's wife is an inso, and a spouse's grandmother sits $2$ steps away by affinity.

**Check:** Verify blood steps, spouse steps without adding for marriage, and the three named ties.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Blood degree cap: include first cousins at the fourth civil degree ($2$ up, $2$ down). {#fourth-degree-cap}
- Affinity counting: mirror the spouse's blood degree directly without adding a step for marriage. {#affinity-uses-spouse-degree}
- Marriage ties: disclose affinity relatives within four degrees plus bilas, inso, and balae. {#marriage-relatives-included}
:::
