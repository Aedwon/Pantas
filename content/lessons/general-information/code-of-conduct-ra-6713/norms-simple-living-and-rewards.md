## Core Concept

### Simple Living

**Simple living** is the eighth and final norm under Section $4$(A)(h). Public officials, employees, and their families must lead modest lives appropriate to their positions and income.

They must not indulge in **extravagant** (excessively costly) or **ostentatious** (show-off) display of wealth in any form. Modesty matches position and income instead of forcing identical lifestyles, yet high pay never excuses flashy luxury.

Evaluate a city assessor whose spouse posts weekly luxury yacht parties online while earning a modest salary:

1. Check who is covered: Section $4$(A)(h) expressly covers both officials and their families.
2. Check the standard: showing off luxury beyond position and income violates Simple living.

:::pair #simple-living-families
Right: Simple living binds officials, employees, and their families, and bars flashy wealth displays at any salary level.
Wrong: Limiting simple living to elected officials alone while excluding appointed employees and family members.
Section $4$(A)(h) expressly covers families too, and high pay never permits extravagant display.
:::

:::pair #simple-living-modest-not-equal
Right: Simple living calls for modest living suited to position and income, not identical incomes for everyone.
Wrong: Claiming simple living requires every household to have the exact same income and lifestyle.
Section $4$(A)(h) measures modesty against position and income, not uniform wealth across households.
:::

:::check
A bureau director earns a senior salary, yet arrives at social galas in an armored convoy of imported sports cars for show. Does that violate Simple living?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Even with a higher salary, Section $4$(A)(h) forbids extravagant or ostentatious display of wealth in any form.
:::

### Section $6$: Incentives and Rewards

Section $6$ creates an **annual** system of incentives and rewards for outstanding public officials and employees. A **Committee on Awards** reviews performance and selects honorees using statutory standards.

Possible awards include bonuses, citations, **GOCC** (government-owned or controlled corporation) directorships, scholarships, paid vacations, and **automatic promotion**.

Automatic promotion applies only if the employee meets the **legal conditions** for that higher post. Nomination alone never skips statutory promotion rules.

Review a clerk nominated for an annual Section $6$ award who holds sub-professional eligibility while the next post requires professional eligibility:

1. Check eligible awards: the Committee on Awards may grant a citation, bonus, scholarship, or paid vacation.
2. Check promotion: automatic promotion cannot occur until she meets the legal qualification rules.

:::why
Why put awards alongside penalties in RA $6713$? Section $6$ encourages public workers to keep high ethical standards by honoring steady service every year.
:::

:::pair #rewards-conditions-and-types
Right: Section $6$ gives annual awards in varied forms, and automatic promotion requires meeting legal conditions.
Wrong: Claiming Section $6$ guarantees automatic promotion to every nominee without checking legal conditions.
Non-cash awards like scholarships and citations apply too, while promotion still follows statutory qualification rules.
:::

:::check
A public librarian wins a Section $6$ citation for $15$ years of honest service. May the Committee on Awards grant her a graduate scholarship instead of cash?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Section $6$ authorizes non-cash recognition such as scholarship grants, citations, and paid vacations alongside bonuses.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Family Coverage and Modest Living

A customs examiner earns a mid-level salary and rents a plain flat. Meanwhile, her spouse buys designer watches and hosts lavish parties on public social media. Why does the spouse's conduct matter?

**Solution:**

1. Identify the issue: lavish public display clashes with modest living suited to the examiner's position and income.
2. Apply Section $4$(A)(h): Simple living covers officials, employees, and their families.

Answer: Simple living applies to the family's ostentatious display of wealth.

**Check:** Verify whether the lifestyle fits position and income and avoids flashy luxury across the family.

### #2: Awards Without Unconditional Promotion

An aide is nominated for the annual Section $6$ program. She expects automatic promotion to division chief and assumes scholarships are unavailable. Which two points correct her view?

**Solution:**

1. Check promotion rules: automatic promotion under Section $6$ requires meeting all legal qualifications for the higher position.
2. Check award forms: Section $6$ expressly lists scholarships, citations, paid vacations, bonuses, and GOCC directorships.

Answer: Promotion still depends on legal conditions, and scholarships are included among Section $6$ awards.

**Check:** Section $6$ recognizes ethical service annually, never after guilt in a disciplinary case and never by skipping promotion rules.

### #3: Simple Living and Section $6$ Recognition

A budget officer lives modestly within her salary for $10$ clean audit years. Her agency nominates her for the annual RA $6713$ awards, proposing a paid vacation or promotion if qualified. Which two parts of RA $6713$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Match the lifestyle rule: modest living suited to position and income follows Simple living, the eighth norm under Section $4$(A)(h).
2. Match the awards program: annual review by the Committee on Awards falls under Section $6$.

:::try
Finish the last step: state both applicable provisions and explain what rule governs the proposed promotion.
:::answer
The answer is **Section $4$(A)(h) Simple living and Section $6$ Incentives and Rewards.** Her modest life meets Section $4$(A)(h). Section $6$ allows a paid vacation and grants automatic promotion only when she meets all legal promotion conditions.

**Check:** Link modest family living to Section $4$(A)(h) and annual awards with legal promotion checks to Section $6$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Family scope and high pay: bind families to Simple living and ban flashy luxury at every salary level. {#simple-living-families}
- Modest versus identical living: measure Simple living against position and income, not equal household wealth. {#simple-living-modest-not-equal}
- Section $6$ awards and promotions: include non-cash awards and enforce legal qualifications before automatic promotion. {#rewards-conditions-and-types}
:::
