## Core Concept

### Annual Performance Reports

Under Section $5(b)$ of RA $6713$, heads or responsible officers of every office, agency, or government corporation must render an **annual performance report**.

The report covers the office's work for the year. It is due within **$45$ working days** from the end of the year.

Once submitted, the report must stay open to the public for inspection during **regular office hours**.

Apply Section $5(b)$ when a water agency submits its report $40$ working days after December $31$, then locks it in a cabinet all afternoon:

1. Check the deadline: $40$ working days sits inside the $45$-working-day limit.
2. Check inspection: hiding the report during afternoon office hours breaks Section $5(b)$.

Result: Filing was timely, yet blocking regular-hours inspection violates Section $5(b)$.

:::why
Why keep the annual report open during regular office hours? Citizens can check how agencies spent funds only when yearly results stay accessible.
:::

:::pair #forty-five-working-days-report
Right: Heads or responsible officers must render the annual performance report within $45$ working days from year-end.
Wrong: Using the $15$-working-day letter deadline as the due date for the annual report.
Section $5(a)$ sets $15$ working days for letters, while Section $5(b)$ gives $45$ working days for annual reports.
:::

:::check
A provincial hospital submits its annual performance report $52$ working days after year-end, citing heavy patient loads. Does that meet Section $5(b)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $5(b)$ requires heads or responsible officers to submit the annual performance report within $45$ working days from year-end.
:::

### Prompt Service at the Counter

Under Section $5(d)$ of RA $6713$, public officials and employees must attend promptly and expeditiously to everyone seeking office services.

Counter visitors appearing in person should never be turned away or delayed without good cause.

Section $5(a)$'s $15$-working-day period applies to written letters. It never lets counter staff postpone a routine walk-in transaction.

Test Section $5(d)$ when an idle clerk sees a retiree at $10$:$00$ a.m. with complete tax-clearance papers and tells him to return next Monday:

1. Check the service mode: the retiree appeared in person with complete papers.
2. Apply Section $5(d)$: delaying an unbusy walk-in request violates prompt counter service.

Result: The clerk must attend to the retiree promptly instead of postponing the visit.

:::pair #counter-prompt-not-fifteen-days
Right: Counter staff must attend promptly to visitors in person without citing the $15$-working-day letter rule.
Wrong: Using the $15$-working-day written-reply deadline to postpone routine counter service.
Section $5(a)$ covers written letters, while Section $5(d)$ requires prompt service at the counter.
:::

:::check
At $2$:$00$ p.m., a student brings valid ID and payment for a transcript at an empty window. The clerk tells her to return in $10$ working days. Is that proper?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Section $5(d)$, counter staff must serve walk-in visitors promptly instead of applying letter periods.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Late Report With Open Lobby Copy

A trade office files its annual performance report $50$ working days after year-end. From day $51$ onward, copies sit in the lobby from $8$:$00$ a.m. to $5$:$00$ p.m. Did the office meet Section $5(b)$?

**Solution:**

1. Check the deadline: Section $5(b)$ sets a $45$-working-day limit from year-end, so day $50$ is late.
2. Check public access: open lobby copies during office hours meet the inspection duty.

Answer: No, day $50$ missed the $45$-working-day deadline even though inspection access was proper.

**Check:** Both the $45$-working-day deadline and regular-hours public access are required.

### #2: Refused Inspection During Office Hours

A city engineering office files its annual report $38$ working days after December $31$. On Wednesday afternoon, a clerk tells a taxpayer that only councilors may read it. Which Section $5(b)$ duty failed?

**Solution:**

1. Check timing: $38$ working days sits inside the $45$-working-day limit.
2. Check inspection: Section $5(b)$ requires keeping the report open to the public during regular office hours.

Answer: The office violated Section $5(b)$'s public-inspection duty by restricting access to councilors.

**Check:** Annual reports stay open to the general public during regular office hours.

### #3: Counter Delay and Locked Annual Report

A municipal treasurer submits the annual report $42$ working days after year-end, yet locks it away from public inspection. That morning, a counter clerk tells a walk-in vendor with complete papers to return next month because letters get $15$ working days. Rule on both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check the report: $42$ working days is timely, yet locking the report breaks Section $5(b)$'s inspection duty.
2. Check the counter clerk: test if Section $5(a)$'s $15$-day rule excuses delaying a walk-in vendor.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the locked report and the counter clerk's excuse under Section $5$.
:::answer
The answer is **Both actions violate Section $5$.** Locking the report breaks Section $5(b)$'s inspection rule, and delaying the vendor breaks Section $5(d)$'s prompt counter service duty.

**Check:** Match Section $5(b)$ to the $45$-working-day open report and Section $5(d)$ to prompt counter service.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Annual performance report deadline: submit within $45$ working days from year-end, not $15$ working days. {#forty-five-working-days-report}
- Counter service delays: attend promptly in person under Section $5(d)$ instead of citing the $15$-day letter period. {#counter-prompt-not-fifteen-days}
:::
