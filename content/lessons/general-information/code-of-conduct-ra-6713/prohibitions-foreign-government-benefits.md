## Core Concept

### Foreign Souvenirs, Study Grants, and Medical Treatment

Section $7(d)$ sets three distinct rules for benefits offered by a **foreign government**. Keep each category's conditions separate.

First, a worker may accept and keep a foreign-government gift of **nominal value** received as a **souvenir** or mark of **courtesy**. Here, nominal value is mandatory, so a luxury watch fails even if called a souvenir.

Second, Section $7(d)$ separately allows a **scholarship**, **fellowship grant** (a grant for study, training, or research), or **medical treatment** from a foreign government. Because this group sits apart from souvenirs, these three benefits need not be nominal.

Examine a foreign health ministry offering a public hospital doctor $6$ months of cancer-research training abroad under a named fellowship:

1. Check category: research training is a **fellowship grant**, listed separately from courtesy souvenirs.
2. Apply Section $7(d)$: the grant qualifies without shrinking down to nominal souvenir value.

:::pair #souvenir-vs-fellowship
Right: Foreign-government scholarships, fellowships, and medical treatment are listed separately and need not be nominal souvenirs.
Wrong: Requiring foreign-government scholarships or fellowships to meet the nominal courtesy-souvenir cap.
Nominal courtesy souvenirs stand apart from scholarships, fellowships, and medical treatment under Section $7(d)$.
:::

:::check
A foreign embassy funds specialized eye surgery abroad for a public teacher under an official bilateral health program. Must she refuse because costs exceed nominal value?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Foreign-government medical treatment is separately allowed under Section $7(d)$ and needs no nominal value.
:::

### Foreign-Government Travel Conditions

Third, Section $7(d)$ allows foreign travel grants or expenses above nominal value, covering allowances, transport, food, and lodging, when **all three conditions** hold:

1. Travel takes place **entirely outside the Philippines**.
2. Acceptance is **appropriate or consistent with Philippine interests**.
3. Acceptance is **permitted by the head of office, branch, or agency**.

Reporting or disclosure rules may still apply. Educational, scientific, and cultural exchanges also remain subject to **national-security requirements**.

Test a foreign government inviting a trade analyst to Seoul plus a $2$-day workshop in Davao, approved by her agency head:

1. Check interests and permission: Philippine interests and agency-head permission are both present.
2. Check location: a domestic stop in Davao breaks the entirely-outside-the-Philippines rule and defeats the travel exception.

:::why
Why require foreign-only routes and office-head approval? Keeping trips abroad prevents foreign funding of domestic travel, while head review protects Philippine interests and security.
:::

:::pair #travel-entirely-abroad
Right: Section $7(d)$'s travel exception covers foreign-government grants only for travel entirely outside the Philippines.
Wrong: Allowing foreign-funded travel inside the Philippines simply because the agency head approves it.
Confirm both a foreign-government donor and a route with zero domestic Philippine legs.
:::

:::pair #travel-needs-permission
Right: Foreign-government travel abroad requires explicit permission from the head of the worker's office, branch, or agency.
Wrong: Accepting foreign-government travel abroad without permission from the head of office, branch, or agency.
Even when foreign-only location and Philippine interests match, missing office-head permission defeats the travel exception.
:::

:::check
A foreign ministry offers airfare and hotel for a $4$-day climate meeting in Bangkok fitting Philippine policy, yet the officer leaves without agency-head permission. Does that qualify?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Foreign-government travel above nominal value requires permission from the head of office, branch, or agency first.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Nominal Souvenir Versus Watch

During a delegation visit, a foreign official gives a bureau chief a plain brass desk pen as a courtesy souvenir. Another host offers a luxury Swiss watch as a "personal souvenir." Which meets Section $7(d)$?

**Solution:**

1. Check the pen: given by a foreign government as a nominal courtesy souvenir, it fits Section $7(d)$.
2. Check the watch: calling an expensive watch a souvenir never meets the nominal-value rule.

Answer: Only the nominal brass pen fits the foreign courtesy souvenir exception.

**Check:** Foreign courtesy souvenirs always require nominal value.

### #2: Private Contractor Versus Government Grant

A private overseas contractor seeking a port contract offers airfare and hotel for a port official to tour Busan. Even with the port head's approval, does Section $7(d)$ cover that trip?

**Solution:**

1. Check donor: a private contractor is not a foreign government.
2. Check conflict: contractor-funded travel falls outside the foreign-government travel rule.

Answer: No, Section $7(d)$'s travel exception covers only foreign-government grants, never private contractors.

**Check:** Confirm the donor is a foreign government before testing location, Philippine interests, and permission.

### #3: Two Foreign Offers Together

A foreign ministry awards a meteorologist a $1$-year graduate scholarship abroad covering tuition and living costs. It also invites her deputy director to a Geneva summit fitting Philippine interests, with explicit approval from her agency head. Rule on both offers.

**Solution:**

1. Check scholarship: foreign scholarships sit in their own listed group with fellowships and treatment, without a nominal cap.
2. Check Geneva trip: travel stays entirely outside the Philippines, fits Philippine interests, and carries agency-head permission.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the scholarship and the approved Geneva trip under Section $7(d)$, noting remaining duties.
:::answer
The answer is **Both the scholarship and the Geneva travel grant qualify under Section $7(d)$.** Scholarships need not be nominal. The Geneva trip satisfies all three travel conditions, while remaining subject to reporting and national-security rules.

**Check:** Separate study grants from nominal souvenirs, and confirm foreign location, Philippine interests, and office-head permission for travel.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Study and treatment grants: keep foreign scholarships, fellowships, and medical treatment separate from nominal souvenirs. {#souvenir-vs-fellowship}
- Donor and route for travel: limit Section $7(d)$ travel grants to foreign-government travel entirely outside the Philippines. {#travel-entirely-abroad}
- Missing approval: require permission from the head of office, branch, or agency before taking foreign travel grants. {#travel-needs-permission}
:::
