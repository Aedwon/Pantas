## Core Concept

### Red Tape Versus Fixing

Under **RA $9485$, Section $4$**, amended by **RA $11032$**, **red tape** means ineffective procedures causing poor service.

A **fixer** uses access to workers to speed a transaction for money or benefit.

Delay alone does not prove a payoff. Red tape shows broken workflow, while fixing needs proof of a demand or benefit. Lacking payoff proof never proves effective service either.

Test Section $4$ when a desk uses $5$ duplicate stamps for $10$ days with no payment demand:

1. Payoff: no worker or broker sought money.
2. Cause: duplicate stamps show ineffective procedures.

Result: The delay shows red tape, not proved fixing or bribery.

:::pair #delay-not-payoff
Right: Slow service points to red tape unless evidence shows a demand for money or benefit.
Wrong: Treating repeated delays alone as proof that an employee took money from a fixer.
Broken workflow causes delays alone, while corruption needs proof of an improper exchange.
:::

:::pair #red-tape-not-fixing
Right: Red tape is an ineffective procedure, while fixing uses access to workers for a paid advantage.
Wrong: Calling ineffective procedures fixing even without access used to secure a paid advantage.
Confusing them blurs bad rules with illegal brokering.
:::

:::check
An office loses folders twice and takes $3$ weeks on a clearance, with no bribe demand or broker. What does this show?
:::answer
The answer is **Red tape without a proved payoff**. Repeated delays show poor procedures, while fixing or bribery lacks evidence.
:::

### The $3$-$7$-$20$ Working-Day Limits

Under **RA $9485$, Section $9$**, amended by **RA $11032$**, general limits depend on transaction type:

1. **$3$ working days** for **simple** requests.
2. **$7$ working days** for **complex** requests.
3. **$20$ working days** for **highly technical** requests.

The clock starts once requirements are **complete** and **applicable fees** are paid. Fee-free requests still have deadlines once complete.

One **same-length extension** needs three conditions. It must appear in the **Citizen's Charter**, with written notice **before the deadline** stating the reason and release date. The applicant signs proof of notice.

Specified **disasters or system failures** pause the clock. **Local council** items get **$45$ working days**, extendible by **$20$ working days**.

Trace a technical permit filed complete with fee paid Tuesday:

1. Start: complete papers and payment start the $20$-day clock.
2. Extension: adding $20$ days needs Charter listing, prior written notice, and signature.

Result: A properly noticed extension is lawful.

:::why
Why wait for complete papers and paid fees before starting the clock? Counting missing-paper wait time would blame offices before they hold a complete file.
:::

:::check
A fee-free simple request is filed complete, and on day $4$ the office gives written notice for a Charter extension and gets a signature. Is it valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. For a $3$-working-day simple request, notice on day $4$ came after the deadline expired.
:::

### Public Rules Against Fixers

**RA $11032$** requires offices to post a **Citizen's Charter** showing steps, requirements, fees, and timelines. Public rules block fake fees and delays.

Apply this when a booth claims a $3$-day permit needs a rush fee:

1. Charter: the applicant checks official fees and the $3$-day limit inside.
2. Compare: the applicant spots the fake fee and skips the fixer.

Result: Public rules expose fake charges early.

:::pair #keep-fees-public
Right: Offices must post requirements, fees, and timelines publicly for verification.
Wrong: Keeping fees unpublished so staff can adjust charges for each applicant.
Hidden fees let fixers invent charges applicants cannot check.
:::

:::check
A supervisor removes the lobby fee chart so clerks quote prices privately. How does this affect fixer risk?
:::answer
The answer is **It increases fixer risk**. Without published fees and timelines, applicants cannot verify whether extra demands are real.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Counting a Valid Extension

A complex license is filed complete with payment under a $7$-day limit. On day $6$, the office gives notice with a Charter reason and release date, and the applicant signs. What is the total limit?

**Solution:**

1. Base: complex requests allow $7$ working days under Section $9$.
2. Extension: Charter listing, timely notice, and signature add $7$ working days.

$$
7 + 7 = 14
$$

Answer: Up to $14$ working days total.

**Check:** One valid extension doubles $7$ working days to $14$.

### #2: Council Item and Fee-Free Copy

One applicant files a land-use change needing local council approval. Another files a complete, fee-free request for a simple certificate. What limits apply?

**Solution:**

1. Council item: council approval gets $45$ working days, extendible by $20$.
2. Fee-free item: simple requests get $3$ working days once complete.

Answer: $45$ working days (plus up to $20$) for council action, and $3$ for the copy.

**Check:** Fee-free requests and council items both carry fixed limits under Section $9$.

### #3: Slow Desk, Missing Chart, and a Broker

At a desk with no fee chart, a clerk takes $12$ working days on a simple request without notice or pause. A broker offers fast release for cash, with no proof the clerk took money. Classify each.

**Solution:**

1. Broker: using office access to speed a deal for cash is fixing.
2. Missing chart: hiding fees removes the public check blocking fake charges.
3. Clerk's delay: decide if unexcused delay without payment proof shows bribery or red tape.

:::try
Finish the last step: classify the clerk's unexcused $12$-day delay with no proof of payment under RA $11032$.
:::answer
The answer is **The delay breaks the $3$-day limit and may show red tape, not proved bribery.** Without proof of a demand or payoff, slowness does not prove payment.

**Check:** Separate broken timelines and red tape from proved payoffs unless payment or demand is shown.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Unexplained delays: require proof of a demand or benefit before calling slowness a payoff. {#delay-not-payoff}
- Red tape versus fixing: reserve fixing for paid access advantages, not broken rules alone. {#red-tape-not-fixing}
- Hidden fees: post requirements, fees, and timelines publicly so fixers cannot invent charges. {#keep-fees-public}
:::
