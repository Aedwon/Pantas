## Core Concept

### Appointing Authority and Regular Term

Article IX-B, Section $1(2)$, sets how CSC members enter office and how long they serve.

1. The **President** appoints the Chairman and Commissioners.
2. Appointments require the consent of the **Commission on Appointments (CA)**.
3. The regular term is **$7$ years without reappointment**.

The Judicial and Bar Council screens judges and Ombudsman nominees, never CSC members. Neither the President nor a unanimous CA vote can grant a second term after a full $7$-year term.

Test a proposed appointment after a Commissioner finishes $7$ years:

1. A completed $7$-year term leaves zero room for reappointment under Section $1(2)$.
2. Relabeling the post or gaining unanimous CA support cannot create another term.

:::pair #president-with-ca-consent
Right: The President appoints CSC members only with the consent of the Commission on Appointments.
Wrong: Routing CSC appointments through the Judicial and Bar Council instead of the Commission on Appointments.
The JBC screens judicial and Ombudsman posts, while Article IX-B requires presidential appointment with CA consent.
:::

:::pair #seven-years-no-reappointment
Right: Every regular CSC term is $7$ years without reappointment, regardless of performance or consent.
Wrong: Treating the regular CSC term as $5$ years or allowing reappointment for strong performance.
Section $1(2)$ fixes regular terms at $7$ years and bars reappointment once a full term ends.
:::

:::check
A senator proposes skipping Commission on Appointments consent if the President and the CSC nominee both sign a written agreement. Does that process follow Article IX-B?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article IX-B, Section $1(2)$, requires both presidential appointment and Commission on Appointments consent.
:::

### Staggered Terms, Vacancies, and the Acting Ban

Under Article IX-B, Section $1(2)$, the first appointees under the $1987$ Constitution received different initial lengths:

1. The Chairman served **$7$ years**.
2. One Commissioner served **$5$ years**.
3. The other Commissioner served **$3$ years**.

Those three lengths created **staggered terms**, so seats expire in different years instead of all at once.

Two more rules govern vacancies:

1. Appointment to any vacancy is **only for the unexpired term** of the predecessor, never a fresh $7$-year term.
2. In no case shall any member be appointed or designated in a **temporary or acting capacity**.

:::why
Why stagger initial terms and ban acting members? Staggered expirations keep experienced members on the Commission, and banning acting appointments keeps members free from presidential pressure.
:::

Compute the term of a replacement when a Commissioner leaves $4$ years into a $7$-year term:

$$
7 - 4 = 3
$$

The replacement serves only the remaining $3$ years, and cannot serve in an acting role while waiting.

:::pair #staggered-and-unexpired-terms
Right: Initial $7$-, $5$-, and $3$-year lengths created staggered expirations, and vacancy replacements serve only the remaining years.
Wrong: Treating the initial $7$-, $5$-, and $3$-year appointments as synchronized terms ending together.
Different initial lengths ensured seats expire in different years, protecting continuity on the Commission.
:::

:::check
A CSC Commissioner resigns after serving $2$ years of a $7$-year term. How many years may the permanent replacement serve?
:::answer
The answer is **$5$ years**. Subtracting $7 - 2 = 5$ leaves $5$ years in the unexpired term, because a vacancy never restarts a $7$-year term.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Checking the Appointment Path

An order names a new CSC Commissioner using only a Judicial and Bar Council shortlist and a mayor's endorsement. It skips Commission on Appointments review. Why does this appointment fail?

**Solution:**

1. Article IX-B, Section $1(2)$, gives appointment power to the President with Commission on Appointments consent.
2. Neither JBC nomination nor local endorsement substitutes for CA consent.

Answer: It fails because the appointment lacks the required consent of the Commission on Appointments.

**Check:** Match CSC appointments to the President plus CA consent, never JBC or unilateral steps.

### #2: Counting a Vacancy Term

Commissioner Reyes starts a regular $7$-year term in $2021$ and steps down in $2024$ after $3$ full years. Her successor takes office permanently with CA consent. How long does the successor serve?

**Solution:**

1. A vacancy appointment covers only the unexpired term of the predecessor.
2. Subtract the $3$ years already served from the $7$-year term:

$$
7 - 3 = 4
$$

Answer: The successor serves only the $4$ unexpired years, not a fresh $7$-year term.

**Check:** Add served years and replacement years ($3 + 4 = 7$) to verify the original term ends on schedule.

### #3: Reappointment and Acting Designations

A CSC Chairman finishes a full $7$-year term on February $2$. Separately, a Commissioner retires $1$ year before her term ends. Officials propose giving the Chairman a second $7$-year term for strong performance and naming an acting Commissioner for $6$ months. Evaluate both proposals.

**Solution:**

1. Check the Chairman's proposal: Article IX-B, Section $1(2)$, sets a $7$-year term without reappointment.
2. Strong performance does not create an exception to the reappointment ban.

:::try
Finish the last step: evaluate the acting-Commissioner proposal and give the constitutional answer for both proposals.
:::answer
The answer is **Both proposals violate Article IX-B, Section $1(2)$.** The Chairman cannot be reappointed after a full $7$-year term. Also, no CSC member may be appointed or designated in a temporary or acting capacity.

**Check:** Remember the double rule for vacancies: no acting designation is allowed, and a permanent replacement serves only the unexpired term.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Appointing route: require the President plus Commission on Appointments consent, never the JBC. {#president-with-ca-consent}
- Regular term limit: keep regular terms at $7$ years without reappointment, even with CA support. {#seven-years-no-reappointment}
- Staggered and unexpired terms: initial $7$-, $5$-, and $3$-year terms staggered exits, and replacements finish only the unexpired balance with no acting seats. {#staggered-and-unexpired-terms}
:::
