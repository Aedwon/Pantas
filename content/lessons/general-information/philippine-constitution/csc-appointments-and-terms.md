## Core Concept

### Appointment and Term

The President appoints CSC members with the consent of the **Commission on Appointments**. That body reviews and gives consent to appointments covered by this rule.

A regular term is $7$ years without reappointment. The first appointees under the Constitution had staggered terms: $7$ years for the Chairman, $5$ for one Commissioner, and $3$ for the other. Their terms ended at different times.

A replacement for a member who leaves early serves only the **unexpired term**, meaning the time left in that member's term. The replacement does not begin a fresh $7$-year term. No member may be appointed or designated in a temporary or acting capacity.

## Examples

### #1: Who Appoints Members?

Which authorities take part in appointing a regular CSC Commissioner?

**Solution:**

The President appoints the Commissioner with the consent of the Commission on Appointments.

### #2: Full Term and Vacancy

A Commissioner completes a full $7$-year term. Another Commissioner leaves with $2$ years remaining. May the first receive another full term, and how long does the second member's replacement serve?

**Solution:**

1. The completed term is without reappointment. The first Commissioner cannot receive another term.
2. A vacancy appointment covers only the remaining term.
3. The replacement serves the remaining $2$ years.

### #3: Acting Membership

An official proposes naming an acting CSC Commissioner while a permanent appointment is being arranged. Is an acting designation allowed?

**Solution:**

No. The Constitution bars both temporary and acting appointments or designations as CSC members.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Replacement Terms Do Not Restart

> A vacancy does not create a fresh $7$-year term.

The replacement finishes the unexpired term. Temporary or acting CSC membership is also barred.
