## Core Concept

### Section $16$: Covered Bodies Beyond Criminal Courts

Article III, Section $16$ guarantees everyone the right to **speedy disposition of cases**, meaning cases resolved without unreasonable delay. It covers **all judicial, quasi-judicial, or administrative bodies**.

A **quasi-judicial body** is a government body outside the courts that hears facts and decides disputes. An **administrative body** is a government office deciding matters under law, such as an employee's disciplinary case or a license dispute.

Section $16$ reaches cases before trial courts, appellate courts, and non-court adjudicative agencies alike. It does not apply to a legislative committee merely debating a proposed bill where no case is decided.

Test Section $16$ when a civil service board stalls a teacher's promotion dispute for four years, claiming only judges are bound:

1. Classify the board: hearing facts and deciding a promotion dispute makes it an administrative or quasi-judicial body.
2. Apply Section $16$: non-court adjudicative bodies are expressly bound even with no judge presiding.

:::pair #covers-all-three-bodies
Right: Section $16$ covers cases before judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies.
Wrong: Limiting Section $16$ only to criminal trial courts while excluding administrative bodies.
Non-court bodies that hear and decide cases fall directly under Article III, Section $16$.
:::

:::check
A city council committee studies a proposed market ordinance for seven months before holding a vote. Does Section $16$ govern that bill debate?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $16$ protects speedy disposition of cases being decided, not lawmaking debates on a proposed bill.
:::

### Section $16$ vs. Section $14(2)$: Scope and Delay Review

**Speedy trial** under Article III, Section $14(2)$ applies to criminal prosecutions in court. **Speedy disposition** under Section $16$ is broader and covers criminal, civil, quasi-judicial, and administrative cases, whether or not a criminal charge exists.

Coverage under Section $16$ still does not make every postponement an automatic violation. Just like speedy trial, a long wait calls for checking the delay's reasons and surrounding circumstances.

Test an environmental permit board that pauses a quarry hearing twice to inspect a slope landslide risk:

1. Check coverage: the permit board is an administrative or quasi-judicial body covered by Section $16$.
2. Check circumstances: inspecting a real safety hazard explains the pause, so postponement alone proves no violation.

:::why
Why make speedy disposition broader than speedy trial? Agency delays over jobs, licenses, or benefits can harm ordinary citizens just as heavily as stalled court trials.
:::

:::pair #speedy-trial-vs-disposition
Right: Speedy trial covers criminal trials, while speedy disposition covers both criminal trials and agency cases.
Wrong: Claiming neither protection reaches an agency case solely because the agency sits outside the courts.
An administrative or quasi-judicial case enjoys Section $16$ protection even with no criminal charge filed.
:::

:::pair #coverage-not-automatic-violation
Right: Once Section $16$ applies, examine the delay's reasons and circumstances before finding a violation.
Wrong: Assuming every postponed hearing at a covered body automatically violates Section $16$.
Valid postponements for fair fact-finding differ from unjustified stalls without a sound reason.
:::

:::check
A customs review panel sits on an importer's refund petition for five years with no hearings or explanation. Which protection applies, and what must be checked?
:::answer
The answer is **Speedy disposition under Section $16$, checking the delay's reasons and circumstances**. An agency dispute is covered outside criminal court. Facts still determine whether the delay is unreasonable.
:::

## Examples

### #1: An Administrative Case

A disciplinary case before a government agency remains unresolved for an unreasonable period without a sound reason. Which Bill of Rights safeguard should be checked?

**Solution:**

1. Classify the forum: a government agency deciding a disciplinary case is an administrative body.
2. Apply Article III, Section $16$: speedy disposition covers judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies.

Answer: The right to speedy disposition of cases under Article III, Section $16$.

**Check:** Non-criminal agency proceedings fall directly under Section $16$, unlike Section $14(2)$'s criminal-trial rule.

### #2: Court Suit vs. Regulatory Board Case

A clinic owner has a civil contract suit in a trial court and a separate license-revocation case before a health regulatory board. Both drag on for years without cause. Which Article III speed safeguard protects each case?

**Solution:**

1. Check Section $14(2)$: speedy trial applies only to criminal prosecutions, so it covers neither non-criminal case.
2. Check Section $16$: speedy disposition covers judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies, so it covers both proceedings.

Answer: Article III, Section $16$'s speedy-disposition safeguard protects both the civil court suit and the regulatory board case.

**Check:** Section $14(2)$ applies only to criminal prosecutions, while Section $16$ reaches judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies alike.

### #3: Postponement at a Land Board

A land board outside the courts hears evidence and resolves boundary disputes. It postpones one scheduled hearing for two weeks so surveyors can submit a verified map. A claimant argues that since Section $16$ covers the board, any postponed hearing automatically breaks the Constitution. Assess that claim.

**Solution:**

1. Check board coverage: hearing evidence and deciding boundary disputes makes the board a quasi-judicial body covered by Section $16$.
2. Test the two-week pause for verified surveyor maps against Section $16$'s unreasonable-delay standard.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether the claimant's automatic-violation rule is right and explain what must be reviewed instead.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the claimant is wrong because Section $16$ requires reviewing the delay's reasons and circumstances**. Being covered subjects the board to Section $16$, yet a justified two-week pause for verified maps does not automatically violate it.

**Check:** Coverage brings the board under Section $16$, while the reasons and surrounding facts decide whether the postponement was unreasonable.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Case sits before a non-court agency: remember Section $16$ covers judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies. {#covers-all-three-bodies}
- Question pairs a criminal trial with an agency proceeding: match speedy trial to criminal trials and speedy disposition to both. {#speedy-trial-vs-disposition}
- Covered agency postpones a hearing: check the delay's reasons and circumstances rather than calling every pause unlawful. {#coverage-not-automatic-violation}
:::
