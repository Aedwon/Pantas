## Core Concept

### Supreme Court Qualifications

Under Article VIII, Section $7(1)$, a Supreme Court Member must meet three requirements:

1. Be a **natural-born** Philippine citizen.
2. Be at least $40$ years of age.
3. Have at least $15$ years as a lower-court judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines.

Every Member of the Judiciary must also show proven **competence, integrity, probity, and independence** (Section $7(3)$). These mean ability, honesty, upright conduct, and freedom from improper influence.

Screen two applicants for a Supreme Court seat:

1. A natural-born lawyer, age $44$, with $13$ years of practice fails the $15$-year experience rule.
2. A natural-born judge, age $47$, with $17$ years on the bench who took bribes fails Section $7(3)$'s integrity rule.

:::pair #supreme-court-all-three
Right: A Supreme Court Member must meet all three rules: natural-born, age $40$, and $15$ years of Philippine judicial or law practice.
Wrong: Letting extra practice years excuse an age under $40$, or letting $20$ years of practice excuse naturalized citizenship.
All three requirements apply together, so extra years never waive age or natural-born citizenship.
:::

:::check
A naturalized citizen is $48$ years old and has practiced law in the Philippines for $22$ years. Does she meet the Supreme Court citizenship rule?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article VIII, Section $7(1)$ requires natural-born citizenship for Supreme Court Members regardless of age or experience.
:::

### Lower-Court Judges and Judicial Qualities

Under Article VIII, Section $7(2)$, Congress sets lower-court qualifications. Still, every lower-court judge must be at least a **Philippine citizen** and a **member of the Philippine Bar** (an admitted lawyer).

Judges of **lower collegiate courts** (courts where judges decide as a group, like the Court of Appeals) must also be **natural-born** citizens (Section $7(1)$). All lower-court judges still need proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence (Section $7(3)$).

Compare a Regional Trial Court opening and a Court of Appeals opening:

1. The Court of Appeals is a lower collegiate court, so its nominee must be natural-born under Section $7(1)$.
2. For a single-judge trial court, Congress sets added rules beyond citizenship, Bar membership, and judicial character.

:::why
Why require natural-born status on lower collegiate courts while letting Congress set trial-court details? Collegiate panels settle major appeals nationwide, whereas Congress tailors trial-court experience to each court tier.
:::

:::pair #congress-sets-lower-court-rules
Right: Congress may prescribe further qualifications for lower-court judges beyond citizenship and Philippine Bar membership.
Wrong: Treating citizenship and Philippine Bar membership as stopping Congress from setting further lower-court qualifications.
Article VIII, Section $7(2)$ expressly authorizes Congress to prescribe additional qualifications for lower courts.
:::

:::pair #qualities-beyond-bar
Right: Every judge needs proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence alongside Bar membership.
Wrong: Treating Philippine Bar membership as removing the requirement of proven integrity.
Section $7(3)$ requires proven character and independence from every judge without exception.
:::

:::check
A candidate is a citizen and Bar member, yet has a record of taking bribes. Does Bar membership satisfy Section $7$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $7(3)$ requires proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence for every judge.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Judicial Qualifications

A natural-born lawyer is $40$ with $14$ years of Philippine law practice and no judicial service. Does she meet the Supreme Court experience rule? Are citizenship and Bar membership enough for every lower court?

**Solution:**

1. Under Section $7(1)$, $14$ years falls $1$ year short of the $15$-year Supreme Court requirement.
2. Under Sections $7(1)$ and $7(2)$, citizenship and Bar membership are minimums; Congress adds rules, and lower collegiate courts require natural-born status.

Answer: No to the Supreme Court ($14 < 15$); no to every lower court because Congress adds rules and collegiate courts require natural-born status.

**Check:** Supreme Court needs $15$ years of experience, and lower collegiate courts require natural-born citizenship.

### #2: Lower Collegiate Court Citizenship

A naturalized citizen is $46$, a Philippine Bar member for $18$ years, and known for integrity. May she be appointed to the Court of Appeals, a lower collegiate court?

**Solution:**

1. The Court of Appeals is a lower collegiate court where justices decide as a group.
2. Article VIII, Section $7(1)$ bars appointing anyone to a lower collegiate court unless natural-born.

Answer: No, because lower collegiate courts require natural-born citizenship.

**Check:** Naturalized citizens cannot sit on lower collegiate courts under Section $7(1)$.

### #3: Screening Two Supreme Court Candidates

Candidate A is natural-born, age $38$, with $15$ years of Philippine law practice. Candidate B is natural-born, age $49$, with $18$ years of Philippine law practice. Both have proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence. Who qualifies for the Supreme Court, and why does the other fail?

**Solution:**

1. Check Candidate A against Section $7(1)$: $15$ years passes, yet compare age $38$ to the minimum age.
2. Check Candidate B against Section $7(1)$: test natural-born status, age $49$, and $18$ years of Philippine law practice.

:::try
Finish the last step: identify the qualified candidate and explain why Candidate A's $15$ years of practice do not cure age $38$.
:::answer
The answer is **Candidate B qualifies for the Supreme Court, while Candidate A fails the $40$-year age rule.** Under Article VIII, Section $7(1)$, citizenship, age $40$, and $15$ years of experience must all pass together. Fifteen years of practice cannot waive age $38$.

**Check:** Candidate B passes $49 \ge 40$ and $18 \ge 15$; Candidate A fails $38 < 40$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Supreme Court seats: verify natural-born status, age $40$, and $15$ years of experience together. {#supreme-court-all-three}
- Lower courts: remember Congress sets further qualifications beyond citizenship and Bar membership, while collegiate courts need natural-born status. {#congress-sets-lower-court-rules}
- Judicial character: Bar membership never replaces proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence. {#qualities-beyond-bar}
:::
