## Core Concept

### Legislative power: Congress

Congress alone may declare that a state of war exists. Section $23(1)$ requires two-thirds of both Houses in joint session, voting separately. The Houses meet together but vote as separate bodies.

A **veto** is the President's rejection of a bill. The President must communicate it within $30$ days of receiving the bill. Otherwise, the bill becomes law as if signed (Section $27$).

To **override** means to pass the bill despite the veto. This requires two-thirds of all Members of each House. **All its Members** means the full membership, not just those present.

## Examples

### #1: Declaring a State of War

Both Houses meet in joint session. Supporters propose to pool their votes to reach two-thirds for declaring a state of war. Is that the required procedure?

**Solution:**

1. Article VI, Section $23(1)$ requires two-thirds of both Houses in joint session.
2. They must vote separately. A pooled total does not meet that voting rule.

### #2: Overriding a Veto

A vetoed bill returns to Congress. Suppose the House has $300$ Members and the Senate has $24$. What votes must approve an override?

**Solution:**

1. Take two-thirds of all House Members.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times300=200
$$

2. Take two-thirds of all Senators.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16
$$

3. At least $200$ House votes and $16$ Senate votes are needed. Without a communicated veto within $30$ days of receipt, the bill would become law as if signed.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Present Is Not All

> Count the membership named in the rule.

Two-thirds of those present may fall short of two-thirds of all Members. Read the rule before calculating.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Name the voting body before calculating. Veto override needs two-thirds of each House's full membership; treaty concurrence needs two-thirds of all Senators. War requires the Houses to vote separately, even though they meet together.
