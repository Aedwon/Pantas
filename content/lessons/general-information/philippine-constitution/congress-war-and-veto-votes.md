## Core Concept

### Declaring a State of War

The President commands the armed forces as Commander-in-Chief (Article VII, Section $18$). Congress alone declares that a state of war exists (Article VI, Section $23(1)$).

That declaration requires **two-thirds of both Houses in joint session, voting separately**. Both Houses meet in one chamber, yet tally their votes apart.

Check a war vote with $24$ Senators and $240$ Representatives in joint session:

1. Take two-thirds of the Senate separately: $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$ Senate votes.
2. Take two-thirds of the House separately: $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 240 = 160$ House votes.
3. Both Houses must reach their own two-thirds mark; pooled totals do not count.

:::pair #war-vote-separate
Right: The President commands the armed forces, and Congress declares war by two-thirds of both Houses voting separately in joint session.
Wrong: Letting the President declare war alone, or pooling both Houses' votes into one combined total.
Meeting together does not merge ballots; each House votes separately.
:::

:::check
In a joint session of $24$ Senators and $300$ Representatives, $210$ Representatives and $15$ Senators vote to declare war. Is it approved?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Each House votes separately under Section $23(1)$: $210$ meets the House mark of $200$, yet $15$ Senators is short of $16$.
:::

### Veto Deadline and Veto Override

A **veto** is the President's rejection of a bill. Under Article VI, Section $27(1)$, the President must communicate it within $30$ days of receipt. Otherwise, the bill becomes law as if signed.

To **override** means to pass a vetoed bill despite the veto. Each House must separately approve it by **two-thirds of all its Members**, meaning full membership, not just those present.

Test an override in a $270$-Member House and a $24$-Member Senate:

1. Two-thirds of all House Members is $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 270 = 180$ votes.
2. Two-thirds of all Senators is $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$ votes.
3. With only $178$ House votes, the override fails even if $20$ Senators vote yes.

:::why
Why require two-thirds of all Members instead of a simple majority? Passing a law over a veto demands broad support across both chambers.
:::

:::pair #override-separate-two-thirds
Right: Each House must separately pass a veto override by two-thirds of all its Members.
Wrong: Pooling both Houses' votes, or using a simple majority to override a timely veto.
Article VI, Section $27(1)$ bars both pooled totals and simple-majority overrides.
:::

:::pair #override-exact-two-thirds
Right: A veto override requires two-thirds of all Members in each House, never three-fourths.
Wrong: Requiring three-fourths of a House, such as $225$ votes in a $300$-Member House, to override a veto.
Two-thirds of $300$ is $200$; three-fourths ($225$) is for constitutional amendments.
:::

:::check
The President receives a bill on May $1$ and sends no veto message by June $5$. Does the bill become law?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Without a veto communicated within $30$ days of receipt, the bill becomes law as if signed.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Declaring a State of War

Both Houses meet in joint session. Supporters propose to pool their votes to reach two-thirds for declaring a state of war. Is that the required procedure?

**Solution:**

1. Apply Article VI, Section $23(1)$: declaring a state of war requires two-thirds of both Houses in joint session.
2. Check how they vote: each House must vote separately.

Answer: No, pooling votes violates the separate-voting rule.

**Check:** Even in joint session, the Senate and House count their two-thirds votes apart.

### #2: Overriding a Veto

A vetoed bill returns to Congress. Suppose the House has $300$ Members and the Senate has $24$. What votes must approve an override?

**Solution:**

1. Take two-thirds of all House Members.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times300=200
$$

2. Take two-thirds of all Senators.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16
$$

Answer: At least $200$ House votes and $16$ Senate votes.

**Check:** Both Houses count two-thirds of full membership separately, and missing the $30$-day veto deadline makes the bill law as if signed.

### #3: Present vs. Full Membership

Suppose the House has $270$ Members and the Senate has $24$. On a veto override vote, $21$ Senators attend and $15$ vote yes, while $190$ Representatives vote yes. Does the override pass?

**Solution:**

1. Check the House: $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 270 = 180$, so $190$ House votes pass.
2. Check the Senate: two-thirds of all $24$ Senators is $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$, even with $21$ present.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule whether the override passes and explain why $15$ of $21$ present Senators is not enough.
:::answer
The answer is **The override fails because the Senate needs at least $16$ votes.** Section $27(1)$ counts two-thirds of all $24$ Senators, not just the $21$ attending.

**Check:** $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$, and $15 < 16$, so the veto stands even though the House cleared $180$.
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Name the body and fraction first. Veto override uses two-thirds of each House's full membership separately. Treaty concurrence uses two-thirds of all Senators, and war declarations vote separately in joint session.

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- State of war vote: President commands the military, while Congress declares war voting separately in joint session. {#war-vote-separate}
- Veto override vote: check two-thirds of all Members in each House separately, never pooled or simple majority. {#override-separate-two-thirds}
- Override fraction: use two-thirds ($200$ of $300$), not three-fourths ($225$ of $300$). {#override-exact-two-thirds}
:::
