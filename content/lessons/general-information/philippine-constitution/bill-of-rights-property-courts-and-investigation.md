## Core Concept

### Sections $9$ and $10$: Property and Contracts

Section $9$ says private property shall not be taken for public use without **just compensation** (fair payment). Section $10$ bars laws **impairing the obligation of contracts**, protecting agreed duties from arbitrary law changes.

Test Section $9$ when a town widens a road across private land:

1. Check the taking: private land goes to public road use.
2. Check payment: public use triggers just compensation.

Test Section $10$ on a special law erasing interest owed under an existing private loan, with no valid public-welfare regulation behind it:

1. Check the duty: the loan fixed a lawful payment obligation.
2. Apply Section $10$: wiping out that duty without a valid public-welfare basis impairs the contract.

:::pair #just-compensation-always
Right: Public taking under Section $9$ always requires just compensation.
Wrong: Claiming no payment is owed because the project serves the public.
Public use triggers payment rather than excusing it.
:::

:::check
A city takes a private lot to build a public school. Must it pay just compensation?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Public use always requires just compensation under Section $9$.
:::

### Section $11$: Court Access and Legal Help

Section $11$ protects **free access to the courts and quasi-judicial bodies and adequate legal assistance**. Neither may be denied by reason of poverty. **Quasi-judicial bodies** are non-court agencies that hear facts and decide disputes.

Test a zoning board that lets a poor farmer file a dispute, then denies legal help for poverty:

1. Check the forum: deciding disputes makes it quasi-judicial.
2. Check both rights: filing access never excuses denying legal help for poverty.

:::pair #court-access-and-legal-help
Right: Section $11$ protects both access to file and legal help despite poverty.
Wrong: Treating permission to file as enough when legal help is denied for poverty.
Check access and legal help separately in courts and quasi-judicial bodies.
:::

:::check
A labor commission lets a poor worker file a claim, then refuses legal help due to poverty. Is that lawful?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $11$ covers quasi-judicial bodies and bars denying legal help for poverty.
:::

### Section $12$: Custodial Investigation and Waivers

Section $12$ protects suspects under **custodial investigation** (police custody or serious restraint, including suspect invitations under Republic Act No. $7438$).

Under Section $12(1)$, officers must warn a suspect of the right to remain silent. They must also tell the suspect about **competent and independent counsel**, preferably of the person's choice. If the person cannot afford counsel, one must be provided.

Waiving these rights requires **both**:

1. A waiver **in writing**, and
2. The **presence of counsel**.

:::why
Why require a lawyer during a written waiver? A suspect alone under police questioning faces heavy pressure, so counsel ensures the choice is truly free and informed.
:::

:::pair #waiver-needs-writing-and-counsel
Right: A custodial waiver needs both writing and counsel's presence, with free counsel if indigent.
Wrong: Treating a written signature alone as replacing counsel's presence.
Both writing and counsel's presence are mandatory under Section $12(1)$.
:::

Section $12(2)$ bans torture, force, threats, and secret detention. Section $12(3)$ bars statements taken against Section $12$ or $17$.

Test an invited fraud suspect whose confession is forced through threats during precinct questioning:

1. Check custody: a suspect invitation triggers Section $12$ under Republic Act No. $7438$.
2. Check Sections $12(2)$ and $12(3)$: threats are banned, making the forced statement inadmissible.

:::check
A suspect tells police aloud, with a lawyer listening, that she waives her right to silence without signing anything. Is it valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $12(1)$ requires both a written waiver and the presence of counsel.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Contract Duties and Later Law

A farm co-op signs a five-year crop contract. Congress later cancels unpaid fees under existing contracts without any public-welfare basis. Does Section $10$ allow that law?

**Solution:**

1. Agreed duty: the contract fixed a valid payment obligation.
2. Apply Section $10$: erasing contract duties without a public-welfare basis impairs contracts.

Answer: No, Section $10$ bars laws impairing contract obligations.

**Check:** Valid contract duties cannot be erased without a valid public-welfare reason.

### #2: Filing Access at a Quasi-Judicial Board

A regional labor board hears evidence and settles wage claims. An indigent janitor has a volunteer lawyer, yet the clerk rejects his complaint for unpaid fees. Does Section $11$ allow that refusal?

**Solution:**

1. Classify the board: hearing evidence and deciding disputes makes it quasi-judicial.
2. Check filing access: having counsel never excuses denying access for poverty.

Answer: No, poverty cannot bar free access to a quasi-judicial board.

**Check:** Section $11$ protects filing access and legal help separately.

### #3: Witness Interview Versus Suspect Invitation

Police ask a passing witness what truck sped past, and she answers freely. Later, officers invite a suspect to the precinct under Republic Act No. $7438$, lock the door, and take his signed waiver without counsel. Rule on both interviews and the waiver.

**Solution:**

1. Compare the witness reply and suspect invitation to see when custodial investigation begins.
2. Test the signed waiver against Section $12(1)$'s waiver rules.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on Section $12$'s reach in both interviews and on the waiver.
:::answer
The answer is **Section $12$ does not cover the witness's voluntary reply, yet it covers the invited suspect and invalidates his uncounseled waiver.** Under Republic Act No. $7438$, suspect invitations trigger Section $12$, so a waiver signed without counsel fails Section $12(1)$ and bars any resulting statement.

**Check:** Custodial safeguards attach to a questioned suspect under restraint, and a valid waiver requires both writing and counsel's presence.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Public project takes private land: always pay just compensation. {#just-compensation-always}
- Quasi-judicial body lets poor person file: check legal help separately. {#court-access-and-legal-help}
- Suspect signs waiver alone: require writing and counsel's presence. {#waiver-needs-writing-and-counsel}
:::
