## Core Concept

### Section $11$: Access to Courts and Quasi-Judicial Bodies

Under Article III, Section $11$, poverty cannot be the reason to deny free access to courts or **quasi-judicial bodies**. A quasi-judicial body is a government body outside the courts that hears facts and decides certain disputes.

This protection belongs to any person, before any ruling on who wins the dispute. A body's dispute-deciding role brings it under Section $11$, regardless of its name.

Test Section $11$ on a provincial agrarian board that hears evidence on tenancy disputes:

1. Check the board's work: hearing facts and deciding disputes makes it a quasi-judicial body.
2. Apply Section $11$: refusing to docket a poor tenant's claim for lack of money breaks free access.

:::why
Why protect access before a person wins a case? No poor claimant can prove a valid claim if poverty locks the hearing door first.
:::

:::pair #quasi-judicial-included
Right: Section $11$ protects free access to both courts and quasi-judicial bodies despite poverty.
Wrong: Limiting Section $11$ access only to bodies formally named courts.
Any government agency that hears facts and decides disputes is covered by Section $11$.
:::

:::check
A local housing arbitration panel rejects an indigent family's complaint until they pay full filing fees or win elsewhere first. Does Section $11$ allow that bar?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $11$ protects free access to quasi-judicial bodies despite poverty before any case result exists.
:::

### Section $11$: Adequate Legal Assistance Despite Poverty

Article III, Section $11$ also states that **adequate legal assistance** shall not be denied to any person by reason of poverty.

Access to a hearing and help from a lawyer are related protections, yet they are separate. Allowing a person to file a case does not settle whether needed legal help was given. Nor does letting someone file guarantee winning the case.

Apply Section $11$ when a civil court accepts an indigent tenant's answer, yet denies public legal help as outside police custody:

1. Check scope: Section $11$ protects adequate legal assistance in court and quasi-judicial cases, beyond Section $12$ custody.
2. Check the denial: refusing legal help to an indigent civil litigant breaks Section $11$.

:::pair #access-and-help-separate
Right: Free access to file and adequate legal assistance are separate Section $11$ protections.
Wrong: Thinking a person must be blocked from filing before denial of legal help matters.
Even after a court or board accepts a filing, poverty cannot justify denying needed legal help.
:::

:::pair #legal-help-not-custody-only
Right: Section $11$ protects adequate legal assistance in court and quasi-judicial cases despite poverty.
Wrong: Treating legal assistance as protected only during police custodial investigation.
Section $12$ covers police custody, while Section $11$ separately protects legal help in court and agency cases.
:::

:::check
An official claims Section $11$ protects legal help only for government employees who cannot pay a lawyer. Is that correct?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article III, Section $11$ forbids denying court access or adequate legal assistance to any person by reason of poverty.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Court Access and Poverty

A court refuses to accept a person's case solely because the person is poor. Which protection is involved?

**Solution:**

1. Check the reason: the refusal rests solely on poverty.
2. Apply Article III, Section $11$: free access to the courts cannot be denied because of poverty.

Answer: Free access to the courts under Article III, Section $11$.

**Check:** Section $11$ bars closing court doors to any person solely for lack of money.

### #2: Access to a Quasi-Judicial Body

A government body hears evidence and decides disputes outside the court system. It refuses a hearing solely because the applicant is poor. Does Section $11$ cover this kind of body?

**Solution:**

1. Classify the body: hearing evidence and deciding disputes makes it a quasi-judicial body.
2. Apply Article III, Section $11$: poverty cannot justify denying access to courts or quasi-judicial bodies.

Answer: Yes, Section $11$ covers access to this quasi-judicial body.

**Check:** Dispute-deciding power outside regular courts brings the body directly inside Section $11$.

### #3: Legal Assistance Denied

An applicant is told that adequate legal assistance will be denied solely because the applicant has no money. Which protection directly applies?

**Solution:**

1. Identify the denial: the applicant is refused needed legal help, not merely barred from filing papers.
2. Apply Article III, Section $11$: adequate legal assistance cannot be denied to any person by reason of poverty.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the denial and explain why allowing the applicant to file a case would not cure it.
:::answer
The answer is **The denial violates Article III, Section $11$**. Section $11$ protects adequate legal assistance separately from filing access. Letting a poor litigant file papers does not excuse denying needed legal help.

**Check:** Section $11$ protects both access to be heard and adequate legal assistance, and each right must be respected on its own.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Non-court board decides disputes: treat it as a quasi-judicial body covered by Section $11$. {#quasi-judicial-included}
- Poor litigant is allowed to file: check adequate legal assistance separately under Section $11$. {#access-and-help-separate}
- Denial of legal help in a civil or agency case: Section $11$ protects legal help outside police custody too. {#legal-help-not-custody-only}
:::
