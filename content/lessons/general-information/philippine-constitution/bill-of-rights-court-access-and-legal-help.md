## Core Concept

### Courts and Legal Help

Under Section $11$, poverty cannot be the reason to deny free access to courts or quasi-judicial bodies. A **quasi-judicial body** is a government body outside the courts that hears facts and decides certain disputes.

The same section protects adequate legal assistance despite poverty. Access to a hearing and help from a lawyer are related protections, but they are not the same thing. Allowing a person to file a case does not by itself settle whether needed legal help has been provided.

## Examples

### #1: Court Access and Poverty

A court refuses to accept a person's case solely because the person is poor. Which protection is involved?

**Solution:**

1. Section $11$ protects free access to courts despite poverty.
2. Poverty alone cannot justify that denial.

### #2: Access to a Quasi-Judicial Body

A government body hears evidence and decides disputes outside the court system. It refuses a hearing solely because the applicant is poor. Does Section $11$ cover this kind of body?

**Solution:**

1. A body that performs this dispute-deciding role is quasi-judicial.
2. Section $11$ protects access to quasi-judicial bodies as well as courts.
3. Poverty alone cannot be the reason for denying access.

### #3: Legal Assistance Denied

An applicant is told that adequate legal assistance will be denied solely because the applicant has no money. Which protection directly applies?

**Solution:**

1. The issue is legal help, not merely permission to file a case.
2. Section $11$ says adequate legal assistance cannot be denied because of poverty.
3. The stated reason conflicts with that protection.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Access and Legal Help Are Separate

> Being allowed to file a case does not answer every legal-assistance question.

Section $11$ protects both access and adequate legal assistance against denial because of poverty.
