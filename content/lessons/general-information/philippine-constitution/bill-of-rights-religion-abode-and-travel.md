## Core Concept

### Section $5$: Freedom of Religion

Article III, Section $5$ sets three rules on religion:

1. **Non-establishment:** No law may establish a State religion or favor one faith.
2. **Free exercise:** Religious profession and worship are allowed without discrimination or preference, though belief gives no general right to harm others or ignore every law.
3. **No religious test:** No religious test may be required to exercise civil or political rights, such as voting.

Test a city rule requiring food-stall applicants to show a baptism certificate from one parish before receiving a permit:

1. Identify the right restricted: applying for a civil business permit is a civil right.
2. Match the condition: demanding church membership for a civil permit breaks the ban on religious tests.

:::pair #religious-test-political-rights
Right: Conditioning voting or civil rights on religion breaks Section $5$'s ban on religious tests.
Wrong: Confusing a religious membership requirement for voting with involuntary servitude.
A permit is a civil right, and voting is a political right. A faith rule for either is a religious test, not forced labor.
:::

:::check
A town charter bars residents from running for barangay captain unless they belong to one faith. Which Section $5$ rule is broken?
:::answer
The answer is **No religious test for civil or political rights**. Running for public office is a political right protected from religious tests under Section $5$.
:::

### Section $6$: Liberty of Abode and Right to Travel

Article III, Section $6$ sets separate rules for two freedoms:

1. **Liberty of abode** (choosing where to live): may be impaired only **within the limits prescribed by law** and **upon lawful order of the court**.
2. **Right to travel**: may be limited only for **national security, public safety, or public health**, as provided by law.

Test a barangay council ordering a family to leave their neighborhood permanently on public-health grounds without a court order:

1. Identify the freedom affected: forcing a family out of their home targets liberty of abode, not travel.
2. Check the abode rule: a lawful court order is missing, so public health alone cannot save the order.

:::why
Why does abode require a court order? Moving someone's home uproots their daily life, so a judge must review it first. By contrast, urgent health or security risks can require legal travel limits.
:::

:::pair #abode-needs-court-order
Right: Restricting abode needs a lawful court order within legal limits; restricting travel needs national security, public safety, or public health by law.
Wrong: Relocating a person's home on national security grounds without a court order, or treating abode and travel rules as identical.
Whether officials cite public health or national security, always check if the order controls residence or travel first.
:::

:::pair #abode-restriction-not-absolute
Right: Liberty of abode may be impaired within limits set by law upon a lawful court order.
Wrong: Claiming liberty of abode permits no restrictions even with a lawful court order.
Section $6$ allows restrictions on abode when both a law and a lawful court order back them.
:::

:::check
Under a valid national-security law, immigration officers stop a traveler from boarding a flight into an active border conflict zone without a court order. Is that lawful?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. National security, as provided by law, is a valid Section $6$ ground for restricting travel without a court order.
:::

## Examples

### #1: A Religious Voting Test

A local charter bars residents from voting in barangay elections unless they hold membership in one recognized church. Which constitutional rule does that charter violate?

**Solution:**

1. Identify the right affected: voting in a barangay election is a political right.
2. Identify the condition: required church membership is a religious test.

Answer: No religious test for civil or political rights under Article III, Section $5$.

**Check:** Requiring faith membership to vote directly violates Section $5$, unlike rules on bail, forced labor, or unpaid debt.

### #2: Establishment and Worship

One law declares a faith the State's official religion. Another bans peaceful worship solely because officials dislike that faith. Which Section $5$ rules apply?

**Solution:**

1. Match the first law: naming a State religion breaks **non-establishment**.
2. Match the second rule: blocking peaceful worship breaks **free exercise**.

Answer: Non-establishment and free exercise of religion under Section $5$.

**Check:** Section $5$ protects both government neutrality among religions and peaceful worship without discrimination.

### #3: Residence and Travel

A regional director orders a witness to relocate permanently to another province without any court order, citing public safety. In a separate matter, Congress passes a law letting health authorities restrict travel out of a quarantine zone during an epidemic. How does Section $6$ apply to each action?

**Solution:**

1. Classify the first action: forced relocation affects **liberty of abode**, which requires legal limits and a lawful court order.
2. Classify the second action: blocking movement out of a quarantine zone affects the **right to travel**, which allows limits by law for public health.

:::try
Finish the last step: state the outcome for each action and explain why public safety cannot save the relocation order.
:::answer
The answer is **The relocation order breaks liberty of abode for lacking a court order, while the travel limit fits public health.** Public safety is a Section $6$ ground for restricting travel, so it cannot replace the required lawful court order for impairing liberty of abode.

**Check:** Section $6$ gives abode and travel separate rules, and a lawful court order is mandatory before restricting where a person lives.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Faith required to vote: apply Section $5$'s ban on religious tests for political rights. {#religious-test-political-rights}
- Forced relocation without a court order: do not transfer travel grounds to liberty of abode. {#abode-needs-court-order}
- Absolute claim on residence: remember liberty of abode can be restricted within legal limits upon a lawful court order. {#abode-restriction-not-absolute}
:::
