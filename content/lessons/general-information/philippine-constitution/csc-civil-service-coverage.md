## Core Concept

### Branches, Subdivisions, and Agencies

Article IX-B, Section $2(1)$, sets the reach of the Philippine civil service.

The civil service covers **all branches, subdivisions, instrumentalities, and agencies of the Government**.

1. **All branches:** executive departments, Congress staff, and court employees are covered.
2. **Subdivisions:** provinces, cities, municipalities, and barangays are covered.
3. **Instrumentalities and agencies:** government bodies carrying out public duties are covered.

Check three public offices for coverage:

1. A Senate legislative researcher works in the legislative branch, so the civil service covers the post.
2. A provincial treasurer's clerk works in a local subdivision, so the civil service covers the post.
3. A regional health bureau nurse works in a government agency, so the civil service covers the post.

:::pair #all-branches-and-subdivisions
Right: Civil service coverage includes all three branches and local subdivisions like cities and provinces.
Wrong: Limiting the civil service to executive agencies or leaving out cities and provinces.
Article IX-B, Section $2(1)$, names all branches, subdivisions, instrumentalities, and agencies together.
:::

:::check
A city council argues that city hall clerks sit outside the civil service because they serve a local government. Is the council right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article IX-B, Section $2(1)$, expressly covers government subdivisions, including cities and provinces.
:::

### The Original-Charter Rule for GOCCs

Article IX-B, Section $2(1)$, also covers **government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs) with original charters**.

An **original charter** is a special law passed by Congress creating the corporation itself.

1. A GOCC created by special law is inside the civil service.
2. A corporation formed under the **general corporation law** lacks an original charter.
3. Without an original charter, it sits outside the civil service, even with $100\%$ state ownership.

:::why
Why base GOCC coverage on the creating law? The original charter sets a clear legal line instead of shifting with share ownership.
:::

Test two state-owned firms:

1. Water Authority was created by special statute, so it has an original charter and civil service coverage.
2. Harbor Logistics Inc. registered under general corporation law with $100\%$ state ownership, so it lacks an original charter.

:::pair #original-charter-test
Right: Cover a government corporation only if a special creating law gave it an original charter.
Wrong: Covering a government corporation simply because it registered under the general corporation law.
General corporation law registration never grants an original charter under Section $2(1)$.
:::

:::pair #ownership-not-charter
Right: Check how the firm was created; full state ownership or public-like work never replaces an original charter.
Wrong: Treating $100\%$ government ownership or public-service work as proving an original charter.
Ownership share and daily work never create a special statutory charter.
:::

:::check
Metro Ferry Inc. formed under general corporation law, and a state bank owns $90\%$ of its shares. Does Article IX-B cover it under the corporation clause?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. General incorporation leaves Metro Ferry Inc. without an original charter, despite $90\%$ state ownership.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Sorting Offices Across Branches

A personnel audit reviews the Office of the Court Administrator, a municipal engineering office, and a private security contractor. Which offices belong to the civil service?

**Solution:**

1. Court Administrator staff work in the judicial branch, so they are covered.
2. Municipal engineering staff work in a government subdivision, so they are covered.
3. A private contractor is not a government branch, subdivision, agency, or chartered GOCC.

Answer: The court office and municipal office are covered, while the private firm is not.

**Check:** Civil service coverage reaches all three branches and all local subdivisions.

### #2: Testing a Wholly State-Owned Subsidiary

A chartered state firm creates a warehouse subsidiary under general corporation law. The parent firm holds $100\%$ of the subsidiary's shares. Is the subsidiary covered under Article IX-B's corporation clause?

**Solution:**

1. The subsidiary formed under general corporation law, not by a special statute.
2. Full public ownership by the parent firm does not supply an original charter.

Answer: No, because the subsidiary lacks an original charter of its own.

**Check:** Inspect the creating law of the specific corporation being tested, not its parent's charter.

### #3: Comparing Two Public Enterprises

State Grid Corp. was created by Republic Act No. $1234$ to transmit power. State Logistics Inc. incorporated under general corporation law to haul relief goods, and the treasury owns every share. Which firm is covered?

**Solution:**

1. State Grid Corp. has an original charter from a special statute.
2. State Logistics Inc. performs public-style work and is wholly owned, yet formed under general law.

:::try
Finish the last step: name the covered enterprise and explain why the other is excluded.
:::answer
The answer is **Only State Grid Corp. is covered, while State Logistics Inc. is excluded.** State Grid Corp. has an original charter from a special law, while general incorporation never creates one even with full state ownership.

**Check:** Match special creating law to coverage, and general corporation law to exclusion.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Scope across government: include all three branches plus local subdivisions like cities and provinces. {#all-branches-and-subdivisions}
- General incorporation: registration under general corporation law never counts as an original charter. {#original-charter-test}
- Ownership and tasks: $100\%$ state ownership or public-style work never substitutes for a special creating law. {#ownership-not-charter}
:::
