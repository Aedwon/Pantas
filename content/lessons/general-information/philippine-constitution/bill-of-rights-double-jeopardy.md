## Core Concept

### Section $21$: When Double Jeopardy Attaches

Article III, Section $21$ states that **no person shall be twice put in jeopardy of punishment for the same offense**. **Double jeopardy** means facing trial risk a second time for the same offense after a protected ending in the first case.

Under Rule $117$, Section $7$, these are the basic general conditions for double jeopardy to attach in the first case:

1. A **valid complaint or information** (formal charge),
2. Before a **court of competent jurisdiction** (a court with legal authority),
3. **Arraignment** (formal reading of the charge) and a **valid plea** (such as guilty or not guilty), and
4. A protected end: **conviction**, **acquittal** (not-guilty judgment), or **dismissal without the accused's express consent**.

Recognized exceptions, such as certain dismissals requested by the accused, follow specific rules under Rule $117$, Section $7$.

Test a theft complaint dropped before arraignment:

1. Check arraignment and plea: none occurred.
2. Since those required steps are missing, double jeopardy never attached, so refiling is allowed.

:::why
Why wait until arraignment, plea, and a protected end? Until an accused answers a valid charge before a proper court, trial risk has never begun.
:::

:::pair #complaint-not-jeopardy
Right: Double jeopardy requires a valid charge, competent court, arraignment, valid plea, and a protected end.
Wrong: Treating a filed complaint alone as triggering double jeopardy without arraignment, plea, or a protected end.
Check every step from charge and plea through the ending before barring a second case.
:::

:::check
An accused enters a valid plea in a proper court and is acquitted after trial. Can prosecutors retry the same offense?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A valid charge, proper court, arraignment, plea, and acquittal trigger double jeopardy under Section $21$.
:::

### Included Offenses and Law vs. Ordinance

Section $21$ and Rule $117$, Section $7$ also cover two related situations:

- **Included offenses:** An offense whose parts all sit inside the first charge (or one that includes it), plus attempt or frustrated stages. Renaming the charge does not evade the bar.
- **National law and local ordinance:** One act may violate both a national law and a local ordinance. Conviction or acquittal under either bars another prosecution for the **same act** under the other.

Test a conviction under a city noise ordinance when prosecutors later file a national-law charge for the exact same late-night act:

1. Check the first case: valid charge, proper court, arraignment, plea, and conviction under the ordinance.
2. Apply Section $21$: a conviction under the ordinance bars prosecuting the same act under national law.

:::pair #law-and-ordinance
Right: Under a law and an ordinance, conviction or acquittal under either bars prosecuting the same act under the other.
Wrong: Allowing a second trial for the same act because a city ordinance and a national statute come from different lawmakers.
Section $21$ treats both conviction and acquittal under one as barring prosecution for the same act under the other.
:::

:::check
After arraignment and plea in a proper court, a vendor is acquitted under a national food-safety law. May city prosecutors charge that same act under a local market ordinance?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $21$ works both ways: acquittal or conviction under national law bars prosecuting the same act under a local ordinance.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Locating Two Protections

Which two sections of Article III protect against compelled self-incrimination and double jeopardy, respectively?

**Solution:**

1. Locate forced testimony against oneself: Section $17$.
2. Locate double jeopardy: Section $21$.

Answer: Section $17$ and Section $21$, in that order.

**Check:** Section $17$ sits among trial protections, while Section $21$ sits right before Section $22$'s prohibited laws.

### #2: Dismissal by a Court Lacking Authority

A municipal court takes a not-guilty plea on an estafa charge, then dismisses the case because the amount exceeds its legal jurisdiction. When prosecutors refile the charge in the proper Regional Trial Court, the accused claims double jeopardy. Is the refiled case barred?

**Solution:**

1. Check the Rule $117$, Section $7$ conditions: arraignment, plea, and dismissal happened, yet a court of competent jurisdiction was missing.
2. Apply Section $21$: because the first court lacked legal authority, double jeopardy never attached.

Answer: No, refiling in the proper court is allowed because the first court lacked competent jurisdiction.

**Check:** Every general condition must be met in the first case, including a court with legal authority to try the charge.

### #3: Ordinance Conviction Followed by National Charge

Selling firecrackers without a permit violates both a city safety ordinance and a national explosives law. A stall owner enters a guilty plea in a proper court under the ordinance and pays the court fine. Prosecutors then file a national charge for the same sale, seeking a prison term. May the national case proceed?

**Solution:**

1. Check the first case against the four Rule $117$, Section $7$ conditions (valid charge, proper court, plea, and protected end).
2. Check the second charge against Section $21$'s rule on the same act under a law and an ordinance.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether the national prosecution may proceed and explain why the heavier penalty and different lawmaker do not allow it.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the national case is barred because Section $21$ forbids prosecuting the same act after an ordinance conviction.** Section $21$ makes either conviction or acquittal under the ordinance a bar under national law, even when the national law carries a heavier penalty.

**Check:** Different lawmakers and a heavier national penalty do not permit trying the same act twice after a valid ordinance conviction.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Only a complaint was filed: require a valid charge, competent court, arraignment, valid plea, and protected ending. {#complaint-not-jeopardy}
- Same act under law and ordinance: conviction or acquittal under either bars prosecuting the same act under the other. {#law-and-ordinance}
:::
