## Core Concept

The $1987$ Constitution has a Preamble and $18$ Articles. An **article** is a major division of the Constitution. Its title helps you find a topic.

A title tells where to look. It does not tell every rule, power, requirement, or exception inside the article. For a question about what the government may do, read or recall the exact rule too.

### The Preamble: Shared Aims

The **Preamble** is the opening statement. It explains why the Filipino people adopted the Constitution and the kind of country they seek. It is not one of the $18$ Articles.

A **just and humane society** treats people fairly and with care for human dignity. The **common good** means the good of the community, not only the advantage of one person or group.

**Patrimony** means the nation's shared heritage and resources. Conserving and developing it means caring for that inheritance while using it for the country's good.

The Preamble also seeks independence, democracy, truth, justice, freedom, love, equality, and peace. The **rule of law** means that public power must follow the law. An official's personal wish does not replace a legal rule.

These are broad aims. They do not by themselves answer every question about a power or a legal procedure.

### Articles I to V: Country, Principles, and Rights

**Article I: National Territory.** This describes the land, waters, and air that make up Philippine territory. It includes waters around, between, and connecting the islands as internal waters. In simple terms, it identifies the areas the Philippines governs or has legal authority over.

**Article II: Declaration of Principles and State Policies.** This states basic rules and goals for government. The Philippines is democratic and republican: the people hold the highest political authority, and government authority comes from them. The word **sovereignty** refers to that highest authority.

Article II also says civilian authority is always supreme over the military. The government's prime duty is to serve and protect the people. When applying a principle, match the action to the specific rule, not only to the article's title.

For example, a claim that the military stands above civilian government conflicts with the civilian-supremacy principle. A question about protecting health points to the State policy on health. These are different rules within the same article.

**Article III: Bill of Rights.** This protects people against unlawful government action. It includes fair legal process, equal protection, privacy, speech, and religion. For example, taking property without fair legal steps raises due process, not simply a question about where Article III is found.

**Article IV: Citizenship.** This states who are Philippine citizens. A person whose father or mother is a Philippine citizen is included. **Natural-born citizens** are generally citizens from birth who need no act to acquire or complete their citizenship; the article also states a specific exception for certain people who elect Philippine citizenship.

**Article V: Suffrage.** Suffrage means the right to vote. The article sets constitutional voting requirements, including citizenship, an age of at least $18$, and required residence, subject to disqualification by law. It bars literacy, property, or other added substantive requirements for voting.

### Articles VI to VIII: The Three Departments

**Article VI: The Legislative Department.** Legislative power means the power to make laws. Congress has this power and consists of the Senate and House of Representatives. Some lawmaking power is reserved to the people through the Constitution's rules on initiative and referendum, ways for people to propose or vote on laws directly.

**Article VII: The Executive Department.** Executive power is placed in the President. It includes carrying out the laws. This article also gives rules about the President and Vice-President, including who may hold office and how long they may serve.

**Article VIII: The Judicial Department.** Judicial power is placed in the Supreme Court and lower courts established by law. It includes deciding actual disputes over rights that can be enforced in court. It also includes checking grave abuse of discretion, a serious misuse of power, by any government branch or body.

The article titles locate the departments. They do not mean that each department may use its power without constitutional limits.

### Article IX: Constitutional Commissions

Article IX covers independent commissions. **Independent** means they have constitutional protection from control that would stop them from doing their assigned work fairly.

Article IX has four parts:

- **IX-A: Common Provisions**, rules shared by the commissions.
- **IX-B: Civil Service Commission**, the government's central personnel agency.
- **IX-C: Commission on Elections**, which administers and enforces election laws within its assigned powers.
- **IX-D: Commission on Audit**, which examines government accounts and the use of public funds and property.

Article IX-B is part of Article IX. It is not an extra article added to the total.

### Articles X to XIII: Local Government and Public Welfare

**Article X: Local Government.** This covers provinces, cities, municipalities, barangays, and autonomous regions. **Local autonomy** means local governments have authority to manage local affairs within the law. It does not mean they are separate countries or free from the Constitution.

**Article XI: Accountability of Public Officers.** Public office is a public trust. Officials must serve responsibly and honestly. This article also covers **impeachment**, a constitutional process for charging and trying specified high officials for removal from office.

**Article XII: National Economy and Patrimony.** This covers the economy and the nation's resources. It states that natural resources belong to the State, with rules on their use and protection. For an ownership or resource-use question, the exact provision matters because different resources and activities have different limits.

**Article XIII: Social Justice and Human Rights.** Social justice seeks fairer opportunities and protection for people who face disadvantage. The article covers labor, land reform, housing, health, women, people's organizations, and human rights. It also creates the Commission on Human Rights, whose powers include investigating human rights violations involving civil and political rights.

### Articles XIV to XVIII: Other Fields and Constitutional Change

**Article XIV: Education, Science and Technology, Arts, Culture and Sports.** The State must protect and promote the right to quality education at all levels and take steps to make education accessible. The article also sets policies for science, culture, and sports. Its title lists several fields, so a sports-policy question can belong here as well as an education question.

**Article XV: The Family.** The Filipino family is the foundation of the nation. The State must protect and strengthen it. The article also protects marriage as the foundation of the family.

**Article XVI: General Provisions.** This covers several subjects, including the flag, the armed forces, and mass media. The flag is red, white, and blue, with a sun and three stars. A question on one of these subjects may belong here even though the title does not name it.

**Article XVII: Amendments or Revisions.** An **amendment** changes a part of the Constitution. A **revision** makes broader changes to its basic plan. This article gives ways to propose changes and requires approval by the people in a **plebiscite**, a vote on a specific public question.

**Article XVIII: Transitory Provisions.** Transitory means used during a change from one system to another. This article includes rules for the shift to the $1987$ Constitution. For example, existing laws remain effective if they do not conflict with the Constitution, until they are changed or repealed.

## Examples

### #1: Counting the Articles

How many Articles are in the $1987$ Constitution, excluding the Preamble?

**Solution:**

1. The Articles run from I through XVIII.
2. XVIII is the Roman numeral for $18$.
3. The answer is $18$ Articles. The Preamble is not included.

### #2: Locating Public Accountability

Which Article contains rules on accountability of public officers and impeachment?

**Solution:**

Article XI, Accountability of Public Officers, contains these rules.

### #3: Finding the Civil Service Commission

Which main Article and sub-part cover the Civil Service Commission?

**Solution:**

1. Article IX covers the Constitutional Commissions.
2. Part B concerns the Civil Service Commission.
3. The specific location is Article IX-B, within Article IX.

### #4: Interpreting a Preamble Aim

Which aim of the Preamble is most directly served by a policy designed to benefit the community as a whole instead of giving a private advantage to one favored group?

**Solution:**

1. The policy's stated purpose is the good of the whole community.
2. This matches the Preamble's aim of promoting the common good.

### #5: Applying a State Principle

A military commander claims that military orders take priority over lawful civilian authority. Which Article II principle conflicts with that claim?

**Solution:**

1. Article II says civilian authority is always supreme over the military.
2. The commander's claim reverses that relationship.
3. It conflicts with the principle of civilian supremacy.

### #6: A Title Does Not Supply a Qualification

A reviewer knows that Article VII is titled The Executive Department. Is that title alone enough to state every constitutional qualification for President?

**Solution:**

1. The title tells the reviewer where to find executive-department rules.
2. It does not list the qualifications for President.
3. The reviewer must read or recall the actual provision. The title alone is not enough.

### #7: Matching Similar Topics

Which Articles should be consulted for changing the Constitution and for rules used during the shift to the $1987$ Constitution, respectively?

**Solution:**

1. Changes to the Constitution belong to Article XVII, Amendments or Revisions.
2. Rules for the shift to the new system belong to Article XVIII, Transitory Provisions.
3. The answer is Article XVII followed by Article XVIII.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Articles VI, VII, and VIII place the lawmaking, executive, and judicial departments in that order. Use that sequence to check a department-location answer.

Article IX concerns the commissions. Its parts follow A for common rules, B for Civil Service, C for Elections, and D for Audit. Keep the part letter attached to IX.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: A Title Is Not the Full Rule

> Finding the article does not finish a question about its rules.

Use the title to locate the topic. Then check the actual power, requirement, procedure, or exception asked about.

### #2: The Preamble Is Not Article I

> Do not add the Preamble to the article count.

The Preamble gives broad aims. Article I concerns National Territory. The Constitution has $18$ Articles.

### #3: IX-B Is a Part of IX

> A sub-part does not increase the number of Articles.

The Civil Service Commission is in Article IX-B. All four parts remain within Article IX.

### #4: General Provisions Covers Specific Subjects

> An article title may not name every topic inside it.

The flag and armed forces are in Article XVI. Search by the actual subject when the title alone gives little detail.

### #5: A State Goal Is Not a Complete Procedure

> A broad policy does not supply every step for carrying it out.

Article II can state a goal, such as protecting health. A question about how a program operates may also require the relevant law or a more specific constitutional rule.
