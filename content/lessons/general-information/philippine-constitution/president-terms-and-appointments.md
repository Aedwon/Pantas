## Core Concept

### Executive power: the President

The President serves $6$ years without reelection (Article VII, Section $4$). A successor who serves as President for more than $4$ years cannot later be elected President. The Vice-President serves $6$ years and may serve at most $2$ successive terms.

The Vice-President becomes President upon the President's death, permanent disability, removal, or resignation (Section $8$).

The President controls executive departments, bureaus, and offices and ensures laws are carried out (Section $17$). The President submits the budget within $30$ days from the opening of each regular congressional session (Section $22$).

The **Commission on Appointments** is a body of Senators and Representatives that approves certain appointments. Article VII, Section $16$ requires its consent for department heads, ambassadors, other public ministers and consuls, and military officers from colonel or naval captain upward.

Other offices have their own appointment rules in the Constitution. For example, Constitutional Commission members need this consent. Judges do not, and neither does the Vice-President when appointed to the Cabinet (Article VII, Section $3$).

## Examples

### #1: Appointment Consent

Which needs Commission on Appointments consent: an ambassador or a Supreme Court Justice?

**Solution:**

1. The ambassador needs consent under Article VII, Section $16$.
2. The Justice follows the Judicial and Bar Council process without confirmation under Article VIII, Section $9$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Different Terms

> The President and Vice-President have different reelection rules.

The President cannot be reelected. The Vice-President may serve $2$ successive terms. Regular $7$-year terms without reappointment belong to Constitutional Commission members and the Ombudsman.

### #2: Different Appointment Rules

> Presidential appointment does not always require confirmation.

An ambassador needs Commission on Appointments consent. A judge does not. Check the rule for the specific office.
