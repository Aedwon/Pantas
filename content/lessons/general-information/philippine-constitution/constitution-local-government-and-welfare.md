## Core Concept

### Articles X and XI: Local Government and Accountability

Articles X and XI govern local communities and the standards binding public officials:

1. **Article X: Local Government** covers provinces, cities, municipalities, barangays, and autonomous regions. **Local autonomy** lets local units manage local affairs within the law, yet they remain part of the Republic, not independent nations.
2. **Article XI: Accountability of Public Officers** declares public office a public trust and sets standards of integrity and modest living. It also establishes **impeachment** and the Ombudsman.

Public-officer accountability belongs strictly to Article XI. Never file it under **Article XVI (General Provisions)**, **Article XVII (Amendments or Revisions)**, or **Article XVIII (Transitory Provisions)**.

Route two governance issues between Articles X and XI:

1. Checking how a province forms a new municipality by plebiscite belongs to **Article X** (Local Government).
2. Checking which $5$ officer categories face impeachment belongs to **Article XI** (Accountability of Public Officers).

:::pair #local-gov-article-x
Right: Locate rules on provinces, cities, municipalities, barangays, and local autonomy in Article X (Local Government).
Wrong: Placing Local Government in Article VIII (Judicial Department) or Article XII (National Economy and Patrimony).
Local units sit in Article X right after the Commissions (IX) and before Accountability (XI).
:::

:::pair #accountability-in-xi-not-xvi
Right: Place public-officer accountability under Article XI, never under General Provisions (Article XVI) or Amendments (Article XVII).
Wrong: Keeping accountability under Article XVI because "general" covers everything, or moving it to Article XVII or XVIII.
A broad label like General Provisions never replaces a dedicated article like Article XI.
:::

:::check
A municipal council passes an ordinance claiming local autonomy lets the town suspend constitutional due process locally. Does Article X allow that?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Local autonomy lets local units manage local affairs within the law, never override the Constitution.
:::

### Articles XII and XIII: Economy, Patrimony, and Social Justice

Articles XII and XIII move from national wealth to social protection and human rights:

1. **Article XII: National Economy and Patrimony** governs the economy and national resources. Natural resources belong to the State, so check the exact section for land, mines, or corporations.
2. **Article XIII: Social Justice and Human Rights** covers labor, land reform, urban housing, health, women, and human rights. It also creates the **Commission on Human Rights (CHR)** to investigate civil and political rights violations (Section $18(1)$).

:::why
Why separate Article XII from Article XIII? Article XII focuses on national resources and economic rules, while Article XIII protects workers, farmers, disadvantaged groups, and human rights.
:::

Sort two public-policy questions between Articles XII and XIII:

1. Checking whether foreign firms may own $100\%$ of Philippine public-utility land belongs to **Article XII** (National Economy and Patrimony).
2. Checking whether agrarian reform protects landless farmworkers belongs to **Article XIII** (Social Justice and Human Rights).

:::pair #economy-xii-vs-rights-xiii
Right: Match natural resources and economic patrimony to Article XII, and match social justice and CHR powers to Article XIII.
Wrong: Mixing up Article XII (National Economy and Patrimony) and Article XIII (Social Justice and Human Rights).
Think of XII as national wealth and economy, and XIII as labor, social reform, and human rights.
:::

:::check
A researcher wants the section protecting urban poor housing and women workers, plus the rule that minerals belong to the State. Which two Articles should be checked?
:::answer
The answer is **Article XIII for housing and women workers, and Article XII for State ownership of minerals.** Social welfare sits in XIII, while natural resources sit in XII.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Sorting Local Rules vs. Official Accountability

Question A asks how autonomous regions are created. Question B asks how the Ombudsman investigates corrupt officials. Assign each question to Article X or Article XI.

**Solution:**

1. Autonomous regions are local-government units governed by Article X (Local Government).
2. The Ombudsman and public-officer accountability sit in Article XI (Accountability of Public Officers).

Answer: Question A belongs to Article X, and Question B belongs to Article XI.

**Check:** Match territorial local units to X and public-trust oversight to XI.

### #2: Why General Provisions Never Replaces Article XI

A reviewer places "Ombudsman Remedies" under Article XVI because "General Provisions" sounds broad enough to hold everything about staff. Correct the index and explain why.

**Solution:**

1. Article XI carries the title Accountability of Public Officers, covering public trust, impeachment, and the Ombudsman.
2. Article XVI (General Provisions) collects miscellaneous subjects not given their own article, never replacing Article XI.

Answer: Move Ombudsman Remedies to Article XI because specific subject articles govern their own topics.

**Check:** Always prefer the exact dedicated article (XI) over a broad catch-all title (XVI).

### #3: Routing Economy, Social Reform, and CHR

Assign three items to Article XII or Article XIII. Check (a) State timberlands, (b) labor unions and wages, and (c) CHR civil-rights probes.

**Solution:**

1. Check item (a): natural resources like timberlands belong to Article XII (National Economy and Patrimony).
2. Check item (b): labor protection belongs to Article XIII (Social Justice and Human Rights).

:::try
Finish the last step: assign item (c) to its Article and explain why it belongs there rather than Article XII.
:::answer
The answer is **Assign item (c) to Article XIII, because Article XIII creates the CHR and grants its civil-rights investigative power.** Article XII covers national economy and natural resources, not human-rights bodies.

**Check:** Match XII to State resources and economy, and XIII to labor, social reform, and CHR human rights.
:::
