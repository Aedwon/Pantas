## Core Concept

Article IX-B of the $1987$ Constitution covers the **Civil Service Commission**, or CSC. The CSC administers the civil service and serves as the government's central personnel agency.

As the central personnel agency, the CSC builds the career service and strengthens the merit and rewards system. It also supports training and development, good service, integrity, efficiency, and public accountability.

### Composition and Qualifications

The CSC is composed of a **Chairman and two Commissioners**.

Each member must meet these qualifications:

- **Citizenship:** The member must be a **natural-born citizen** of the Philippines. A natural-born citizen is a citizen from birth who did not need to perform an act to acquire or perfect Philippine citizenship.
- **Age:** The member must be at least **$35$ years old** at the time of appointment.
- **Public administration:** The member must have **proven capacity for public administration**. In simple terms, the person must have shown an ability to manage public work well. The Constitution gives this requirement but does not name one required degree, job, or test that proves it.
- **Prior candidacy:** The member must not have been a candidate for any elective position in the election immediately before the appointment.

### Appointment and Tenure

The President appoints the Chairman and Commissioners with the consent of the Commission on Appointments.

A regular term is **$7$ years without reappointment**. The first appointees under the Constitution had staggered terms of $7$, $5$, and $3$ years.

If a vacancy occurs before a term ends, the replacement serves only the unexpired part of that term. No CSC member may be appointed or designated in a temporary or acting capacity.

### Merit, Fitness, and Civil Service Coverage

The civil service covers all branches, subdivisions, instrumentalities, and agencies of the Government. It also covers government-owned or controlled corporations that have original charters.

Civil service appointments must be based on **merit and fitness**. As far as practicable, these are determined by competitive examination.

The competitive-examination rule has exceptions for positions that are policy-determining, primarily confidential, or highly technical. The exceptions are to the competitive examination rule, not to the need for merit and fitness.

An officer or employee in the civil service may be removed or suspended only for a cause provided by law. Civil service officers and employees also may not take part, directly or indirectly, in electioneering or a partisan political campaign.

**Electioneering** means working to help a candidate or political party win an election.

### Independence and Common Commission Rules

Article IX-A says the Constitutional Commissions are independent. This includes the CSC.

During their tenure, Commission members may not hold another office or job. They also may not practice a profession, actively manage or control a business affected by their official work, or have a financial interest in a government contract, franchise, or privilege covered by the constitutional rule.

The salary of the Chairman and Commissioners is fixed by law and cannot be decreased during their tenure. The Commission also has fiscal autonomy, and its approved annual funding is released automatically and regularly.

These safeguards and conflict rules help protect the Commission's independence.

## Examples

### #1: Composition of the Commission

How many members make up the Civil Service Commission, and what are their positions?

**Solution:**

1. The Commission has one Chairman and two Commissioners.
2. The total is:

$$
1 + 2 = 3
$$

3. Therefore, the CSC has three members: one Chairman and two Commissioners.

### #2: Age Qualification at the Boundary

An otherwise qualified appointee is exactly $35$ years old on the date of appointment. Does the age requirement disqualify the appointee?

**Solution:**

1. Article IX-B requires a member to be at least $35$ years old at the time of appointment.
2. "At least $35$" includes a person who is exactly $35$.
3. Therefore, the age requirement is satisfied.

### #3: Disqualification Based on Prior Candidacy

An applicant for Civil Service Commissioner was a candidate in the election immediately preceding the planned appointment. Is the applicant qualified for appointment under Article IX-B?

**Solution:**

1. A CSC appointee must not have been a candidate for any elective position in the election immediately preceding the appointment.
2. The applicant was a candidate in that exact election.
3. Therefore, the applicant does not meet this qualification.

### #4: Competitive Examination Exception

A government position is highly technical. Does the Constitution require that merit and fitness for that position be determined by competitive examination?

**Solution:**

1. Civil service appointments must be based on merit and fitness.
2. Competitive examination is the usual method as far as practicable.
3. Highly technical positions are one of the stated exceptions to the competitive-examination rule.
4. Therefore, a competitive examination is not constitutionally required for that position, but merit and fitness still matter.

### #5: Removal for Cause

A permanent civil service employee is suspended only because a supervisor dislikes the employee. No cause provided by law is given. Which constitutional protection is involved?

**Solution:**

1. A civil service officer or employee may be removed or suspended only for a cause provided by law.
2. Personal dislike is not a stated legal cause in the facts given.
3. Therefore, the action conflicts with the constitutional protection against removal or suspension without legal cause.

### #6: Partisan Electioneering

A civil service employee uses official work time to campaign for a political party. Which constitutional restriction applies?

**Solution:**

1. Civil service officers and employees may not engage directly or indirectly in electioneering or partisan political campaigns.
2. Campaigning for a political party is partisan political activity.
3. Therefore, the constitutional restriction applies.

### #7: Term Length and Reappointment

A Commissioner completes a full $7$-year term. Can the President reappoint the same Commissioner for another full term?

**Solution:**

1. A regular CSC term is $7$ years.
2. Article IX-B states that the term is without reappointment.
3. Therefore, the Commissioner cannot be reappointed for another term.

### #8: Appointment to a Vacancy

A Commissioner leaves office before the regular term ends. A replacement is appointed. Does the replacement receive a new full $7$-year term?

**Solution:**

1. An appointment to a vacancy is only for the unexpired term of the predecessor.
2. The vacancy occurred before the original term ended.
3. Therefore, the replacement serves only the remaining part of that term, not a new full $7$-year term.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Age Means At Least $35$

> Do not read the age rule as "older than $35$."

A person who is exactly $35$ years old at appointment meets the age requirement.

### #2: Competitive Exam Exceptions Are Limited

> Do not treat every civil service position as an exception from competitive examination.

The listed exceptions are policy-determining, primarily confidential, and highly technical positions. Merit and fitness still govern appointments.

### #3: A Vacancy Does Not Start a New Full Term

> A replacement does not automatically receive a new $7$-year term.

The replacement serves only the unexpired part of the predecessor's term.

### #4: No Temporary or Acting CSC Member

> Do not confuse ordinary temporary government work with membership in the Commission.

A Chairman or Commissioner cannot be appointed or designated in a temporary or acting capacity.

### #5: Independence Comes With Conflict Rules

> A Commission member cannot freely keep another office, job, profession, or conflicting business interest.

Article IX-A sets limits on outside work and financial interests during a member's tenure to protect independent public service.
