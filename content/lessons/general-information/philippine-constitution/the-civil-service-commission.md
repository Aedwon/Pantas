## Core Concept

Article IX-B of the $1987$ Constitution covers the **Civil Service Commission**, or CSC. It is the government's **central personnel agency**. This means it leads the system for managing government workers.

### What the CSC Does

The CSC builds the **career service**, where people can develop a lasting government career based on their skills and work. It strengthens the system for choosing qualified workers and rewarding good service. Personal ties should not replace proof that someone can do the job.

The Commission brings together training programs for workers at different levels. Training can help a worker learn better ways to serve the public and prepare for greater duties. Career development means helping workers build these skills over time.

The CSC also promotes honest, efficient, helpful, and courteous service. It works to make public officials answer for how they do their jobs. These duties go beyond giving civil service examinations.

### Composition and Qualifications

The CSC has one Chairman and two Commissioners. Each member must meet all of these requirements:

- Be a natural-born Philippine citizen.
- Be at least $35$ years old at appointment.
- Have proven capacity for public administration.
- Not have been a candidate for any elective position in the election immediately before appointment.

A **natural-born citizen** is a Philippine citizen from birth who needs no act to acquire or complete that citizenship. For example, a child born to a Filipino parent can be a citizen from birth. Becoming a citizen later through naturalization does not by itself make someone natural-born.

Article IV also gives a specific exception. People born before January $17$, $1973$, to Filipino mothers who elect Philippine citizenship upon reaching the age of majority are deemed natural-born. Here, **elect** means formally choose Philippine citizenship, and **age of majority** means the legal age of adulthood.

**Proven capacity for public administration** means a person has shown the ability to run government work well. A record of managing public staff, services, and resources can help show this ability. The Constitution does not require one named degree, job, or test as the sole proof.

The candidacy rule concerns the election immediately before appointment. It does not say that a person who ever ran for office is barred forever.

### Appointment and Term

The President appoints CSC members with the consent of the **Commission on Appointments**. That body reviews and gives consent to appointments covered by this rule.

A regular term is $7$ years without reappointment. The first appointees under the Constitution had staggered terms: $7$ years for the Chairman, $5$ for one Commissioner, and $3$ for the other. Their terms ended at different times.

A replacement for a member who leaves early serves only the **unexpired term**, meaning the time left in that member's term. The replacement does not begin a fresh $7$-year term. No member may be appointed or designated in a temporary or acting capacity.

### Civil Service Coverage

The civil service covers all government branches and their offices. It includes subdivisions, such as cities and provinces, and agencies that carry out public duties. **Instrumentalities** are government bodies used to perform public work.

Coverage also includes government-owned or controlled corporations with **original charters**. An original charter is a special law that creates the corporation itself. This differs from forming a corporation under the general corporation law.

For a government corporation, check how it was created. Government ownership alone does not establish that it has an original charter.

### Merit, Fitness, and Examination Exceptions

Appointments must be based on **merit and fitness**. Merit concerns a person's qualifications and demonstrated ability. Fitness concerns whether the person is suited to the duties of the position.

For example, relevant skills and a strong work record can support a hiring decision. Friendship with an official does not show the ability to do the work. Passing an exam alone does not guarantee an appointment.

As far as practicable, merit and fitness are determined by **competitive examination**. This is an exam used to assess applicants against a common standard. The Constitution names exceptions for these positions:

- **Policy-determining:** the main work involves setting government policies or directions.
- **Primarily confidential:** the work mainly requires close personal trust with the official served, beyond the trust expected in an ordinary job.
- **Highly technical:** the work requires a high level of special knowledge or skill.

These are exceptions to the competitive-examination rule. Merit and fitness still apply. A position does not become primarily confidential just because it handles some private records.

### Worker Protection and Political Limits

A civil service officer or employee may be removed or suspended only for a cause provided by law. **Security of tenure** is this protection against losing a position without a lawful cause. It does not excuse misconduct or prevent lawful discipline.

Civil service officers and employees may not take part, directly or indirectly, in electioneering or a partisan political campaign. Officials holding political offices, such as elected mayors, are an exception. They may campaign within legal limits. **Electioneering** means working to help a candidate or political party win. Voting is different from campaigning for a candidate.

### Independence and Conflict Rules

Article IX-A makes the CSC an independent Constitutional Commission. Its decisions and work must be protected from outside control. Several rules support that independence.

Members' salaries are fixed by law and cannot be reduced during their tenure, meaning their time in office. The CSC also has **fiscal autonomy**, or independence in managing its approved funds. Its approved annual funding must be released automatically and regularly.

During their tenure, members cannot hold another office or job. They cannot practice a profession, such as taking private clients as a lawyer. They also cannot actively manage or control a business that their official duties may affect.

A member cannot have a direct or indirect financial interest in a government contract. The same restriction covers a government-granted franchise or privilege, meaning official permission or a special right to conduct an activity. It also covers dealings with government subdivisions, agencies, instrumentalities, government corporations, and their subsidiaries, which are companies they control.

These rules prevent private work and money interests from interfering with Commission duties.

## Examples

### #1: Commission Membership

How many members make up the CSC, and what positions do they hold?

**Solution:**

1. The CSC has one Chairman and two Commissioners.
2. Add the positions:

$$
1 + 2 = 3
$$

3. There are $3$ members in total.

### #2: Age at Appointment

Ana is exactly $35$ years old on her appointment date. Ben will turn $35$ the next day. Who meets the age requirement on that date?

**Solution:**

1. A member must be at least $35$ at appointment.
2. Ana has reached that age. Ben has not.
3. Ana meets the age requirement. Ben does not yet meet it.

### #3: Citizenship from Birth

Lina was a Philippine citizen from birth through her Filipino father. She needed no later act to acquire or complete her citizenship. Does she meet the CSC's citizenship requirement?

**Solution:**

Lina meets it because she is a natural-born Philippine citizen. She must still meet the other appointment requirements.

### #4: Deemed Natural-Born

Rosa was born before January $17$, $1973$, to a Filipino mother. She elected Philippine citizenship upon reaching the age of majority under Article IV, Section $1(3)$. Does the act of electing citizenship keep her from meeting the natural-born requirement?

**Solution:**

1. Article IV expressly deems people covered by this election-of-citizenship rule natural-born.
2. Rosa's facts meet that rule.
3. She meets the natural-born requirement despite having performed that act.

### #5: Evidence of Administrative Ability

A nominee presents records of successfully managing staff and public services in a government office. Another person says only that the nominee is a close friend. Which evidence better supports proven capacity for public administration?

**Solution:**

1. The requirement concerns shown ability to manage government work.
2. The management records give evidence of that ability. Friendship does not.
3. The records better support the requirement. No single job title automatically proves the nominee meets it.

### #6: Prior Candidacy

A CSC nominee ran for mayor in the election immediately preceding the planned appointment. The nominee lost. Does losing remove the constitutional bar?

**Solution:**

1. The rule concerns being a candidate in that election.
2. It does not depend on winning.
3. The nominee does not meet this qualification.

### #7: Who Appoints Members?

Which authorities take part in appointing a regular CSC Commissioner?

**Solution:**

The President appoints the Commissioner with the consent of the Commission on Appointments.

### #8: Government Corporation Coverage

Corporation A was created by a special law as a government corporation. Corporation B is government-owned but was formed under the general corporation law and has no original charter. Which meets Article IX-B's original-charter condition?

**Solution:**

1. An original charter is a special law creating the corporation itself.
2. Corporation A has that charter. Corporation B does not.
3. Corporation A meets the condition. Government ownership alone does not make Corporation B meet it.

### #9: Merit in Hiring and Career Development

An agency compares applicants' relevant skills and work records. It also trains current workers to handle greater duties. How do these actions relate to the CSC's role?

**Solution:**

1. Comparing skills and records supports appointments based on merit and fitness.
2. Training helps workers build skills for their careers.
3. Both actions support the CSC's personnel and career-development duties.

### #10: Examination Exception

A position is correctly classified as highly technical. May the appointing authority ignore merit and fitness because the competitive-examination exception applies?

**Solution:**

1. Highly technical positions are excepted from the competitive-examination rule.
2. The exception does not remove merit and fitness.
3. The authority must still choose someone qualified and suited to the work.

### #11: Removal for Cause

A permanent civil service employee is suspended only because a supervisor dislikes the employee. No cause provided by law is given. Which protection is involved?

**Solution:**

1. Removal or suspension must have a cause provided by law.
2. The stated personal dislike supplies no such cause.
3. The suspension conflicts with security of tenure.

### #12: Partisan Campaigning

A civil service employee uses official work time to ask people to vote for a political party. Which constitutional restriction applies?

**Solution:**

The employee is taking part in a partisan political campaign. Article IX-B bars civil service officers and employees from that activity.

### #13: Full Term and Vacancy

A Commissioner completes a full $7$-year term. Another Commissioner leaves with $2$ years remaining. May the first receive another full term, and how long does the second member's replacement serve?

**Solution:**

1. The completed term is without reappointment. The first Commissioner cannot receive another term.
2. A vacancy appointment covers only the remaining term.
3. The replacement serves the remaining $2$ years.

### #14: Acting Membership

An official proposes naming an acting CSC Commissioner while a permanent appointment is being arranged. Is an acting designation allowed?

**Solution:**

No. The Constitution bars both temporary and acting appointments or designations as CSC members.

### #15: Outside Work and Business Interests

A sitting Commissioner plans to keep a paid teaching job and actively manage a company whose business is affected by CSC decisions. Are these arrangements allowed during the Commissioner's tenure?

**Solution:**

1. A Commission member may not hold another job. The paid teaching job falls under that ban.
2. The member also may not actively manage a business affected by official duties.
3. Both arrangements are barred.

### #16: A Government Contract

A Commissioner plans to gain an indirect financial interest in a company contract with a city government. Does the restriction apply even though the contract is not with the CSC?

**Solution:**

1. The rule covers direct and indirect financial interests in government contracts.
2. It includes contracts with government subdivisions, such as cities.
3. The restriction applies to this arrangement.

### #17: Protection of Funds

An official threatens to delay the CSC's approved annual funding to pressure it into changing a decision. Which safeguard answers this threat?

**Solution:**

Fiscal autonomy protects the Commission's control over its approved funds. The Constitution requires automatic and regular release of its approved annual funding.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Exactly $35$ Is Enough

> Check age on the appointment date.

“At least $35$” includes exactly $35$. Turning $35$ after appointment does not meet the rule on that date.

### #2: Natural-Born Has an Express Exception

> Do not reject every person who elected Philippine citizenship.

Article IV deems people covered by Section $1(3)$ natural-born. Check the birth date, Filipino mother, and election of citizenship at the age of majority.

### #3: Candidacy Means the Immediately Preceding Election

> Do not replace the rule with “ran last year” or “ever ran.”

The relevant election is the one immediately before appointment. Losing that election does not remove the bar.

### #4: Exam Exceptions Still Require Ability

> No competitive exam does not mean no qualifications.

Policy-determining, primarily confidential, and highly technical positions still require merit and fitness. A job's actual duties matter when applying these categories.

### #5: Government Ownership Is Not an Original Charter

> Check the law that created the corporation.

For the constitutional coverage rule, a special creating law differs from registration under the general corporation law.

### #6: Replacement Terms Do Not Restart

> A vacancy does not create a fresh $7$-year term.

The replacement finishes the unexpired term. Temporary or acting CSC membership is also barred.

### #7: Outside-Work Rules Are Separate

> A second job need not involve a government contract to be barred.

Holding another job and practicing a profession are separate bans. The business-management ban concerns businesses affected by official duties, while the financial-interest rule covers government contracts, franchises, and privileges.
