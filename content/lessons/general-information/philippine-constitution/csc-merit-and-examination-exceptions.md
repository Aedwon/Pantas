## Core Concept

### Merit and Fitness Beyond the Exam

Article IX-B, Section $2(2)$, sets the rule for civil service hiring.

Appointments must be made **only according to merit and fitness**.

1. **Merit** means qualifications and proven ability.
2. **Fitness** means suitability for the exact duties of the post.

As far as practicable, merit and fitness are measured by **competitive examination** against a common standard.

Passing an exam never guarantees hiring on its own. Agencies must still verify duty-specific skills, and friendship never replaces merit.

Check two applicants for an accounting clerk post:

1. Applicant A passed the civil service exam and has $3$ years of government bookkeeping work.
2. Applicant B passed the same exam, lacks bookkeeping skill, and relies on friendship with the mayor.

Applicant A meets merit and fitness for the post; Applicant B lacks job fitness.

:::pair #merit-and-fitness-fit
Right: Check both qualifications and job fitness; passing an exam alone never guarantees hiring.
Wrong: Treating a passing exam score as forcing appointment without checking suitability for the job.
Section $2(2)$ requires merit and fitness for the specific duties, not an exam score alone.
:::

:::check
A city driver with $15$ years of service brings a mayor's endorsement for chief civil engineer, skipping credential review. Does that follow Section $2(2)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $2(2)$ requires appointments based on merit and fitness for the post's duties, which seniority in another job and endorsements cannot replace.
:::

### The Three Competitive-Exam Exceptions

Article IX-B, Section $2(2)$, lists three closed exceptions to **competitive examination**:

1. **Policy-determining:** main work sets government policies or agency directions.
2. **Primarily confidential:** work mainly relies on close personal trust with the official served.
3. **Highly technical:** work demands high specialized scientific, technical, or professional skill.

Handling some private files alone does not make a clerk primarily confidential.

These categories waive **only the competitive exam**. Merit and fitness still apply to every appointment.

:::why
Why waive competitive exams for these three roles? Written group tests cannot measure close personal trust, top policy judgment, or rare specialist mastery.
:::

Sort three posts by the three exceptions:

1. A department head setting national transport policy is **policy-determining**.
2. A governor's personal confidant giving sensitive political counsel is **primarily confidential**.
3. A nuclear safety physicist advising on reactor limits is **highly technical**.

:::pair #policy-determining-category
Right: Match a post whose main work sets government policies to policy-determining, using actual duties.
Wrong: Calling a policy-setting post primarily confidential or highly technical because policy touches trust or skill.
Match the primary duty: policy-setting is policy-determining, close trust is confidential, and rare skill is technical.
:::

:::pair #competitive-exam-exceptions-exist
Right: Competitive exams apply as far as practicable, with three exceptions that still require merit and fitness.
Wrong: Claiming every civil service post without exception must use a competitive examination.
Section $2(2)$ expressly exempts policy-determining, primarily confidential, and highly technical posts from competitive exams.
:::

:::check
A clerk opens sealed payroll mail each morning as part of routine filing. Does touching private files make the post primarily confidential?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Primarily confidential status requires close personal trust beyond ordinary job duties, not merely seeing private records.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Exam Score vs. Job Duties

Applicant M passed a general civil service exam and seeks a regional language translator post without translation skill. Applicant N is the director's cousin with no test or translation record. May either skip a translation fitness check?

**Solution:**

1. Apply Article IX-B, Section $2(2)$, to both applicants: hiring requires merit and fitness for the job's duties.
2. Check Applicant M: passing a general exam alone does not prove translation skill.
3. Check Applicant N: family ties never substitute for required merit and fitness.

Answer: Neither applicant may skip a check of merit and fitness for translation duties.

**Check:** Verify both proven qualifications and suitability for the specific post.

### #2: Sorting Three Exempt Positions

An agency fills three non-exam posts. One undersecretary shapes national housing policy. A personal secretary guards a cabinet officer's private schedule. A marine seismologist analyzes fault data. Match each post to its exception.

**Solution:**

1. Shaping national housing policy matches **policy-determining**.
2. Holding close personal trust for private schedules matches **primarily confidential**.
3. Analyzing specialized fault data matches **highly technical**.

Answer: Policy-determining (undersecretary), primarily confidential (personal secretary), and highly technical (seismologist).

**Check:** Match primary job duties directly to the three closed constitutional exceptions.

### #3: Merit Inside Exempt Appointments

An agency classifies a senior toxicologist post as highly technical under Section $2(2)$. Because no competitive exam applies, a board member nominates a relative trained only in retail sales, with no toxicology background. Is that appointment valid?

**Solution:**

1. Identify what Section $2(2)$ waives for a highly technical post and what hiring standard remains in force.
2. Compare the toxicologist post's scientific duties with the candidate's retail-sales background.

:::try
Finish the last step: assess whether the candidate's background fits the post and rule on the appointment.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the appointment fails merit and fitness.** Classifying a toxicologist post as highly technical waives only the competitive examination under Article IX-B, Section $2(2)$. Retail-sales work does not show merit and fitness for specialized toxicology duties.

**Check:** Three posts waive the competitive exam, while zero posts waive merit and fitness.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Passing an exam: exam success never guarantees hiring without fitness for the job's duties. {#merit-and-fitness-fit}
- Policy-setting posts: classify policy-making posts as policy-determining, not confidential or technical. {#policy-determining-category}
- Exam exceptions: remember the three exceptions exist, and keep checking merit and fitness. {#competitive-exam-exceptions-exist}
:::
