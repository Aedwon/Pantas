## Core Concept

Article III of the 1987 Constitution is the Bill of Rights. It contains exactly **22 sections** that safeguard individual liberties against state infringement.

### Civil and Political Rights (Sections 1 to 11)

Sections 1 to 11 establish core civil and political guarantees:
- Due process of law and equal protection of the laws.
- Security against unreasonable searches and seizures.
- Privacy of communication and correspondence.
- Freedom of speech, expression, the press, peaceful assembly, and petitioning government for redress of grievances.
- Free exercise and enjoyment of religious profession and worship.
- Liberty of abode and changing the same, and freedom of travel.
- Right of the people to information on matters of public concern.
- Right to form associations, unions, or societies.
- Non-impairment of obligations of contracts.
- Free access to courts and adequate legal assistance.

### Rights of the Accused (Sections 12 to 22)

Sections 12 to 22 safeguard persons accused of crimes:
- Rights under custodial investigation, including the right to remain silent and to competent counsel.
- Right to bail, except for offenses punishable by reclusion perpetua when evidence of guilt is strong.
- Presumption of innocence, right to be heard, right to counsel, and right to speedy, impartial trial.
- Privilege of the writ of habeas corpus.
- Right to speedy disposition of cases before all judicial, quasi-judicial, or administrative bodies.
- Protection against self-incrimination.
- Freedom from political detention and involuntary servitude.
- Prohibition of excessive fines and cruel, degrading, or inhuman punishment.
- Freedom from imprisonment for debt or non-payment of a poll tax.
- Protection against double jeopardy.
- Absolute prohibition of ex post facto laws and bills of attainder.

## Examples

### #1: Total Number of Sections in the Bill of Rights

A student reviewing Article III of the 1987 Constitution needs to know how many sections make up the Bill of Rights. What is the exact number of sections?

**Solution:**
Recall the official section count of Article III.

1. Article III of the 1987 Constitution is the Bill of Rights.
2. It contains Sections 1 through 22.
3. Therefore, the Bill of Rights contains exactly 22 sections.

### #2: Categorizing Bill of Rights Sections

A question asks which group of sections in Article III covers the rights of the accused, such as protection against self-incrimination and double jeopardy. Which section range is correct?

**Solution:**
Divide Article III into its two main functional groups.

1. Sections 1 to 11 establish civil and political rights, such as free speech and due process.
2. Sections 12 to 22 establish protections for accused persons, such as bail, presumption of innocence, and protection against double jeopardy.
3. Therefore, Sections 12 to 22 protect the accused.

### #3: Imprisonment for Debt

A citizen is threatened with jail time for failing to pay a personal financial debt. Does Article III allow imprisonment for non-payment of debt?

**Solution:**
Check the specific protections under Article III.

1. Article III explicitly forbids imprisonment for debt or non-payment of a poll tax.
2. This protection is found in the second group of sections protecting individuals from improper state detention.
3. Therefore, the state cannot imprison a citizen for non-payment of a debt.
