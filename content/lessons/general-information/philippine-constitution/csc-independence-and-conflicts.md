## Core Concept

### Constitutional Commissions and Fiscal Autonomy

Article IX-A, Section $1$, creates three **independent Constitutional Commissions**:

1. The **Civil Service Commission (CSC)**.
2. The **Commission on Elections (COMELEC)**.
3. The **Commission on Audit (COA)**.

Agencies like the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), planning bodies like NEDA, and departments like DBM are not Article IX Commissions.

Two financial shields protect the CSC from outside control:

1. **Salary protection (Section $3$):** member salaries are fixed by law and cannot decrease during tenure.
2. **Fiscal autonomy (Section $5$):** approved annual appropriations must be released **automatically and regularly**.

:::why
Why protect salaries and annual fund releases? If budget officials could freeze funds or cut pay, they could pressure CSC rulings.
:::

Test a finance memo cutting a sitting CSC Commissioner's salary by $10\%$ during a budget shortfall:

1. Article IX-A, Section $3$, fixes member salaries by law and bars any decrease during tenure.
2. Even during tight budgets, a sitting member's salary cannot be lowered.

:::pair #only-three-commissions
Right: Treat only CSC, COMELEC, and COA as Article IX Constitutional Commissions.
Wrong: Assuming bodies like the Securities and Exchange Commission or NEDA are Article IX Commissions.
A statutory agency or department does not become an Article IX Commission from having Commission in its name.
:::

:::pair #fiscal-autonomy-automatic-release
Right: Fiscal autonomy requires automatic, regular release of the CSC's approved annual budget without conditions.
Wrong: Claiming approved annual CSC funds may be delayed until the Commission changes a ruling.
Article IX-A, Section $5$, protects the entire approved annual budget from conditional budget holds.
:::

:::check
A budget officer argues that fiscal autonomy protects only member paychecks and allows delaying CSC operating funds. Is that claim valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article IX-A, Section $5$, mandates automatic and regular release of the CSC's entire approved annual budget.
:::

### Outside Work, Practice, and Financial Interests

Article IX-A, Section $2$, sets four **separate** conflict rules for CSC members during **tenure**, meaning time in office:

1. Never hold any **other office or employment** (like a paid teaching job).
2. Never engage in the **practice of any profession** (like taking private legal clients).
3. Never actively **manage or control any business** that CSC duties may affect.
4. Never hold a **direct or indirect financial interest** in any government **contract, franchise, or privilege**.

Here, a **privilege** means official permission or a special right to run an activity. The financial-interest rule covers contracts with national offices, subdivisions, agencies, instrumentalities, GOCCs, and **subsidiaries** (companies they control).

Screen two side activities by a sitting CSC Commissioner:

1. Paid evening teaching breaks the ban on holding another employment.
2. Taking private non-government legal clients breaks the separate ban on practicing any profession.

:::pair #separate-tenure-conflict-bans
Right: Outside jobs and practicing any profession are barred during tenure, even with private clients and zero CSC contracts.
Wrong: Allowing private law practice during tenure when clients pay privately and hold no CSC contract.
Section $2$ sets four separate bans; private fees never excuse practicing a profession.
:::

:::check
A sitting CSC Commissioner wants to manage an unpaid family review center preparing examinees for civil service tests. Does Section $2$ bar that role?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Article IX-A, Section $2$, bars actively managing or controlling any business affected by CSC functions.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Identifying Article IX Commissions and Pay Shields

A guide lists CSC, SEC, and DBM as the three Article IX Commissions. It adds that Congress may lower a sitting Commissioner's pay during budget cuts. Correct both errors.

**Solution:**

1. Under Article IX-A, Section $1$, the three Constitutional Commissions are CSC, COMELEC, and COA, not SEC or DBM.
2. Under Article IX-A, Section $3$, member salaries cannot decrease during tenure.

Answer: Only CSC, COMELEC, and COA are Article IX Commissions, and member salaries cannot be cut during tenure.

**Check:** Keep the three Article IX Commissions distinct from statutory agencies and executive departments.

### #2: Private Practice and City Contracts

A sitting CSC Commissioner plans to accept Saturday family-law clients for private fees. The Commissioner also keeps a silent share in a firm bidding on a city supply contract. Which plans break Section $2$?

**Solution:**

1. Taking private legal clients is practicing a profession, which is barred during tenure despite private fees.
2. Holding an indirect financial interest in a city supply contract breaks the ban covering local subdivisions.

Answer: Both plans violate Article IX-A, Section $2$.

**Check:** Test all four bans separately: second job, profession practice, affected business control, and government contract interest.

### #3: Quarterly Fund Holds and Subsidiary Contracts

A budget bureau freezes the CSC's approved quarterly release until the Commission postpones a personnel audit. Meanwhile, a sitting CSC Commissioner holds a silent $20\%$ stake in a firm bidding on a GOCC subsidiary's catering contract. Rule on both.

**Solution:**

1. Under Article IX-A, Section $5$, fiscal autonomy requires automatic and regular release of approved annual CSC appropriations.
2. Under Section $2$, members cannot hold any direct or indirect financial interest in contracts with GOCCs or their subsidiaries.

:::try
Finish the last step: state the constitutional ruling for both the quarterly fund freeze and the silent stake.
:::answer
The answer is **Both the quarterly fund freeze and the silent stake violate Article IX-A.** Fiscal autonomy bars conditioning or delaying approved CSC releases. Section $2$ also bars indirect financial interests in contracts with any GOCC or its subsidiary.

**Check:** Match conditional fund holds to Section $5$ fiscal autonomy, and indirect GOCC-subsidiary interests to Section $2$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Article IX Commissions: count only CSC, COMELEC, and COA, never statutory agencies or departments. {#only-three-commissions}
- Fiscal autonomy: release the entire approved annual budget automatically and regularly with zero conditions. {#fiscal-autonomy-automatic-release}
- Separate conflict bans: bar any second job or professional practice during tenure even with private fees. {#separate-tenure-conflict-bans}
:::
