## Core Concept

### Amendments Only and Both Signature Minimums

Article XVII, Section $2$, allows **people's initiative**, a proposal made directly through a voters' petition, for **amendments only**. Unlike Congress and a convention, initiative can never propose a revision.

A petition must meet two signature minimums at once:

1. At least $12\%$ of total registered voters nationwide.
2. At least $3\%$ of registered voters in **every legislative district**.

A **legislative district** is an area whose voters elect a district House representative. A large national total never replaces a missing $3\%$ district minimum.

Check a hypothetical petition for a single term-limit edit, with $500,000$ voters nationwide and $50,000$ in District X:

1. Check scope: one term-limit edit is an amendment, fitting initiative.
2. Check the $12\%$ national minimum: $0.12 \times 500,000 = 60,000$ signatures nationwide.
3. Check District X's $3\%$ minimum: $0.03 \times 50,000 = 1,500$ signatures in District X, plus $3\%$ in every other district.

:::pair #initiative-amendments-only
Right: People's initiative may propose **amendments only**; Congress and a convention may propose either amendments or revisions.
Wrong: Treating all three routes as able to propose revisions when enough voters sign.
Enough signatures never let people's initiative propose a revision under Article XVII, Section $2$.
:::

:::pair #national-12-every-district-3
Right: A petition needs at least $12\%$ of registered voters nationwide **and** at least $3\%$ in **every** legislative district.
Wrong: Dropping the district minimum once $12\%$ nationwide is met, or checking $3\%$ in only one district.
Both the $12\%$ national minimum and the $3\%$ minimum in every district must pass.
:::

:::check
In a country with $10$ districts, a petition gathers $15\%$ of registered voters nationwide and $5\%$ in $9$ districts, yet only $2\%$ in District $10$. Does it pass Section $2$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $2$ requires at least $3\%$ of registered voters in **every** legislative district, so $2\%$ in District $10$ fails.
:::

### Five-Year Limits and Implementing Law

Article XVII, Section $2$, sets two $5$-year limits on initiative amendments:

1. **Initial bar**: no initiative amendment was allowed within $5$ years after ratification of the $1987$ Constitution.
2. **Ongoing interval**: afterward, initiative is allowed **no more than once every $5$ years**.

Congress must provide the implementing law. These are the constitutional rules, without any claim about whether this route is usable today.

Check two dates under Section $2$: a petition filed in $1990$, and one filed $4$ years after a prior ratified initiative amendment:

1. Check $1990$: within $5$ years of $1987$ ratification, so the initial $5$-year bar blocks it.
2. Check $4$ years after a prior initiative: under $5$ years passed, so the spacing limit blocks it.

:::why
Why require $3\%$ in every district and $5$-year spacing? Every-district rules stop dense areas from acting alone, and $5$-year spacing avoids nonstop constitutional petitions.
:::

:::pair #both-five-year-limits
Right: Section $2$ barred initiative for $5$ years after $1987$ ratification and allows it at most once every $5$ years afterward.
Wrong: Allowing yearly initiatives after the first $5$-year bar, or using a $3$-year waiting period.
Both the initial post-$1987$ bar and the later spacing rule are $5$ years.
:::

:::check
A hypothetical initiative amendment was ratified in May $2020$. What is the earliest month and year another initiative amendment could be authorized?
:::answer
The answer is **May $2025$**. Article XVII, Section $2$ allows initiative amendments no oftener than once every $5$ years.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Check Both Signature Rules

Assume $2,000,000$ registered voters nationwide and $60,000$ in one district. What minimums apply to an initiative petition?

**Solution:**

1. Find the $12\%$ national minimum:

$$
0.12\times2{,}000{,}000=240{,}000
$$

2. Find that district's $3\%$ minimum:

$$
0.03\times60{,}000=1{,}800
$$

3. Check every other district's $3\%$ minimum too, since a high national total cannot replace a missing district minimum.

Answer: At least $240,000$ signatures nationwide, $1,800$ in that district, and $3\%$ in every other legislative district.

**Check:** Verify $12\%$ nationwide and $3\%$ per district independently, because failing either fails Section $2$.

### #2: Initiative Timing

An initiative amendment was ratified $3$ years ago. May another be authorized now under Article XVII, Section $2$?

**Solution:**

1. Recall Section $2$'s interval: at most once every $5$ years.
2. Compare elapsed time ($3$ years) with $5$ years: $3 < 5$.

Answer: No, because only $3$ years have passed under the once-every-$5$-years limit.

**Check:** Five full years must pass between authorized initiative amendments.

### #3: Reviewing a Complete Petition

A citizens' group files a petition to replace the two-house Congress with a single appointed assembly. It collects $16\%$ of registered voters nationwide and $4\%$ in every district, $7$ years after the last initiative. Does this satisfy Article XVII, Section $2$?

**Solution:**

1. Check signatures and timing: $16\%$ clears $12\%$ nationwide, $4\%$ clears $3\%$ per district, and $7$ years clears $5$ years.
2. Check scope: replacing the bicameral Congress with an appointed assembly alters the basic plan of government.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether the petition satisfies Section $2$ and explain why signatures and timing do not save it.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the petition fails because people's initiative can propose amendments only, never a revision.** Replacing Congress with an appointed assembly alters basic structure, which is a revision. Meeting $12\%$ nationwide, $3\%$ per district, and the $5$-year limit cannot make a revision valid under Section $2$.

**Check:** Check scope (amendment vs. revision) first on any initiative petition, because Section $2$ excludes revisions regardless of signature counts.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Initiative scope: people's initiative proposes **amendments only**, never revisions. {#initiative-amendments-only}
- National and district signatures: check $12\%$ nationwide **and** $3\%$ in **every** legislative district. {#national-12-every-district-3}
- Five-year limits: apply both the initial $5$-year bar after $1987$ and the $5$-year spacing limit afterward. {#both-five-year-limits}
:::
