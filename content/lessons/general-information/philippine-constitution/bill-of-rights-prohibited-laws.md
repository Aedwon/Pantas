## Core Concept

### Section $22$: Ex Post Facto Laws and Bills of Attainder

Article III, Section $22$ states: **No ex post facto law or bill of attainder shall be enacted.** Each ban targets a different legislative abuse:

1. An **ex post facto law** applies a harmful criminal-law change **backward in time**. For example, it may criminalize an earlier lawful act, raise a past crime's penalty, or lower required proof.
2. A **bill of attainder** is a law that **inflicts punishment without a judicial trial** on named people or an easily identified group.

Keep Section $22$ separate from Section $10$ (laws impairing contracts). Section $10$ covers contract duties, not backward criminal laws or legislative punishment.

Test a law raising the penalty for smuggling from $2$ years to $6$ years and applying it to shipments from last year:

1. Check trial process: courts still hold trials, so it is not a bill of attainder.
2. Check backward penal change: raising the penalty for past acts makes it an **ex post facto law**.

:::why
Why does Section $22$ ban both laws? People must know what the law forbids before acting. Also, only courts after a trial may decide guilt and punish.
:::

:::pair #ex-post-facto-vs-attainder
Right: An *ex post facto* law applies harmful criminal changes backward; a *bill of attainder* punishes named people without a trial.
Wrong: Reversing the two labels between backward penal changes and legislative punishment without trial.
Ask whether the law reaches backward in criminal law (*ex post facto*) or punishes without a court trial (*bill of attainder*).
:::

:::check
Congress passes a law naming four former officials and ordering them jailed without a court trial. Which Section $22$ ban is broken?
:::answer
The answer is **The ban on bills of attainder**. Punishing named people by legislation without a judicial trial is a bill of attainder.
:::

### When One Law Breaks Both Bans

A single statute can violate **both** bans in Section $22$ at once. Check each test separately:

1. Does it punish a named person or identifiable group without a judicial trial? If yes, **bill of attainder**.
2. Does it punish or worsen penalties for acts done before the law took effect? If yes, **ex post facto law**.

Test a statute naming former town treasurers and fining them without trial for lawful budget transfers done three years ago:

1. Check trial process: fining named treasurers without a court trial makes it a **bill of attainder**.
2. Check backward reach: punishing transfers that were lawful three years ago also makes it an **ex post facto law**.

:::pair #one-law-both-bans
Right: Check both tests; a law punishing an identified group without trial for earlier lawful acts violates both bans.
Wrong: Picking only one ban when a law both punishes without trial and reaches back to earlier lawful acts.
Punishing an identified group without trial breaks the bill-of-attainder ban, and punishing earlier lawful acts breaks the *ex post facto* ban.
:::

:::check
A new statute raises prison terms for last year's illegal fishing while still requiring a full court trial. Which Section $22$ ban applies?
:::answer
The answer is **Only the ex post facto ban**. The law increases past penalties retroactively, yet courts still conduct the trial.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Lowering Proof for Past Offenses

A $2026$ statute lets courts convict drivers for $2025$ hit-and-run cases using only one unsworn traffic ticket instead of full trial proof. Regular courts still hold trials. Which Section $22$ ban applies?

**Solution:**

1. Check for trial: courts still hold trials, so the statute is not a bill of attainder.
2. Check backward reach: lowering the proof needed to convict for past acts makes the law worse for the accused backward in time.

Answer: The ban on *ex post facto* laws under Article III, Section $22$.

**Check:** An *ex post facto* law includes any backward criminal change that lowers required proof for earlier offenses, even when courts hold trials.

### #2: A New Penalty for a Past Act

In $2026$, Congress raises the prison term for illegal logging from $4$ years to $10$ years. The statute applies that heavier term to loggers charged for cutting trees in $2024$. Regular courts still conduct trials. Which Section $22$ ban does the statute violate?

**Solution:**

1. Check for trial: regular court trials still take place, so it is not a bill of attainder.
2. Check backward effect: the heavier prison term applies to logging done before the statute took effect.

Answer: The ban on *ex post facto* laws under Article III, Section $22$.

**Check:** Raising criminal punishment retroactively defines an *ex post facto* law, distinct from a bill of attainder or contract impairment.

### #3: Identifiable Group and Past Conduct

In $2026$, a statute targets every customs clerk who attended a lawful $2024$ budget forum. By statute alone and with no court trial, it fines those clerks and bars them from public jobs. Rule on the statute under both Section $22$ tests.

**Solution:**

1. Test the statute's targeted group and lack of a court trial against Section $22$'s bill-of-attainder rule.
2. Test the statute's punishment of $2024$ forum attendance against Section $22$'s *ex post facto* rule.

:::try
Finish the last step: state which Section $22$ bans the statute violates and explain why avoiding personal names does not save it.
:::answer
The answer is **The statute violates both bans in Section $22$: the ban on bills of attainder and the ban on ex post facto laws.** Targeting an identified group without trial triggers the bill-of-attainder ban, and penalizing lawful $2024$ attendance violates the *ex post facto* ban.

**Check:** Both Section $22$ tests apply whenever a law punishes an identified group without a trial for earlier lawful acts.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Swapping terms: link backward penal changes to *ex post facto* laws and non-trial punishment to bills of attainder. {#ex-post-facto-vs-attainder}
- Law names a group and reaches backward: test both Section $22$ bans separately, since one law can violate both. {#one-law-both-bans}
:::
