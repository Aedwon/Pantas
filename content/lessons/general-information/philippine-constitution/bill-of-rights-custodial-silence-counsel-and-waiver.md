## Core Concept

### Section $12(1)$: When Custodial Safeguards Apply

**Custodial investigation** is questioning a suspect taken into custody or deprived of freedom in a significant way. Under Republic Act No. $7438$, it includes a police invitation to question someone suspected of an offense.

Once liberty is significantly restricted during questioning, Article III, Section $12(1)$ applies at once. A formal arrest label or a signed statement is not needed first.

Test Section $12(1)$ when police summon a delivery driver to a precinct under an RA $7438$ invitation and guard the door during suspect questioning:

1. Check freedom: guarding the door during suspect questioning significantly restricts liberty.
2. Apply Section $12(1)$: custodial safeguards attach right away without waiting for a formal arrest label.

:::pair #custody-by-restraint
Right: Custodial safeguards attach when a suspect's freedom is significantly restricted during questioning.
Wrong: Waiting for a formal arrest announcement or a signed statement before giving warnings.
Significant restraint during suspect questioning, not an arrest label, triggers Section $12(1)$.
:::

:::check
Officers ask a sidewalk vendor which way a shoplifter ran, and the vendor can leave anytime. Is that custodial investigation?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The vendor is neither suspected of the offense nor deprived of freedom in a significant way.
:::

### Section $12(1)$: Silence and Independent Counsel

Under Article III, Section $12(1)$, officers must tell the suspect of the **right to remain silent** and to **competent and independent counsel**. These warnings are commonly called **Miranda rights**.

Silence lets the suspect refuse to answer. **Competent counsel** gives proper legal help, while **independent counsel** protects the suspect free from investigator control. Counsel should preferably be of the suspect's choice, and free counsel must be provided if the suspect cannot afford one.

Test an indigent mechanic questioned over missing tools who cannot pay for a lawyer and asks for independent counsel:

1. Check payment: inability to pay never removes the right to counsel.
2. Check quality: Section $12(1)$ requires providing a free lawyer who is both competent and free from police control.

:::pair #counsel-free-if-indigent
Right: When a suspect in custody cannot afford a lawyer, the state must provide competent and independent counsel.
Wrong: Requiring proof that the suspect can pay a lawyer before custodial counsel applies.
Poverty never cancels the right to competent and independent counsel under Section $12(1)$.
:::

:::check
A suspect in custody asks for counsel of her choice and stays silent while waiting. May police keep questioning?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article III, Section $12(1)$ protects both silence and counsel during custodial questioning.
:::

### Section $12(1)$: Writing and Counsel for a Waiver

To **waive** a right means to give it up. Under Article III, Section $12(1)$, custodial rights may be waived **only in writing and in the presence of counsel**.

Both conditions must hold together. An oral agreement before a lawyer fails writing, and a signature signed alone fails counsel's presence.

Test a suspect who tells officers aloud, with counsel watching, that she waives her rights without signing:

1. Check counsel: a lawyer heard the statement.
2. Check writing: nothing is in writing, so the waiver fails Section $12(1)$.

:::why
Why require both writing and counsel? Writing prevents disputes over what was agreed, and counsel guards against quiet pressure in custody.
:::

:::pair #waiver-both-writing-and-counsel
Right: A valid custodial waiver requires both a written form and counsel's presence.
Wrong: Treating an oral agreement before counsel, or an officer's memory, as enough.
Both writing and counsel's presence are mandatory under Section $12(1)$.
:::

:::check
A suspect signs a written waiver alone while waiting for an appointed lawyer to arrive. Is that waiver valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A written waiver signed without counsel present fails Section $12(1)$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Choosing Silence

During custodial investigation, a suspect says that she will remain silent. Investigators insist that she must answer their questions. Which right directly applies?

**Solution:**

1. Check the setting: questioning a suspect in custody triggers Article III, Section $12(1)$.
2. Apply the rule: Section $12(1)$ protects the right to remain silent.

Answer: The right to remain silent under Article III, Section $12(1)$.

**Check:** Custodial questioning never lets investigators force answers against a suspect's choice of silence.

### #2: No Money for Counsel

A suspect in custodial investigation asks for a lawyer but cannot afford one. Investigators say that questioning must continue without counsel because the suspect cannot pay. What does Section $12$ require?

**Solution:**

1. Check the right: Section $12(1)$ guarantees competent and independent counsel, preferably of the suspect's choice.
2. Check inability to pay: counsel must be provided if the suspect cannot pay.

Answer: Investigators must provide free competent and independent counsel before questioning.

**Check:** Lack of money never removes the right to counsel under Section $12(1)$.

### #3: Waiver While Alone

A suspect signs a written waiver of custodial rights while no lawyer is present. Does the signature alone meet the constitutional waiver requirements?

**Solution:**

1. Check writing: signing a form satisfies the written requirement.
2. Check counsel: Section $12(1)$ also requires the presence of counsel.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the signature and contrast it with an oral waiver made in counsel's presence.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the signature fails without counsel present**. An oral waiver before counsel also fails because writing is missing. Both requirements in Article III, Section $12(1)$ must exist together.

**Check:** A written waiver without counsel fails presence, while an oral waiver with counsel fails the writing rule.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Suspect detained without a formal arrest label: apply Section $12(1)$ once liberty is significantly restricted. {#custody-by-restraint}
- Suspect cannot pay for a lawyer in custody: provide competent, independent counsel free of charge. {#counsel-free-if-indigent}
- Custodial waiver offered orally or signed alone: require both writing and counsel's presence. {#waiver-both-writing-and-counsel}
:::
