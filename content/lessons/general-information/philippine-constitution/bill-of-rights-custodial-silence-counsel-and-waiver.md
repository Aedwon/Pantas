## Core Concept

### Rights During Custodial Investigation

**Custodial investigation** involves questioning a person suspected of an offense after the person is taken into custody or otherwise deprived of freedom in a significant way. Section $12$ protects the person's freedom to decide whether to answer.

Investigators must inform the person of the right to remain silent. This means the person may refuse to answer questions during that investigation. They must also explain the right to competent and independent counsel, preferably a lawyer the person chooses.

**Competent counsel** is a lawyer able to give proper legal help. **Independent counsel** protects the person's interests, free from control by the investigators. If the person cannot afford a lawyer, one must be provided.

To **waive** a right means to give it up. These custodial rights may be waived only in writing and in the presence of counsel. A casual oral agreement or a signature obtained while the suspect is alone is not enough.

## Examples

### #1: Choosing Silence

During custodial investigation, a suspect says that she will remain silent. Investigators insist that she must answer their questions. Which right directly applies?

**Solution:**

1. Section $12$ protects the right to remain silent during custodial investigation.
2. The suspect may refuse to answer.
3. Investigators must respect that choice and inform her of the protected rights.

### #2: No Money for Counsel

A suspect in custodial investigation asks for a lawyer but cannot afford one. Investigators say that questioning must continue without counsel because the suspect cannot pay. What does Section $12$ require?

**Solution:**

1. The suspect has a right to competent and independent counsel.
2. Counsel must be provided when the suspect cannot afford one.
3. Lack of money does not remove this protection.

### #3: Waiver While Alone

A suspect signs a written waiver of custodial rights while no lawyer is present. Does the signature alone meet the constitutional waiver requirements?

**Solution:**

1. A waiver must be in writing and in the presence of counsel.
2. The written form meets only one of those conditions.
3. Without counsel present, the waiver does not meet the constitutional requirements.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: A Signature Is Not Enough

> A custodial waiver needs writing and counsel's presence.

An oral waiver fails the writing requirement. A written waiver signed alone fails the counsel requirement.
