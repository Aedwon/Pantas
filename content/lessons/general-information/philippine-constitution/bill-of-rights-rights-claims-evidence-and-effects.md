## Core Concept

### Checking Facts, Evidence, and Rules

To **analyze** a rights claim means to study its parts and explain how the facts fit a legal rule. Follow three clear steps:

1. Identify **who acted** and **what happened** using available **evidence**, such as records, witness accounts, or medical findings.
2. Match those facts against the exact Article III right, its conditions, and any stated exceptions.
3. Determine lawful next steps, such as seeking a lawyer, legal aid, or a proper court remedy.

An accusation or a "rights violation" label alone is never proof. Lawful or unlawful detention never strips away Article III, Section $19$'s ban on cruel or degrading treatment.

Test this process when a prisoner reports guards withheld food for three days:

1. Check evidence: review kitchen logs, cellmate accounts, and clinic notes to verify who acted and what happened.
2. Match the rule: compare proven facts against Article III, Section $19$'s protection for detainees.

:::pair #label-is-not-proof
Right: Verify who acted and what happened with evidence, then compare those facts against the right's conditions.
Wrong: Treating a "rights violation" label as proof without checking records or witnesses.
Protection depends on proven facts matching the rule, never on a bare label alone.
:::

:::pair #detainees-keep-protections
Right: Detention never removes Section $19$'s protection against cruel, degrading, or inhuman treatment.
Wrong: Dismissing a detainee's abuse report because being detained removes punishment protections.
Detainees keep their Section $19$ dignity rights, so reported abuse still requires checking the facts.
:::

:::check
A complaint sheet says "Section $19$ violated" with no names, dates, records, or witnesses attached. Is the violation already proved?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A label alone is not proof; an investigator must check records, witnesses, and facts against Section $19$.
:::

### Stating Supported Personal and Family Effects

A sound rights analysis states only the **personal effects** and **family effects** that the record actually supports.

For example, unlawful detention takes away personal **liberty** and causes **missed paid work**, which can leave a family unable to meet daily expenses. Abuse can cause injury and fear.

Never assume a specific harm without supporting facts. If no medical findings appear, never claim a proven physical injury. Likewise, never invent debt cancellation, because Article III, Section $20$ leaves valid debts intact.

Test a record showing a carpenter jailed solely for political views, with three missed workdays and zero medical notes:

1. Check supported effects: lost liberty and lost wages hurting daily family expenses follow from the record.
2. Reject unsupported claims: do not add unproven physical injuries or canceled family loans.

:::why
Why stick strictly to facts in the record? Guessing unproved injuries or fake legal consequences weakens a real rights claim and misstates the law.
:::

:::pair #only-record-supported-effects
Right: Name only personal and family effects backed by the record, such as lost liberty and unpaid workdays.
Wrong: Adding a proven physical injury when the record contains no medical findings.
Match every stated personal or family effect directly to a fact in the record.
:::

:::check
A worker's file proves four days of unlawful custody and missed wages while her bank loan remains unpaid. Does the unlawful detention erase her bank loan?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. State only effects backed by the record, and unlawful detention never cancels a valid loan.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Evidence of Abuse

A detainee reports degrading physical punishment by officers. What should be checked when analyzing the claim?

**Solution:**

1. Gather evidence: check available records, witness accounts, and medical findings to establish who acted and what happened.
2. Compare with the rule: test the proven conduct against Article III, Section $19$ protections for detainees.

Answer: Check the evidence on who acted and what happened, then test it against Section $19$ and lawful remedies.

**Check:** A report starts an inquiry rather than proving guilt by label alone, and detention never removes Section $19$ safeguards.

### #2: Seized Tools and Family Income

Police seize a seamstress's sewing machine during an unlawful warrantless search. Receipts prove she lost three weeks of dress orders, leaving her family short on rent. No medical record appears in the file. State one personal effect and one family effect backed by the record.

**Solution:**

1. Check the personal effect: losing her machine and three weeks of orders deprived her of property use and earned wages.
2. Check the family effect: lost sewing income left her household short on rent, while no medical record backs any injury.

Answer: Lost use of her tool with missed wages, and family difficulty paying rent.

**Check:** Both effects rest directly on the seizure and order receipts without adding unproved physical injuries.

### #3: Clinic Notes and Claimed Harms

A market vendor is unlawfully held overnight solely for political leaflets. Jail logs prove her custody and one missed day of stall sales, cutting household grocery money. A clinic note shows no injury, and her cooperative loan stays unpaid. A supporter claims the record proves broken ribs and erases her loan. What does the record support?

**Solution:**

1. Check what personal and family effects the jail logs and missed stall-sales records establish.
2. Check the clinic note and Section $20$ against the supporter's claims of broken ribs and loan cancellation.

:::try
Finish the last step: state the supported effects and explain why the supporter's two extra claims fail.
:::answer
The answer is **The record supports lost liberty and reduced grocery money, while broken ribs and loan cancellation fail.** No clinic evidence shows broken ribs, and unlawful detention never cancels a valid loan.

**Check:** Supported effects match the logs and sales facts, while unsupported injury and debt claims fail.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Report uses a strong rights label: verify who acted and what happened with evidence first. {#label-is-not-proof}
- Abuse claim comes from a detained person: remember detainees keep Section $19$ protections. {#detainees-keep-protections}
- Listing personal and family effects: include only harms backed by the record, never guessed injuries. {#only-record-supported-effects}
:::
