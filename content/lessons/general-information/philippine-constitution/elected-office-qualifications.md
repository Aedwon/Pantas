## Core Concept

### Citizenship and Legislative Qualifications

A **natural-born citizen** is Filipino from birth without an act needed to complete citizenship. Article IV, Section $2$ also covers electors under Section $1(3)$ (see Articles IV and V: Citizenship and Suffrage).

National and congressional offices require natural-born citizenship, literacy, and minimum age **on election day**:

1. **Representative** (Article VI, Section $6$): natural-born, at least $25$ on election day, able to read and write. District seats also need district registration and $1$ year of district residence right before election day. Party-list seats exclude those district rules.
2. **Senator** (Article VI, Section $3$): natural-born, at least $35$ on election day, able to read and write, registered voter. The Senator must also live in the Philippines for $2$ years right before election day.

Screen a $36$-year-old natural-born Senate candidate who returned to Manila $18$ months before election day:

1. Citizenship and age ($36 \ge 35$) pass Article VI, Section $3$.
2. Eighteen months of Philippine residence is under $2$ years, so the candidate fails.

:::pair #district-one-year-residence
Right: A district Representative needs at least $1$ year of district residence right before election day.
Wrong: Treating $6$ months of district residence as enough for a district Representative.
Article VI, Section $6$ requires $1$ full year of district residence, separate from local voting rules.
:::

:::pair #age-measured-election-day
Right: Minimum ages ($25$, $35$, $40$) are measured on election day, not when filing candidacy.
Wrong: Disqualifying a candidate who turns the required age between candidacy filing and election day.
The Constitution measures each minimum age on the day of the election, never at filing.
:::

:::check
A natural-born citizen is $24$ at filing and turns $25$ one week before election day. She meets the voter and $1$-year district rules. Is she old enough?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Article VI, Section $6$ measures the $25$-year minimum on election day, not at filing.
:::

### President and Vice-President Qualifications

Under Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$, the President and Vice-President share identical qualifications. Both must be natural-born citizens, registered voters, and able to read and write. Both must be at least $40$ on election day and Philippine residents for at least $10$ years right before election day.

Age, registration, and long residence never waive natural-born citizenship.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: minimum-age ladder 25: Representative; 35: Senator, COMELEC, COA; 40: President, Vice-President, Supreme Court Member, Ombudsman; footer Age alone is not full qualification -->

Check a $38$-year-old natural-born presidential candidate who has lived in the Philippines for $20$ years and is a registered voter:

1. Citizenship, registration, and $20$ years of residence pass Article VII, Section $2$.
2. Age $38$ falls below the $40$-year minimum on election day, so the candidate fails.

:::why
Why give the Vice-President identical qualifications to the President? The Vice-President can succeed to the presidency anytime, so both need equal standards.
:::

:::pair #vp-ten-year-residence
Right: The Vice-President shares the President's $10$-year Philippine residence requirement.
Wrong: Applying the Senate's $2$-year residence rule to the Vice-President.
Article VII, Section $3$ gives the Vice-President identical $10$-year Philippine residence rules to the President.
:::

:::check
A natural-born citizen turns $40$ one day before election day, has lived in the Philippines for $11$ years, and is a registered voter. Does his late birthday bar him from running for President?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article VII, Section $2$ measures the $40$-year minimum on election day, and he meets the $10$-year residence and natural-born rules.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Minimum Ages

On election day, Ana is $25$, Ben is $35$, and Cara is $40$. All meet the other requirements. Who meets the age minimum for Representative, Senator, and President?

**Solution:**

1. Check Ana ($25$) against the minimums ($25$, $35$, $40$): she clears Representative only.
2. Check Ben ($35$): he clears Representative ($25$) and Senator ($35$).
3. Check Cara ($40$): she clears Representative ($25$), Senator ($35$), and President ($40$).

Answer: Ana meets Representative; Ben meets Representative and Senator; Cara meets Representative, Senator, and President.

**Check:** Higher ages clear lower minimums ($40 > 35 > 25$) when other rules pass.

### #2: District vs. Party-List Voter Registration

On election day, Marco is $27$, natural-born, and literate. He has lived in District B for $2$ years, yet remains registered to vote in District A. Does he qualify as District B's Representative, and do district voter rules bar him as a party-list nominee?

**Solution:**

1. Check District B: Article VI, Section $6$ requires voter registration inside the district sought, so District A registration fails.
2. Check party-list scope: Section $6$ excludes party-list seats from district rules, while RA $7941$, Section $9$ sets separate party-list qualifications.

Answer: He fails District B's voter rule, yet district voter rules do not bar a party-list nomination.

**Check:** District seats need both district registration and $1$-year district residence; party-list seats follow separate statutory rules.

### #3: Checking a Vice-Presidential Candidate

Elena runs for Vice-President. She is natural-born, literate, a registered voter, and has lived in the Philippines for $12$ years right before election day. She turns $40$ two days after election day. Does she pass age and residence?

**Solution:**

1. Check age: test Elena's age on election day itself under Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$.
2. Check residence: compare her $12$ years of Philippine residence against the Vice-President's residence requirement.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether Elena passes each requirement under Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$.
:::answer
The answer is **She passes the $10$-year residence rule ($12 \ge 10$), yet fails the $40$-year age rule at age $39$ on election day.** Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$ measure minimum age on election day itself, not two days later.

**Check:** Residence passes ($12 \ge 10$), whereas election-day age fails ($39 < 40$).
:::

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Remember $25$, $35$, $40$ for Representative, Senator, and President on election day. Vice-President, Supreme Court Member, and Ombudsman also start at $40$. Residence jumps from $1$ year (district) to $2$ years (Senate) to $10$ years (President and Vice-President).

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- District Representative: require $1$ full year of district residence alongside district registration. {#district-one-year-residence}
- Minimum ages: measure $25$, $35$, and $40$ on election day, not at filing. {#age-measured-election-day}
- Vice-President residence: apply the President's $10$-year Philippine residence rule, not $2$ years. {#vp-ten-year-residence}
:::
