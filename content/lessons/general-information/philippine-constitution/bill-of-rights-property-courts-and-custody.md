## Core Concept

### Private Property

Article III, Section $9$ says that private property cannot be taken for public use without **just compensation**. Just compensation means payment for the property that the government takes for public use.

The key idea has two parts: the taking must be for public use, and just compensation must be given.

### Contracts

Section $10$ says that no law may impair the obligation of contracts. This protects the duties that parties accepted in a valid contract from being unfairly weakened by a later law.

This rule does not mean that every law affecting a contract is automatically unconstitutional. Contract rights can be subject to valid government regulation for public welfare. The question is whether the law improperly impairs the contract obligation.

### Access to Courts and Legal Help

Section $11$ says that poverty cannot be a reason to deny a person free access to courts or **quasi-judicial bodies**. A quasi-judicial body is a government body that is not a court but can hear facts and decide certain disputes.

Poverty also cannot be a reason to deny adequate legal assistance.

### Rights During Custodial Investigation

Section $12$ protects a person under investigation for an offense. The person must be informed of the right to remain silent and the right to competent and independent counsel, preferably counsel of the person's own choice.

If the person cannot afford counsel, counsel must be provided. These rights may be waived only in writing and in the presence of counsel.

Investigators may not use torture, force, violence, threats, intimidation, or other means that destroy free choice. Secret detention places and similar forms of detention are also prohibited.

A confession or admission obtained in violation of Section $12$, or of the right against compelled self-incrimination in Section $17$, cannot be used as evidence against the person.

### Bail Before Conviction

Section $13$ protects the right to bail **before conviction**. Bail allows a person to remain out of detention while the case is pending, subject to lawful conditions.

A person may be released by sufficient sureties. The Constitution also allows release on **recognizance** when provided by law. Recognizance is release based on a lawful undertaking instead of a bail bond.

There is an important exception. A person charged with an offense punishable by **reclusion perpetua**, a severe prison penalty named by law, is not entitled to bail as a matter of right when the evidence of guilt is strong.

The right to bail is not lost just because the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus is suspended. Excessive bail is also prohibited.

## Examples

### #1: Property Taken for a Public Road

The government takes part of private land to build a public road but offers no compensation. Which Article III protection is involved?

**Solution:**

1. The land is private property.
2. It is being taken for public use.
3. Section $9$ requires just compensation for that taking.
4. Taking the land for public use without just compensation violates that protection.

### #2: Law Weakens an Existing Contract

A new law says that one party no longer has to perform a duty that it already promised in a valid contract. Which constitutional protection should be checked?

**Solution:**

1. The problem concerns a law changing an existing contract duty.
2. Section $10$ protects the obligation of contracts from impairment.
3. The next legal question is whether the law improperly impairs that obligation.

### #3: Court Access Denied Because of Poverty

A person is told that a court will not accept the case only because the person is poor. Which protection applies?

**Solution:**

1. Section $11$ says poverty cannot be a reason to deny free access to courts.
2. It also protects access to quasi-judicial bodies and adequate legal assistance.
3. Denial for poverty alone conflicts with this protection.

### #4: Questioning Without Counsel

A suspect is under investigation for an offense. Investigators tell the suspect to sign an oral waiver of counsel and then answer questions alone. Is that enough to waive the right to counsel?

**Solution:**

1. The suspect must be informed of the rights to remain silent and to competent and independent counsel.
2. A waiver of these rights must be in writing and made in the presence of counsel.
3. An oral waiver while alone does not meet those constitutional conditions.

### #5: Coerced Confession

Investigators threaten a person until the person signs a confession. May a confession obtained through that threat be used against the person?

**Solution:**

1. Section $12$ prohibits threats and other means that destroy free choice.
2. A confession or admission obtained in violation of this protection is inadmissible against the person.
3. Therefore, the threatened confession cannot be used against the person under this rule.

### #6: Bail and Strong Evidence

Before conviction, a person is charged with an offense punishable by reclusion perpetua. The evidence of guilt is strong. Is bail guaranteed as a matter of right under Section $13$?

**Solution:**

1. The general rule protects bail before conviction.
2. The exception applies when the charge is punishable by reclusion perpetua and the evidence of guilt is strong.
3. Because both conditions are present, bail is not guaranteed as a matter of right under Section $13$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Public Use Does Not Remove the Compensation Rule

> Public use does not mean the government may take private property for free.

Section $9$ joins public use with just compensation. Check both parts.

### #2: A Casual Waiver Is Not Enough

> Custodial rights cannot be waived by a simple oral statement.

The waiver must be in writing and in the presence of counsel.

### #3: The Bail Exception Has Two Conditions

> Do not stop after seeing the words reclusion perpetua.

For the constitutional exception, the charge must carry that punishment and the evidence of guilt must be strong.
