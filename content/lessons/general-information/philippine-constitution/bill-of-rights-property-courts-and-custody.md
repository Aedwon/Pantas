## Core Concept

### Private Property

Article III, Section $9$ protects private property taken for public use. The government must give **just compensation**, the full and fair value of the property taken. A public purpose does not make the property free.

The rule concerns a taking for public use. It does not mean that every government rule affecting property requires payment.

### Contracts

Section $10$ bars laws that impair the obligation of contracts. **Impair** means weaken or reduce. The protection concerns duties agreed to in a valid contract, such as a duty to pay or provide a service.

A later law cannot simply erase those duties without constitutional limits. Still, contract rights are subject to valid government regulation for public welfare. A law affecting a contract is not automatically unconstitutional.

### Courts and Legal Help

Under Section $11$, poverty cannot be the reason to deny free access to courts or quasi-judicial bodies. A **quasi-judicial body** is a government body outside the courts that hears facts and decides certain disputes.

The same section protects adequate legal assistance despite poverty. Access to a hearing and help from a lawyer are related protections, but they are not the same thing. Allowing a person to file a case does not by itself settle whether needed legal help has been provided.

### Rights During Custodial Investigation

**Custodial investigation** involves questioning a person suspected of an offense after the person is taken into custody or otherwise deprived of freedom in a significant way. Section $12$ protects the person's freedom to decide whether to answer.

Investigators must inform the person of the right to remain silent. This means the person may refuse to answer questions during that investigation. They must also explain the right to competent and independent counsel, preferably a lawyer the person chooses.

**Competent counsel** is a lawyer able to give proper legal help. **Independent counsel** protects the person's interests, free from control by the investigators. If the person cannot afford a lawyer, one must be provided.

To **waive** a right means to give it up. These custodial rights may be waived only in writing and in the presence of counsel. A casual oral agreement or a signature obtained while the suspect is alone is not enough.

Torture, force, violence, threats, intimidation, and other means that destroy free choice are forbidden. Secret detention places, solitary detention, detention that cuts off communication, and similar forms are also prohibited.

Section $17$ protects against **compelled self-incrimination**. This means being forced to give testimony that could help prove one's own guilt. A confession admits guilt, while an admission acknowledges a fact that may help prove the case.

A confession or admission obtained in violation of Section $12$ or Section $17$ cannot be used as evidence against the person. Calling a statement voluntary does not settle the issue if the facts show threats or denial of the required rights.

### Bail Before Conviction

Section $13$ protects bail before conviction, subject to its stated exception. **Bail** is security for a person's release from custody, with a duty to appear as required. It does not decide whether the person is guilty.

Release may be secured by sufficient **sureties**, who give a guarantee for the person's appearance. Release on **recognizance** is also allowed when provided by law. This uses a lawful undertaking instead of a bail bond.

The constitutional exception concerns a charge punishable by **reclusion perpetua**, a severe prison penalty named in Philippine law. Bail is not a matter of right under Section $13$ when that penalty applies and the evidence of guilt is strong. Both conditions matter.

Excessive bail is forbidden. The right to bail is not lost merely because the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus is suspended. That writ is a court order requiring a custodian to bring a detained person before the court so the detention can be examined.

These sections give a guide to the rights. Each has conditions, so a short description alone cannot decide every real case.

## Examples

### #1: Land for a Road

The government takes private land for a public road and refuses to pay for it. Which constitutional protection applies?

**Solution:**

1. The land is private property taken for public use.
2. Section $9$ requires just compensation.
3. The public purpose does not excuse the refusal to pay.

### #2: A Later Law and a Contract

A new law releases one party from a duty already accepted in a valid contract. Which constitutional protection should be examined?

**Solution:**

1. The issue is a later law weakening an existing contract duty.
2. Section $10$ protects the obligation of contracts.
3. Whether the law is invalid still requires checking its effect and any valid public-welfare basis.

### #3: Court Access and Poverty

A court refuses to accept a person's case solely because the person is poor. Which protection is involved?

**Solution:**

1. Section $11$ protects free access to courts despite poverty.
2. Poverty alone cannot justify that denial.

### #4: Access to a Quasi-Judicial Body

A government body hears evidence and decides disputes outside the court system. It refuses a hearing solely because the applicant is poor. Does Section $11$ cover this kind of body?

**Solution:**

1. A body that performs this dispute-deciding role is quasi-judicial.
2. Section $11$ protects access to quasi-judicial bodies as well as courts.
3. Poverty alone cannot be the reason for denying access.

### #5: Legal Assistance Denied

An applicant is told that adequate legal assistance will be denied solely because the applicant has no money. Which protection directly applies?

**Solution:**

1. The issue is legal help, not merely permission to file a case.
2. Section $11$ says adequate legal assistance cannot be denied because of poverty.
3. The stated reason conflicts with that protection.

### #6: Choosing Silence

During custodial investigation, a suspect says that she will remain silent. Investigators insist that she must answer their questions. Which right directly applies?

**Solution:**

1. Section $12$ protects the right to remain silent during custodial investigation.
2. The suspect may refuse to answer.
3. Investigators must respect that choice and inform her of the protected rights.

### #7: No Money for Counsel

A suspect in custodial investigation asks for a lawyer but cannot afford one. Investigators say that questioning must continue without counsel because the suspect cannot pay. What does Section $12$ require?

**Solution:**

1. The suspect has a right to competent and independent counsel.
2. Counsel must be provided when the suspect cannot afford one.
3. Lack of money does not remove this protection.

### #8: Waiver While Alone

A suspect signs a written waiver of custodial rights while no lawyer is present. Does the signature alone meet the constitutional waiver requirements?

**Solution:**

1. A waiver must be in writing and in the presence of counsel.
2. The written form meets only one of those conditions.
3. Without counsel present, the waiver does not meet the constitutional requirements.

### #9: Threatened Confession

Investigators threaten a person until the person signs a confession. May that confession be used against the person under Section $12$?

**Solution:**

1. Threats that destroy free choice violate the custodial safeguards.
2. A confession obtained through that violation is inadmissible, meaning it cannot be used as evidence against the person.

### #10: The Bail Exception

Before conviction, an accused faces a charge punishable by reclusion perpetua. The evidence of guilt is strong. Is bail guaranteed as a matter of right under Section $13$?

**Solution:**

1. The charge carries the penalty stated in the exception.
2. The evidence of guilt is also strong.
3. With both conditions present, bail is not guaranteed as a matter of right under that section.

### #11: A Serious Charge Without Strong Evidence

Before conviction, an accused faces a charge punishable by reclusion perpetua, but the evidence of guilt is not strong. Does the named penalty alone remove the constitutional right to bail?

**Solution:**

1. The exception requires both the stated penalty and strong evidence of guilt.
2. Strong evidence is absent in the given facts.
3. The penalty alone does not satisfy the exception.

### #12: Release Without a Bail Bond

A law allows an eligible accused to be released on a lawful undertaking instead of a bail bond. What form of release is described?

**Solution:**

This describes release on recognizance, which Section $13$ allows when provided by law.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Public Use Still Requires Payment

> A public road does not make private land free.

A taking for public use must meet the just-compensation requirement.

### #2: Access and Legal Help Are Separate

> Being allowed to file a case does not answer every legal-assistance question.

Section $11$ protects both access and adequate legal assistance against denial because of poverty.

### #3: A Signature Is Not Enough

> A custodial waiver needs writing and counsel's presence.

An oral waiver fails the writing requirement. A written waiver signed alone fails the counsel requirement.

### #4: Check Both Bail Conditions

> Reclusion perpetua alone does not establish the exception.

The evidence of guilt must also be strong. Bail concerns release, not a finding of innocence or guilt.
