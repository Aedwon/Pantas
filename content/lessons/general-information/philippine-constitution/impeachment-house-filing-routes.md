## Core Concept

### The Regular House Complaint Route

Under Article XI, Section $3(1)$, the House of Representatives has the exclusive power to initiate all cases of impeachment. A **verified complaint** is formally sworn to as true.

Section $3(2)$ and $3(3)$ set the **regular route**. It applies when fewer than $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members file a complaint, or a citizen files with a Member's endorsement:

1. Include the complaint in the Order of Business within $10$ session days.
2. Refer it to the proper Committee within $3$ session days after that.
3. The Committee holds a hearing and submits its report by majority vote of all its Members within $60$ session days from referral.
4. The House calendars the resolution within $10$ session days from receipt.
5. At least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members must vote to affirm a favorable resolution or override a contrary one to adopt **Articles of Impeachment**.

Track $1$ citizen's verified complaint endorsed by $1$ Representative in a $300$-Member House:

1. One Member's endorsement lets the citizen file validly, so the complaint is not dismissed.
2. Because fewer than $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members filed it, it must go through committee review and a $\dfrac{1}{3}$ House floor vote.

:::pair #citizen-endorsement-regular-route
Right: A citizen's verified complaint endorsed by a House Member enters the regular route through committee review and House action.
Wrong: Treating $1$ Member's endorsement as turning a citizen's complaint straight into the Articles of Impeachment.
Citizens may file with a House Member's endorsement, yet committee review and House voting still apply below $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

:::check
In a $300$-Member House, $10$ Representatives file a verified impeachment complaint together. Does that filing skip committee review?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $300$ is $100$, so $10$ Members must follow the regular committee route.
:::

### The One-Third Direct Filing Route

Article XI, Section $3(4)$, sets the **direct route**. If a verified complaint or resolution is filed by at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all Members of the House, it directly constitutes the **Articles of Impeachment**.

Trial by the Senate then proceeds **forthwith**, meaning immediately without delay. Keep the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ **filing** rule (which skips committee review) separate from the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ **floor vote** at the end of the regular route.

:::why
Why allow a direct one-third filing route? When at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members already sign sworn charges, repeating committee screening is unnecessary.
:::

Test a $300$-Member House where $105$ Members sign and file a verified impeachment resolution:

1. Compute the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ threshold: $\dfrac{1}{3} \times 300 = 100$ Members needed.
2. Since $105 \ge 100$, the resolution itself becomes the Articles of Impeachment, and Senate trial proceeds forthwith.

:::pair #one-third-direct-articles
Right: A verified complaint or resolution filed by at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members becomes the Articles of Impeachment for immediate Senate trial.
Wrong: Requiring a verified complaint filed by at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members to undergo committee review first.
Direct filing skips committee review to form the formal charges, while the Senate still holds the trial.
:::

:::check
Suppose the House has $300$ total Members, and $95$ Members file a verified impeachment complaint together. Does their filing become the Articles of Impeachment at once?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. At least $100$ Members ($\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $300$) are required for direct filing, so $95$ Members enter the regular committee route.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Checking Citizen Filings With and Without Endorsement

Citizen A delivers a sworn impeachment complaint with zero House endorsements. Citizen B files a sworn impeachment complaint endorsed by $2$ Representatives. Rule on both filings under Section $3(2)$.

**Solution:**

1. Under Article XI, Section $3(2)$, a citizen complaint requires a resolution of endorsement by a House Member.
2. Citizen A has no endorsement, so that filing fails the Section $3(2)$ requirement.
3. Citizen B has House endorsement and enters the regular committee route.

Answer: Citizen A's unendorsed filing fails, while Citizen B's endorsed complaint goes to committee review.

**Check:** Citizens need a House Member's endorsement to file, and fewer than $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of Members uses the regular route.

### #2: Committee Deadlines and Floor Override

In a $300$-Member House, a committee holds a hearing on an endorsed complaint and reports within $60$ session days recommending dismissal. Once calendared within $10$ session days, how many House votes can override that report?

**Solution:**

1. Under Article XI, Section $3(3)$, overriding a contrary committee resolution requires at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members.
2. Compute $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $300$ total Members: $\dfrac{1}{3} \times 300 = 100$.

Answer: At least $100$ Members voting to override can adopt the Articles of Impeachment.

**Check:** Even after a negative committee report, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members ($100$ of $300$) can override it.

### #3: Applying the Direct Route Threshold

Suppose the House has $312$ total Members, and $104$ Members file a verified impeachment resolution. Determine whether the regular committee route applies and what happens next.

**Solution:**

1. Compute the Section $3(4)$ direct-filing threshold: $\dfrac{1}{3} \times 312 = 104$ Members.
2. The filing has $104$ Members, which meets the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ threshold exactly.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether committee review is needed and what the filing becomes.
:::answer
The answer is **Committee review is skipped, because the resolution itself becomes the Articles of Impeachment for forthwith Senate trial.** Direct filing forms the formal charges right away, while the Senate holds the trial.

**Check:** Multiply $104 \times 3 = 312$ to confirm $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is met, then send the Articles straight to the Senate.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Endorsed citizen complaint: route through committee review and House action, never direct conversion. {#citizen-endorsement-regular-route}
- One-third direct filing: forms the Articles without committee review, yet still requires a Senate trial. {#one-third-direct-articles}
- Filing threshold vs. floor vote: keep the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ direct-filing rule separate from the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ regular-route floor vote. {#the-one-third-direct-filing-route}
:::
