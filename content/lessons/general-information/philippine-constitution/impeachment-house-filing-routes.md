## Core Concept

### The Regular House Complaint Route

Under Article XI, Section $3(1)$, the House of Representatives has the exclusive power to initiate all cases of impeachment. A **verified complaint** is formally sworn to as true.

Sections $3(2)$ and $3(3)$ set the **regular route** for filings by fewer than $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members or endorsed citizen complaints:

1. Include the complaint in the Order of Business within $10$ session days.
2. Refer it to the proper Committee within $3$ session days after that.
3. The Committee holds a hearing and reports by majority of all its Members within $60$ session days.
4. The House calendars the resolution within $10$ session days from receipt.
5. At least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members must affirm a favorable report or override a contrary one to adopt **Articles of Impeachment**.

A Member's endorsement permits a citizen to file; it does not block dismissal. Track $3$ Representatives filing a verified complaint in a $300$-Member House:

1. With $3 < \dfrac{1}{3}$ of $300$, it enters the regular route and reaches the Order of Business within $10$ session days.
2. The House refers it within $3$ session days; the Committee reports within $60$ session days for a $\dfrac{1}{3}$ floor vote.

:::pair #citizen-endorsement-regular-route
Right: An endorsed citizen complaint enters the regular committee and House vote route.
Wrong: Treating $1$ Member's endorsement as turning a citizen's complaint straight into Articles of Impeachment.
Citizens may file with endorsement, yet committee review and House voting apply below $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

:::check
In a $300$-Member House, $10$ Representatives file a verified impeachment complaint together. Does that filing skip committee review?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $300$ is $100$, so $10$ Members must follow the regular committee route.
:::

### The One-Third Direct Filing Route

Article XI, Section $3(4)$, sets the **direct route**. A verified complaint filed by at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members constitutes the **Articles of Impeachment**. Supreme Court rulings require valid verifications and access to supporting evidence, not signatures alone.

Senate trial proceeds **forthwith**. In *Generillo v. Senate*, the Supreme Court clarified that *forthwith* means within a reasonable time under the circumstances, allowing necessary preparations. Direct filing skips ordinary committee screening, though permitted verification checks still apply. Keep the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ **filing** rule separate from the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ **floor vote** ending the regular route.

:::why
Why allow a direct route? When at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members validly verify charges with supporting evidence, repeating ordinary committee screening is unnecessary.
:::

Test $105$ Members in a $300$-Member House signing a validly verified complaint after examining supporting evidence:

1. Threshold: $\dfrac{1}{3} \times 300 = 100$ Members.
2. With $105 \ge 100$, valid verifications, and evidence access, it skips ordinary committee screening to form the Articles.
3. Senate trial proceeds forthwith, within a reasonable time for preparations.

:::pair #one-third-direct-articles
Right: A validly verified $\dfrac{1}{3}$ filing with evidence access skips ordinary committee screening to form the Articles for a reasonable-time Senate trial.
Wrong: Requiring a validly filed $\dfrac{1}{3}$ direct complaint to undergo the same ordinary committee review as a citizen's endorsed complaint.
Direct filing skips ordinary committee screening (while allowing permitted verification checks), and Senate trial proceeds within a reasonable time.
:::

:::check
In a $300$-Member House, $110$ Representatives sign a direct impeachment complaint without valid verifications or access to supporting evidence. Does reaching $110$ signatures alone satisfy Section $3(4)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Although $110$ clears $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $300$, Supreme Court rulings require valid verifications and access to supporting evidence, not signatures alone.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Checking Citizen Filings With and Without Endorsement

Citizen A files a sworn complaint with zero House endorsements. Citizen B files one endorsed by $2$ Representatives. Rule on both under Section $3(2)$.

**Solution:**

1. Section $3(2)$ requires a House Member's endorsement for any citizen complaint.
2. Citizen A lacks endorsement, so that filing fails.
3. Citizen B's endorsement permits filing, sending it to the regular committee route.

Answer: Citizen A's filing fails, while Citizen B's enters committee review.

**Check:** Endorsement permits a citizen filing without replacing committee review below $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

### #2: Committee Deadlines and Floor Override

In a $300$-Member House, a committee reports within $60$ session days recommending dismissal. Once calendared within $10$ session days, how many House votes can override it?

**Solution:**

1. Section $3(3)$ requires at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members to override a contrary report.
2. Compute $\dfrac{1}{3} \times 300 = 100$.

Answer: At least $100$ Members can override the report and adopt the Articles.

**Check:** Even after a negative committee report, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all Members ($100$ of $300$) can override it.

### #3: Applying the Direct Route Threshold

In a $312$-Member House, $104$ Members file a resolution with valid verifications after examining supporting evidence. Does ordinary committee screening apply, and what happens next?

**Solution:**

1. Find the Section $3(4)$ threshold: $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all $312$ House Members.
2. Compare $104$ valid filers with that threshold, and apply Section $3(4)$ on committee screening and Senate trial timing.

:::try
Finish the last step: compute the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ threshold, rule on ordinary committee screening, and explain what *forthwith* requires in the Senate.
:::answer
The answer is **Ordinary committee screening is skipped, and the filing forms the Articles of Impeachment.** Since $\dfrac{1}{3} \times 312 = 104$, the $104$ validly verified filers with evidence access skip ordinary committee screening, though permitted verification checks apply. Senate trial then proceeds forthwith, meaning within a reasonable time allowing necessary preparations.

**Check:** Confirm $104 \times 3 = 312$ with valid verifications and evidence access, then check that *forthwith* means within a reasonable time.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Endorsed citizen complaint: enters committee review and House action, never direct conversion. {#citizen-endorsement-regular-route}
- Direct filing: skips ordinary committee screening, yet needs valid verifications, evidence access, and Senate trial. {#one-third-direct-articles}
- Two one-third rules: keep $\dfrac{1}{3}$ direct filing separate from the $\dfrac{1}{3}$ regular-route floor vote. {#the-one-third-direct-filing-route}
:::
