## Core Concept

### Probable Cause and the Judge

Article III, Section $2$ protects people, houses, papers, and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures. A **search** looks for evidence. A **seizure** takes control of a person or thing.

Before any search warrant or arrest warrant issues, there must be **probable cause** (reasonable grounds supported by facts) personally determined by a judge.

Test a search order signed by a city mayor instead of a judge:

1. Check who determined probable cause: the mayor, an executive official.
2. Since Section $2$ requires a judge, the mayor's order is not a valid warrant.

:::pair #judge-determines-probable-cause
Right: Only a judge personally determines probable cause before a warrant issues.
Wrong: Accepting a local chief officer's approval or a prosecutor's finding instead of a judge's decision.
Officers and prosecutors supply facts, while the judge makes the independent decision.
:::

:::why
Why must a judge decide probable cause? Neutral judges test police and prosecutor claims before officers enter homes or take liberty.
:::

:::check
A city chief of police signs a search order based on a tip. Does that meet Section $2$'s warrant rule?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Only a judge can personally determine probable cause for a warrant.
:::

### Search Warrant Examination and Specific Description

Under Article III, Section $2$, a search warrant requires two extra safeguards:

1. The judge must personally examine the complainant and witnesses in writing under **oath or affirmation** (a formal promise to tell the truth).
2. The warrant must particularly describe the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized.

Test a warrant naming "$14$ Rizal Street" and ordering officers to seize "any illegal items inside":

1. Check the place: "$14$ Rizal Street" is specific.
2. Check the items: "any illegal items" is too broad, so the warrant fails the particular description rule.

:::check
A search warrant lists three exact laptop serial numbers yet states "any warehouse in Quezon City" as the location. Which requirement fails?
:::answer
The answer is **Particular description of the place to be searched**. Both the place and the items must be specific.
:::

### Arrest Warrant Review

An arrest warrant must particularly identify the person to be arrested. The judge must still determine probable cause independently, yet the procedure differs from a search warrant.

Supreme Court case law interpreting Section $2$ allows a different step for arrest warrants. A judge may personally evaluate the prosecutor's report and supporting evidence. Personal questioning of witnesses is not always required when written records already show probable cause.

Test an arrest warrant issued after a judge reads the prosecutor's report and sworn affidavits without calling witnesses:

1. Check independence: the judge personally reviewed the supporting records.
2. Check witness questioning: written records suffice here, so personal questioning is unnecessary.

:::pair #arrest-warrant-records
Right: For an arrest warrant, personal review of the prosecutor's report and sufficient supporting evidence meets the judge's duty.
Wrong: Requiring personal witness questioning for every arrest warrant.
Search warrants need personal examination under oath; arrest warrants allow personal review of sufficient records.
:::

:::check
A judge signs an arrest warrant relying solely on a prosecutor's bare conclusion without reading the supporting affidavits. Does that satisfy Section $2$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The judge must personally evaluate sufficient supporting evidence instead of rubber-stamping a prosecutor's conclusion.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Who Determines Probable Cause

Police receive a tip about stolen goods in a shop and get written approval from the city mayor instead of a court. Does that approval satisfy Article III, Section $2$?

**Solution:**

1. Recall the rule: Section $2$ requires probable cause personally determined by a judge.
2. Check who approved the search: the city mayor, an administrative official.

Answer: No, only a judge may personally determine probable cause for a warrant.

**Check:** Executive or local officials cannot replace the judge's personal determination under Section $2$.

### #2: A General Search Warrant

After examining witnesses under oath, a judge issues a search warrant naming a house and letting officers take any object they wish. Which requirement is missing?

**Solution:**

1. Check the place: the house is named.
2. Check the items: "any object they wish" fails to particularly describe what may be seized.

Answer: A particular description of the things to be seized.

**Check:** Section $2$ requires specific descriptions of both the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized.

### #3: An Arrest Warrant Based on Records

A judge personally reads a prosecutor's report and sworn witness affidavits in the record, then finds probable cause for an arrest warrant. Defense counsel argues that every arrest warrant still requires calling the witnesses for live questioning. Is defense counsel right?

**Solution:**

1. Check whether the judge acted independently: yes, the judge personally evaluated the report and sworn affidavits.
2. Check the arrest-warrant rule: sufficient written records can support probable cause for an arrest warrant.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on defense counsel's claim and explain how a search warrant differs.
:::answer
The answer is **No, defense counsel is wrong because personally reviewing sufficient written records meets the judge's duty for an arrest warrant.** Unlike a search warrant, which requires personally examining the complainant and witnesses under oath, an arrest warrant does not always require live questioning.

**Check:** Section $2$ demands independent judicial review for both warrants, while permitting reliance on sufficient written records for an arrest warrant.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Non-judge approves a warrant: only a judge can personally determine probable cause under Section $2$. {#judge-determines-probable-cause}
- Arrest warrant rests on written records: personal questioning is unnecessary if the judge independently reviews sufficient evidence. {#arrest-warrant-records}
- Search warrant allows taking anything: require a particular description of both place and things to be seized. {#search-warrant-examination-and-specific-description}
:::
