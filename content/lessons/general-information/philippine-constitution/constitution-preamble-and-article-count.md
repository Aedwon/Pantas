## Core Concept

### The Unnumbered Preamble and Eighteen Articles

The $1987$ Constitution opens with an unnumbered **Preamble**, followed by **$18$ Articles** from Article I through Article XVIII (Roman numeral XVIII equals $18$).

An **article** is a major division of the Constitution. An article title shows where to find a topic, yet never lists every rule, power, requirement, or exception inside it.

Check how a constitutional outline counts its divisions from start to finish:

1. Count the Preamble as $0$ articles because it stands before Article I without an article number.
2. Count Article I (National Territory) through Article XVIII (Transitory Provisions) as $18$ numbered articles, without stopping at Article XVII ($17$).

:::pair #preamble-not-nineteenth-article
Right: The $1987$ Constitution has an unnumbered Preamble plus $18$ Articles (Article I through Article XVIII).
Wrong: Counting $19$ articles by treating the Preamble as a numbered article, or stopping at $17$ before Article XVIII.
The Preamble carries no article number, and Article XVIII (Transitory Provisions) closes the $18$-Article list.
:::

:::check
A reviewer counts Article I through Article XVII and adds the Preamble, getting $18$ articles for the wrong reasons. What did the reviewer miscount?
:::answer
The answer is **Both ends were miscounted**. The Preamble is not an article, while Article XVIII (Transitory Provisions) is the $18$th article.
:::

### Broad Aims Stated in the Preamble

The **Preamble** is the opening statement explaining why the sovereign Filipino people adopted the Constitution. It is neither an operational article nor a commission subdivision.

Key phrases in the Preamble use plain national ideals:

1. **Just and humane society**: treating people fairly with respect for human dignity.
2. **Common good**: benefitting the whole community, not just one favored person or group.
3. **Patrimony**: the nation's shared heritage and resources, cared for and developed for the country.
4. **Rule of law**: public power must follow written law, never an official's personal wish.
5. Core ideals: independence, democracy, truth, justice, freedom, love, equality, and peace.

:::why
Why open with a Preamble if the $18$ Articles hold the legal procedures? The Preamble states the shared national goals that guide how the $18$ Articles are understood.
:::

Match two civic examples to Preamble ideals:

1. Preserving coral reefs and historic plazas for future Filipinos reflects conserving and developing our **patrimony**.
2. Requiring mayors and police to follow written statutes instead of personal preferences reflects the **rule of law**.

:::pair #preamble-states-aims
Right: The Preamble states the Filipino people's purposes and aims, without acting as a commission part or transition rule.
Wrong: Treating the Preamble as a subdivision of Article IX or as a replacement for Article XVIII's transition rules.
Article IX-A through IX-D hold the commissions, Article XVIII holds transitions, and the Preamble sets broad national aims.
:::

:::check
A civic club reads the Preamble's promise of "truth, justice, freedom, love, equality, and peace" and claims it replaces Article III's specific warrant rules. Is that right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The Preamble states broad national aims, while specific enforceable rules and limits sit inside the $18$ Articles.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Separating Articles From Sections

A student remembers that the Bill of Rights has $22$ sections and writes "$22$ Articles" on a review index. Fix the count and explain the difference.

**Solution:**

1. Sections are smaller numbered parts inside an article, such as the $22$ sections of Article III.
2. Major divisions are the Articles, running from Article I through Article XVIII ($18$ in total).
3. The Preamble sits before Article I without an article number.

Answer: The $1987$ Constitution has $18$ Articles (excluding the Preamble), while $22$ is the section count of Article III.

**Check:** Match Roman numeral XVIII to $18$, and never mix an article's section count with the whole Constitution's article count.

### #2: Matching Public Actions to Preamble Ideals

A town builds free flood-safe parks for all residents instead of reserving riverbanks for one private club, and enforces strict transparency rules on the mayor. Which two Preamble concepts fit best?

**Solution:**

1. Opening public parks for the whole community serves the **common good** rather than private favor.
2. Binding the mayor to transparent written standards reflects the **rule of law**.

Answer: Common good and rule of law match the two town policies.

**Check:** Connect community-wide benefit to common good and law-bound officials to rule of law.

### #3: Checking the Preamble's Structural Role

A study sheet claims the Preamble is Article $0$ and sets up a fourth commission. It also says it covers $1987$ transition rules. Identify the errors and state its real role.

**Solution:**

1. Check the numbering: the Preamble is unnumbered, and the $18$ Articles run from Article I through Article XVIII.
2. Check the commissions and transitions: Article IX governs Constitutional Commissions, while Article XVIII governs Transitory Provisions.

:::try
Finish the last step: state the Preamble's real role and why knowing it does not replace reading the specific Articles.
:::answer
The answer is **The Preamble states the Filipino people's broad purposes and aims, while specific procedures live inside the $18$ Articles.** Knowing the Preamble guides your purpose, yet you still check the relevant Article for rules and powers.

**Check:** Confirm all three points: Preamble is unnumbered, holds broad aims (not commissions or transitions), and the $18$ Articles carry enforceable rules.
:::
