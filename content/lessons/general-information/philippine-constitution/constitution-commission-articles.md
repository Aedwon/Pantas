## Core Concept

### Article IX and Its Four Subdivisions

**Article IX: Constitutional Commissions** governs the three independent constitutional commissions. **Independent** means the Constitution shields them from outside control so they can carry out their assigned duties fairly.

Article IX has $4$ lettered parts, ordered from common rules to the three commissions:

1. **IX-A: Common Provisions** covers rules shared by all three commissions (such as automatic release of approved budgets and bans on private practice).
2. **IX-B: Civil Service Commission** covers the Government's central personnel agency.
3. **IX-C: Commission on Elections** covers the body that administers and enforces election laws.
4. **IX-D: Commission on Audit** covers the body that examines government accounts, funds, and property.

Keep the **Commission on Human Rights (CHR)** separate: it is created under Article XIII (Social Justice and Human Rights), not Article IX.

Route three constitutional questions to the exact subdivision of Article IX:

1. Checking the ban against commissioners practicing law during office points to **IX-A** (Common Provisions).
2. Checking competitive merit hiring in government offices points to **IX-B** (Civil Service Commission).
3. Checking audits of municipal road spending points to **IX-D** (Commission on Audit).

:::pair #article-ix-vs-chr-and-others
Right: Place Common Provisions and the CSC, COMELEC, and COA under Article IX, while keeping CHR in Article XIII.
Wrong: Confusing Article IX (Constitutional Commissions) with Article XIII (Social Justice and Human Rights) or Article XI (Accountability).
Only CSC, COMELEC, and COA sit inside Article IX; CHR belongs to Article XIII.
:::

:::check
A town mayor disputes a provincial election tally and wants the constitutional rules on the Commission on Elections. Which Article and subdivision should be opened?
:::answer
The answer is **Article IX-C**. Article IX covers Constitutional Commissions, and subdivision C governs the Commission on Elections.
:::

### Subdivisions Stay Inside Article IX

Adding letters **A**, **B**, **C**, and **D** organizes Article IX without creating extra numbered articles. All $4$ parts sit inside the same single Article IX, so the Constitution still has $18$ Articles in total.

Remember the sequence cleanly: **A = All (Common Provisions), B = Civil Service, C = Elections, D = Audit**. Keep the letter attached to IX.

:::why
Why place Common Provisions first in IX-A? Putting shared protections once in IX-A avoids repeating identical rules inside IX-B, IX-C, and IX-D.
:::

Test a student's claim that IX-A, IX-B, IX-C, and IX-D add $3$ extra articles, turning Local Government into Article $13$:

1. Count Article IX-A through IX-D together as $1$ Article (Article IX).
2. The next Article immediately after IX-D is still Article X (Local Government), keeping the total at $18$ Articles.

:::pair #ix-a-vs-named-commissions
Right: Keep IX-A for Common Provisions shared by all three bodies, and use IX-B, IX-C, and IX-D for each named commission.
Wrong: Assigning Article IX-A to the Civil Service Commission or Commission on Elections instead of Common Provisions.
Common rules always sit in IX-A first, followed by CSC in B, COMELEC in C, and COA in D.
:::

:::check
A reviewer opens Article IX-A expecting rules on auditing public funds, and opens Article IX-D expecting shared rules on fiscal autonomy for all three commissions. What swap happened?
:::answer
The answer is **Swapped IX-A and IX-D**. Shared fiscal autonomy rules sit in IX-A (Common Provisions), while fund-auditing rules sit in IX-D (Commission on Audit).
:::

## Examples

### #1: Sorting Commissions Across Articles

Sort four bodies into Article IX or another Article: Civil Service Commission, Commission on Audit, Commission on Human Rights, and Commission on Elections.

**Solution:**

1. Check Article IX: it covers CSC (IX-B), COMELEC (IX-C), and COA (IX-D).
2. Check the Commission on Human Rights: it is created under Article XIII (Social Justice and Human Rights).

Answer: CSC, COMELEC, and COA sit in Article IX, while CHR sits in Article XIII.

**Check:** Only the three commissions in IX-B, IX-C, and IX-D belong to Article IX.

### #2: Counting Articles With Subdivisions

A study guide lists IX-A, IX-B, IX-C, and IX-D as "Articles $9$, $10$, $11$, and $12$" and pushes Local Government to Article $13$. Fix the error and explain why.

**Solution:**

1. Subdivisions IX-A through IX-D are four parts inside the single Article IX.
2. Sub-parts never add new numbered articles to the $18$-Article total.
3. Local Government remains Article X, right after Article IX.

Answer: Keep IX-A through IX-D inside Article IX so Local Government stays Article X and the total remains $18$.

**Check:** Remember that lettered parts divide $1$ Article rather than creating extra Articles.

### #3: Routing Shared vs. Specific Rules

Which Article IX part covers (a) salary protection for all three commissions, (b) enforcing election-day precinct rules, and (c) examining agency property records?

**Solution:**

1. Check item (a): a shared protection binding all three commissions sits in Article IX-A (Common Provisions).
2. Check items (b) and (c): match election-law enforcement and government property examination to their specific commissions.

:::try
Finish the last step: assign items (b) and (c) to their subdivisions and verify the full sequence A-B-C-D.
:::answer
The answer is **Precinct election enforcement sits in Article IX-C (COMELEC), and property-record examination sits in Article IX-D (COA).** That completes the sequence IX-A Common Provisions, IX-B CSC, IX-C COMELEC, and IX-D COA.

**Check:** Verify A = shared rules, B = personnel, C = elections, and D = government accounts and property.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Article IX scope: keep CSC, COMELEC, and COA in Article IX, and place CHR in Article XIII. {#article-ix-vs-chr-and-others}
- IX-A vs. named bodies: reserve IX-A for Common Provisions, and put CSC in B, COMELEC in C, and COA in D. {#ix-a-vs-named-commissions}
- Lettered sub-parts: IX-A through IX-D stay inside $1$ Article IX, adding zero extra articles to the $18$-Article total. {#subdivisions-stay-inside-article-ix}
:::
