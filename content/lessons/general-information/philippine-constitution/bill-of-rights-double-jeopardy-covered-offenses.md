## Core Concept

### Section $21$ and Rule $117$: Attempted and Frustrated Stages

Under Article III, Section $21$ and **Rule $117$, Section $7$**, double jeopardy covers the same offense plus any **attempt** or **frustrated** form.

Revised Penal Code, **Article $6$** defines both incomplete stages, though a crime's own legal elements decide whether they apply:

1. **Attempted offense**: the person starts direct acts toward the crime yet stops before all execution acts, without freely giving up.
2. **Frustrated offense**: the person completes all execution acts needed, yet the result fails for a reason outside that person's will.

Test Article $6$ when a shooter fires at a victim's chest and doctors save the victim:

1. Check execution acts: firing completed every act needed to kill.
2. Classify the stage: outside medical care stopped death, making it **frustrated**.

:::pair #attempt-vs-frustrated
Right: Attempted stops before all execution acts; frustrated finishes all execution acts while the result fails.
Wrong: Calling completed execution acts an attempted offense simply because the intended result failed.
Check whether all execution acts finished before checking the result.
:::

:::check
A thief reaches into an open cash drawer and grips cash bills, yet a guard grabs his wrist before he can remove the money. Which stage is this?
:::answer
The answer is **Attempted**. Direct acts toward taking the money began, yet the guard stopped him before completing all execution acts.
:::

### Rule $117$: Necessarily Included Offenses

Under Rule $117$, Section $7$, double jeopardy covers any offense that **necessarily includes**, or is **necessarily included in**, the first charge. An **included offense** has all its required parts inside the larger charge, which adds an extra part.

Under Revised Penal Code Articles $248$, $249$, and $14(16)$, **homicide** is an unlawful killing. **Murder** adds a qualifying circumstance such as **treachery**. Treachery means using attack methods that directly ensure execution without risk from the victim's defense.

Homicide sits inside a murder charge alleging those facts. A valid conviction or acquittal of murder bars a homicide trial for the same killing. Changing the title or charging fewer elements never bypasses Section $21$.

Test Rule $117$ after a valid murder acquittal alleging unlawful killing plus treachery:

1. Compare elements: every required part of homicide sat inside the murder charge.
2. Apply Section $21$: a homicide trial for the same killing is barred.

:::why
Why bar a lesser included charge after acquittal? Retrying the same core elements gives the state a forbidden second chance to convict.
:::

:::pair #included-offense-same-elements
Right: Section $21$ bars a second charge whose elements sat inside the first charge, despite a new name or fewer parts.
Wrong: Allowing a second trial for an included offense merely because it requires fewer elements.
Compare required factual elements instead of charge titles.
:::

:::pair #acquittal-also-bars-included
Right: Either a valid acquittal or a valid conviction bars a later trial for an included offense.
Wrong: Claiming only a prior conviction, never an acquittal, bars trying an included offense.
Both conviction and acquittal close off every included offense.
:::

:::check
A driver is acquitted of reckless imprudence resulting in damage to property, then charged with driving with an expired license from the same stop. Do both charges share the same included elements?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. An expired-license charge requires a license fact outside reckless driving, so double jeopardy does not bar it.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Attempted Stage After Frustrated Acquittal

A proper court acquits a defendant after arraignment and plea on a charge of frustrated murder for shooting a victim who survived. Prosecutors then file a second case charging attempted murder for that exact shooting, citing the different stage name. Is the second trial allowed?

**Solution:**

1. Check first jeopardy: valid charge, proper court, arraignment, plea, and acquittal all occurred.
2. Check covered forms: Rule $117$, Section $7$ covers attempt and frustration, and attempted murder's elements sit inside frustrated murder.

Answer: No, double jeopardy under Section $21$ bars the attempted-murder trial.

**Check:** A different stage name cannot bypass an acquittal when all elements of the attempt sat inside the first charge.

### #2: Incomplete Stages of Arson

One person splashes fuel on a shed and strikes a match before neighbors tackle him. Another person lights a gasoline-soaked rag beside a storeroom door and leaves. Sudden rain puts the fire out before any part of the building burns. Under Article $6$, classify each stage.

**Solution:**

1. Check the first person: neighbors stopped him before he finished all acts needed to burn the shed.
2. Check the second person: she finished all execution acts, yet rain stopped the building from burning.

Answer: Attempted arson for the first person, and frustrated arson for the second.

**Check:** Finished execution acts separate frustrated from attempted arson, and Rule $117$ covers both under double jeopardy.

### #3: Lesser Injury Charge After Acquittal

A proper court acquits an accused after arraignment and plea on serious physical injuries alleging a deliberate strike and forty hospital days. Prosecutors then file slight physical injuries for the same strike, citing fewer elements, a new name, and no conviction. Is this trial barred?

**Solution:**

1. Check first jeopardy: valid charge, proper court, arraignment, plea, and acquittal occurred.
2. Compare the required elements of slight physical injuries with the serious-injury charge.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the slight-injury charge and answer the prosecutors' name, element-count, and acquittal claims.
:::answer
The answer is **Yes, double jeopardy bars the slight-injury trial.** Every required element sat inside the first charge, so a new name or fewer parts fail. Acquittal bars an included offense just like a conviction.

**Check:** Rule $117$, Section $7$ turns on shared elements and a valid ending, not charge titles or convictions alone.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Incomplete crime shown: separate unfinished acts (attempted) from completed acts with failed result (frustrated). {#attempt-vs-frustrated}
- Lesser charge requires fewer elements: compare required elements against the first charge. {#included-offense-same-elements}
- First case ended in acquittal: remember acquittal bars included offenses too. {#acquittal-also-bars-included}
:::
