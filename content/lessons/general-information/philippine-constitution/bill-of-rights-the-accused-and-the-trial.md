## Core Concept

### Rights of an Accused Person

Article III, Section $14$ says that no person may be held to answer for a criminal offense without due process of law. Due process means the government must follow a fair legal process before taking away protected rights.

An accused person is **presumed innocent** until the contrary is proved. An accusation alone is not proof of guilt.

The accused has the right to be heard personally and through counsel. The accused must also be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation, which means knowing what criminal charge is being answered.

The trial must be speedy, impartial, and public. **Impartial** means the decision-maker must not favor one side.

The accused has the right to meet witnesses face to face. This lets the defense challenge testimony given against the accused.

The accused also has the right to **compulsory process**. This means the court can require needed witnesses to attend and can require needed evidence to be produced for the defense.

After arraignment, a trial may continue even if the accused is absent when the accused was duly notified and the failure to appear is unjustifiable. **Arraignment** is the stage when the charge is formally read and the accused enters a plea.

### Habeas Corpus

The **writ of habeas corpus** is a court remedy used to question unlawful detention. It requires the legal basis for detention to be examined.

Section $15$ says the privilege of the writ may be suspended only in cases of invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it. Both the stated emergency and the public-safety need matter.

Suspension of the privilege does not mean every detention automatically becomes lawful.

### Speedy Disposition of Cases

Section $16$ gives all persons the right to a speedy disposition of cases before judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies.

A **quasi-judicial body** is a government body that is not a court but can hear facts and decide certain disputes. An **administrative body** is a government office or agency that handles matters under the law.

This right is broader than the speedy-trial right in a criminal prosecution. It can apply to cases outside an ordinary criminal trial.

### Protection Against Compelled Self-Incrimination

Section $17$ says that no person may be compelled to be a witness against himself or herself. This is the right against **compelled self-incrimination**.

The key word is compelled. The protection stops the government from forcing a person to give testimonial evidence against himself or herself.

## Examples

### #1: Presumption of Innocence

A person is charged with a crime. Before evidence is heard, an official says the person must prove innocence. Which constitutional rule is being ignored?

**Solution:**

1. Section $14$ says the accused is presumed innocent until the contrary is proved.
2. The burden cannot simply be reversed because a charge was filed.
3. An accusation alone does not remove the presumption of innocence.

### #2: Knowing the Accusation and Having Counsel

An accused person is brought to trial without being told the criminal charge and is not allowed counsel. Which rights are affected?

**Solution:**

1. Section $14$ protects the right to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation.
2. It also protects the right to be heard personally and through counsel.
3. Both protections are affected by the stated facts.

### #3: Impartial and Public Trial

A criminal trial is secretly conducted without a valid legal basis, and the decision-maker openly favors the prosecution. Which Section $14$ rights are involved?

**Solution:**

1. Section $14$ protects a public trial.
2. It also protects an impartial trial.
3. The stated facts raise both protections.

### #4: Meeting Witnesses and Securing Defense Evidence

The prosecution presents a witness against the accused. The accused is not allowed to meet that witness, and the court refuses to require an important defense witness to attend. Which rights are involved?

**Solution:**

1. The accused has the right to meet witnesses face to face.
2. The accused also has the right to compulsory process for defense witnesses and evidence.
3. Both rights are affected.

### #5: Suspending the Privilege of the Writ

There is no invasion and no rebellion, but the government seeks to suspend the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus for convenience. Does Section $15$ allow this?

**Solution:**

1. Section $15$ allows suspension only in cases of invasion or rebellion.
2. Public safety must also require the suspension.
3. The stated facts do not meet those constitutional conditions.

### #6: Slow Administrative Case

A person's case before a government administrative body remains unresolved for an unreasonable period. Which Bill of Rights protection should be checked?

**Solution:**

1. Section $16$ covers speedy disposition of cases.
2. It applies to judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies.
3. An administrative case can therefore raise a speedy-disposition issue.

### #7: Forced Testimony Against Oneself

A person is forced to give testimonial evidence that would be used against that same person. Which protection applies?

**Solution:**

1. Section $17$ protects against compelled self-incrimination.
2. The evidence is testimonial and is being forced from the person.
3. The stated conduct raises that constitutional protection.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Charged Does Not Mean Guilty

> A criminal charge does not erase the presumption of innocence.

The accused remains presumed innocent until the contrary is proved.

### #2: Habeas Corpus Has Strict Suspension Conditions

> Invasion or rebellion alone is not the whole rule.

Public safety must also require the suspension.

### #3: Speedy Trial and Speedy Disposition Are Related but Different

> Do not treat the two phrases as identical.

Speedy trial is part of the rights of an accused in criminal prosecution. Speedy disposition also covers judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative cases.

### #4: Custodial Silence and Self-Incrimination Are Not the Same Rule

> The right to remain silent during custodial investigation is taught in Section $12$.

Section $17$ separately protects against being compelled to be a witness against oneself.
