## Core Concept

### Rights During a Criminal Trial

An **accused person** is someone charged with a crime. Article III, Section $14$ requires **due process**, a fair legal process, before that person may be made to answer for a crime.

The accused is **presumed innocent** until the contrary is proved. A charge is a claim that a crime was committed. It is not proof of guilt, and the prosecution must prove its case.

The accused may be heard personally and through **counsel**, a lawyer. This protects the chance to answer the charge and present a defense. Keeping the accused and the lawyer from answering raises this right.

The accused must know the **nature and cause of the accusation**. This means knowing the crime charged and the acts claimed to make up that crime. A person cannot prepare a defense to a hidden charge.

A trial must be **speedy, impartial, and public**. Speedy means it must move forward without unreasonable delay. It does not mean every postponement breaks the right.

For a delay question, check how long the delay lasted, why it happened, whether the accused asked for a timely trial, and how the delay harmed the accused. Time needed for a fair hearing differs from repeated delays with no sound reason. Do not decide from the number of days alone.

Impartial means the judge does not favor either side. Public means the trial is open to public observation, subject to lawful limits. A hearing closed without a valid legal reason raises the public-trial right.

The accused may meet witnesses face to face. This lets the defense question people who testify against the accused and test what they say.

**Compulsory process** means using court orders to bring defense witnesses and evidence to court. It helps the defense obtain needed proof even when a witness will not attend willingly.

### Trial When the Accused Is Absent

**Arraignment** is when the charge is formally read and the accused enters a plea, such as guilty or not guilty. After arraignment, trial may continue without the accused only if the accused was properly notified and has no valid reason for being absent.

Check all these conditions. Absence alone does not let the court ignore the accused's rights.

### Habeas Corpus

The **writ of habeas corpus** is a court order used to examine whether someone is being held lawfully. The person holding the detainee must account for the detention before the court.

Section $15$ allows suspension of the privilege of this writ only during invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it. **Invasion** involves an outside force entering the country with force. **Rebellion** involves an armed uprising against the government.

An emergency label is not enough. There must be invasion or rebellion, and public safety must require suspension. Suspension does not make every detention lawful.

### Speedy Disposition of Cases

Section $16$ protects everyone's right to **speedy disposition**, which means having a case handled and resolved without unreasonable delay. It covers courts and also quasi-judicial and administrative bodies.

A **quasi-judicial body** is a government body outside the courts that hears facts and decides certain disputes. An **administrative body** is a government office handling matters under the law, such as an employee's disciplinary case.

Speedy trial concerns a criminal trial. Speedy disposition is broader and also reaches cases before these other bodies. A long wait calls for checking the reasons and circumstances, not assuming that every delay is unlawful.

### Protection Against Forced Self-Incrimination

Section $17$ says no person may be forced to be a witness against himself or herself. This is protection against **compelled self-incrimination**: being made to give an answer that can help show one's own guilt.

**Testimonial evidence** means statements that reveal what a person knows or thinks. A forced spoken answer or written admission can be testimonial evidence. The protection is not a blanket right to refuse every kind of evidence, such as physical evidence.

Look for both force and a statement that could be used against the person giving it. The protection applies to persons, not only to someone already on criminal trial.

## Examples

### #1: Presumption of Innocence

An official says that filing a criminal charge requires the accused to prove innocence. Which constitutional safeguard does this disregard?

**Solution:**

1. Section $14$ presumes the accused innocent until the contrary is proved.
2. Filing a charge does not prove guilt or shift the prosecution's duty to prove its case.
3. The safeguard is the presumption of innocence.

### #2: Knowing and Answering the Charge

An accused person is brought to trial without being told the charge. The court also bars both the accused and the defense lawyer from answering it. Which rights are affected?

**Solution:**

1. The accused must know the crime charged and the acts behind the charge.
2. The accused must be allowed to be heard personally and through counsel.
3. The facts affect both the right to know the accusation and the right to be heard with counsel.

### #3: Impartial and Public Trial

A criminal trial is closed to the public without a lawful reason. The judge also openly favors the prosecution. Which trial safeguards are involved?

**Solution:**

1. Closing the hearing without a lawful reason raises the right to a public trial.
2. Favoring the prosecution raises the right to an impartial trial.
3. Both are protected by Section $14$.

### #4: Repeated Trial Delays

A criminal trial is repeatedly postponed without a sound reason. The accused has asked for the trial to proceed, and the delay has caused a defense witness to become unavailable. Which right is most directly at issue?

**Solution:**

1. This is delay during a criminal trial, so check the speedy-trial right.
2. The repeated unexplained delays, requests to proceed, and harm to the defense all matter.
3. These facts raise a speedy-trial issue. A justified postponement would need a different assessment.

### #5: Witnesses and Defense Evidence

The defense cannot question a witness testifying against the accused. The court also refuses, without a valid reason, to order a needed defense witness to attend and bring records. Which safeguards are involved?

**Solution:**

1. The right to meet witnesses face to face lets the defense test testimony against the accused.
2. Compulsory process lets the defense seek court orders for witnesses and records.
3. The facts involve both safeguards.

### #6: Absence After Arraignment

After arraignment, an accused person receives proper notice of trial but stays away without a valid reason. May the trial proceed despite the absence?

**Solution:**

1. Arraignment has already taken place.
2. The accused was properly notified and the absence has no valid excuse.
3. Section $14$ permits trial to proceed under these conditions.

### #7: Suspension Without the Required Grounds

Officials seek to suspend the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus for convenience. There is neither invasion nor rebellion. Is the stated ground enough?

**Solution:**

1. Section $15$ requires invasion or rebellion.
2. Public safety must also require suspension.
3. Convenience meets neither condition, so it is not enough.

### #8: An Administrative Case

A disciplinary case before a government agency remains unresolved for an unreasonable period without a sound reason. Which Bill of Rights safeguard should be checked?

**Solution:**

1. Section $16$ covers cases before administrative bodies as well as courts and quasi-judicial bodies.
2. The case concerns delay in an agency's decision.
3. Check the right to speedy disposition of cases.

### #9: Forced Admission

An official forces a person to write an admission that the person committed a crime. Which right is most directly involved?

**Solution:**

1. The admission reveals what the person knows and is testimonial evidence.
2. It is forced and can be used against the person who wrote it.
3. Section $17$, the right against compelled self-incrimination, applies.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: A Charge Is Not Proof

> Filing a charge does not make the accused guilty.

The accused remains presumed innocent. Do not choose an answer that makes the charge itself proof of guilt.

### #2: Speedy Does Not Mean Instant

> A postponement alone does not prove a rights violation.

Check its length, reason, the accused's request for a timely trial, and harm caused by the delay. Repeated delays without good reason need closer review.

### #3: Public Safety Is Also Required

> Invasion or rebellion is only part of the suspension rule.

Public safety must also require suspension of the privilege of the writ. Ordinary convenience does not meet this rule.

### #4: The Two Speed Protections Differ

> Speedy disposition covers more than criminal trials.

Section $14$ protects speedy criminal trials. Section $16$ also covers cases before quasi-judicial and administrative bodies.

### #5: Silence and Self-Incrimination

> Section $17$ is not limited to police questioning.

Section $12$ protects silence and counsel during **custodial investigation**. This is police questioning of a suspect who is held or whose freedom to act is greatly limited. Under RA 7438, it also includes police invitations to question someone about an offense they are suspected of committing. These safeguards do not apply to every general inquiry. Section $17$ separately protects against forced testimony against oneself.
