## Core Concept

### Who Is a Philippine Citizen?

**Citizenship** means legal membership in a country. Some rights and public offices depend on it.

Article IV, Section $1$, names four groups of Philippine citizens:

1. Citizens when the $1987$ Constitution was adopted.
2. Those whose fathers or mothers are Philippine citizens.
3. Those born before January $17$, $1973$, to Filipino mothers, who elect Philippine citizenship upon reaching majority.
4. Those naturalized under law.

Check each parent's citizenship at the child's birth. Birth abroad does not block citizenship, and birth here alone does not grant it.

Test a child born in Tokyo in $2009$ to a Filipino mother and a Japanese father:

1. Check the birth date: $2009$ is after January $17$, $1973$, so either Filipino parent passes citizenship.
2. Check the parents at birth: the mother was Filipino, so the child is Filipino from birth under Section $1(2)$.

:::pair #birthplace-alone
Right: Base citizenship on a Filipino parent at birth or legal naturalization, not birthplace alone.
Wrong: Treating birth inside the Philippines as granting citizenship regardless of the parents' citizenship.
Article IV, Section $1$ follows blood parentage, not birthplace.
:::

:::pair #either-parent-no-naturalization
Right: Either a Filipino father or a Filipino mother makes a child Filipino from birth under Section $1(2)$.
Wrong: Thinking a child with one Filipino parent and one alien parent must be naturalized.
One Philippine citizen parent at birth satisfies Article IV, Section $1(2)$.
:::

:::check
A baby is born in Manila in $2018$ to two foreign citizens on work visas. Is the baby a Philippine citizen from birth?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Birth in the Philippines alone does not grant citizenship under Article IV, Section $1$, when neither parent is Filipino.
:::

### The Pre-$1973$ Election Rule

Under Section $1(3)$, **elect** means formally choose citizenship under law, not vote. **Age of majority** means legal adulthood.

RA $6809$, Section $1$, lowered the general age from $21$ to $18$; older cases require the law that applied then.

For pre-$1973$ births, check the father first. A child already Filipino through a Filipino father needs no election. Section $1(3)$ covers children born before January $17$, $1973$, to Filipino mothers and alien fathers.

Screen two people born abroad to a Filipino mother and an alien father, Marco in $1968$ and Liza in $1996$:

1. Check Marco ($1968$, before January $17$, $1973$): his father was an alien, so Marco must elect citizenship upon reaching majority.
2. Check Liza ($1996$, after January $17$, $1973$): her mother's citizenship makes her Filipino from birth with no election needed.

:::why
Why does January $17$, $1973$ matter? Before the $1973$ Constitution took effect on that date, automatic citizenship at birth generally followed the father.
:::

:::pair #post-1973-no-election
Right: A child born on or after January $17$, $1973$, to a Filipino mother is Filipino from birth, even abroad.
Wrong: Requiring a child born abroad after $1973$ to a Filipino parent to elect citizenship at majority.
Birth abroad never places a post-$1973$ child under the historical Section $1(3)$ election rule.
:::

:::check
Mateo was born in Cebu in $1969$ to a Filipino father and a foreign mother. Must Mateo elect Philippine citizenship upon reaching majority?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Mateo was already Filipino from birth through his Filipino father, so he never needed the Section $1(3)$ election step.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Filipino Mother

A child was born in $2005$. The mother was a Filipino citizen when the child was born. The father was an alien. Is the child Filipino from birth?

**Solution:**

1. Check the birth date: $2005$ falls after January $17$, $1973$, so either Filipino parent suffices.
2. Check the parents at birth: the mother was a Filipino citizen.

Answer: Yes, under Article IV, Section $1(2)$, because the mother was Filipino at birth.

**Check:** Under Section $1(2)$, a Filipino mother passes citizenship from birth even when the father is an alien.

### #2: Birth Abroad

Both parents were Filipino when their child was born abroad in $1970$. Must the child elect Philippine citizenship because of the birth date?

**Solution:**

1. Check the father first: the father was Filipino when the child was born.
2. Check birthplace: birth abroad never prevents citizenship through a Filipino parent.

Answer: No, the child was already Filipino from birth through the father and needs no election.

**Check:** Section $1(3)$ election applies only when a pre-$1973$ child needs citizenship through a Filipino mother because the father was an alien.

### #3: Boundary Date and Birthplace

Maya was born in Rome on January $18$, $1973$, to a Filipino mother and an Italian father. Paolo was born in Iloilo in $2004$ to two foreign citizens, neither naturalized. Which person is Filipino from birth, and must Maya elect citizenship?

**Solution:**

1. Check Maya: compare January $18$, $1973$ with the January $17$, $1973$ cutoff and note her Filipino mother.
2. Check Paolo: test birth inside the Philippines when neither parent is a Philippine citizen.

:::try
Finish the last step: state which person is Filipino from birth and explain whether Maya must elect citizenship.
:::answer
The answer is **Only Maya is Filipino from birth, and she needs no election.** Born after January $17$, $1973$, to a Filipino mother, Maya is Filipino from birth under Section $1(2)$ even abroad. Paolo is not Filipino from birth because birthplace alone grants no citizenship under Article IV, Section $1$.

**Check:** Match January $18$, $1973$ to the post-$1973$ equal-parent rule, and check parentage rather than Iloilo birthplace for Paolo.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Birth inside the Philippines: check the parents' citizenship at birth, not birthplace alone. {#birthplace-alone}
- One alien parent: one Filipino parent at birth gives citizenship with no naturalization needed. {#either-parent-no-naturalization}
- Birth abroad after $1973$ or Filipino father before $1973$: no Section $1(3)$ election is needed. {#post-1973-no-election}
:::
