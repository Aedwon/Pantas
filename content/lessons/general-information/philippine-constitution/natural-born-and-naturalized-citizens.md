## Core Concept

### Natural-Born Status and High Offices

Under Article IV, Section $2$, a **natural-born citizen** is Filipino from birth without an act to acquire or complete citizenship. Those who validly elect under Section $1(3)$ are also deemed natural-born.

**Naturalization** is the legal process by which an **alien**, someone not a citizen of the country concerned, becomes Filipino. A naturalized citizen is not natural-born.

The President, Senators, Representatives, and Civil Service Commission members must be natural-born. Citizenship alone is not enough.

Test a naturalized Philippine citizen with $15$ years of citizenship who plans to run for Senator:

1. Check citizenship status: naturalization gives citizenship, not natural-born status.
2. Check Article VI, Section $3$: Senators must be natural-born, so the candidate fails.

Section $2$ expressly places valid Section $1(3)$ electors inside natural-born citizenship.
:::

### Judicial vs. Administrative Naturalization

Two naturalization laws use different age minimums, residence rules, and measuring events:

1. **Commonwealth Act $473$, Section $2$** (judicial): generally requires age $21$ at the hearing and $10$ continuous years of Philippine residence. Section $3$ lowers residence to $5$ years for listed cases. Other conditions and disqualifications apply.
2. **RA $9139$** (administrative): covers aliens born in the Philippines and residing here since birth. It requires age $18$ at filing and other qualifications.

Do not apply one process's age rule to every naturalization case. Birth here alone also grants no citizenship without completing the process.

Compare two $19$-year-old applicants. Carlo was born abroad with $10$ years in Manila and applies under Commonwealth Act $473$. Bea was born in Iloilo, lived there since birth, and files under RA $9139$:

1. Check Carlo: Commonwealth Act $473$ requires age $21$ at the hearing, so age $19$ fails.
2. Check Bea: RA $9139$ covers Philippine-born lifelong residents and uses age $18$ at filing, so age $19$ passes.

:::pair #two-naturalization-age-rules
Right: Match each law and event: Commonwealth Act $473$ uses age $21$ at the hearing, while RA $9139$ uses age $18$ at filing.
Wrong: Applying age $21$ to RA $9139$, or applying age $18$ to Commonwealth Act $473$.
Check both the statute and the event at which minimum age is measured.
:::

:::check
A foreign citizen born in Japan has lived in Cebu for $12$ years and will be $22$ at his Commonwealth Act $473$ hearing. Does he meet the age and general residence minimums?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Commonwealth Act $473$, Section $2$, generally requires age $21$ at the hearing and $10$ continuous years of Philippine residence.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Whether Election Was Needed

Ramon was born in March $1973$ to a Filipino mother and a foreign father. He never filed an election of citizenship at majority. Is he still a natural-born citizen?

**Solution:**

1. Compare his birth date with January $17$, $1973$: March $1973$ is after the cutoff, so Section $1(3)$ election did not apply.
2. Apply Article IV, Section $2$: because his mother was Filipino at birth, he became Filipino from birth with no act required.

Answer: Yes, Ramon is natural-born because no election was required after January $17$, $1973$.

**Check:** Check the January $17$, $1973$ cutoff before assuming a child of a Filipino mother needed Section $1(3)$ election.

### #2: Senate Qualification

Ana is natural-born. Ben is naturalized. Both meet all other Senate qualifications. Who meets the citizenship requirement?

**Solution:**

1. Check Article VI, Section $3$: Senators must be natural-born citizens.
2. Compare Ana and Ben: Ben is naturalized, while Ana is natural-born.

Answer: Ana meets the requirement because Senators must be natural-born.

**Check:** Naturalization grants citizenship, yet offices requiring natural-born status exclude naturalized citizens.

### #3: Screening Two Naturalization Paths

Rosa was born in Bacolod to foreign parents, lived there since birth, and files under RA $9139$ at age $17$. Tomas was born in Seoul, has lived in Davao for $11$ years, and files under RA $9139$ at age $23$. Does either applicant qualify under RA $9139$, and does either fit Commonwealth Act $473$'s age and residence minimums?

**Solution:**

1. Check Rosa's birthplace, lifelong residence, and filing age against RA $9139$, Section $3$.
2. Check Tomas's birthplace against RA $9139$, then compare his age and continuous residence with Commonwealth Act $473$, Section $2$.

:::try
Finish the last step: decide whether either applicant qualifies under RA $9139$, and test Tomas against Commonwealth Act $473$'s age and residence minimums.
:::answer
The answer is **Neither qualifies under RA $9139$, and Tomas fits Commonwealth Act $473$'s age and residence minimums.** Rosa was born and raised in Bacolod, yet age $17$ is under RA $9139$'s age $18$ minimum at filing. Foreign birth bars Tomas from RA $9139$, while age $23$ and $11$ continuous years clear Commonwealth Act $473$'s age $21$ and $10$-year minimums.

**Check:** Match RA $9139$ to Philippine-born lifelong residents at age $18$ at filing, and Commonwealth Act $473$ to age $21$ at the hearing.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- High offices and naturalization: naturalization gives citizenship, not natural-born status. {#naturalized-not-natural-born}
- Pre-$1973$ maternal election: valid Section $1(3)$ electors are deemed natural-born under Section $2$. {#proper-election-is-natural-born}
- Naturalization age rules: use $21$ at the hearing for Commonwealth Act $473$ and $18$ at filing for RA $9139$. {#two-naturalization-age-rules}
:::
