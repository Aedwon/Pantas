## Core Concept

### Secrecy, Sanctity, and Accessible Voting

Article V, Section $2$, directs Congress to secure the **secrecy and sanctity of the ballot**. Secrecy keeps choices private. Sanctity keeps voting free from interference.

Congress must also design unassisted voting for voters with disabilities and those who cannot read. Until then, they vote under existing laws and **COMELEC** (Commission on Elections) rules protecting ballot secrecy.

Test two precinct plans where unassisted machines are unavailable for voters who cannot read:

1. Precinct A follows COMELEC secrecy rules so voters choose privately: this follows Section $2$'s interim rule.
2. Precinct B tells voters to announce choices aloud to a clerk: this violates ballot secrecy under Section $2$.

:::why
Why require unassisted voting while keeping interim rules? Helpers risk exposing votes or swaying choices, yet interim secrecy rules prevent excluding voters meantime.
:::

:::pair #ballot-secrecy-and-sanctity
Right: Article V, Section $2$ requires protecting ballot secrecy and sanctity so choices stay private and free from interference.
Wrong: Announcing or exposing a voter's marked choices publicly so officials can verify them.
Every regular and interim voting rule must protect the privacy of the ballot.
:::

:::pair #interim-voting-without-exclusion
Right: Congress must enable unassisted voting for disabled and non-reading voters, while existing laws and COMELEC secrecy rules apply meanwhile.
Wrong: Excluding voters who cannot read until a fully unassisted system is built.
Article V, Section $1$ bars literacy tests, and Section $2$ preserves interim voting under secrecy rules.
:::

:::check
A local rule lets a relative choose candidates for a voter who uses a wheelchair, even though the voter decides independently. Does that follow Article V, Section $2$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article V, Section $2$ aims at independent voting for the voter's own choice, not letting someone else pick candidates.
:::

### Overseas Absentee Voting

**Absentee voting** means voting while away from your normal voting place. Article V, Section $2$ requires an absentee voting system for qualified Filipinos abroad.

RA $9189$, as amended by RA $10590$, sets overseas voting rules, including eligibility and registration. Being abroad alone does not qualify someone to vote.

Check a $24$-year-old Filipino engineer in Singapore who skipped overseas registration and arrives at the embassy on election day:

1. Check constitutional coverage: as a Filipino abroad, she falls under Article V, Section $2$.
2. Check statutory rules: under RA $9189$ and RA $10590$, she lacks overseas registration and cannot vote.

:::pair #overseas-voting-conditions
Right: Under Article V, Section $2$ and RA $9189$ (amended by RA $10590$), Filipinos abroad must meet overseas eligibility and registration rules.
Wrong: Letting every Filipino abroad vote without registration because Philippine citizenship alone is enough.
The Constitution mandates the system, while statute sets registration and eligibility conditions.
:::

:::check
A foreign citizen born in Paris has a Filipino aunt in Manila and asks to register as an overseas absentee voter. May he register?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article V, Section $2$ covers qualified **Filipinos** abroad, not foreign nationals with Filipino relatives.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Ballot Protection

What must Congress provide under Article V, Section $2$?

**Solution:**

1. Secure the secrecy and sanctity of the ballot.
2. Provide absentee voting for qualified Filipinos abroad.
3. Design unassisted voting for disabled and non-reading voters, while allowing interim voting under existing laws and COMELEC secrecy rules.

Answer: Ballot secrecy and sanctity, overseas absentee voting, and unassisted voting (with interim COMELEC secrecy rules meanwhile).

**Check:** Each duty in Article V, Section $2$ protects private, independent voting at home and abroad.

### #2: Registering from Abroad

Marco and Nadia are Filipino nurses in Toronto. Marco completed overseas voter registration under RA $9189$, as amended by RA $10590$. Nadia never registered. May both cast ballots at the consulate?

**Solution:**

1. Check Marco: he is a qualified Filipino abroad who met statutory eligibility and registration rules.
2. Check Nadia: being abroad as a Filipino does not bypass required overseas registration.

Answer: Only Marco may vote abroad; Nadia cannot vote without overseas registration.

**Check:** Overseas voting requires both Philippine citizenship abroad and registration under RA $9189$ and RA $10590$.

### #3: Reviewing Two Precinct Proposals

A town lacks tactile ballots for voters with visual impairments. Official X proposes barring those voters until machines arrive. Official Y proposes letting watchers call out their choices at the table. Evaluate both proposals under Article V, Section $2$.

**Solution:**

1. Check Official X against the interim rule: Section $2$ keeps voters voting under existing laws and COMELEC rules meanwhile.
2. Check Official Y against ballot secrecy: calling choices aloud exposes the voter's private vote.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both proposals and state what procedure Article V, Section $2$ requires meanwhile.
:::answer
The answer is **Both proposals violate Article V, Section $2$: voters cannot be excluded, and choices cannot be read aloud.** Until Congress designs unassisted voting, these voters vote under existing laws and COMELEC rules that protect ballot secrecy.

**Check:** Under Section $2$, interim rules must keep access open while preserving ballot secrecy.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Public ballot checks: never expose a voter's choices, even during interim help. {#ballot-secrecy-and-sanctity}
- Missing unassisted tools: allow voting under existing laws and COMELEC secrecy rules instead of excluding voters. {#interim-voting-without-exclusion}
- Filipinos abroad: check overseas registration and eligibility under RA $9189$ and RA $10590$. {#overseas-voting-conditions}
:::
