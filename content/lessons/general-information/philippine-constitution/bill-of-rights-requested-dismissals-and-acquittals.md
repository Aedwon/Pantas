## Core Concept

### Section $21$ and Rule $117$: Requested Dismissals

Under Article III, Section $21$ and **Rule $117$, Section $7$**, a dismissal **requested by the accused** generally allows retrial.

Never decide double jeopardy from the word "dismissed" alone. A dismissal requested for a procedural defect leaves guilt undecided, so prosecutors may usually refile.

Test Rule $117$, Section $7$ when a defendant wins dismissal because a clerk omitted a verification signature:

1. Check consent and ground: the accused sought dismissal for a procedural defect, not weak proof or speedy trial.
2. Apply Rule $117$, Section $7$: double jeopardy does not bar refiling a proper charge.

:::pair #requested-dismissal-reason-matters
Right: A requested procedural dismissal allows retrial unless based on insufficient evidence or speedy-trial denial.
Wrong: Treating every dismissal after a plea as an acquittal that bars retrial.
Always inspect why the court dismissed the case, since a requested procedural dismissal leaves retrial open unless an exception applies.
:::

:::check
After pleading not guilty, an accused wins dismissal because the charge listed the wrong street number. Is retrial barred?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A requested procedural dismissal generally leaves double jeopardy open to a new valid charge.
:::

### Rule $119$, Section $23$ and Section $14(2)$: Exceptions That Bar Retrial

Two key exceptions bar retrial even when the accused requested dismissal:

1. **Granted demurrer to evidence**: Under **Rule $119$, Section $23$**, after the prosecution rests, the accused may request dismissal for insufficient proof. This **demurrer to evidence** counts as an **acquittal** by the judge (Philippine trials have no juries), barring retrial.
2. **Speedy-trial dismissal**: A requested dismissal granted because the **Section $14(2)$** right to a speedy trial was violated also bars retrial. No prior conviction is needed.

Test Rule $119$, Section $23$ when a judge grants a demurrer because no witness linked the accused to the robbery:

1. Check the ruling: the court weighed the prosecution's evidence and found it insufficient to convict.
2. Apply Section $21$: a granted demurrer is an acquittal that bars a second trial.

:::why
Why bar retrial after a granted demurrer or speedy-trial dismissal? In both cases, the state failed either to prove guilt or to try the case promptly.
:::

:::pair #granted-demurrer-is-acquittal
Right: A granted demurrer to evidence finds proof insufficient and operates as a binding acquittal.
Wrong: Claiming another trial is allowed because every requested dismissal waives double jeopardy.
In Philippine courts, the judge decides guilt, and granting a demurrer for weak proof is an acquittal.
:::

:::pair #speedy-trial-exception
Right: A requested dismissal for violating the Section $14(2)$ right to a speedy trial bars retrial.
Wrong: Claiming only a granted demurrer can protect an accused who asked for dismissal.
Both insufficient-evidence demurrers and constitutional speedy-trial dismissals bar reprosecution.
:::

:::check
After the prosecution rests, the judge denies the defense's demurrer to evidence because the proof is strong enough to continue trial. Does filing a demurrer alone acquit the accused?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Rule $119$, Section $23$, only a **granted** demurrer for insufficient evidence operates as an acquittal barring retrial.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Misspelled Name Dismissal vs. Unconsented Dismissal

After pleading not guilty to estafa in a proper court, Ana asks to dismiss the charge for a misspelled middle name. In another proper court after plea, Ben's theft case is dismissed without his express consent. Which case may prosecutors refile?

**Solution:**

1. Check Ana's case: she requested dismissal for a procedural defect, with no weak-proof acquittal or speedy-trial ruling.
2. Check Ben's case: dismissal without the accused's express consent after a valid plea triggers double jeopardy.

Answer: Prosecutors may refile Ana's case, yet double jeopardy bars refiling Ben's case.

**Check:** A requested procedural dismissal leaves retrial open, unlike an unconsented dismissal after a valid plea.

### #2: Granted Demurrer for Missing Proof

During a falsification trial after arraignment and plea, the prosecution rests without producing the allegedly forged land deed or handwriting proof. The judge grants the defense's demurrer to evidence for insufficient proof. Prosecutors argue that only a jury verdict can acquit after a defense request. Can the State retry the case?

**Solution:**

1. Check the demurrer ruling: under Rule $119$, Section $23$, the judge weighed the proof and found it insufficient to convict.
2. Check Philippine trial procedure: Philippine courts have no juries, so a judge's granted demurrer operates as a binding acquittal.

Answer: No, double jeopardy bars another prosecution because the granted demurrer is an acquittal.

**Check:** A granted demurrer for weak proof ends the case as an acquittal despite being requested by the defense.

### #3: Comparing Two Requested Dismissals

Two cases proceed past arraignment and plea in proper courts. In Case A, the accused wins dismissal because the complaint used an unsigned copy. In Case B, the accused wins dismissal because the court finds a four-year stall violated Section $14(2)$'s speedy-trial right. May prosecutors refile either case?

**Solution:**

1. Check Case A's dismissal ground against Rule $117$, Section $7$'s general rule on requested dismissals.
2. Check Case B's dismissal ground against the constitutional speedy-trial exception.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on whether prosecutors may refile Case A, Case B, or both.
:::answer
The answer is **Prosecutors may refile Case A, while double jeopardy bars refiling Case B.** Case A ended on a requested procedural defect with no exception. Case B's dismissal for violating Section $14(2)$'s speedy-trial right bars retrial despite the defense request.

**Check:** Always inspect why the court granted a requested dismissal before deciding whether retrial is barred.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Case ended on defense request: check the dismissal reason before deciding double jeopardy. {#requested-dismissal-reason-matters}
- Judge granted a demurrer for weak proof: treat the ruling as an acquittal barring retrial. {#granted-demurrer-is-acquittal}
- Court dismissed for speedy-trial denial: block retrial even though the accused requested it. {#speedy-trial-exception}
:::
