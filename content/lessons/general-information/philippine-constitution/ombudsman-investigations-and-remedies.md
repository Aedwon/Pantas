## Core Concept

### Protectors of the People and Investigations

Under Article XI, Section $12$, the Ombudsman and Deputies act as **protectors of the people**. They act promptly on complaints in any form against government officials or employees, including government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs).

Under Section $13(1)$, the Ombudsman may investigate **on its own** or **on complaint by any person**. It covers any act or omission of a public office or worker that appears illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient.

Filing a complaint never proves guilt or automatically removes anyone. For impeachable officers, the Ombudsman investigates without replacing congressional impeachment.

Check two situations where the Ombudsman reviews possible misconduct:

1. An audit reveals apparent phantom road repairs though no citizen complained yet; the Ombudsman investigates on its own initiative.
2. A resident files a complaint alleging an unjust permit refusal; the Ombudsman acts promptly without removing the officer on day one.

:::pair #ombudsman-own-initiative
Right: The Ombudsman may investigate on its own initiative or on complaint when conduct appears illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient.
Wrong: Claiming a citizen's complaint is always required first, or that only already-proved crimes can be investigated.
Section $13(1)$ allows self-started probes into apparently illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient conduct.
:::

:::check
Citizens complain to the Ombudsman about an impeachable officer's alleged graft. Does the Ombudsman's investigation replace impeachment by Congress?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The Ombudsman may investigate complaints, yet only Congress holds the constitutional power to impeach and remove listed impeachable officers.
:::

### Corrective Orders, Records, and Recommendations

Article XI, Section $13(2)$-$(7)$, equips the Ombudsman with concrete investigative and corrective powers:

1. Direct officials or employees (including GOCCs with original charters) to **perform and expedite** any duty required by law, or stop abuse.
2. Request government assistance, inspect pertinent records and contracts, and report irregularities to the Commission on Audit (COA).
3. Direct the proper officer to act against an at-fault worker and **recommend** removal, suspension, demotion, fine, **censure** (formal disapproval), or prosecution. It ensures compliance as provided by law.
4. Study red tape, fraud, poor management, and corruption, and recommend fixes.

:::why
Why let the Ombudsman direct delayed duties alongside recommending discipline? Citizens need stalled public services finished promptly, rather than waiting months for penalties.
:::

Apply Section $13$ to a registrar who stalls a lawful land-title release for $8$ months:

1. Under Section $13(2)$, the Ombudsman directs the office to perform and expedite the delayed legal duty.
2. Under Section $13(5)$ and $13(3)$, it inspects office records directly and recommends censure, suspension, or prosecution.

:::pair #ombudsman-direct-vs-remove
Right: For an unfinished legal duty, the Ombudsman investigates the omission and directs performance without automatically firing the worker on receipt.
Wrong: Treating the receipt of a complaint as automatically removing the employee before any investigation.
Investigation precedes any recommended discipline, while Section $13(2)$ expedites the stalled legal duty.
:::

:::pair #ombudsman-records-without-consent
Right: The Ombudsman may request government assistance and examine pertinent records without the investigated employee's consent.
Wrong: Waiting for the investigated employee to consent before requesting government records.
Section $13(5)$ empowers the Ombudsman to gather agency records directly during an investigation.
:::

:::check
During a probe of a chartered GOCC's padded fuel contracts, the manager refuses to release vouchers without his signed consent. He also claims the Ombudsman cannot report irregularities to COA. Is he right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Article XI, Section $13$, the Ombudsman covers chartered GOCCs, examines pertinent records without consent, and reports irregularities to COA.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Own-Initiative Inquiry Into Agency Red Tape

Without a citizen complaint, the Ombudsman sees $4$-month unexplained backlogs in school-permit renewals across a regional office. Which Section $13$ powers let it examine records and fix the backlog?

**Solution:**

1. Under Section $13(1)$, the Ombudsman may investigate apparently inefficient omissions on its own initiative.
2. Under Section $13(5)$ and $13(7)$, it requests records, studies red tape, and recommends reforms.

Answer: It may open an own-initiative inquiry, inspect records, and recommend fixes for red tape.

**Check:** Confirm no citizen complaint is required before investigating apparent inefficiency or red tape.

### #2: Unfinished Legal Duty vs. Automatic Firing

A citizen complains that a licensing officer left a required clearance unissued for $10$ months without reason. Does filing automatically remove the officer, and what power targets the stalled clearance?

**Solution:**

1. A complaint alone never proves wrongdoing or automatically removes an employee.
2. Under Section $13(2)$, the Ombudsman may direct the officer to perform and expedite the required duty.

Answer: Removal is not automatic, and the Ombudsman may direct performance and expedite the clearance.

**Check:** Separate automatic firing (wrong) from directing performance of an unfinished duty (Section $13(2)$).

### #3: Irregular Contracts at a Public Hospital

Ombudsman investigators inspect a public hospital's supply contracts without the purchasing chief's permission and uncover double billing. The chief says reading contracts uninvited broke the rules. He adds that the Ombudsman can issue only a private warning with no follow-up. Rule on both claims.

**Solution:**

1. Check record access under Article XI, Section $13(5)$: test whether the chief's permission is needed to inspect pertinent contracts.
2. Check remedies under Sections $13(3)$ and $13(4)$: check what reports and sanctions the Ombudsman may pursue after finding double billing.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both claims and name the remedies and compliance duty available to the Ombudsman.
:::answer
The answer is **Both claims fail: the Ombudsman inspects contracts without consent, reports irregularities to COA, recommends sanctions or prosecution, and ensures lawful compliance.** Section $13(5)$ grants direct access to agency records. Section $13(3)$ covers recommending removal, suspension, demotion, fine, censure, or prosecution, with compliance ensured as provided by law.

**Check:** Verify direct record access, COA reporting, formal sanctions beyond private warnings, and compliance monitoring under law.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Own-initiative probes: investigate apparently illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient acts even without a complaint. {#ombudsman-own-initiative}
- Delayed duties: investigate omissions and direct performance without treating a complaint as instant firing. {#ombudsman-direct-vs-remove}
- Agency records: request government files directly without waiting for the employee's consent. {#ombudsman-records-without-consent}
:::
