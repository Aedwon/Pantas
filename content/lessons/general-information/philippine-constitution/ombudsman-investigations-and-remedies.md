## Core Concept

### Protectors of the People and Investigations

Under Article XI, Section $12$, the Ombudsman and Deputies act as **protectors of the people**. They act promptly on complaints in any form against public workers, including GOCCs.

Under Section $13(1)$, the Ombudsman investigates **on its own** or **on complaint** any public act or omission appearing illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient.

Filing a complaint never proves guilt or removes anyone. Under RA $6770$, Section $22$, the Ombudsman investigates alleged serious misconduct in office by impeachable officers. Its goal is filing a verified impeachment complaint if warranted, not replacing Congress.

Check two Ombudsman reviews:

1. An audit shows phantom road repairs with no citizen complaint; the Ombudsman investigates on its own.
2. A resident complains of an unjust permit denial; the Ombudsman acts promptly without day-one removal.

:::pair #ombudsman-own-initiative
Right: Under Section $13(1)$, the Ombudsman may investigate on its own initiative or on complaint.
Wrong: Claiming a citizen's complaint is always required before an investigation begins.
Section $13(1)$ allows self-started probes into apparently illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient conduct.
:::

:::check
Citizens complain to the Ombudsman of alleged serious misconduct in office by an impeachable officer. What is the probe's purpose under RA $6770$, Section $22$, and does it replace Congress?
:::answer
The answer is **To file a verified impeachment complaint if warranted, and no, it does not replace Congress.** Under RA $6770$, Section $22$, the Ombudsman investigates alleged serious misconduct for that purpose, while only Congress impeaches and removes listed officers.
:::

### Corrective Orders, Records, and Recommendations

Article XI, Section $13(2)$-$(7)$, grants concrete investigative and corrective powers:

1. Section $13(2)$: direct officials (including chartered GOCCs) to **perform and expedite** required legal duties, or stop abuse.
2. Section $13(4)$: in appropriate cases subject to legal limits, direct officers to furnish public-fund or public-property contract documents and report irregularities to COA.
3. Section $13(5)$: request government assistance and examine pertinent agency records and documents.
4. Section $13(3)$: direct action against at-fault workers and **recommend** removal, suspension, demotion, fine, **censure** (formal disapproval), or prosecution, ensuring lawful compliance.
5. Section $13(7)$: study red tape, fraud, poor management, and corruption, and recommend fixes.

:::why
Why direct delayed duties alongside recommending discipline? Citizens need stalled services finished quickly, without waiting months for penalties.
:::

Apply Section $13$ when a treasurer charges an illegal market surcharge:

1. Section $13(2)$: direct the treasurer to stop and correct the improper collection.
2. Sections $13(5)$ and $13(3)$: examine receipt records directly and recommend censure, suspension, or prosecution.

:::pair #ombudsman-direct-vs-remove
Right: Investigate omissions and direct performance without firing the worker on receipt.
Wrong: Treating receipt of a complaint as automatically removing the employee before investigation.
Investigation precedes recommended discipline, while Section $13(2)$ expedites stalled legal duties.
:::

:::pair #ombudsman-records-without-consent
Right: Request government assistance and examine pertinent records without employee consent.
Wrong: Waiting for the investigated employee to consent before requesting government records.
Section $13(5)$ grants direct access to agency records during investigations.
:::

:::check
In a probe of a chartered GOCC's padded fuel contracts, the manager refuses vouchers without his signed consent and denies COA reporting. Is he right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Article XI, Section $13(5)$, the Ombudsman examines pertinent agency records without consent. Under Section $13(4)$, in an appropriate case subject to legal limits, it obtains public-fund contract documents and reports irregularities to COA.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Own-Initiative Inquiry Into Agency Red Tape

With no citizen complaint, the Ombudsman sees $4$-month school-permit backlogs at a regional office. Which Section $13$ powers let it inspect records and fix the backlog?

**Solution:**

1. Section $13(1)$ lets the Ombudsman investigate apparent inefficiency on its own.
2. Sections $13(5)$ and $13(7)$ let it examine records, study red tape, and recommend fixes.

Answer: Start an own-initiative probe, examine records, and recommend red-tape fixes.

**Check:** No citizen complaint is required to probe apparent inefficiency or red tape.

### #2: Investigating Misconduct vs. Automatic Firing

A citizen accuses a payroll officer of padding overtime logs. Does filing automatically remove the officer, and what Section $13(3)$ power applies if fault is confirmed?

**Solution:**

1. Receiving a complaint never proves fault or removes an employee before investigation.
2. If fault is confirmed, Section $13(3)$ lets the Ombudsman direct action and recommend removal, suspension, demotion, fine, censure, or prosecution, ensuring lawful compliance.

Answer: No automatic removal on receipt; after investigation confirms fault, Section $13(3)$ lets it recommend sanctions and ensure compliance.

**Check:** Separate instant firing on receipt (wrong) from investigation followed by Section $13(3)$ discipline.

### #3: Irregular Contracts at a Public Hospital

Ombudsman investigators inspect a public hospital's supply contracts without the purchasing chief's consent and find double billing. The chief says uninvited inspection broke the rules and only a private warning can follow. Rule on both claims.

**Solution:**

1. Test record access under Section $13(5)$ and public-fund contract access under Section $13(4)$ without the chief's consent.
2. Check COA reporting under Section $13(4)$ and remedies under Section $13(3)$ after finding double billing.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both claims and name the specific Section $13$ powers available to the Ombudsman.
:::answer
The answer is **Both claims fail.** Under Section $13(5)$, the Ombudsman examines pertinent agency records without consent. Under Section $13(4)$, in an appropriate case subject to legal limits, it directs officers to furnish public-fund contract documents and reports irregularities to COA. Under Section $13(3)$, it recommends removal, suspension, demotion, fine, censure, or prosecution, with lawful compliance.

**Check:** Match pertinent agency records to Section $13(5)$, public-fund contract copies and COA reports to Section $13(4)$, and sanctions to Section $13(3)$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Own-initiative probes: the Ombudsman may start an investigation on its own without waiting for a citizen's complaint. {#ombudsman-own-initiative}
- Delayed duties: investigate omissions and direct performance without treating a complaint as instant firing. {#ombudsman-direct-vs-remove}
- Agency records: request government files directly without waiting for the employee's consent. {#ombudsman-records-without-consent}
:::
