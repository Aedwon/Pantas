## Core Concept

### Age at Appointment

Under Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, a CSC member must be **at least $35$ years old at appointment**.

1. Exactly $35$ years old on appointment day passes.
2. Age $34$ on appointment day fails, even if the birthday arrives soon after.
3. Neither the President nor the Commission on Appointments can waive this minimum.

Check two nominees appointed on June $10$:

1. Ana turned $35$ on June $10$, so she passes.
2. Ben turns $35$ on June $15$, so at appointment he is $34$ and fails.

:::pair #age-at-appointment
Right: Measure age on the exact appointment date; the nominee must already be at least $35$.
Wrong: Treating a birthday after appointment as meeting the $35$-year rule retroactively.
Aging during a $7$-year term never cures being under $35$ at appointment.
:::

:::check
May the President and the Commission on Appointments waive the $35$-year minimum for a $33$-year-old nominee with $15$ years of bureau leadership?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Neither the President nor the Commission on Appointments can waive Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$'s $35$-year minimum at appointment.
:::

### Proven Capacity for Public Administration

Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, requires **proven capacity for public administration**.

This means shown ability to run government work well. Managing public staff, budgets, and services shows this capacity.

1. The Constitution requires no single degree, job title, or test as sole proof.
2. A private business title alone or a promise to learn later never proves capacity.
3. Auditing, diplomacy, or litigation alone never replaces public-administration capacity.

Compare two candidates' records:

1. Candidate A led a public health office of $200$ staff for $8$ years, showing proven public administration.
2. Candidate B won local races and promises to learn management later, proving nothing yet.

:::pair #proven-public-administration
Right: Require proven public-administration capacity from real work, not a promise or title alone.
Wrong: Accepting a promise to learn administration later or a private business title without proof.
Proven means demonstrated before appointment through actual administrative work.
:::

:::check
A nominee has $12$ years of appellate litigation work and zero public management record. Does litigation alone prove public-administration capacity?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Appellate litigation alone never substitutes for proven public-administration capacity.
:::

### The Immediately Preceding Election Rule

Under Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, an appointee must **not have been a candidate for any elective position in the elections immediately preceding appointment**.

1. The bar covers **any elective position**, national or local.
2. It bars every candidate, whether **winner or loser**.
3. It checks the **immediately preceding election**, not "ever ran" or "ran last year."
4. Meeting age, citizenship, or capacity never removes this bar.

:::why
Why bar recent candidates? Keeping recent politicians out shields civil service hiring and discipline from partisan payback.
:::

Screen two former candidates for a $2026$ CSC seat after $2025$ elections:

1. Candidate C lost a $2025$ council race and is barred.
2. Candidate D ran in $2016$ and skipped $2019$, $2022$, and $2025$, so D is clear.

:::pair #immediately-preceding-candidacy
Right: Bar anyone who ran for any national or local office, win or lose, in the immediately preceding election.
Wrong: Limiting the CSC candidacy bar to losing candidates or to national elective races.
Section $1(1)$ bars all candidates in any elective race immediately before appointment.
:::

:::check
A $42$-year-old natural-born bureau head won a provincial board seat in the immediately preceding election. Can she join the CSC next?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Running in the immediately preceding local race bars her despite winning and meeting age rules.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Checking the Birthday Cutoff

Elena is $34$ on March $1$, her appointment date, and turns $35$ on March $2$. Marco turns $35$ on March $1$. Who meets the age rule?

**Solution:**

1. Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, requires at least age $35$ at appointment.
2. On March $1$, Marco is $35$ and passes.
3. On March $1$, Elena is $34$, and a March $2$ birthday cannot fix March $1$.

Answer: Only Marco meets the age requirement at appointment.

**Check:** Measure age on appointment day itself; $35$ passes and $34$ fails.

### #2: Screening Administrative Proof

Applicant X directed regional agency offices for $10$ years. Applicant Y holds a private cafe manager title with no public record. Applicant Z promises to study administration after confirmation. Who meets the capacity rule?

**Solution:**

1. Proven capacity requires a shown record of managing public work.
2. Applicant X's $10$-year agency leadership proves that capacity.
3. Applicant Y's private title and Applicant Z's promise show zero proven public administration.

Answer: Only Applicant X meets the proven-capacity requirement.

**Check:** Look for real public-administration work done before appointment.

### #3: Applying All Qualifications Together

A natural-born citizen is $38$ and ran a regional bureau for $9$ years. In the immediately preceding election, she ran for mayor and lost. Does she qualify for the CSC?

**Solution:**

1. Check citizenship and age: natural-born and $38 \ge 35$ both pass.
2. Check capacity: $9$ years leading a bureau shows proven public-administration capacity.

:::try
Finish the last step: apply the candidacy rule and state whether she qualifies.
:::answer
The answer is **No, she is disqualified by her candidacy in the immediately preceding election.** Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, bars any candidate in that race, winner or loser. Age and administrative credentials never waive this rule.

**Check:** Check all four qualifications together; failing one rule disqualifies the nominee.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Age cutoff: verify age $35$ on appointment day, not after. {#age-at-appointment}
- Capacity proof: require real public-administration work, never a promise or private title. {#proven-public-administration}
- Prior candidacy: bar winners and losers from any race in the immediately preceding election. {#immediately-preceding-candidacy}
:::
