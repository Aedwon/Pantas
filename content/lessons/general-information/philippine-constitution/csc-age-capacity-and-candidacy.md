## Core Concept

### Age at Appointment

Under Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, a CSC member must be **at least $35$ years old at appointment**.

1. Exactly $35$ years old on appointment day passes.
2. Age $34$ on appointment day fails, even if the birthday arrives soon after.
3. Neither the President nor the Commission on Appointments can waive this minimum.

Check two nominees appointed in April:

1. Ana is $38$ years old on appointment day, so she passes.
2. Ben is $34$ and will not turn $35$ until October, so at appointment he fails.

:::pair #age-at-appointment
Right: Measure age on the exact appointment date; the nominee must already be at least $35$.
Wrong: Treating a birthday after appointment as meeting the $35$-year rule retroactively.
Aging during a $7$-year term never cures being under $35$ at appointment.
:::

:::check
Luis turns $35$ on November $3$, the exact morning he takes his CSC appointment. Does he satisfy the age requirement?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Under Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, being at least $35$ on the exact appointment date satisfies the minimum age.
:::

### Proven Capacity for Public Administration

Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, requires **proven capacity for public administration**.

This means shown ability to run government work well. Managing public staff, budgets, and services shows this capacity.

1. The Constitution requires no single degree, job title, or test as sole proof.
2. A private business title alone or a promise to learn later never proves capacity.
3. Specialized work outside public management never replaces proven public-administration capacity.

Compare two candidates' records:

1. Candidate A led a public health office of $200$ staff for $8$ years, showing proven public administration.
2. Candidate B won local races and promises to learn management later, proving nothing yet.

:::pair #proven-public-administration
Right: Require proven public-administration capacity from real work, not a promise or title alone.
Wrong: Accepting a promise to learn administration later or a private business title without proof.
Proven means demonstrated before appointment through actual administrative work.
:::

:::check
A nominee lacks a public-administration degree, yet directed provincial hospital staffing and public budgets for $9$ years. Does lacking that degree bar her?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, requires proven capacity for public administration through real work, not any single academic degree.
:::

### The Immediately Preceding Election Rule

Under Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, an appointee must **not have been a candidate for any elective position in the elections immediately preceding appointment**.

1. The bar covers **any elective position**, national or local.
2. It bars every candidate, whether **winner or loser**.
3. It checks the **immediately preceding election**, not "ever ran" or "ran last year."
4. Meeting age, citizenship, or capacity never removes this bar.

:::why
Why bar recent candidates? Keeping recent politicians out shields civil service hiring and discipline from partisan payback.
:::

Screen two former candidates for a $2026$ CSC seat after $2025$ elections:

1. Candidate C lost a $2025$ council race and is barred.
2. Candidate D ran in $2016$ and skipped $2019$, $2022$, and $2025$, so D is clear.

:::pair #immediately-preceding-candidacy
Right: Bar every candidate, winner or loser, from the immediately preceding election.
Wrong: Limiting the CSC candidacy bar only to losing candidates in the immediately preceding election.
Section $1(1)$ uses the word candidates without separating winners from losers.
:::

:::check
A $42$-year-old natural-born bureau head ran for mayor two election cycles ago and skipped the immediately preceding election. Does that older race bar her?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, bars only candidates in the elections immediately preceding appointment, not earlier election cycles.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Picking the Controlling Date

Elena is $34$ when she submits her resume to the search committee in February. She turns $35$ on April $15$, and the President appoints her on May $1$. Officials ask whether her February age bars her. Which date controls under Article IX-B?

**Solution:**

1. Identify the controlling event in Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$: minimum age $35$ is measured **at the time of appointment**, not during earlier screening.
2. Check Elena's age on May $1$: having turned $35$ on April $15$, she is already $35$ on appointment day.

Answer: May $1$ (appointment day) controls, so Elena meets the $35$-year minimum.

**Check:** Measure age strictly at appointment; neither an earlier screening date nor a later birthday replaces appointment day.

### #2: Screening Administrative Proof

Applicant X directed regional agency offices for $10$ years. Applicant Y holds a private cafe manager title with no public record. Applicant Z promises to study administration after confirmation. Who meets the capacity rule?

**Solution:**

1. Test all three applicants against Section $1(1)$'s rule requiring a shown record of managing public work.
2. Check Applicant X: $10$ years directing regional agency offices proves public-administration capacity.
3. Check Applicants Y and Z: a private cafe title and a future promise show zero proven public administration.

Answer: Only Applicant X meets the proven-capacity requirement.

**Check:** Look for real public-administration work done before appointment.

### #3: Applying All Qualifications Together

A natural-born citizen is $46$ and managed a national bureau for $12$ years. In the immediately preceding election, she won a House district seat. Does winning that national election clear her for a CSC seat?

**Solution:**

1. Check citizenship, age, and capacity: natural-born status, age $46$ ($\ge 35$), and $12$ years leading a bureau all pass.
2. Check the candidacy rule: identify whether winning a race in the immediately preceding election creates an exception under Section $1(1)$.

:::try
Finish the last step: apply the candidacy rule and state whether she qualifies for the CSC.
:::answer
The answer is **No, she is disqualified because she was a candidate in the immediately preceding election.** Article IX-B, Section $1(1)$, bars every candidate in that election, winner or loser. Winning the race and strong administrative credentials never waive this rule.

**Check:** Test every qualification separately; winning an immediately preceding race still disqualifies the nominee.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Age cutoff: verify age $35$ on appointment day, not after. {#age-at-appointment}
- Capacity proof: require real public-administration work, never a promise or private title. {#proven-public-administration}
- Prior candidacy: bar winners as well as losers from the immediately preceding election. {#immediately-preceding-candidacy}
:::
