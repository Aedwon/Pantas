## Core Concept

### Age, Capacity, and Prior Candidacy

- Be at least $35$ years old at appointment.
- Have proven capacity for public administration.
- Not have been a candidate for any elective position in the election immediately before appointment.

**Proven capacity for public administration** means a person has shown the ability to run government work well. A record of managing public staff, services, and resources can help show this ability. The Constitution does not require one named degree, job, or test as the sole proof.

The candidacy rule concerns the election immediately before appointment. It does not say that a person who ever ran for office is barred forever.

## Examples

### #1: Age at Appointment

Ana is exactly $35$ years old on her appointment date. Ben will turn $35$ the next day. Who meets the age requirement on that date?

**Solution:**

1. A member must be at least $35$ at appointment.
2. Ana has reached that age. Ben has not.
3. Ana meets the age requirement. Ben does not yet meet it.

### #2: Evidence of Administrative Ability

A nominee presents records of successfully managing staff and public services in a government office. Another person says only that the nominee is a close friend. Which evidence better supports proven capacity for public administration?

**Solution:**

1. The requirement concerns shown ability to manage government work.
2. The management records give evidence of that ability. Friendship does not.
3. The records better support the requirement. No single job title automatically proves the nominee meets it.

### #3: Prior Candidacy

A CSC nominee ran for mayor in the election immediately preceding the planned appointment. The nominee lost. Does losing remove the constitutional bar?

**Solution:**

1. The rule concerns being a candidate in that election.
2. It does not depend on winning.
3. The nominee does not meet this qualification.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Exactly $35$ Is Enough

> Check age on the appointment date.

“At least $35$” includes exactly $35$. Turning $35$ after appointment does not meet the rule on that date.

### #2: Candidacy Means the Immediately Preceding Election

> Do not replace the rule with “ran last year” or “ever ran.”

The relevant election is the one immediately before appointment. Losing that election does not remove the bar.
