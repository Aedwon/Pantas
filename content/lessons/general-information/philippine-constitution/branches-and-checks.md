## Core Concept

### Three Kinds of Power

The $1987$ Constitution divides government power among three co-equal branches so no single office holds total control.

1. **Legislative power** means making laws. Article VI, Section $1$ gives it to Congress, except powers reserved to people through initiative and referendum. Those let citizens propose or vote on laws directly.
2. **Executive power** means carrying out laws. Article VII, Section $1$ gives it to the President.
3. **Judicial power** means settling actual legal disputes and checking grave abuse of government power. Article VIII, Section $1$ gives it to the Supreme Court and lower courts.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: columns Legislative: Congress makes laws; Executive: President carries out laws; Judicial: Supreme Court and lower courts settle legal disputes and check government acts -->

Match each branch role when Congress passes a clean-water law, an agency enforces it, and a shop sues over a fine:

1. Passing the clean-water law is legislative power in Congress.
2. Enforcing the rules through an agency is executive power under the President.
3. Deciding the shop's lawsuit over the fine is judicial power in the courts.

:::pair #execute-vs-decide
Right: The President executes laws, while the courts settle actual disputes over legal rights.
Wrong: Giving law execution to Congress or the courts, or letting the President settle legal disputes alone.
Carrying out a law is executive work, and deciding cases over legal rights is judicial work.
:::

:::check
The President directs the Department of Health to carry out a new immunization statute nationwide. Which branch exercises this power?
:::answer
The answer is **The executive branch (the President)**. Under Article VII, Section $1$, carrying out and enforcing statutes is executive power.
:::

### Checks and Balances

A **check and balance** lets one branch limit another branch's use of power.

1. President to Congress: the President rejects a bill by **veto** (Article VI, Section $27(1)$).
2. Congress to President: Congress repasses a vetoed bill by **veto override** (Article VI, Section $27(1)$).
3. Commission on Appointments to President: the Commission gives or withholds consent for listed presidential appointments (Article VII, Section $16$).
4. Congress to impeachable officers: the House files charges, and the Senate holds the trial (Article XI, Section $3$).
5. Courts to Congress and President: courts check laws and government acts through **judicial review** (Article VIII, Section $1$).

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: Congress, President, Courts connected by arrows President to Congress: veto; Congress to President: override; Commission on Appointments to presidential appointments: consent; Congress to impeachable officers: impeachment; Courts to Congress and President: judicial review -->

Trace the checks when the President rejects a tariff bill and Congress repasses it anyway:

1. Rejecting the bill is the President's veto under Article VI, Section $27(1)$.
2. Repassing the bill despite that rejection is Congress's veto override.

:::why
Why give each branch specific checks on the others? Separated powers prevent abuse by forcing branches to answer to constitutional limits.
:::

:::pair #veto-and-override-check
Right: Rejecting a bill is a veto, and repassing it over that rejection is a veto override.
Wrong: Calling a bill rejection judicial review, or pairing a veto with appointment consent.
Check what is blocked: bills use veto and override, appointees use consent, and courts use judicial review.
:::

:::check
A constitutional body of the Senate President, $12$ Senators, and $12$ Representatives votes on whether to confirm the President's nominee for Secretary of Health. Which check is operating?
:::answer
The answer is **Commission on Appointments consent**. Under Article VI, Section $18$ and Article VII, Section $16$, that bicameral Commission confirms listed presidential appointments.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Match the Branch

A governor asks which branch writes a new fishing law, which directs coast-guard enforcement, and which decides a harbor rights lawsuit. Match each task to its branch.

**Solution:**

1. Writing the fishing law uses legislative power in Congress under Article VI, Section $1$.
2. Directing coast-guard enforcement uses executive power under the President (Article VII, Section $1$).
3. Deciding the harbor rights lawsuit uses judicial power in the courts (Article VIII, Section $1$).

Answer: Congress (legislative), the President (executive), and the courts (judicial).

**Check:** Lawmaking belongs to Congress, enforcement to the President, and dispute settlement to the courts.

### #2: Two Checks on a Law

Congress overrides a veto. Later, a court declares the law unconstitutional in a proper case. Which powers were used?

**Solution:**

1. Congress used its **veto-override** power under Article VI, Section $27(1)$ to pass the bill despite the President's rejection.
2. The court used **judicial review** under Article VIII, Section $1$ to measure the law against the Constitution.

Answer: Veto override by Congress and judicial review by the court.

**Check:** Each check belongs to a different branch: override counters a veto, and judicial review tests constitutionality in court.

### #3: Sorting Checks by Action

The House files formal removal charges against an impeachable official, and the Senate prepares for trial. Separately, a factory owner files a proper court case claiming a government board acted beyond its legal authority. Which constitutional check applies to each event?

**Solution:**

1. Check the congressional action: the House charging and the Senate trying an impeachable official falls under Article XI, Section $3$.
2. Check the court case against the board: reviewing whether a government body acted beyond its authority falls under Article VIII, Section $1$.

:::try
Finish the last step: name both checks and explain why neither event uses a veto or appointment consent.
:::answer
The answer is **The high official faces impeachment, and the government board faces judicial review.** A veto rejects a bill and appointment consent reviews nominees, so neither fits removing a high official or a court challenge.

**Check:** Match removal charges and trial to impeachment (Article XI, Section $3$). Match court challenges against government acts to judicial review (Article VIII, Section $1$).
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Execution vs. dispute settlement: the President carries out laws, while courts settle actual disputes over legal rights. {#execute-vs-decide}
- Bill rejected and repassed: match that pair to veto and veto override, not judicial review or appointment consent. {#veto-and-override-check}
:::
