## Core Concept

### Private Property

Article III, Section $9$ protects private property taken for public use. The government must give **just compensation**, the full and fair value of the property taken. A public purpose does not make the property free.

The rule concerns a taking for public use. It does not mean that every government rule affecting property requires payment.

### Contracts

Section $10$ bars laws that impair the obligation of contracts. **Impair** means weaken or reduce. The protection concerns duties agreed to in a valid contract, such as a duty to pay or provide a service.

A later law cannot simply erase those duties without constitutional limits. Still, contract rights are subject to valid government regulation for public welfare. A law affecting a contract is not automatically unconstitutional.

## Examples

### #1: Land for a Road

The government takes private land for a public road and refuses to pay for it. Which constitutional protection applies?

**Solution:**

1. The land is private property taken for public use.
2. Section $9$ requires just compensation.
3. The public purpose does not excuse the refusal to pay.

### #2: A Later Law and a Contract

A new law releases one party from a duty already accepted in a valid contract. Which constitutional protection should be examined?

**Solution:**

1. The issue is a later law weakening an existing contract duty.
2. Section $10$ protects the obligation of contracts.
3. Whether the law is invalid still requires checking its effect and any valid public-welfare basis.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Public Use Still Requires Payment

> A public road does not make private land free.

A taking for public use must meet the just-compensation requirement.
