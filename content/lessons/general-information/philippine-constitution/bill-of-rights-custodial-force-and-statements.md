## Core Concept

### Rights During Custodial Investigation

Torture, force, violence, threats, intimidation, and other means that destroy free choice are forbidden. Secret detention places, solitary detention, detention that cuts off communication, and similar forms are also prohibited.

Section $17$ protects against **compelled self-incrimination**. This means being forced to give testimony that could help prove one's own guilt. A confession admits guilt, while an admission acknowledges a fact that may help prove the case.

A confession or admission obtained in violation of Section $12$ or Section $17$ cannot be used as evidence against the person. Calling a statement voluntary does not settle the issue if the facts show threats or denial of the required rights.

## Examples

### #1: Threatened Confession

Investigators threaten a person until the person signs a confession. May that confession be used against the person under Section $12$?

**Solution:**

1. Threats that destroy free choice violate the custodial safeguards.
2. A confession obtained through that violation is inadmissible, meaning it cannot be used as evidence against the person.
