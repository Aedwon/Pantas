## Core Concept

### Section $20$: Freedom From Jail for Debt or Poll Tax

Article III, Section $20$ states that no person shall be imprisoned for **debt** or **nonpayment of a poll tax**. A **poll tax** is a fixed charge per person, not based on income or property.

Both unpaid debts, even written loan contracts, and unpaid poll taxes are protected from imprisonment.

Test Section $20$ when a town treasurer threatens to jail a resident who missed paying a fixed $50$-peso annual community tax:

1. Classify the unpaid charge: a fixed per-person levy is a poll tax.
2. Apply Section $20$: the treasurer cannot imprison the resident solely for nonpayment.

:::pair #debt-and-poll-tax-both-covered
Right: Section $20$ protects both private debts, even written ones, and unpaid poll taxes from jail.
Wrong: Limiting Section $20$ to poll taxes only, or allowing jail because a loan was in writing.
Both debt and unpaid poll tax sit inside Section $20$'s plain ban on imprisonment.
:::

:::check
A lender holds a signed promissory note for an unpaid store bill and asks a court to jail the buyer for nonpayment. Does Section $20$ bar jail?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Writing a loan into a contract never removes Section $20$'s ban on imprisonment for debt.
:::

### Section $20$: Debt Still Payable and Separate Crimes

Section $20$ stops jail solely for the unpaid debt itself. It never cancels a valid debt, so a lender may use lawful civil collection steps.

A separate crime involving money, such as criminal fraud, raises a different issue from simple nonpayment. Courts may punish proven fraud, yet never punish nonpayment of a pure debt alone.

Test a shopkeeper who cannot pay an honest supplier loan after a typhoon ruined her goods:

1. Check for fraud: the facts show simple inability to pay, with no separate crime.
2. Apply Section $20$: the debt stays owed for civil collection, yet jail is barred.

:::why
Why keep the debt valid while barring jail? Society stops debtors' prisons to protect personal liberty, while civil courts still let lenders recover valid debts from property.
:::

:::pair #debt-still-payable
Right: Section $20$ stops imprisonment for unpaid debt, while the valid debt remains payable through civil remedies.
Wrong: Treating the constitutional ban on jail as canceling a borrower's legal duty to repay.
Freedom from jail for debt never erases the underlying obligation to pay.
:::

:::pair #separate-crime-still-punishable
Right: Section $20$ bars jail solely for debt or poll-tax nonpayment, not for a separate proven crime.
Wrong: Treating Section $20$ as immunity for any financial crime or any case involving a written contract.
Check whether punishment rests on unpaid debt alone or on a distinct proven offense.
:::

:::check
A contractor takes advance cash by using forged land titles and is convicted of fraud. Does Section $20$ bar his prison sentence?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. He is punished for the separate crime of fraud, not merely for failing to pay a debt.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Debt and Jail

A citizen is threatened with jail solely for failing to pay a personal debt. Does Article III permit imprisonment for the debt itself?

**Solution:**

1. Apply Article III, Section $20$: imprisonment for debt is expressly forbidden.
2. Check the threat: only simple nonpayment is involved, with no separate crime.

Answer: No, the citizen cannot be jailed solely for that debt, though the debt remains payable.

**Check:** Section $20$ blocks jail for simple nonpayment while leaving civil collection remedies available.

### #2: An Unpaid Fixed Levy

A city charges every adult resident a fixed $100$-peso annual head tax regardless of income or land owned. An unemployed resident misses the deadline. An officer warns that missing payment brings five days in jail. Is the jail threat lawful?

**Solution:**

1. Classify the tax: a fixed per-person levy unrelated to income or property is a **poll tax**.
2. Apply Section $20$: imprisonment for nonpayment of a poll tax is prohibited.

Answer: No, Article III, Section $20$ forbids jailing the resident for an unpaid poll tax.

**Check:** Poll taxes are named alongside debt in Section $20$, so nonpayment alone cannot trigger jail.

### #3: Unpaid Shop Lease and Civil Remedies

A mechanic falls three months behind on shop rent after a slow season, with no deception or separate crime. The landlord threatens criminal arrest for the unpaid lease, while the mechanic insists Section $20$ stops the landlord from filing a civil collection suit. Rule on both positions.

**Solution:**

1. Check the landlord's threat: unpaid lease rent is a civil contract debt, so Section $20$ forbids imprisonment for nonpayment.
2. Check the mechanic's claim: Section $20$ bars jail without wiping out lawful civil collection suits.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both the arrest threat and the civil collection suit under Section $20$.
:::answer
The answer is **The landlord cannot have the mechanic jailed, yet the landlord may still pursue a lawful civil collection suit.** Article III, Section $20$ protects against imprisonment for honest civil debt while leaving valid payment duties enforceable in civil court.

**Check:** Section $20$ stops criminal jail for unpaid rent without canceling the debt or blocking civil collection.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Borrower owes a written loan or poll tax: block jail for nonpayment under Section $20$. {#debt-and-poll-tax-both-covered}
- Debtor claims Section $20$ erases the loan: keep the debt valid for civil collection. {#debt-still-payable}
- Case involves proven criminal fraud: separate the crime from mere debt under Section $20$. {#separate-crime-still-punishable}
:::
