## Core Concept

### Habeas Corpus

The **writ of habeas corpus** is a court order used to examine whether someone is being held lawfully. The person holding the detainee must account for the detention before the court.

Section $15$ allows suspension of the privilege of this writ only during invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it. **Invasion** involves an outside force entering the country with force. **Rebellion** involves an armed uprising against the government.

An emergency label is not enough. There must be invasion or rebellion, and public safety must require suspension. Suspension does not make every detention lawful.

## Examples

### #1: Suspension Without the Required Grounds

Officials seek to suspend the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus for convenience. There is neither invasion nor rebellion. Is the stated ground enough?

**Solution:**

1. Section $15$ requires invasion or rebellion.
2. Public safety must also require suspension.
3. Convenience meets neither condition, so it is not enough.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Public Safety Is Also Required

> Invasion or rebellion is only part of the suspension rule.

Public safety must also require suspension of the privilege of the writ. Ordinary convenience does not meet this rule.
