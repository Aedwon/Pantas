## Core Concept

### Three kinds of power

**Legislative power** means making laws. Article VI, Section $1$ gives it to Congress, except for powers reserved to people through initiative and referendum. These let people propose or vote on laws directly under set rules.

**Executive power** means carrying out laws. Article VII, Section $1$ gives it to the President. **Judicial power** belongs to the Supreme Court and lower courts under Article VIII, Section $1$.

A **check and balance** lets one branch limit another branch's use of power.

### Legislative power: Congress

Congress has two Houses. The Senate has $24$ Senators elected **at large**, meaning nationwide. They serve $6$ years, with at most $2$ consecutive terms (Article VI, Sections $2$ and $4$).

The House has district and party-list representatives. Section $5$ sets not more than $250$ Members, unless law fixes otherwise. Party-list representatives form $20\%$ of the total House, including party-list seats.

Representatives serve $3$ years, with at most $3$ consecutive terms (Section $7$). Consecutive means in a row, not a lifetime total.

A **bill** is a proposed law. To **originate** means to start. Section $24$ requires these bills to start in the House:

- Appropriation bills authorize spending. Revenue bills concern government income; tariff bills concern customs duties on goods.
- Bills increasing public debt add to government borrowing or debt.
- Bills of local application apply to a particular place.
- Private bills concern particular persons or private interests.

The Senate may propose or agree to amendments to these bills. Section $25(1)$ bars Congress from increasing the President's recommended appropriations for government operations as specified in the budget.

Either House or its committees may hold inquiries to help make laws. They must follow duly published rules and respect people's rights (Section $21$).

Congress alone may declare a state of war exists. Section $23(1)$ requires two-thirds of both Houses in joint session, voting separately.

A **veto** is the President's rejection of a bill. It must be communicated within $30$ days of receipt, or the bill becomes law as if signed (Section $27$).

To **override** means to pass the bill despite the veto. This requires two-thirds of all Members of each House. **All its Members** means full membership, not just Members present.

### Executive power: the President

The President serves $6$ years without reelection (Article VII, Section $4$). A successor who serves as President for more than $4$ years cannot later be elected President. The Vice-President may serve $2$ successive terms.

The Vice-President becomes President upon the President's death, permanent disability, removal, or resignation (Section $8$).

The President controls executive departments, bureaus, and offices and ensures laws are carried out (Section $17$). The President submits the budget within $30$ days from the opening of each regular congressional session (Section $22$).

Section $16$ requires Commission on Appointments consent for department heads, ambassadors, other public ministers and consuls, and military officers from colonel or naval captain upward. It also covers other officers whose appointments the Constitution vests in the President, subject to its specific appointment rules. Other appointments follow their own rules.

As Commander-in-Chief, the President heads the armed forces. **Martial law** places an area under emergency military authority, subject to constitutional limits. Section $18$ allows it only during invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it.

The President may also suspend the **privilege of the writ of habeas corpus** under those conditions. The writ normally lets a court require that a detained person be brought before it to check the detention.

Either measure has an initial limit of $60$ days. The President must report to Congress within $48$ hours. Congress may revoke it by at least a majority of all its Members, voting jointly.

At the President's initiative, Congress may extend it if invasion or rebellion persists and public safety requires it. Any citizen may ask the Supreme Court to review the factual basis. Martial law does not suspend the Constitution, replace functioning civil courts, or automatically suspend the writ's privilege.

A **pardon** grants forgiveness for a criminal offense after conviction by final judgment. It cannot cover impeachment (Section $19$). For violations of election laws, rules, or regulations, it requires a favorable COMELEC recommendation (Article IX-C, Section $5$).

**Amnesty** forgives offenses covered by a grant, often for a group. It needs concurrence, meaning agreement, of a majority of all Members of Congress. Election offenses also require a favorable COMELEC recommendation.

A **treaty** is an agreement between states. Article VII, Section $21$ requires concurrence by at least two-thirds of all Senators for a treaty to be valid and effective.

### Judicial power: the courts

Article VIII, Section $1$, second paragraph, states:

> Judicial power includes the duty of the courts of justice to settle actual controversies involving rights which are legally demandable and enforceable, and to determine whether or not there has been a grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction on the part of any branch or instrumentality of the Government.

Courts settle real disputes over legal rights. They also check serious misuse of government power that amounts to acting without legal authority or beyond it.

**Judicial review** means checking whether a law or government act follows the Constitution. Courts need a proper case, not just a request for an opinion.

The Supreme Court has a Chief Justice and $14$ Associate Justices. It may sit **en banc**, as the full Court, or in divisions of $3$, $5$, or $7$ Members (Section $4$).

The President appoints judges from at least $3$ nominees for each vacancy supplied by the Judicial and Bar Council. The council screens and recommends people for judicial office. Commission on Appointments confirmation is not required (Sections $8$ and $9$).

Judges serve during good behavior until age $70$ or incapacity (Section $11$).

### Who may hold each office

A **natural-born citizen** is Filipino from birth without an act needed to gain or complete citizenship. Article IV, Section $2$ also includes those who properly elect citizenship under Section $1(3)$. Articles IV and V: Citizenship and Suffrage explains that special case.

Representative: natural-born, at least $25$, able to read and write. District representatives must be registered voters in their district and residents there for at least $1$ year immediately before election day. The district voter and residence rules exclude party-list representatives (Article VI, Section $6$).

Senator: natural-born, at least $35$, able to read and write, and a registered voter. Philippine residence must cover at least $2$ years immediately before election day (Article VI, Section $3$).

President and Vice-President: natural-born, at least $40$, registered voters, and able to read and write. Philippine residence must cover at least $10$ years immediately before election day (Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$). These elected offices measure age on election day.

Supreme Court Member: natural-born, at least $40$, with at least $15$ years as a lower-court judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines. Lower-court judges must be citizens and Bar members, meaning admitted lawyers. Lower collegiate courts, where judges decide as a group, also require natural-born status.

Law sets further judicial qualifications. All judges need proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence. These mean ability, honesty, upright conduct, and freedom from improper influence (Article VIII, Section $7$).

The **Commission on Elections (COMELEC)** enforces election laws. It has a Chair and $6$ Commissioners. Members must be natural-born, at least $35$ at appointment, and college graduates.

A majority, including the Chair, must be Bar members with at least $10$ years of law practice (Article IX-C, Section $1$).

The **Commission on Audit (COA)** checks government accounts. It has a Chair and $2$ Commissioners. Members must be natural-born and at least $35$ at appointment.

They must be certified public accountants, or **CPAs**, with at least $10$ years of auditing experience, or lawyers with at least $10$ years of practice. CPAs are licensed accounting professionals; auditing means checking financial records. All Members cannot belong to the same profession (Article IX-D, Section $1$).

The **Ombudsman** investigates complaints against public officials. The Ombudsman must be natural-born, at least $40$ at appointment, a lawyer, and known for honesty and independence. At least $10$ years as a judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines is required (Article XI, Section $8$).

These three offices bar candidates in the election immediately before appointment. See The Civil Service Commission for CSC qualifications. Regular full terms of Constitutional Commission members and the Ombudsman are $7$ years without reappointment.

### Checks and balances

President → Congress: veto. Congress → President: veto override and consent to listed appointments through the Commission on Appointments.

Congress → impeachable officers: the House starts impeachment cases; the Senate tries them. **Impeachment** is charging and trying certain high officials for removal. See Accountability and Impeachment for Article XI, Section $3$.

Courts → government acts: judicial review.

## Examples

### #1: Match the Branch

Which branches pass a law, direct an executive department to enforce it, and decide a dispute under it?

**Solution:**

Congress uses legislative power. The President uses executive power. Courts use judicial power.

### #2: Congressional Terms

What term and consecutive-term limit apply to each House?

**Solution:**

Senators serve $6$ years, with at most $2$ consecutive terms. Representatives serve $3$ years, with at most $3$ consecutive terms.

### #3: Starting a Bill

Must a revenue bill start in the House? Must every education bill start there?

**Solution:**

A revenue bill must start there. An education bill need not, unless it falls in a Section $24$ category, such as an appropriation bill.

### #4: Overriding a Veto

A vetoed bill returns to Congress. Suppose the House has $300$ Members and the Senate has $24$. What votes must approve an override?

**Solution:**

1. Use two-thirds of all House Members.

$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times300=200$$

2. Use two-thirds of all Senators.

$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16$$

3. The bill needs at least $200$ House votes and $16$ Senate votes. If the President had not communicated a veto within $30$ days of receipt, it would become law as if signed.

### #5: Appointment Consent

Which needs Commission on Appointments consent: an ambassador or a Supreme Court Justice?

**Solution:**

The ambassador does. The Justice follows the Judicial and Bar Council process without confirmation.

### #6: Emergency Powers and Clemency

During rebellion, the President proclaims martial law for $90$ days without an extension. The President also pardons an impeachment conviction and grants an election-offense pardon without COMELEC's favorable recommendation. Are these valid?

Separately, the President grants amnesty for rebellion without the concurrence of a majority of all Members of Congress. Is the amnesty valid?

**Solution:**

1. The initial martial law period cannot exceed $60$ days. A report is due within $48$ hours; Congress may revoke and the Supreme Court may review the factual basis.
2. A pardon cannot cover impeachment.
3. The election-offense pardon lacks the required COMELEC recommendation.
4. The amnesty is invalid without the required congressional concurrence under Article VII, Section $19$. The President cannot grant it alone.

### #7: Treaty Concurrence

With $24$ Senators, how many must concur in a treaty?

**Solution:**

1. Apply the requirement of at least two-thirds of all Senators.
2. Multiply the full membership by that fraction.

$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16$$

At least $16$ Senators must concur.

### #8: Court Composition and Review

How many Members form the Supreme Court, which division sizes are allowed, and which branch may declare a law unconstitutional in a proper case?

**Solution:**

1. The Court has $15$ Members, with divisions of $3$, $5$, or $7$.
2. The judicial branch uses judicial review. A conflict with the Constitution is the ground. Supreme Court cases on a law's constitutionality are heard en banc.

### #9: Minimum Ages

On election day, Ana is $25$, Ben is $35$, and Cara is $40$. All meet other requirements. Who meets the age minimum for Representative, Senator, and President?

**Solution:**

Ana meets the Representative minimum. Ben meets the Representative and Senator minimums. Cara meets all three.

### #10: Judicial Qualifications

A natural-born lawyer is $40$ with $14$ years of Philippine law practice and no judicial service. Does she meet the Supreme Court experience rule? Are citizenship and Bar membership enough for every lower court?

**Solution:**

1. She lacks the required $15$ years of experience.
2. Citizenship and Bar membership are minimums. Law adds qualifications, and lower collegiate courts also require natural-born status.

### #11: Other Constitutional Offices

A natural-born college graduate is $35$, has practiced law for $10$ years, and was a candidate in the immediately preceding election. Can he be appointed to COMELEC or COA? Does he meet the Ombudsman's age rule?

**Solution:**

1. Prior candidacy bars both commission appointments despite his age and credentials.
2. He is below the Ombudsman's minimum age of $40$.

### #12: Two Checks on a Law

Congress overrides a veto. Later, a court declares the law unconstitutional in a proper case. Which powers were used?

**Solution:**

Congress used its veto-override power. The court used judicial review to check the law against the Constitution.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Remember $25$, $35$, $40$: Representative, Senator, President. Vice-President and Supreme Court Member also start at $40$. Age alone never proves full qualification.

Name the voting body before calculating. Veto override needs two-thirds of each House's full membership; treaty concurrence needs two-thirds of all Senators. Do not combine House and Senate totals for either rule.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Present Is Not All

> Count the membership named in the rule.

Two-thirds of those present may fall short of two-thirds of all Members. Read the rule before calculating.

### #2: Different Ages

> Representative, Senator, and President start at $25$, $35$, and $40$.

The Senator's minimum does not qualify someone for President. Citizenship and residence still need checking.

### #3: Different Terms

> The President and Vice-President have different reelection rules.

The President cannot be reelected. The Vice-President may serve $2$ successive terms. The regular $7$-year terms without reappointment belong to Constitutional Commissions and the Ombudsman.

### #4: Limits on Pardon

> Pardon cannot erase impeachment or bypass COMELEC for election offenses.

Pardon follows conviction by final judgment. Amnesty needs congressional concurrence, with COMELEC's favorable recommendation also needed for election offenses.

### #5: Different Appointment Rules

> Presidential appointment does not always require confirmation.

An ambassador needs Commission on Appointments consent. A judge does not.

### #6: Courts and Congress

> Judicial review does not replace Congress's lawmaking role.

Courts interpret and apply laws and check unconstitutional acts in proper cases. Disagreement with a policy alone does not make it unconstitutional.
