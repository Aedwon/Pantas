## Core Concept

### Three kinds of power

**Legislative power** means making laws. Article VI, Section $1$ gives it to Congress, except for powers reserved to people through initiative and referendum. These let people propose or vote on laws directly under set rules.

**Executive power** means carrying out laws. Article VII, Section $1$ gives it to the President. **Judicial power** belongs to the Supreme Court and lower courts under Article VIII, Section $1$.

A **check and balance** lets one branch limit another branch's use of power.

### Legislative power: Congress

Congress has two Houses. The Senate has $24$ Senators elected **at large**, meaning nationwide. They serve $6$ years, with at most $2$ consecutive terms (Article VI, Sections $2$ and $4$).

The House has district and party-list representatives. Section $5(1)$ sets not more than $250$ Members, unless law fixes otherwise. Section $5(2)$ sets the party-list share at $20\%$ of all representatives, including party-list representatives.

Representatives serve $3$ years, with at most $3$ consecutive terms (Section $7$). Consecutive means in a row, not a lifetime total. For both Houses, Article VI, Sections $4$ and $7$ state: "Voluntary renunciation of the office for any length of time shall not be considered as an interruption in the continuity of his service for the full term for which he was elected." This means choosing to give up the office does not break the term count.

A **bill** is a proposed law. To **originate** means to start. Section $24$ requires these bills to start in the House:

- Appropriation bills authorize spending. Revenue bills concern government income; tariff bills concern customs duties on goods.
- Bills increasing public debt authorize added government debt.
- Bills of local application apply to a particular place.
- Private bills concern particular persons or private interests.

The Senate may propose or agree to changes to these bills. An **appropriation** is authority to spend public money. Section $25(1)$ bars Congress from increasing the President's recommended appropriations for government operations as specified in the budget.

Either House or its committees may hold inquiries to help make laws. They must follow duly published rules and respect the rights of people appearing in or affected by the inquiry (Section $21$).

Congress alone may declare that a state of war exists. Section $23(1)$ requires two-thirds of both Houses in joint session, voting separately. The Houses meet together but vote as separate bodies.

A **veto** is the President's rejection of a bill. The President must communicate it within $30$ days of receiving the bill. Otherwise, the bill becomes law as if signed (Section $27$).

To **override** means to pass the bill despite the veto. This requires two-thirds of all Members of each House. **All its Members** means the full membership, not just those present.

### Executive power: the President

The President serves $6$ years without reelection (Article VII, Section $4$). A successor who serves as President for more than $4$ years cannot later be elected President. The Vice-President serves $6$ years and may serve at most $2$ successive terms.

The Vice-President becomes President upon the President's death, permanent disability, removal, or resignation (Section $8$).

The President controls executive departments, bureaus, and offices and ensures laws are carried out (Section $17$). The President submits the budget within $30$ days from the opening of each regular congressional session (Section $22$).

The **Commission on Appointments** is a body of Senators and Representatives that approves certain appointments. Article VII, Section $16$ requires its consent for department heads, ambassadors, other public ministers and consuls, and military officers from colonel or naval captain upward.

Other offices have their own appointment rules in the Constitution. For example, Constitutional Commission members need this consent. Judges do not, and neither does the Vice-President when appointed to the Cabinet (Article VII, Section $3$).

As **Commander-in-Chief**, the President heads the armed forces. **Martial law** places an area under emergency military authority within constitutional limits. Section $18$ allows it only during invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it.

The President may also suspend the **privilege of the writ of habeas corpus** under those conditions. The writ normally lets a court order a detained person brought before it to check the detention.

Suspension covers only people charged in court with rebellion or offenses that are part of or directly connected with invasion. A person arrested or detained under the suspension must be charged in court within $3$ days. Otherwise, that person must be released.

Either measure has an initial limit of $60$ days. The President must report to Congress within $48$ hours. Congress may revoke it by at least a majority of all its Members, voting jointly.

At the President's request, Congress may extend it if invasion or rebellion continues and public safety requires it. Any citizen may ask the Supreme Court to review its factual basis. Martial law does not suspend the Constitution, replace functioning civil courts, or automatically suspend the writ's privilege.

**Impeachment** is a process for charging and trying certain high officials for removal. The House brings the charges, and the Senate holds the trial (Article XI, Section $3$). See Accountability and Impeachment for that process.

A **pardon** forgives a criminal offense after conviction by final judgment, when the conviction is final. It cannot cover impeachment (Article VII, Section $19$). An election-offense pardon also needs a favorable COMELEC recommendation (Article IX-C, Section $5$).

**Amnesty** forgives offenses covered by a grant, often for a group. It needs concurrence, meaning agreement, of a majority of all Members of Congress. Amnesty for election offenses also needs a favorable COMELEC recommendation.

A **treaty** is an agreement between states. Article VII, Section $21$ requires concurrence by at least two-thirds of all Senators for a treaty to be valid and effective.

### Judicial power: the courts

Article VIII, Section $1$, second paragraph, states:

> Judicial power includes the duty of the courts of justice to settle actual controversies involving rights which are legally demandable and enforceable, and to determine whether or not there has been a grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction on the part of any branch or instrumentality of the Government.

Courts settle real disputes over legal rights. They also check serious misuse of government power that amounts to acting without legal authority or beyond it.

**Judicial review** means checking whether a law or government act follows the Constitution. Courts need a proper case, not just a request for an opinion.

The Supreme Court has a Chief Justice and $14$ Associate Justices. It may sit **en banc**, as the full Court, or in divisions of $3$, $5$, or $7$ Members (Section $4$). Supreme Court cases on a law's constitutionality are heard en banc.

The **Judicial and Bar Council** screens and recommends people for judicial office. The President appoints judges from its list of at least $3$ nominees for each vacancy. These appointments need no confirmation (Sections $8$ and $9$).

Judges serve during good behavior until age $70$ or until they cannot perform their duties because of incapacity (Section $11$).

### Who may hold each office

A **natural-born citizen** is Filipino from birth without an act needed to gain or complete citizenship. Article IV, Section $2$ also includes those who elect citizenship under Section $1(3)$. See Articles IV and V: Citizenship and Suffrage for that special case.

A Representative must be natural-born, at least $25$, and able to read and write. A district representative must be a registered voter in that district and have lived there for at least $1$ year immediately before election day. Those district voter and residence rules exclude party-list representatives (Article VI, Section $6$).

A Senator must be natural-born, at least $35$, able to read and write, and a registered voter. The Senator must have lived in the Philippines for at least $2$ years immediately before election day (Article VI, Section $3$).

The President and Vice-President must be natural-born, at least $40$, registered voters, and able to read and write. They must have lived in the Philippines for at least $10$ years immediately before election day (Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$). These elected offices measure age on election day.

A Supreme Court Member must be natural-born and at least $40$. At least $15$ years as a lower-court judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines is required (Article VIII, Section $7$).

Lower-court judges must be citizens and Philippine Bar members, meaning admitted lawyers. Judges of lower collegiate courts, where judges decide as a group, must also be natural-born. Law sets further qualifications.

All judges need proven competence, integrity, probity, and independence. These mean ability, honesty, upright conduct, and freedom from improper influence.

The **Commission on Elections (COMELEC)** enforces election laws. It has a Chair and $6$ Commissioners. Members must be natural-born, at least $35$ at appointment, and college graduates.

A majority, including the Chair, must be lawyers with at least $10$ years of law practice (Article IX-C, Section $1$). This does not require every Commissioner to be a lawyer.

The **Commission on Audit (COA)** checks government accounts. It has a Chair and $2$ Commissioners. Members must be natural-born and at least $35$ at appointment. They must also be "of proven probity and independence" (Article IX-D, Section $1(1)$). This means they must have shown honest conduct and freedom from improper influence.

A member must be a certified public accountant, or **CPA**, with at least $10$ years of auditing experience, or a lawyer with at least $10$ years of practice. CPAs are licensed accounting professionals; auditing means checking financial records. All Members cannot belong to the same profession (Article IX-D, Section $1$).

The **Ombudsman** investigates complaints against public officials. The Ombudsman must be natural-born, at least $40$ at appointment, a lawyer, and known for upright conduct and independence. At least $10$ years as a judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines is required (Article XI, Section $8$).

These three offices bar candidates in the election immediately before appointment. See The Civil Service Commission for CSC qualifications. Regular full terms of Constitutional Commission members and the Ombudsman are $7$ years without reappointment (Article IX-B, IX-C, and IX-D, Section $1(2)$; Article XI, Section $11$).

### Checks and balances

President → Congress: veto. Congress → President: veto override.

Commission on Appointments → presidential appointments: consent for listed offices. House → impeachable officers: charges; Senate → impeachable officers: trial.

Courts → government acts: judicial review.

## Examples

### #1: Match the Branch

Which branches pass a law, direct an executive department to enforce it, and decide a dispute under it?

**Solution:**

1. Congress passes the law using legislative power.
2. The President directs the department using executive power.
3. Courts decide the dispute using judicial power.

### #2: Congressional Terms

What term and consecutive-term limit apply to each House?

**Solution:**

Senators serve $6$ years, with at most $2$ consecutive terms. Representatives serve $3$ years, with at most $3$ consecutive terms.

### #3: Starting a Bill

Must a revenue bill start in the House? Must every education bill start there?

**Solution:**

1. A revenue bill must start in the House under Article VI, Section $24$.
2. An education bill need not start there unless it falls in a listed category, such as an appropriation bill.

### #4: Inquiry Safeguards

A Senate committee investigates a problem to help draft a law. It uses unpublished inquiry rules and ignores witnesses' rights. Does its lawmaking purpose satisfy Article VI, Section $21$?

**Solution:**

1. The purpose supports an inquiry in aid of legislation.
2. That purpose is not enough. The committee must use duly published rules and respect the rights of people appearing in or affected by it.

### #5: Budget Limit

Congress proposes to increase the President's recommended appropriations for government operations as specified in the budget. Does Article VI, Section $25(1)$ allow this increase?

**Solution:**

No. That section bars Congress from increasing those recommended appropriations.

### #6: Declaring a State of War

Both Houses meet in joint session. Supporters propose to pool their votes to reach two-thirds for declaring a state of war. Is that the required procedure?

**Solution:**

1. Article VI, Section $23(1)$ requires two-thirds of both Houses in joint session.
2. They must vote separately. A pooled total does not meet that voting rule.

### #7: Overriding a Veto

A vetoed bill returns to Congress. Suppose the House has $300$ Members and the Senate has $24$. What votes must approve an override?

**Solution:**

1. Take two-thirds of all House Members.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times300=200
$$

2. Take two-thirds of all Senators.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16
$$

3. At least $200$ House votes and $16$ Senate votes are needed. Without a communicated veto within $30$ days of receipt, the bill would become law as if signed.

### #8: Appointment Consent

Which needs Commission on Appointments consent: an ambassador or a Supreme Court Justice?

**Solution:**

1. The ambassador needs consent under Article VII, Section $16$.
2. The Justice follows the Judicial and Bar Council process without confirmation under Article VIII, Section $9$.

### #9: Emergency Powers and Clemency

During rebellion, the President declares martial law for $90$ days without an extension. A person detained under a suspension of the writ's privilege remains uncharged after $3$ days. What limits apply?

Separately, may the President pardon an impeachment conviction, pardon an election offense without a favorable COMELEC recommendation, or grant amnesty without congressional concurrence?

**Solution:**

1. The initial martial law period cannot exceed $60$ days. A report is due within $48$ hours. Congress may revoke it, and the Supreme Court may review its factual basis.
2. The detained person must be released if not charged in court within $3$ days.
3. Pardon cannot cover impeachment. The election-offense pardon needs COMELEC's favorable recommendation.
4. Amnesty needs concurrence by a majority of all Members of Congress. The President cannot grant it alone.

### #10: Treaty Concurrence

With $24$ Senators, how many must concur in a treaty?

**Solution:**

1. Use at least two-thirds of all Senators.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16
$$

2. At least $16$ Senators must concur.

### #11: Court Composition and Review

How many Members form the Supreme Court, which division sizes are allowed, and which branch may declare a law unconstitutional in a proper case?

**Solution:**

1. The Court has $15$ Members. Divisions may have $3$, $5$, or $7$ Members.
2. The judicial branch uses judicial review. A conflict with the Constitution is the ground. The Supreme Court hears cases on a law's constitutionality en banc.

### #12: Minimum Ages

On election day, Ana is $25$, Ben is $35$, and Cara is $40$. All meet the other requirements. Who meets the age minimum for Representative, Senator, and President?

**Solution:**

1. Ana meets the Representative minimum of $25$.
2. Ben meets the Representative and Senator minimums.
3. Cara meets all three age minimums.

### #13: Judicial Qualifications

A natural-born lawyer is $40$ with $14$ years of Philippine law practice and no judicial service. Does she meet the Supreme Court experience rule? Are citizenship and Bar membership enough for every lower court?

**Solution:**

1. She lacks the required $15$ years of experience.
2. Citizenship and Bar membership are minimums. Law adds qualifications, and lower collegiate courts also require natural-born status.

### #14: Other Constitutional Offices

A natural-born college graduate is $35$, has practiced law for $10$ years, and was a candidate in the immediately preceding election. Can he be appointed to COMELEC or COA? Does he meet the Ombudsman's age rule?

**Solution:**

1. Prior candidacy bars both commission appointments despite his age and credentials.
2. He is below the Ombudsman's minimum age of $40$.

### #15: Two Checks on a Law

Congress overrides a veto. Later, a court declares the law unconstitutional in a proper case. Which powers were used?

**Solution:**

1. Congress used its veto-override power to pass the bill despite the President's rejection.
2. The court used judicial review to check the law against the Constitution.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Remember $25$, $35$, $40$: Representative, Senator, President. Vice-President and Supreme Court Member also start at $40$. Age alone never proves full qualification.

Name the voting body before calculating. Veto override needs two-thirds of each House's full membership; treaty concurrence needs two-thirds of all Senators. War requires the Houses to vote separately, even though they meet together.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Present Is Not All

> Count the membership named in the rule.

Two-thirds of those present may fall short of two-thirds of all Members. Read the rule before calculating.

### #2: Different Ages

> Representative, Senator, and President start at $25$, $35$, and $40$.

The Senator's minimum does not qualify someone for President. Citizenship and residence still need checking.

### #3: Different Terms

> The President and Vice-President have different reelection rules.

The President cannot be reelected. The Vice-President may serve $2$ successive terms. Regular $7$-year terms without reappointment belong to Constitutional Commission members and the Ombudsman.

### #4: Limits on Pardon

> Pardon cannot erase impeachment or bypass COMELEC for election offenses.

Pardon follows conviction by final judgment. Amnesty needs congressional concurrence. Election-offense amnesty also needs COMELEC's favorable recommendation.

### #5: Different Appointment Rules

> Presidential appointment does not always require confirmation.

An ambassador needs Commission on Appointments consent. A judge does not. Check the rule for the specific office.

### #6: Courts and Congress

> Congress makes laws; courts interpret and apply them in cases.

Judicial review checks laws against the Constitution. Disagreement with a policy alone does not make it unconstitutional.
