## Core Concept

### Three kinds of power

**Legislative power** is the power to make laws. Article VI, Section $1$ gives it to Congress, except for powers reserved to the people through initiative and referendum. These let people propose laws or vote on laws directly under set rules.

**Executive power** is the power to carry out laws. Article VII, Section $1$ gives it to the President. **Judicial power** belongs to the Supreme Court and lower courts under Article VIII, Section $1$.

A **check and balance** lets one branch limit another branch's use of power.

### Legislative power: Congress

Congress has two Houses. The Senate has $24$ senators elected **at large**, meaning by voters nationwide. Senators serve $6$ years, with at most $2$ consecutive terms (Article VI, Sections $2$ and $4$).

The House has district and party-list representatives. Section $5$ sets a size of not more than $250$, unless law fixes otherwise. Party-list seats are $20\%$ of the total House, including party-list representatives.

Representatives serve $3$ years, with at most $3$ consecutive terms (Section $7$). These limits concern terms in a row, not lifetime totals.

A **bill** is a proposed law. Under Section $24$, appropriation bills, which authorize spending, and revenue or tariff bills, which concern taxes or customs duties, must start in the House. So must bills increasing public debt, bills of local application, and private bills.

**Originate** means start. The Senate may propose or agree to amendments to these House bills. Section $25(1)$ bars Congress from increasing the President's recommended appropriations for government operations as specified in the budget.

Either House or its committees may hold inquiries to help make laws. They must follow duly published rules and respect people's rights (Section $21$).

Congress alone may declare the existence of a state of war. Section $23(1)$ requires a two-thirds vote of both Houses in joint session, voting separately.

A **veto** is the President's rejection of a bill. The President must communicate it within $30$ days after receiving the bill, or the bill becomes law as if signed (Section $27$).

To **override** is to pass the bill despite the veto. This needs two-thirds of all Members of each House. **All its Members** means the full membership, not just those present.

### Executive power: the President

The President serves $6$ years and cannot be reelected (Article VII, Section $4$). Someone who succeeds as President and serves more than $4$ years cannot later be elected President. The Vice-President may serve $2$ successive terms.

The Vice-President becomes President upon the President's death, permanent disability, removal, or resignation (Section $8$).

The President controls executive departments, bureaus, and offices and ensures that laws are carried out (Section $17$). Under Section $22$, the President submits the budget within $30$ days from the opening of each regular session of Congress.

Section $16$ requires Commission on Appointments consent for department heads, ambassadors, other public ministers and consuls, and military officers from colonel or naval captain upward. It also covers other officers whose appointments the Constitution vests in the President. Other appointments follow their own rules.

As Commander-in-Chief, the President heads the armed forces. **Martial law** places an area under military rule subject to constitutional limits. Section $18$ allows it only for invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it.

The President may also suspend the **privilege of the writ of habeas corpus** under those conditions. The writ normally lets a court require a detained person to be brought before it so the detention can be checked.

Either measure has an initial limit of $60$ days. The President must report to Congress within $48$ hours. Congress may revoke it by a majority of all its Members, voting jointly.

At the President's initiative, Congress may extend it if the invasion or rebellion persists and public safety requires it. Any citizen may ask the Supreme Court to review its factual basis. Martial law does not suspend the Constitution or automatically suspend the writ's privilege.

A **pardon** forgives a criminal offense after conviction by final judgment. It cannot be used in impeachment cases, and other constitutional limits apply. **Amnesty** forgives offenses covered by a grant, often for a group, and needs a majority of all Members of Congress to agree (Section $19$).

A **treaty** is an agreement between states. Section $21$ requires concurrence, meaning agreement, by at least two-thirds of all Senators for a treaty to be valid and effective.

### Judicial power: the courts

Article VIII, Section $1$, second paragraph, states:

> Judicial power includes the duty of the courts of justice to settle actual controversies involving rights which are legally demandable and enforceable, and to determine whether or not there has been a grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction on the part of any branch or instrumentality of the Government.

Courts settle real disputes over legal rights. They also check serious misuse of government power that amounts to acting without legal authority or beyond it.

**Judicial review** is the courts' power to check whether a law or government act follows the Constitution. A court needs a proper case, not just a request for its opinion.

The Supreme Court has a Chief Justice and $14$ Associate Justices. It may sit **en banc**, as the full Court, or in divisions of $3$, $5$, or $7$ Members (Section $4$).

The President appoints judges from at least $3$ nominees for each vacancy supplied by the Judicial and Bar Council. No Commission on Appointments confirmation is needed (Sections $8$ and $9$). Judges serve during good behavior until age $70$ or incapacity (Section $11$).

### Who may hold each office

A **natural-born citizen** is generally a citizen from birth without an act needed to gain citizenship. Articles IV and V: Citizenship and Suffrage explains the special election-of-citizenship rule.

Representative: at least $25$, natural-born, and able to read and write. District representatives must be registered voters in their district and residents there for at least $1$ year immediately before election day. The district voter and residence rules except party-list representatives (Article VI, Section $6$).

Senator: at least $35$, natural-born, able to read and write, and a registered voter. Philippine residence must cover at least $2$ years immediately before election day (Article VI, Section $3$).

President and Vice-President: at least $40$, natural-born, registered voters, and able to read and write. Philippine residence must cover at least $10$ years immediately before election day (Article VII, Sections $2$ and $3$). These elected offices measure age on election day.

Supreme Court Member: natural-born, at least $40$, with at least $15$ years as a lower-court judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines. Lower-court judges must be Philippine citizens and Bar members, meaning admitted lawyers; lower collegiate courts also require natural-born status. Law sets further qualifications, and all judges need proven skill, honesty, and independence (Article VIII, Section $7$).

COMELEC has a Chair and $6$ Commissioners. Members must be natural-born, at least $35$ at appointment, and college graduates. A majority, including the Chair, must have practiced law for at least $10$ years (Article IX-C, Section $1$).

COA has a Chair and $2$ Commissioners. Members must be natural-born and at least $35$ at appointment. They must be CPAs with at least $10$ years of auditing experience or lawyers with at least $10$ years of practice. All Members cannot belong to the same profession (Article IX-D, Section $1$).

The Ombudsman must be natural-born, at least $40$ at appointment, a lawyer, and known for honesty and independence. At least $10$ years as a judge or practicing lawyer in the Philippines is required (Article XI, Section $8$).

These three offices bar candidates in the election immediately before appointment. See The Civil Service Commission for the CSC qualifications. Regular full terms for Constitutional Commission members and the Ombudsman are $7$ years without reappointment.

### Checks and balances

President → Congress: veto. Congress → President: veto override and consent to listed appointments through the Commission on Appointments.

Congress → impeachable officers: the House starts impeachment cases; the Senate tries them. See Accountability and Impeachment for the process under Article XI, Section $3$.

Courts → government acts: judicial review.

## Examples

### #1: Match the Branch

Which branches pass a law, direct an executive department to enforce it, and decide a dispute under it?

**Solution:**

Congress uses legislative power; the President uses executive power; courts use judicial power.

### #2: Terms in Congress

What are the term and consecutive-term limit for each House?

**Solution:**

Senators: $6$ years and $2$ consecutive terms. Representatives: $3$ years and $3$ consecutive terms.

### #3: Where a Bill Starts

Must a revenue bill start in the House? Must every education bill do so?

**Solution:**

A revenue bill must start there. An education bill need not, unless it falls within a Section $24$ category, such as an appropriation bill.

### #4: Override Votes

A vetoed bill returns to Congress. Assume the House has $300$ Members and the Senate has $24$. What votes are needed to override it?

**Solution:**

1. Use all Members of each House.

$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times300=200$$

$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16$$

2. At least $200$ House votes and $16$ Senate votes are needed. Without a veto communicated within $30$ days of receipt, the bill becomes law as if signed.

### #5: Appointment Consent

Which needs Commission on Appointments consent: an ambassador or a Supreme Court Justice?

**Solution:**

The ambassador needs consent. The Justice follows the Judicial and Bar Council process without confirmation.

### #6: Emergency Powers and Forgiveness

During rebellion, the President proclaims martial law for $90$ days without an extension and pardons an impeachment conviction. Are these valid?

**Solution:**

1. The initial limit is $60$ days, with a report within $48$ hours. Congress may revoke; the Supreme Court may review the factual basis.
2. A pardon cannot cover impeachment. Amnesty is separate and needs a majority of all Members of Congress to concur.

### #7: Treaty Votes

With $24$ Senators, how many must concur in a treaty?

**Solution:**

$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16$$

At least $16$ Senators must concur.

### #8: Court Composition and Review

How many Supreme Court Members are there, what division sizes are allowed, and which branch may declare a law unconstitutional in a proper case?

**Solution:**

1. The Court has $15$ Members. Divisions may have $3$, $5$, or $7$ Members.
2. The judicial branch uses judicial review. A conflict with the Constitution is the ground, not dislike of the law. Supreme Court cases on a law's constitutionality are heard en banc.

### #9: Age Requirements

On election day, Ana is $25$, Ben is $35$, and Cara is $40$. All meet other requirements. Who meets the minimum age for Representative, Senator, and President?

**Solution:**

Ana meets the Representative age. Ben meets the Representative and Senator ages. Cara meets all three.

### #10: Judicial Qualifications

A natural-born lawyer is $40$ with $14$ years of Philippine law practice and no service as a judge. Does she meet the Supreme Court experience rule? Are citizenship and Bar membership alone enough for every lower court?

**Solution:**

1. She lacks the required $15$ years of experience.
2. Citizenship and Bar membership are minimums. Law adds qualifications, and lower collegiate courts require natural-born citizenship.

### #11: Commission and Ombudsman Candidates

A natural-born $35$-year-old college graduate with $10$ years of law practice was a candidate in the immediately preceding election. Can he be appointed to COMELEC or COA? Does he meet the Ombudsman's age rule?

**Solution:**

1. The prior candidacy bars both commission appointments, despite his age and credentials.
2. He also falls below the Ombudsman's minimum age of $40$.

### #12: Two Checks on a Law

Congress overrides a veto. Later, in a proper case, a court declares the law unconstitutional. Which powers were used?

**Solution:**

Congress used legislative power to override. The court used judicial review to check the law against the Constitution.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Use the age ladder $25$, $35$, $40$: Representative, Senator, President. Vice-President and Supreme Court Member also start at $40$. Age alone never proves full qualification.

For voting questions, name the body before calculating. Veto override needs two-thirds of each House's full membership; treaty concurrence needs two-thirds of all Senators. Do not combine the House and Senate totals for either rule.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Present Is Not All

> Count the membership named in the rule.

Two-thirds of those present may fall short of two-thirds of all Members. Read the voting rule before doing the math.

### #2: Ages Are Not Interchangeable

> Representative, Senator, and President start at $25$, $35$, and $40$.

Using the Senator's age for President gives the wrong result. Residence and citizenship still need checking.

### #3: Different Reelection Rules

> President and Vice-President have different limits.

The President cannot be reelected. The Vice-President may serve $2$ successive terms. Neither has the regular $7$-year term without reappointment used by Constitutional Commissions and the Ombudsman.

### #4: Pardon Is Not Amnesty

> A pardon cannot erase impeachment.

Pardon follows conviction by final judgment and has constitutional limits. Amnesty needs congressional concurrence.

### #5: Appointment Rules Differ

> Presidential appointment does not always mean confirmation.

An ambassador needs Commission on Appointments consent. A judge does not.

### #6: Courts Check Laws

> Judicial review does not replace Congress's lawmaking job.

Courts interpret and apply law and may reject unconstitutional acts in proper cases. They cannot void a law simply because they prefer another policy.
