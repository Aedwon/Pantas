## Core Concept

### Fines and Punishment

The replacement is **reclusion perpetua** when the violated law uses Revised Penal Code penalty names. It is **life imprisonment** when the violated law does not use those names. These are severe prison penalties with distinct legal names.

The Act's Section $3$ bars parole for persons convicted of offenses punished with reclusion perpetua, or whose sentences are reduced to it, by reason of the Act. **Parole** is conditional release after serving part of a sentence. Do not treat a replacement sentence as a promise of parole.

## Examples

### #1: Selecting the Replacement Penalty

A violated law formerly imposed death and uses the penalty names of the Revised Penal Code. Which replacement does Republic Act No. $9346$ require?

**Solution:**

1. Check the penalty names used by the violated law.
2. It uses Revised Penal Code penalty names.
3. The replacement is reclusion perpetua.

### #2: A Law With Different Penalty Names

A special law formerly imposed death but does not use Revised Penal Code penalty names. Which replacement does Republic Act No. $9346$ require?

**Solution:**

1. The violated law does not use Revised Penal Code penalty names.
2. The Act provides life imprisonment for this case.
3. Do not replace that legal name with reclusion perpetua.

### #3: Parole After a Reduced Sentence

A person's death sentence is reduced to reclusion perpetua by reason of Republic Act No. $9346$. Does the Act make that person eligible for parole?

**Solution:**

1. The sentence was reduced to reclusion perpetua by reason of the Act.
2. Section $3$ bars parole for persons in this category.
3. The person is not eligible for parole under that provision.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Replacement Penalty Names Differ

> Check the penalty terms used by the violated law.

Republic Act No. $9346$ uses reclusion perpetua for laws using Revised Penal Code penalty names. It uses life imprisonment otherwise.
