## Core Concept

### Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $2$: Selecting the Replacement Penalty

Under Article III, Section $19(1)$ and **Republic Act No. $9346$**, Section $1$, courts cannot impose the death penalty. Section $2$ sets the replacement penalty based on the penalty names used in the violated law:

1. Use **reclusion perpetua** when the violated law uses **Revised Penal Code** penalty names.
2. Use **life imprisonment** when the violated law does not use Revised Penal Code penalty names.

These two severe prison penalties carry distinct legal names and effects, so never swap them or apply one to both types of law.

Test Section $2$ on an anti-trafficking law that sets fixed prison years instead of Revised Penal Code names:

1. Check the statute's penalty names: fixed years are not Revised Penal Code penalty names.
2. Apply Section $2$: the replacement penalty is **life imprisonment**, not *reclusion perpetua*.

:::pair #nomenclature-picks-replacement
Right: Laws using Revised Penal Code terms get *reclusion perpetua*; other laws get life imprisonment.
Wrong: Swapping the two replacements, or applying *reclusion perpetua* to every law that had death.
Always read the violated statute's penalty naming style before picking the replacement.
:::

:::check
A Revised Penal Code crime uses the code's named penalties and once carried death. Which replacement penalty applies under Section $2$?
:::answer
The answer is **Reclusion perpetua**. Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $2$ assigns *reclusion perpetua* whenever the violated law uses Revised Penal Code penalty names.
:::

### Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $3$: The Parole Restriction

**Parole** is conditional release after a prisoner serves a minimum sentence under **Act No. $4103$** (the Indeterminate Sentence Law).

Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $3$ bars parole for offenses punished with *reclusion perpetua*. It also bars parole for death sentences reduced to *reclusion perpetua* under the Act. Never treat a reduced sentence as a promise of parole, and the prisoner cannot pick another penalty.

Test Section $3$ on a prisoner whose death sentence was reduced to *reclusion perpetua* by Republic Act No. $9346$:

1. Check how the sentence arose: it was reduced to *reclusion perpetua* by reason of the Act.
2. Apply Section $3$: parole is barred, even after years of good conduct in prison.

:::why
Why bar parole for reduced death sentences? Congress replaced execution with severe prison terms without parole under Section $3$ for crimes that formerly carried death.
:::

:::pair #no-parole-after-reduction
Right: Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $3$ bars parole when a death sentence is reduced to *reclusion perpetua*.
Wrong: Treating the reduction as making parole automatic, or limiting the parole bar to unreduced death sentences.
Section $3$ expressly covers sentences reduced to *reclusion perpetua* under the Act.
:::

:::pair #no-prisoner-penalty-choice
Right: The violated law's penalty names fix the replacement sentence, not the prisoner's choice.
Wrong: Letting a prisoner elect life imprisonment instead of *reclusion perpetua* to remove the parole bar.
Courts apply the statutory replacement dictated by Section $2$, leaving no prisoner option.
:::

:::check
A prisoner convicted of an offense originally punishable by *reclusion perpetua* applies for parole under Act No. $4103$. Does Section $3$ allow parole?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $3$ bars parole for offenses punished with *reclusion perpetua* as well as reduced death sentences.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Selecting the Replacement Penalty

A violated law formerly imposed death and uses the penalty names of the Revised Penal Code. Which replacement does Republic Act No. $9346$ require?

**Solution:**

1. Check the penalty names used by the violated law.
2. Note that it uses Revised Penal Code penalty names.

Answer: *Reclusion perpetua* under Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $2$.

**Check:** Section $2$ pairs Revised Penal Code nomenclature directly with *reclusion perpetua*, rather than life imprisonment.

### #2: A Law With Different Penalty Names

A special law formerly imposed death but does not use Revised Penal Code penalty names. Which replacement does Republic Act No. $9346$ require?

**Solution:**

1. Check the violated law's naming style: it does not use Revised Penal Code penalty names.
2. Apply Section $2$: non-code statutes receive life imprisonment instead of *reclusion perpetua*.

Answer: Life imprisonment under Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $2$.

**Check:** Because the special law lacks Revised Penal Code penalty terms, *reclusion perpetua* cannot replace death here.

### #3: Parole After a Reduced Sentence

A person's death sentence is reduced to *reclusion perpetua* by reason of Republic Act No. $9346$. Does the Act make that person eligible for parole?

**Solution:**

1. Check the source of the sentence: it was reduced to *reclusion perpetua* by reason of Republic Act No. $9346$.
2. Apply Section $3$: the Act expressly removes parole eligibility for persons in this category.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether the person qualifies for parole and whether switching to life imprisonment is allowed.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the person is ineligible for parole and cannot switch to life imprisonment.** Republic Act No. $9346$, Section $3$ bars parole for reduced *reclusion perpetua* sentences. Section $2$ fixes the penalty from the violated law's naming style, not prisoner choice.

**Check:** Section $3$ closes off parole for reduced sentences, and Section $2$ leaves no option to choose another penalty.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Law formerly carried death: match Revised Penal Code names to *reclusion perpetua* and other laws to life imprisonment. {#nomenclature-picks-replacement}
- Death sentence reduced to *reclusion perpetua*: block parole under Section $3$ of the Act. {#no-parole-after-reduction}
- Prisoner asks to swap penalties for parole: apply the violated statute's naming rule instead. {#no-prisoner-penalty-choice}
:::
