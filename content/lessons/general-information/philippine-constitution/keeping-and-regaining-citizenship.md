## Core Concept

### Marriage, Loss, and Regaining Under RA $9225$

**Renunciation** means giving up citizenship. Under Article IV, Section $4$, marriage to an alien keeps citizenship unless an act or omission counts as legal renunciation.

Section $3$ leaves loss and reacquisition to law. Under RA $9225$, Section $3$, natural-born Filipinos who lost citizenship by foreign naturalization reacquire it by taking the **oath of allegiance**. Those naturalized abroad after RA $9225$ retain it upon taking that oath.

An **oath of allegiance** is a sworn promise of loyalty to the Philippines. Under Section $5(2)$, an RA $9225$ elective candidate must also personally swear foreign renunciation before an authorized officer at filing and meet office qualifications.

Trace a natural-born Filipino naturalized in Canada in $2015$ who takes the RA $9225$ oath:

1. Because naturalization occurred after RA $9225$, taking the oath retains Philippine citizenship under Section $3$.
2. No judicial naturalization case is needed.

:::pair #marriage-keeps-citizenship
Right: Under Article IV, Section $4$, marriage to an alien keeps citizenship unless legal renunciation occurs.
Wrong: Assuming marriage to an alien ends or suspends Philippine citizenship.
Look for legal renunciation, not the spouse's nationality.
:::

:::pair #candidacy-sworn-renunciation
Right: Under RA $9225$, Section $5(2)$, an elective candidate must personally swear foreign renunciation at filing.
Wrong: Relying on the RA $9225$ oath alone without separately renouncing foreign citizenship at filing.
Reacquiring citizenship and running for office require separate steps.
:::

:::check
A naturalized Philippine citizen becomes Canadian and applies under RA $9225$, Section $3$. Does Section $3$ cover him?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. RA $9225$, Section $3$, covers **natural-born** Filipinos who lost Philippine citizenship by foreign naturalization, not previously naturalized citizens.
:::

### Repatriation Under RA $8171$

**Repatriation** restores former Philippine citizenship. RA $8171$, Section $1$, covers Filipino women who lost it by alien marriage. It also covers natural-born Filipinos who lost citizenship due to political or economic necessity, plus their minor children.

Steps include an oath and registration with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration, subject to exclusions.

Check a natural-born Filipino who lost Philippine citizenship due to economic necessity applying under RA $8171$ with her minor child:

1. Coverage: RA $8171$, Section $1$, covers loss of citizenship due to political or economic necessity and minor children.
2. Procedure: take the oath and register with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration, subject to exclusions.

:::why
Why keep RA $8171$ alongside RA $9225$? RA $8171$ restores citizenship lost by marriage or by natural-born Filipinos due to political or economic necessity.
:::

:::check
A former natural-born Filipino who lost citizenship due to political necessity takes the RA $8171$ oath. Is the oath enough without registration?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. RA $8171$ requires both the oath and registration with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration, subject to exclusions.
:::

### Dual Citizenship vs. Dual Allegiance

**Dual citizenship** holds two citizenships from birth laws or RA $9225$. **Dual allegiance** voluntarily owes loyalty to two states.

Article IV, Section $5$, declares dual allegiance harmful to the national interest. Dual citizenship alone does not prove it.

Compare Maya, holding Philippine and Japanese citizenship from birth laws, with Leo, who swears ongoing loyalty to a foreign state:

1. Maya has automatic **dual citizenship**, which alone never proves dual allegiance under Section $5$.
2. Leo's voluntary loyalty to two states raises **dual allegiance**, which Section $5$ condemns.

:::pair #dual-citizenship-vs-allegiance
Right: Two citizenships from different laws mean dual citizenship, not dual allegiance.
Wrong: Treating two citizenships alone as establishing harmful dual allegiance under Article IV, Section $5$.
Dual allegiance requires voluntary loyalty to two states.
:::

:::check
A natural-born Filipino reacquires citizenship under RA $9225$ and keeps a foreign passport. Does dual citizenship alone violate Section $5$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Dual citizenship under RA $9225$ does not by itself prove voluntary dual allegiance under Article IV, Section $5$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Marriage and Legal Renunciation

Elena and Dario are Filipinos who marry foreign nationals abroad. Elena takes no act of renunciation, while Dario completes an act counting as legal renunciation. Who keeps citizenship under Article IV, Section $4$?

**Solution:**

1. Elena: under Section $4$, marriage to an alien alone keeps Philippine citizenship.
2. Dario: Section $4$ ends retention when an act counts as legal renunciation under law.

Answer: Only Elena retains citizenship; Dario loses it by renunciation, not marriage.

**Check:** Marriage keeps citizenship unless legal renunciation occurs under law.

### #2: Comparing Two RA $9225$ Candidacy Filings

Carlo and Nina reacquired citizenship under RA $9225$, Section $3$, and seek Senate seats. Carlo personally swears foreign renunciation at filing, yet is age $32$. Nina meets Senate rules, yet her lawyer signs her renunciation. Does either satisfy Section $5(2)$?

**Solution:**

1. Carlo: he swore renunciation personally, yet Section $5(2)$ also requires office qualifications, and age $32$ fails Senate rules.
2. Nina: she meets Senate rules, yet Section $5(2)$ requires **personal** sworn foreign renunciation at filing.

Answer: Neither satisfies Section $5(2)$; Carlo misses the age rule, and Nina skipped personal renunciation.

**Check:** Section $5(2)$ requires both office qualifications and personal sworn renunciation at filing.

### #3: Candidacy Filing and Repatriation

Marco, naturalized in Australia after natural-born status, takes the RA $9225$ oath and runs for governor without swearing foreign renunciation. Rosa lost citizenship by marriage in $1969$. Rule on both.

**Solution:**

1. Check Marco under RA $9225$, Section $5(2)$ beyond the Section $3$ oath.
2. Match Rosa to the repatriation law for women who lost citizenship by marriage.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on Marco's filing and name Rosa's statute and steps.
:::answer
The answer is **Marco's filing is incomplete without personal sworn foreign renunciation, and Rosa uses repatriation under RA $8171$.** Under RA $9225$, Section $5(2)$, an elective candidate must personally swear foreign renunciation at filing. Under RA $8171$, she takes an oath and registers with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration, subject to exclusions.

**Check:** Reacquiring citizenship under RA $9225$ is separate from candidacy renunciation, and RA $8171$ covers women who lost citizenship by marriage.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Marriage to an alien: citizenship stays unless legal renunciation occurs. {#marriage-keeps-citizenship}
- Elective candidate under RA $9225$: make a personal sworn foreign renunciation at filing. {#candidacy-sworn-renunciation}
- Two citizenships: dual citizenship alone does not prove dual allegiance. {#dual-citizenship-vs-allegiance}
:::
