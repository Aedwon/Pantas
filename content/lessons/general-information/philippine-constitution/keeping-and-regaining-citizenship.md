## Core Concept

### Marriage, Loss, and Regaining Under RA $9225$

**Renunciation** means giving up citizenship. Under Article IV, Section $4$, a Filipino marrying an alien keeps citizenship unless an act or omission counts as legal renunciation.

Section $3$ leaves loss and reacquisition to law. Under RA $9225$, Section $3$, natural-born Filipinos who lost citizenship by foreign naturalization reacquire it through the prescribed **oath of allegiance**. Those naturalized abroad after the law took effect retain citizenship upon taking that oath.

An **oath of allegiance** is a sworn promise of loyalty to the Philippines and its Constitution. Under Section $5(2)$, an RA $9225$ elective candidate must also personally swear foreign renunciation before an authorized officer at filing. Office qualifications still apply.

Trace a natural-born Filipino naturalized in Canada who takes the RA $9225$ oath and runs for mayor:

1. Taking the oath restores citizenship under RA $9225$, Section $3$.
2. Running for office also requires personal sworn foreign renunciation at filing under Section $5(2)$, plus mayor qualifications.

:::pair #marriage-keeps-citizenship
Right: Under Article IV, Section $4$, marriage to an alien keeps citizenship unless legal renunciation occurs.
Wrong: Assuming marriage to an alien ends or suspends Philippine citizenship.
Look for legal renunciation, not the spouse's foreign citizenship.
:::

:::pair #candidacy-sworn-renunciation
Right: Under RA $9225$, Section $5(2)$, an elective candidate must personally swear foreign renunciation when filing candidacy.
Wrong: Relying on the RA $9225$ oath alone without separately renouncing foreign citizenship at filing.
Reacquiring citizenship and running for office have separate steps.
:::

:::check
A Filipino marries a Spanish national in Madrid and signs no renunciation. Must she be naturalized to stay Filipino?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Under Article IV, Section $4$, marriage to an alien alone never removes Philippine citizenship.
:::

### Repatriation Under RA $8171$

**Repatriation** means restoring former Philippine citizenship. RA $8171$ covers Filipino women who lost it by marriage to aliens. It also covers natural-born Filipinos displaced by political or economic necessity, including minor children.

Requirements include an oath and registration with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration, subject to exclusions.

Check a Filipino woman who lost citizenship by marriage to an alien in $1970$ and applies under RA $8171$:

1. Coverage: RA $8171$ expressly covers Filipino women who lost citizenship by marriage.
2. Procedure: subject to exclusions, she takes the oath and registers with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration.

:::why
Why keep RA $8171$ alongside RA $9225$? RA $8171$ restores citizenship to women who lost it by marriage and to natural-born citizens displaced by political or economic necessity.
:::

:::check
A former natural-born Filipino who left due to economic necessity takes the RA $8171$ oath. Is that oath enough without registering?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. RA $8171$ requires both the oath and registration with the civil registry and Bureau of Immigration, subject to exclusions.
:::

### Dual Citizenship vs. Dual Allegiance

**Dual citizenship** means holding citizenship in two countries from different birth laws or RA $9225$ retention. **Dual allegiance** means voluntarily owing loyalty to two states.

Article IV, Section $5$, declares dual allegiance harmful to the national interest and directs law to address it. Dual citizenship alone does not prove dual allegiance.

Test a child born in Rome to a Filipino mother and Italian father, holding both citizenships without pledging loyalty to both states:

1. Cause: two countries' birth laws acted automatically at birth.
2. Rule: under Section $5$, dual citizenship by law does not prove voluntary dual allegiance.

:::pair #dual-citizenship-vs-allegiance
Right: Two citizenships from different laws mean dual citizenship, which alone does not prove dual allegiance.
Wrong: Treating two citizenships alone as establishing harmful dual allegiance under Article IV, Section $5$.
Dual allegiance requires voluntary loyalty to two states, not dual status by law.
:::

:::check
A natural-born Filipino reacquires citizenship under RA $9225$ while keeping a foreign passport. Does dual citizenship alone violate Section $5$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Dual citizenship under RA $9225$ does not by itself prove voluntary dual allegiance under Article IV, Section $5$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Marriage to an Alien

A Filipino marries an alien without legally renouncing citizenship. Is citizenship lost?

**Solution:**

1. Apply Article IV, Section $4$: marriage to an alien keeps citizenship unless legal renunciation occurs.
2. Check the facts: no legal renunciation occurred.

Answer: No, citizenship is retained under Article IV, Section $4$.

**Check:** Marriage alone never removes or suspends Philippine citizenship.

### #2: Reacquisition and Candidacy

A natural-born Filipino lost citizenship through foreign naturalization. What separate steps does RA $9225$ require to regain citizenship and seek elective office?

**Solution:**

1. Take the prescribed oath of allegiance under RA $9225$, Section $3$.
2. Meet office qualifications and personally swear foreign renunciation before an authorized officer when filing candidacy under Section $5(2)$.

Answer: Take the oath of allegiance to reacquire citizenship, then personally swear foreign renunciation at candidacy filing while meeting office qualifications.

**Check:** Section $3$ restores citizenship by oath; Section $5(2)$ requires sworn foreign renunciation at filing.

### #3: Two Citizenships

A child is Filipino through a parent who was Filipino when the child was born. The child is also a foreign citizen through birthplace. Does this alone establish dual allegiance?

**Solution:**

1. Trace both citizenships: Filipino parentage gave one, and foreign birthplace law gave the other.
2. Compare status with loyalty: Section $5$ covers voluntary loyalty to two states, not automatic dual status.

:::try
Finish the last step: state whether this alone establishes dual allegiance and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **No, two citizenships from different laws do not alone establish dual allegiance.** Dual citizenship arises automatically from different countries' laws, whereas dual allegiance requires voluntarily owing loyalty to two states.

**Check:** Under Article IV, Section $5$, automatic dual citizenship at birth does not prove voluntary dual allegiance.
:::
