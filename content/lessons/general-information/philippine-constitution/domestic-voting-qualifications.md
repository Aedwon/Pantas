## Core Concept

### Constitutional Qualifications and Overlapping Residence

**Suffrage** means the right to vote. Under Article V, Section $1$, a domestic voter must be a Philippine citizen, not disqualified by law, and meet three minimums:

1. At least $18$ years of age.
2. Residence in the Philippines for at least $1$ year.
3. Residence in the voting place for at least $6$ months right before the election.

These residence periods can overlap, yet check both separately. Long Philippine residence never replaces $6$ months in the voting place.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: Domestic voter checklist with four boxes labeled Filipino citizen, At least 18 on election day, At least 1 year in the Philippines, At least 6 months in the voting place; show Pass only if all boxes pass, Fail if any fails; footer: Also check registration and legal disqualifications. -->

Screen a registered $22$-year-old Filipino with no disqualification who returned from abroad and lived in Davao City for $9$ months before election day:

1. Check citizenship, age, and local residence: citizen, at least $18$, and $9$ months locally clears $6$ months.
2. Check national residence: $9$ months in the Philippines is under $1$ year, so the voter fails.

:::pair #local-residence-separate
Right: Check both $1$ year in the Philippines and $6$ months locally, though the same months can count toward both.
Wrong: Letting long residence in the Philippines replace the $6$-month local residence rule.
Years elsewhere in the Philippines never cure fewer than $6$ months in the voting place.
:::

:::check
A registered $25$-year-old Filipino lived in Manila for $8$ years, then moved to Cebu City $5$ months before election day. May she vote in Cebu City?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Five months in Cebu City falls short of the $6$-month local rule under Article V, Section $1$, despite $8$ years in the Philippines.
:::

### Registration, Disqualifications, and Banned Tests

RA $8189$, Section $9$, allows registration before reaching these minimums if they will be met on election day. Section $10$ requires registration to vote. A **registered voter** has an approved entry in official voting records.

Section $11$ lists disqualifications, including certain final criminal judgments. A charge alone is not a final judgment. Law also provides ways to restore voting rights.

No **literacy** (reading and writing), property, or other substantive requirement may be imposed under Article V, Section $1$.

Screen an applicant who is $17$ at registration and turns $18$ before election day. By then, she will have lived $15$ months in town, cannot read, and faces an untried charge:

1. Check age and residence on election day under Section $9$: age $18$ and $15$ months clear $1$ year and $6$ months.
2. Check disqualifications and banned tests: an untried charge is not a final judgment under Section $11$, and literacy tests are barred.

:::why
Why measure age and residence on election day for registration? Registration closes early, so advance registration lets citizens turning $18$ by election day register.
:::

:::pair #age-and-residence-on-election-day
Right: Under RA $8189$, Section $9$, anyone turning $18$ and meeting both residence rules on election day may register early.
Wrong: Refusing registration because the applicant is still $17$ on the application date.
Section $9$ measures age and residence on election day, not on the registration date.
:::

:::pair #no-literacy-or-property-test
Right: Article V, Section $1$ forbids any literacy, property, or other substantive requirement for qualified voters.
Wrong: Requiring an otherwise qualified voter to pass a reading test or own land.
Only citizenship, age, both residence rules, registration, and lack of disqualification apply.
:::

:::check
A town resolution orders voters to show a land tax receipt or read a ballot line aloud before voting. Is that valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article V, Section $1$ expressly forbids property and literacy requirements for suffrage.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Election-Day Age

A registered Filipino turns $18$ on election day. By then, residence is $2$ years in the country and $8$ months locally. No disqualification applies. May this person vote?

**Solution:**

1. Check election-day age: turning $18$ meets "at least $18$".
2. Check residence: $2$ years clears $1$ year nationally, and $8$ months clears $6$ months locally.

Answer: Yes, the person meets all minimums, is registered, and has no disqualification.

**Check:** Article V, Section $1$ requires at least $18$ on election day, $1$ national year, and $6$ local months.

### #2: Local Residence

Two adult Filipinos have each lived in the country for $10$ years. One has lived in the voting place for $8$ months, the other for $3$ months. Both are registered and not disqualified. Who meets both residence minimums?

**Solution:**

1. Check national residence: both clear $1$ year with $10$ years.
2. Check local residence: $8$ months clears $6$ months, while $3$ months fails.

Answer: Only the first person meets both residence minimums.

**Check:** Ten years in the country never replaces $6$ months in the voting place.

### #3: Property, Literacy, and Disqualification

Two registered $30$-year-old Filipinos have lived in one city for $3$ years. Voter A rents a room with no land and cannot read. Voter B owns a house yet has a final criminal judgment disqualifying him under RA $8189$, Section $11$. Who may vote?

**Solution:**

1. Check Voter A: constitutional minimums and registration pass, and Article V, Section $1$ bars literacy and property tests.
2. Check Voter B: owning property never cures a lawful disqualification under RA $8189$, Section $11$.

:::try
Finish the last step: state who may vote and explain why Voter A passes while Voter B fails.
:::answer
The answer is **Only Voter A may vote, while Voter B is barred.** Article V, Section $1$ forbids literacy and property requirements, so neither renting nor inability to read blocks Voter A. Voter B is disqualified by a final criminal judgment under RA $8189$, Section $11$.

**Check:** Only constitutional qualifications, registration, and lawful disqualifications apply; reading and property tests are barred.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Long Philippine residence: verify the separate $6$-month local residence minimum. {#local-residence-separate}
- Applicant is $17$ at registration: check whether age $18$ and residence pass on election day. {#age-and-residence-on-election-day}
- Literacy or property tests: reject any reading test or land-ownership rule. {#no-literacy-or-property-test}
:::
