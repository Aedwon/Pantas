## Core Concept

Article XVII sets rules for changing the Constitution so no group can change it on its own.

### Amendment or Revision?

An **amendment** changes specific provisions without changing a basic principle. A **revision** changes the basic structure or a large part of the Constitution. These meanings come from Supreme Court case law, not definitions in Article XVII.

In *Lambino v. COMELEC*, the Court used two tests. The **quantitative test** looks at how many provisions change and how widely the changes reach. The **qualitative test** asks whether basic principles or the plan of government change.

Count and effect both matter. Even a short proposal can be a revision if it changes the basic structure.

## Examples

### #1: Compare the Changes

One proposal changes only an office's term length, leaving basic principles intact. Another replaces presidential government with a parliamentary system. Which is an amendment, and which is a revision?

**Solution:**

The first is an amendment. The second is a revision because it changes the basic plan of government.
