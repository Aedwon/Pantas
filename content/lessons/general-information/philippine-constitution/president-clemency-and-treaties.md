## Core Concept

### Pardon, Amnesty, and Impeachment

**Impeachment** charges and tries high officials: the House brings charges, and the Senate holds the trial (Article XI, Section $3$). See Accountability and Impeachment for that process.

A **pardon** forgives a criminal offense **after conviction by final judgment**. It cannot cover impeachment (Article VII, Section $19$).

**Amnesty** forgives offenses covered by a grant, often for a group. It needs **concurrence of a majority of all Members of Congress**. Both pardon and amnesty for election offenses also need a **favorable COMELEC recommendation** (Article IX-C, Section $5$).

Test two clemency requests sent to the President:

1. A defendant still appealing a conviction asks for a pardon: blocked, because conviction is not yet final.
2. A voter-fraud offender with a final conviction seeks a pardon without COMELEC backing: blocked under Article IX-C, Section $5$.

:::why
Why require final conviction for pardons and Congress for amnesty? Waiting for final judgment keeps the President out of trials, while congressional consent checks group grants.
:::

:::pair #pardon-after-final-judgment
Right: A pardon applies only after conviction by final judgment and never covers impeachment.
Wrong: Granting a pardon before final judgment, or using a pardon to erase impeachment.
Article VII, Section $19$ requires final conviction and expressly excludes impeachment cases.
:::

:::pair #amnesty-needs-all-congress
Right: Amnesty requires concurrence of a majority of all Members of Congress, including both Houses.
Wrong: Granting amnesty without Congress, or requiring two-thirds of the Senate alone.
Two-thirds of the Senate applies to treaties; amnesty needs a majority of all Congress Members.
:::

:::check
An impeached high official is removed by the Senate after trial. May the President grant a pardon to overturn that impeachment?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article VII, Section $19$ expressly bars presidential pardons in cases of impeachment.
:::

### Treaty Concurrence

A **treaty** is an agreement between states. Under Article VII, Section $21$, no treaty is valid unless concurred in by **at least two-thirds of all the Members of the Senate**.

With $24$ Senators, two-thirds is $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$ Senators. That count measures against all $24$ Members regardless of attendance.

Test a treaty vote when $19$ of $24$ Senators attend and $15$ vote yes:

1. Compute two-thirds of all $24$ Senators: $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$ votes needed.
2. Compare $15$ to $16$: $15 < 16$, so the treaty fails even though $15$ exceeds half of $24$.

:::pair #treaty-two-thirds-all-senators
Right: A treaty requires concurrence by at least two-thirds of all $24$ Senators ($16$ votes).
Wrong: Treating a simple majority of all Senators, or two-thirds of those present, as enough.
Article VII, Section $21$ counts two-thirds of all Senators, not attendance.
:::

:::check
Suppose $22$ of $24$ Senators attend a treaty vote and $16$ vote in favor. Does the treaty meet Section $21$?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Section $21$ requires at least $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$ votes of all Senators, and $16$ meets that mark.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Emergency Powers and Clemency

During rebellion, the President declares martial law for $90$ days without an extension. A detainee under writ suspension remains uncharged after $3$ days. What limits apply?

Separately, may the President pardon impeachment, pardon an election offense without COMELEC backing, or grant amnesty alone?

**Solution:**

1. Martial law caps at $60$ days initially, with a $48$-hour report, joint revocation, and Supreme Court review.
2. A detainee under writ suspension must be released if not charged in court within $3$ days.
3. Pardon excludes impeachment, election-offense pardons need a favorable COMELEC recommendation, and amnesty needs a majority of all Congress Members.

Answer: Martial law caps at $60$ days with $3$-day charging; none of the three clemency acts are valid alone.

**Check:** Article VII, Sections $18$ and $19$ and Article IX-C, Section $5$ each impose strict limits.

### #2: Treaty Concurrence

With $24$ Senators, how many must concur in a treaty?

**Solution:**

1. Use at least two-thirds of all Senators under Article VII, Section $21$.

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\times24=16
$$

2. Count against all $24$ Senators, even when members are absent.

Answer: At least $16$ Senators must concur.

**Check:** $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$, matching Article VII, Section $21$.

### #3: Election-Offense Amnesty and Treaty Attendance

The President proclaims amnesty for election-code offenders without COMELEC's recommendation, though Congress concurs. That week, $21$ of $24$ Senators attend a treaty vote and $14$ vote yes. Is either act valid?

**Solution:**

1. Check Article IX-C, Section $5$: election-offense amnesty requires a favorable COMELEC recommendation.
2. Check Article VII, Section $21$: two-thirds of all $24$ Senators is $16$, whereas $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 21 = 14$ counts attendance only.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on both acts and explain why congressional concurrence and $14$ of $21$ present fall short.
:::answer
The answer is **Neither is valid: the amnesty lacks a favorable COMELEC recommendation, and the treaty needs $16$ of all $24$ Senators.** Under Article IX-C, Section $5$, congressional concurrence cannot skip COMELEC's recommendation. Under Article VII, Section $21$, $14$ is two-thirds of $21$ present, not all $24$ Senators.

**Check:** Both rules must pass: COMELEC backing for election-offense clemency, and $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16$ Senators for treaties.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Presidential pardon: require conviction by final judgment, and never apply it to impeachment. {#pardon-after-final-judgment}
- Amnesty vote: require concurrence of a majority of all Members of Congress, not the Senate alone. {#amnesty-needs-all-congress}
- Treaty concurrence: count two-thirds of all $24$ Senators ($16$ votes), not simple majority or attendance. {#treaty-two-thirds-all-senators}
:::
