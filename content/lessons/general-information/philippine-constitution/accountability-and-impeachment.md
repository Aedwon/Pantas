## Core Concept

Article XI, Section $1$ says that public office is a public trust. Public officers must answer to the people. They must serve with responsibility, integrity, loyalty, efficiency, patriotism, justice, and modesty.

### Closed List of Impeachable Officers

Article XI, Section $2$ gives a closed list of officers who may be removed through impeachment:

1. The President
2. The Vice-President
3. The Members of the Supreme Court
4. The Members of the Constitutional Commissions: CSC, COMELEC, and COA
5. The Ombudsman

Other public officers may be removed as provided by law, but not by impeachment.

### Closed List of Grounds for Impeachment

Article XI, Section $2$ also gives a closed list of impeachment grounds:

1. **Culpable violation of the Constitution**
2. **Treason**
3. **Bribery**
4. **Graft and corruption**
5. **Other high crimes**
6. **Betrayal of public trust**

A culpable constitutional violation is a willful or intentional breach of the Constitution, not merely an honest mistake. Treason involves betrayal of the country in the ways defined by criminal law, such as levying war against the Philippines or giving aid or comfort to its enemies.

Bribery involves an improper benefit tied to an official act. Graft and corruption cover serious misuse of public office for improper benefit. Using public power to steer an improper benefit to oneself can raise a graft or corruption issue.

"Other high crimes" is a broad impeachment ground. The Constitution does not give a complete list of acts under it.

"Betrayal of public trust" is also broad. It can cover serious abuse, neglect, favoritism, or other conduct that makes a high officer unfit to keep a public trust.

Being on the list only means that the accusation uses an allowed ground. It does not prove that the facts are true, that the case is legally sufficient, or that the officer must be convicted or removed.

### Basic Impeachment Process

The House of Representatives has the exclusive power to initiate impeachment cases. A verified complaint may come from a House Member, or from a citizen if a House Member endorses it.

A vote of at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members is needed to approve Articles of Impeachment through the regular process. If a verified complaint or resolution is filed by at least $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of all House Members, it becomes the Articles of Impeachment and the Senate trial proceeds.

The Senate has the sole power to try and decide impeachment cases. Conviction requires the agreement of at least $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of all Senators.

If the President is on trial, the Chief Justice presides but does not vote. An impeachment judgment may remove the officer and disqualify the officer from public office. A convicted officer may still face a separate prosecution, trial, and punishment under law.

No impeachment proceeding may be initiated against the same official more than once within a period of one year.

### The Ombudsman and Public Accountability

The Ombudsman receives complaints against public officials and employees. The office may also investigate on its own when an official act or failure to act appears illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient.

The Ombudsman may direct officials to perform duties required by law or to stop and correct abuse. It may recommend action such as removal, suspension, demotion, a fine, censure, or prosecution.

The Ombudsman may also request government records and help. It may study causes of inefficiency, red tape, mismanagement, fraud, and corruption, then recommend ways to remove them.

## Examples

### #1: President as an Impeachable Officer

A complaint seeks to remove the President through impeachment for an allowed constitutional ground. Is the President an officer who may be removed through impeachment?

**Solution:**

1. Check the closed list in Article XI, Section $2$.
2. The President is on that list.
3. Therefore, the President is an impeachable officer. The accusation must still go through the constitutional process.

### #2: Trial Court Judge

A petition seeks to remove a Regional Trial Court judge from office through impeachment. Is a Regional Trial Court judge an impeachable officer under Article XI?

**Solution:**

1. The closed list includes the President, Vice-President, Members of the Supreme Court, Members of the Constitutional Commissions, and the Ombudsman.
2. A Regional Trial Court judge is not on that list.
3. Therefore, a Regional Trial Court judge is not removed through impeachment.

### #3: Ground Outside the Closed List

An impeachable officer is accused only of "inefficiency." Is inefficiency by itself one of the six named constitutional grounds for impeachment?

**Solution:**

1. Compare the accusation with the closed list in Article XI, Section $2$.
2. "Inefficiency" is not one of the six named grounds.
3. Therefore, inefficiency by itself is not a named constitutional ground for impeachment.
4. A different accusation must still be judged by its facts and by the proper impeachment process.

### #4: Conduct That May Fit a Named Ground

An impeachable officer is accused of accepting money in exchange for an official act. Which named impeachment ground most directly fits the accusation?

**Solution:**

1. The conduct describes an improper benefit tied to an official act.
2. That description most directly fits **bribery**.
3. This classification does not prove the accusation. The facts and the case still have to be decided through the impeachment process.

### #5: Culpable Constitutional Violation

An impeachable officer knowingly and deliberately disobeys a clear command of the Constitution. Which named ground may most directly fit the accusation?

**Solution:**

1. The alleged breach is of the Constitution itself.
2. The facts say the act was knowing and deliberate, not an honest mistake.
3. The accusation may fit **culpable violation of the Constitution**.
4. The classification still does not prove the facts or require conviction.

### #6: Betrayal of Public Trust

An impeachable officer repeatedly uses official power to favor close allies at the expense of the public interest. Which broad impeachment ground may be relevant?

**Solution:**

1. The conduct concerns misuse of an office held in public trust.
2. Serious favoritism that harms the public interest may fall under **betrayal of public trust**.
3. The House and Senate must still deal with the actual facts and the impeachment case.

### #7: House and Senate Roles

The House approves Articles of Impeachment against an impeachable officer. Which body tries and decides the impeachment case?

**Solution:**

1. The House initiates impeachment.
2. The Senate has the sole power to try and decide the case.
3. Conviction requires at least $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of all Senators.

### #8: Ombudsman Remedy

A public employee is accused of delaying a duty required by law without a proper reason. Can the Ombudsman act on a complaint about this conduct?

**Solution:**

1. The Ombudsman may investigate an act or failure to act that appears illegal, unjust, improper, or inefficient.
2. It may direct a public official or employee to perform and speed up a duty required by law.
3. Therefore, the complaint falls within the kinds of public-accountability matters the Ombudsman may address.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Not Every Government Officer Is Impeachable

> Do not assume that a high-ranking title automatically makes an officer impeachable.

The Constitution gives a closed list. Cabinet Secretaries, legislators, and trial court judges are not on that impeachment list.

### #2: A Named Ground Is Not a Conviction

> Matching conduct to a ground does not prove the case.

The House and Senate still have constitutional roles. Facts, legal sufficiency, and conviction are separate questions.

### #3: Impeachment Is Not the Same as Criminal Punishment

> Impeachment mainly decides removal and possible disqualification from public office.

A person convicted in impeachment may still face a separate prosecution, trial, and punishment under law.
