## Core Concept

### Executive power: the President

As **Commander-in-Chief**, the President heads the armed forces. **Martial law** places an area under emergency military authority within constitutional limits. Section $18$ allows it only during invasion or rebellion when public safety requires it.

The President may also suspend the **privilege of the writ of habeas corpus** under those conditions. The writ normally lets a court order a detained person brought before it to check the detention.

Suspension covers only people charged in court with rebellion or offenses that are part of or directly connected with invasion. A person arrested or detained under the suspension must be charged in court within $3$ days. Otherwise, that person must be released.

Either measure has an initial limit of $60$ days. The President must report to Congress within $48$ hours. Congress may revoke it by at least a majority of all its Members, voting jointly.

At the President's request, Congress may extend it if invasion or rebellion continues and public safety requires it. Any citizen may ask the Supreme Court to review its factual basis. Martial law does not suspend the Constitution, replace functioning civil courts, or automatically suspend the writ's privilege.
