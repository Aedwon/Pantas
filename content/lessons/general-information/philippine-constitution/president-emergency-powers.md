## Core Concept

### Grounds, Initial Limits, and Detention Safeguards

As **Commander-in-Chief**, the President heads the armed forces. **Martial law** places an area under emergency military authority within constitutional limits.

Under Article VII, Section $18$, the President may proclaim martial law or suspend the **privilege of the writ of habeas corpus**. Both require **invasion or rebellion**, when **public safety requires it**. That writ lets a court check whether custody is lawful.

Three strict clocks limit both measures:

1. Initial duration cannot exceed $60$ days.
2. The President must report to Congress in person or in writing within $48$ hours.
3. Anyone detained under writ suspension must be judicially charged within $3$ days, or released.

Suspension covers only people judicially charged with rebellion or offenses inherent in or directly connected with invasion.

<!-- ILLUSTRATION: timeline Day 0: proclamation or suspension; Within 48 hours: report to Congress; Day 60: initial limit; separate note Detained under suspension: charge in court within 3 days or release; notes Congress may revoke and Supreme Court may review -->

Check the clocks when the President suspends the writ during rebellion on April $1$:

1. The report to Congress is due within $48$ hours (April $3$), and the initial suspension ends at $60$ days.
2. Anyone arrested under suspension on April $2$ must be charged by April $5$ ($3$ days) or released.

:::pair #sixty-day-initial-limit
Right: Martial law or writ suspension lasts at most $60$ days initially under Article VII, Section $18$.
Wrong: Setting a $30$-day or $90$-day initial limit instead of $60$ days.
Check the exact Section $18$ initial cap of $60$ days before any congressional extension.
:::

:::check
Police detain a suspect under a valid writ suspension on Tuesday and file no charge by Friday night. What must happen?
:::answer
The answer is **The suspect must be released**. Section $18$ requires judicial charging within $3$ days, or release.
:::

### Congressional and Supreme Court Checks

Congress, voting **jointly**, may revoke martial law or writ suspension by at least a **majority of all its Members**. The President cannot set aside revocation.

Upon the President's initiative, Congress may extend the measure jointly if invasion or rebellion persists and public safety requires it.

Any citizen may ask the Supreme Court to review its factual basis. Martial law never suspends the Constitution, replaces functioning civil courts or legislative assemblies, or automatically suspends the writ privilege.

Test what stays open when martial law is proclaimed:

1. Functioning civil courts stay open, keeping ordinary crimes with civil judges.
2. Any citizen may immediately ask the Supreme Court to review the factual basis.

:::why
Why give Congress and the Supreme Court immediate checks? Emergency power stays bounded when lawmakers can revoke it jointly and citizens can challenge it at once.
:::

:::pair #joint-majority-revocation
Right: Congress revokes martial law or writ suspension by a majority of all its Members voting jointly.
Wrong: Requiring separate two-thirds votes in each House to revoke martial law.
Separate two-thirds votes apply to war and vetoes; revocation needs a joint majority.
:::

:::pair #court-review-never-waits
Right: Any citizen may seek immediate Supreme Court review of the factual basis, and civil courts stay open.
Wrong: Saying only Congress reviews the factual basis, or that courts must wait until martial law ends.
Section $18$ opens immediate Supreme Court review while civil courts keep operating.
:::

:::check
Civil trial courts stay open during martial law, yet an official moves a robbery case to a military panel. Is that allowed?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Martial law does not supplant functioning civil courts under Section $18$.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Grounds and Initial Report

A typhoon cuts island power. A governor urges martial law for $75$ days with a report within $5$ days. Do these facts satisfy Section $18$?

**Solution:**

1. Martial law requires invasion or rebellion plus public safety, not a typhoon.
2. $75$ days exceeds $60$ days initially, and $5$ days exceeds $48$ hours.

Answer: No, both the ground and the $60$-day and $48$-hour clocks fail.

**Check:** Section $18$ requires invasion or rebellion, a $60$-day initial limit, and a $48$-hour report.

### #2: Scope of Writ Suspension

During rebellion, the President validly suspends the writ privilege. Police arrest a vendor for unpaid taxes and hold him uncharged for $4$ days. Identify two violations.

**Solution:**

1. Suspension covers only rebellion or invasion-linked charges, not tax cases.
2. Holding him $4$ days passes the $3$-day limit to charge or release.

Answer: Suspension excludes tax charges, and detention past $3$ days requires release.

**Check:** Section $18$ limits suspension to rebellion or invasion charges and sets a $3$-day deadline.

### #3: Joint Revocation and Court Review

Suppose Congress has $324$ total Members ($24$ Senators and $300$ Representatives) during martial law. In joint session, $163$ vote to revoke, and a citizen petitions the Supreme Court to review the factual basis. Can the President block revocation, and must the citizen wait?

**Solution:**

1. A majority of $324$ total Members voting jointly is $163$.
2. Section $18$ bars presidential reversal and opens citizen review right away.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on revocation, presidential interference, and review timing.
:::answer
The answer is **Revocation passes with $163$ joint votes, the President cannot block it, and the citizen can seek review immediately.** Section $18$ requires a joint majority of all $324$ Members ($163$), bars reversal, and sets no wait for court review.

**Check:** Half of $324$ is $162$, so $163$ is a joint majority, and Section $18$ allows immediate citizen review.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Initial limits: cap martial law and writ suspension at $60$ days initially, with a $48$-hour report. {#sixty-day-initial-limit}
- Congressional revocation: use a majority of all Members voting jointly, not separate two-thirds votes. {#joint-majority-revocation}
- Judicial check: any citizen may seek Supreme Court review right away, and civil courts stay open. {#court-review-never-waits}
:::
