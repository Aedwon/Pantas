## Core Concept

### What the Grounds Mean

Section $2$ names $6$ grounds. The Constitution lists them but does not give a complete definition of each. These explanations help match conduct to a possible ground without settling a legal case.

**Culpable violation of the Constitution** concerns a willful and intentional breach of the Constitution. This reading separates deliberate disobedience from an honest mistake. Knowingly refusing to obey a clear constitutional duty may raise this ground.

**Treason** concerns betrayal of the country in wartime. Under criminal law, it includes levying war against the Philippines or siding with its enemies and giving them aid or comfort. Supplying military information to help a wartime enemy illustrates the second form.

**Bribery** concerns an improper benefit linked to official duties. Accepting money in return for an official favor may raise this ground. The connection between the benefit and the official act matters.

## Examples

### #1: A Ground Outside the List

Is inefficiency, by itself, one of the $6$ named grounds for impeachment?

**Solution:**

1. Compare it with the constitutional list.
2. Inefficiency is not a separately named ground.
3. Whether particular conduct supports a listed ground requires facts and legal judgment.

### #2: Money for an Official Favor

An impeachable officer is accused of accepting money in exchange for an official act. Which named ground most directly matches this conduct?

**Solution:**

1. The money is an improper benefit tied to an official act.
2. The accusation most directly describes bribery.
3. This match does not prove the accusation.

### #3: Deliberate Constitutional Breach

An impeachable officer knowingly and deliberately disobeys a clear command of the Constitution. Which ground may apply?

**Solution:**

1. The alleged act breaches the Constitution itself.
2. The officer is said to have acted deliberately, not by honest mistake.
3. The accusation may fit culpable violation of the Constitution.

### #4: Aid to a Wartime Enemy

During a war, an impeachable Filipino officer deliberately sides with the enemy and supplies military information to help its forces. Which named ground most directly fits?

**Solution:**

1. The facts describe siding with a wartime enemy and giving it aid.
2. This conduct may support a treason accusation.
3. Proof and a decision through the proper process are still required.
