## Core Concept

### Matching Actions to the Threat

**Species conservation** helps wild plants and animals recover. A **threatened species** risks disappearing from the wild.

Match each action to the exact threat:

- **Habitat clearing:** protect connected habitats and restore food or shelter plants.
- **Overharvesting or illegal trade:** enforce lawful harvest limits so adults reproduce.
- **Toxic waste or runoff:** stop discharge at its source instead of merely widening map lines.

Apply this rule on a tourist beach where sea-turtle nests decline:

1. Check threats: poachers take eggs while resort drains leak soapy water onto sand.
2. Match fixes: patrol nests and pipe resort wastewater into treatment tanks.

Result: Each response targets an active threat directly.

:::pair #control-waste-not-just-boundaries
Right: When harmful waste threatens aquatic wildlife, control the pollution source directly.
Wrong: Expanding habitat boundaries while allowing harmful waste to keep entering the water.
Moving a map line never stops dirty runoff from poisoning stream wildlife.
:::

:::check
River clams are vanishing because gatherers take young clams before they spawn. Should officials widen the park map or enforce size and catch limits?
:::answer
The answer is **Enforce size and catch limits**. Overharvesting is the threat, so lawful limits let clams reproduce.
:::

### Critical Habitats Under RA $9147$

**Republic Act (RA) $9147$** (Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act) protects wild species and habitats. Under **Section $25$**, officials designate **critical habitats** outside NIPAS where threatened species live.

Scientific studies of species presence and threats guide boundaries. Living outside NIPAS never makes harming threatened wildlife lawful.

Apply Section $25$ to a community forest outside NIPAS:

1. Field surveys confirm endangered tarsiers feed and sleep in those trees.
2. Clearing threatens the site, so officials use scientific evidence to declare a critical habitat.

Result: Threatened wildlife gains legal habitat protection without joining NIPAS.

:::pair #critical-habitat-outside-nipas
Right: RA $9147$ allows critical-habitat protection outside NIPAS based on scientific evidence.
Wrong: Claiming habitat protection must wait until a site is included inside NIPAS.
Section $25$ specifically protects threatened-species habitats outside NIPAS boundaries.
:::

:::check
A developer argues clearing endangered orchid trees on private woods is lawful because the site lies outside NIPAS. Sound under RA $9147$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Being outside NIPAS never makes harmful wildlife destruction lawful, and RA $9147$ allows critical habitats there.
:::

### Conservation Breeding and Release Habitats

Under **Section $24$ of RA $9147$**, **conservation breeding or propagation** raises threatened species with human care while protecting or restoring release habitat.

Breeding approvals never let random people capture or release wildlife. Legal permits and scientific health and habitat checks remain mandatory before release.

Test Section $24$ at a hornbill center planning a release:

1. Staff raise healthy chicks under permit and screen them for disease.
2. Partners restore fruiting fig trees and guard nest cavities on the release ridge.

Result: Breeding supports recovery only alongside safe release habitat and required approvals.

:::why
Why does conservation breeding fail when release habitats shrink? Raising young animals fails if they return to bare land without food, shelter, or safe nest trees.
:::

:::pair #breeding-needs-safe-habitat
Right: Pair conservation breeding with release-habitat protection or restoration, permits, and scientific checks.
Wrong: Treating breeding alone as replacing the need to protect and restore release habitat.
Raising animals in cages cannot keep a species alive in destroyed habitat.
:::

:::check
A hobby group hatches threatened turtles without permits and plans to drop them into a marsh slated for draining. Name $2$ missing RA $9147$ steps.
:::answer
The answer is **Legal approvals with scientific checks, and protecting or restoring a safe release habitat**. Breeding never waives permits or habitat care.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Stopping Overharvesting in a Reef Lagoon

Giant reef clams drop fast because divers take every large shell before spawning. A trader suggests banning sales in $1$ market while selling the same catch elsewhere. Will that protect clams?

**Solution:**

1. Shifting sales keeps the same total catch leaving the lagoon.
2. Enforcing lawful catch and size limits directly cuts overharvesting.

Answer: No, officials must enforce lawful harvest limits rather than shift markets.

**Check:** Verify the action lowers total harvest below reproduction rate.

### #2: Outside NIPAS, Still Protected

Scientists document rare bats roosting in limestone caves outside NIPAS, beside a planned quarry. Explain how RA $9147$ applies.

**Solution:**

1. The caves sit outside NIPAS yet host threatened bats facing quarry harm.
2. Under Section $25$, scientific evidence supports critical-habitat designation against harmful use.

Answer: RA $9147$ allows critical-habitat protection outside NIPAS based on scientific evidence.

**Check:** Separate NIPAS boundaries from RA $9147$ critical-habitat authority outside NIPAS.

### #3: Captive-Bred Deer and a Logged Valley

A licensed center breeds threatened deer for release in a valley where charcoal cutters clear thickets. Staff claim the breeding license covers release without extra checks. Evaluate this plan.

**Solution:**

1. Check what Section $24$ of RA $9147$ requires for the valley before release.
2. Check whether a breeding permit removes legal approvals and scientific release checks.

:::try
Finish the last step: evaluate both the release site and the permit claim under RA $9147$.
:::answer
The answer is **The plan fails Section $24$ of RA $9147$**. The team must protect and restore the valley habitat first and get required release approvals and scientific checks.

**Check:** Require both safe release habitat and legal and scientific clearances before release.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Waste threats: stop discharges at the source instead of widening boundaries or breeding more animals. {#control-waste-not-just-boundaries}
- Outside NIPAS: apply RA $9147$ critical-habitat protection with scientific evidence without waiting for NIPAS. {#critical-habitat-outside-nipas}
- Captive breeding: pair breeding with habitat restoration, permits, and scientific checks before release. {#breeding-needs-safe-habitat}
:::
