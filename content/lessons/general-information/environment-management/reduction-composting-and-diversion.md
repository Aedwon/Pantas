## Core Concept

### Source Reduction and Composting

**Source reduction** prevents waste from being made in the first place. Buying bulk refills or skipping unneeded plastic wrappers lowers waste before collection ever starts. Recycling acts after waste exists, whereas source reduction stops waste earlier.

**Composting** uses tiny living things, such as bacteria and fungi, to break down suitable organic matter under controlled conditions into a soil-like product. Only suitable organic scraps like vegetable peels, leaves, and grass fit a compost pile. Sorting scraps into a green bin only starts the process; controlled breakdown must still happen.

Compare $2$ garden-shop practices:

1. A shop switches from individual plastic plant sleeves to bare-root trays, cutting plastic waste before sale.
2. Groundskeepers shred fallen leaves, moisten the pile, and turn it weekly until dark compost forms.

Result: Cutting plastic sleeves at purchase is source reduction, while controlled microbial breakdown of leaves is composting.

:::pair #reduction-prevents-before-use
Right: Identify preventing waste before generation, like cutting excess packaging, as source reduction.
Wrong: Calling recycling source reduction and calling less packaging reuse.
Source reduction avoids creating waste first, whereas reuse and recycling handle items already made.
:::

:::pair #composting-needs-breakdown
Right: Require controlled microbial breakdown of organic matter into soil-like compost to count as composting.
Wrong: Treating sorting organic scraps from other trash as completing composting by itself.
Separation only gathers organic material; microbial decomposition turns it into compost.
:::

:::check
A printing office sets double-sided printing as its default, cutting yearly paper waste by $40$ percent before sheets ever reach a bin. Which waste method is shown?
:::answer
The answer is **Source reduction**. Printing double-sided prevents extra waste paper from being generated in the first place.
:::

### Testing Real Waste Diversion

**Waste diversion** keeps solid waste out of final disposal facilities through **reduction, reuse, recycling, and composting**.

The real test of diversion is simple: **less total waste reaches final disposal**. Bigger trash bins, faster daily trucks, temporary yard storage, and hauling trash to another town's landfill all leave total disposed waste unchanged.

Segregation at source prepares materials for diversion, yet sorting alone never proves diversion. If separated streams are later mixed and landfilled, no waste was diverted.

:::why
Why is measuring total disposed waste the only reliable test of diversion? Extra bins or faster pickup only move waste around unless reduction or recovery keeps tonnage out of landfills.
:::

Test $2$ barangay reports against the diversion rule:

1. Barangay A buys bigger street bins and sends trucks twice daily to the same regional landfill.
2. Barangay B cuts market plastic bags and composts $3$ tons of vegetable trimmings into garden soil.

Result: Barangay B achieves waste diversion by keeping waste from disposal, while Barangay A only moves the same waste faster.

:::pair #diversion-cuts-final-disposal
Right: Verify that less total waste enters final disposal before concluding a program achieved diversion.
Wrong: Claiming diversion occurred when waste is simply carried to a different town's disposal site.
Moving waste across town borders changes where it is buried without keeping it out of disposal.
:::

:::check
A factory adds $4$ color-coded bins on its shop floor, yet ships the exact same $12$ tons of waste to a sanitary landfill each month. Did the bins achieve waste diversion?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Total waste sent to final disposal stayed unchanged, so no diversion occurred.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Cutting Disposable Cups at a Clinic

A clinic stops handing out single-use paper cups at its water cooler and asks staff to bring personal mugs. Which diversion method does stopping single-use cups show?

**Solution:**

1. Stopping cup purchases prevents cup waste before it is generated.
2. Preventing waste generation at the source defines source reduction.

Answer: Source reduction keeps that cup waste from ever entering the disposal stream.

**Check:** Confirm waste generation stopped upfront rather than being sorted or reprocessed later.

### #2: Composting Versus Mere Leaf Collection

A subdivision sacks dry mango leaves and piles them beside an open dump. A neighboring school turns its leaves and kitchen peelings in aerated bins into dark garden soil. Which site performs composting?

**Solution:**

1. Piling leaves by a dump involves no controlled microbial breakdown into useful soil.
2. Turning aerated organic piles so microorganisms break them down into soil-like compost is composting.

Answer: Only the school performs composting and diverts organic waste from disposal.

**Check:** Require controlled biological breakdown into a soil-like product, not mere dumping.

### #3: Evaluating a City Waste Audit

A city waste report lists $2$ items. Food markets sorted vegetable scraps into green bins that were later buried at a neighboring town's landfill. A staff buyer also says buying less packaging is reuse. Evaluate diversion for the food scraps and correct the buyer's term.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether sorting market vegetable scraps before burying them in another town's landfill kept them out of final disposal.
2. Check whether preventing packaging waste at purchase fits reuse or source reduction.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on whether the sorted food scraps achieved diversion and name the buyer's true method.
:::answer
The answer is **The buried food scraps achieved zero diversion, and buying less packaging is source reduction**. Sending sorted scraps to another town's landfill still ends in final disposal, while avoiding packaging prevents waste before use.

**Check:** Test the food scraps against total final disposal and match waste prevention to source reduction.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Source reduction: match preventing waste before generation to reduction rather than confusing it with recycling or reuse. {#reduction-prevents-before-use}
- Composting process: require controlled microbial breakdown into compost rather than stopping at organic sorting. {#composting-needs-breakdown}
- Diversion proof: verify less total waste reaches any disposal site rather than relocating trash to another town. {#diversion-cuts-final-disposal}
:::
