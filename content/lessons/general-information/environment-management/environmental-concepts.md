## Core Concept

The Civil Service Examination's General Information scope includes Environment Management and Protection. Two useful ideas in solid-waste management are segregation at source and waste diversion.

### Segregation at Source

**Segregation at source** means separating waste where it is produced. A home, school, office, or business sorts materials before collection instead of mixing everything together first.

Republic Act $9003$ requires waste to be separated at the source. Containers are identified for compostable, recyclable, non-recyclable, and special waste.

- **Compostable waste** is organic material that can break down through composting, such as food scraps and garden waste.
- **Recyclable waste** is material that can be recovered and processed for useful use, such as clean paper, glass, metal, or other accepted materials.
- **Non-recyclable waste** is material that cannot be recovered through the available recycling or composting process and is not special waste.
- **Special waste** includes household hazardous waste, such as paint, thinner, batteries, and spray canisters. Discarded consumer electronics are also treated as special waste under the law.

Sorting matters because mixed or contaminated materials can become harder to recover. Keeping recyclable and reusable materials separate makes recycling and reuse easier, while special waste can be handled apart from ordinary waste.

### Waste Diversion

**Waste diversion** means keeping solid waste away from final disposal through actions such as reduction, reuse, recycling, and composting. The main question is whether the action reduces the amount that must be disposed of.

**Reduction** means creating less waste at the source. Choosing a refillable container or avoiding unnecessary packaging can reduce the amount of waste produced.

**Reuse** means using a material again for the same or a different purpose without changing its basic physical and chemical form. A glass jar used again as a storage container is reuse.

**Recycling** means processing used material so it can become useful material or a new product. Clean paper collected and processed into recycled paper is an example.

**Composting** is the controlled breakdown of organic matter by microorganisms into a soil-like product. Food scraps and garden waste can be separated for composting instead of being sent for final disposal.

## Examples

### #1: Sorting Waste at Home

A household places food scraps, clean bottles, used batteries, and non-recyclable wrappers in separate containers before collection. Which environmental practice is being used?

**Solution:**

1. The waste is separated where it is produced.
2. The materials are sorted into different waste categories before collection.
3. Therefore, the practice is **segregation at source**.

### #2: Reducing Waste Before It Exists

An office replaces single-use drinking cups with washable cups that employees can use every day. Which waste-diversion method is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The office avoids creating many disposable cups in the first place.
2. The main action is reducing the amount of waste generated.
3. Therefore, the method is **reduction**.

### #3: Using an Item Again

A family washes an empty glass jar and uses it to store dry food. Which waste-diversion method is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The jar is used again without being processed into a new material.
2. The same object gets another useful purpose.
3. Therefore, the method is **reuse**.

### #4: Processing Material for New Use

Clean used paper is collected and sent to a facility that processes it into recycled paper products. Which waste-diversion method is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The used paper is not simply used again in its original form.
2. It is processed so the material can become useful again.
3. Therefore, the method is **recycling**.

### #5: Keeping Organic Waste Out of Disposal

A barangay collects food scraps and garden waste separately and turns them into compost. Which waste-diversion method is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The materials are organic waste that can break down through a controlled process.
2. The process turns them into compost instead of sending them for final disposal.
3. Therefore, the method is **composting**.

### #6: Handling Special Waste

A resident has a used household battery. Which sorting action best follows the waste categories under RA $9003$?

**Solution:**

1. Household batteries are included in special waste.
2. They should be kept separate from ordinary compostable, recyclable, and non-recyclable waste.
3. Therefore, the battery belongs in the **special waste** category for proper handling.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Segregation Is Not Recycling

> Sorting material and processing material are different actions.

Segregation at source separates waste where it is produced. Recycling processes recovered material so it can be used again.

### #2: Reuse Is Not Recycling

> Reuse keeps the item in use without first turning it into new material.

Using a jar again is reuse. Processing waste material into another product is recycling.

### #3: Special Waste Is Not Ordinary Trash

> A used household battery should not be treated like an ordinary wrapper.

Special waste can need different handling because it may contain hazardous materials. Keep it separate from the other waste categories.
