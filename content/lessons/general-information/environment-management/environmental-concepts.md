## Core Concept

The Civil Service Examination's General Information scope includes Environment Management and Protection. The following concepts are useful foundations for that area.

### Principles and Concepts

- **Segregation at Source:** A solid-waste management practice of separating different materials at their point of origin to promote recycling and reuse and reduce the volume of waste for collection and disposal. Under RA 9003, segregation must primarily be conducted at the source and waste is classified into categories such as compostable, recyclable, non-recyclable, and special waste.
- **Waste Diversion:** Activities that reduce or eliminate the amount of solid waste sent to disposal facilities, including reduction, reuse, recycling, and composting.

## Examples

### #1: Meaning of Segregation at Source

A household sorts its kitchen waste, paper, and plastic containers into separate trash bins before garbage collection. Which environmental concept does this practice demonstrate?

**Solution:**
Match the practice to its environmental concept.

1. Segregation at source means separating waste at the location where it is produced instead of waiting for later sorting at a disposal facility.
2. Sorting materials into separate bins at home is separation at the point of origin.
3. Therefore, this practice demonstrates segregation at source.

### #2: Understanding Waste Diversion

A community composting program turns organic waste into soil conditioner, reducing the amount of waste sent to disposal facilities. What environmental management principle does this describe?

**Solution:**
Identify the process of keeping waste out of disposal facilities.

1. Waste diversion includes activities such as reduction, reuse, recycling, and composting that reduce the amount of solid waste sent for disposal.
2. Composting organic waste diverts that material from disposal facilities.
3. Therefore, this process describes waste diversion.
