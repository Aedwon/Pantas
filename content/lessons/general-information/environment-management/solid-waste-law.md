## Core Concept

### Solid Waste Duties Under RA $9003$

Republic Act (RA) $9003$ is the **Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of $2000$**. It requires **segregation at source**, meaning people separate waste where they produce it.

The law prohibits open dumps under Section $37$ and open burning of solid waste under Section $48$. Instead of open dumps, towns build **Materials Recovery Facilities (MRFs)** for sorting and use engineered **sanitary landfills** for remaining residuals.

Environmental laws cover different duties. Match solid waste to RA $9003$, air emissions to RA $8749$, and wastewater permits to RA $9275$. A single site can carry duties under several laws at once.

Compare $2$ town waste plans:

1. Town A closes its open dump, runs a barangay MRF, and requires home sorting.
2. Town B piles mixed garbage in an unlined pit and burns plastic crates beside it.

Result: Town A follows RA $9003$, while Town B breaks the open-dump and open-burning bans.

:::pair #waste-law-not-water-or-climate
Right: Match open dumps and open solid-waste burning directly to RA $9003$ first.
Wrong: Treating a proposed open solid-waste dump as solely a wastewater issue.
Check the material and disposal practice directly before examining secondary water effects.
:::

:::pair #match-subject-to-statute
Right: Match each environmental duty to the statute governing its subject.
Wrong: Assuming every environmental law shares the same legal scope despite its title.
Each law targets specific duties, so one problem can involve multiple statutes.
:::

:::check
A coastal barangay clears land behind its market for an unlined garbage pit and burns wrappers there nightly. Which law directly prohibits both actions?
:::answer
The answer is **RA $9003$**. Under Sections $37$ and $48$, RA $9003$ bans open dumps and open burning of solid waste.
:::

### Sorting Versus Waste Diversion

Sorting waste at home does not finish waste management. **Waste diversion** keeps waste out of final disposal through recovery and similar actions.

- **Reuse:** keeps an item in use without changing its basic form.
- **Recycling:** recovers material for useful use, often through processing.
- **Composting:** turns suitable food scraps and leaves into soil-like compost through controlled breakdown.

Sorting alone never proves waste was diverted. If separated bins are later mixed and buried, zero waste left the disposal stream.

:::why
Why does sorting at home not prove diversion by itself? Sorting only prepares materials, whereas diversion requires keeping them out of final disposal.
:::

Trace $2$ collection routes after household sorting:

1. Route $1$ delivers clean bottles to a glass plant and food scraps to compost beds.
2. Route $2$ dumps sorted bins into $1$ compactor truck bound for disposal.

Result: Route $1$ achieves waste diversion, while Route $2$ shows sorting without diversion.

:::pair #sorting-never-proves-diversion
Right: Count waste as diverted only when reuse, recycling, or composting keeps it out of final disposal.
Wrong: Counting separate waste collection as diversion even when every stream goes to final disposal.
Follow the sorted streams past pickup to verify that recovery actually happened.
:::

:::check
A hospital kitchen puts vegetable peels into green bins and cardboard into blue bins. Workers compost the peels and bury the cardboard in a landfill. Was all sorted waste diverted?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The composted peels were diverted, while the landfilled cardboard was sorted without diversion.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Upgrading a Town Disposal System

A municipality shuts down its roadside open dump, opens an MRF for glass and metal, and sends remaining waste to a sanitary landfill. Which law governs these steps?

**Solution:**

1. Shutting the open dump and building an MRF address solid-waste sorting, recovery, and disposal rules.
2. Match solid-waste systems and open-dump bans to the solid-waste statute.

Answer: RA $9003$, the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of $2000$, governs these steps.

**Check:** Confirm the actions deal with solid-waste sorting, MRFs, and replacing open dumps.

### #2: Reviewing a School Waste Log

A high school sets up $4$ labeled hallway bins. A monthly log shows clean office paper went to a pulping mill, while food scraps and wrappers went to a landfill. What does the log establish?

**Solution:**

1. Using labeled hallway bins where waste is made shows segregation at source.
2. Sending paper to a pulping mill proves recycling diversion, while landfilled scraps and wrappers received no recovery.

Answer: The log shows source segregation across the school and diversion only for the recycled paper.

**Check:** Separate what was sorted at origin from what stayed out of final disposal.

### #3: Warehouse Sorting and Pit Burning

A warehouse sorts aluminum scraps and plastic wrap into separate bins. It ships the aluminum to a smelter and burns the plastic wrap in a backyard pit. Evaluate what was diverted and which law the burning violates.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether shipping aluminum to a smelter keeps that material out of disposal.
2. Check whether sorting plastic wrap before burning it in a pit counts as diversion or violates RA $9003$.

:::try
Finish the last step: state what was diverted and what the pit burning violates under RA $9003$.
:::answer
The answer is **Only the smelted aluminum was diverted, while pit burning violates RA $9003$**. Sorting plastic beforehand never creates diversion, and RA $9003$ prohibits open burning of solid waste.

**Check:** Verify useful recovery for the aluminum and apply Section $48$ to the burning pit.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Solid-waste dumps: match open dumps and solid-waste burning to RA $9003$ first rather than water or climate laws. {#waste-law-not-water-or-climate}
- Statute scope: match each environmental duty to its own statute instead of treating all environmental laws alike. {#match-subject-to-statute}
- Sorting vs. diversion: verify reuse, recycling, or composting after pickup before claiming waste diversion. {#sorting-never-proves-diversion}
:::
