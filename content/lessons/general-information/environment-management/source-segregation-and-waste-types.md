## Core Concept

### Segregation at Source and Keeping Streams Apart

Under **RA $9003$ Sections $2$, $3$, and $21$**, **segregation at source** means separating waste where it is produced. Homes, schools, offices, and shops sort waste before pickup.

Place and timing decide this rule, not who sorts. A hired crew sorting at a school before pickup counts as source segregation. Sorting mixed trash later at a truck, warehouse, or landfill does not.

Once sorted, Section $48$ requires keeping separated streams apart during collection and transport. Mixing sorted bins spoils recovery.

Compare $2$ office setups:

1. Janitors sort paper, peels, and wrappers into labeled bins on each floor before pickup.
2. Staff dump all bins into $1$ bag and pay dump workers to pick out cans later.

Result: The office practices segregation at source, while sorting at the dump misses source segregation.

:::why
Why must waste be separated at origin instead of at a landfill? Mixing wet scraps with clean paper ruins materials before recovery crews ever see them.
:::

:::pair #sort-at-origin-before-collection
Right: Sort waste at the home, school, or office where it is made before pickup.
Wrong: Delaying separation of mixed waste until arrival at a municipal landfill.
Sorting after collection or at a dump happens away from the waste's origin.
:::

:::pair #keep-separated-streams-apart
Right: Keep compostable, recyclable, non-recyclable, and special-waste streams separate during transport.
Wrong: Dumping every pre-sorted household stream into $1$ mixed pile after pickup.
Remixing sorted materials undoes source segregation and ruins recoverable items.
:::

:::check
A market hires $2$ cleaners to sort vegetable peels, dry cartons, and dead bulbs beside the stalls before collection trucks arrive. Source segregation?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Sorting occurs where the market produces waste before pickup, regardless of who sorts it.
:::

### Four Waste Categories Under RA $9003$

RA $9003$ Section $22$ requires separate, labeled bins across $4$ categories:

- **Compostable waste:** suitable food scraps, leaves, and grass broken down by tiny living things like bacteria and fungi into compost.
- **Recyclable waste:** clean paper, glass bottles, and metal cans accepted locally; keep them dry and away from wet food.
- **Non-recyclable waste:** ordinary waste that cannot be recovered locally and is not special waste, such as rejected snack wrappers.
- **Special waste:** household hazardous waste like paint, thinner, spray cans, and used batteries, plus discarded consumer electronics like broken radios.

Never toss batteries or broken electronics into ordinary metal recycling. Even when they hold metal, RA $9003$ classifies them as special waste needing separate handling.

Sort $4$ workshop items:

1. Banana peels go into compostables, and dry cardboard goes into recyclables.
2. Foil chip bags go into non-recyclable waste, and dead AA batteries go into special waste.

Result: Each item enters the bin matching its breakdown, recovery, or special-handling traits.

:::pair #special-waste-stays-separate
Right: Place used batteries and broken electronics in special waste even when they contain metal.
Wrong: Placing any metal-containing item into ordinary recyclables regardless of special-waste status.
Hazardous parts in batteries and electronics require special handling apart from ordinary cans.
:::

:::check
A student drops a cracked portable radio and a thinner can into the recyclable bin because the radio holds copper wires. Right category?
:::answer
The answer is **Special waste**. Under RA $9003$, broken electronics and thinner belong in special waste even with recoverable metal.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Checking a Bakery's Sorting Setup

A coastal bakery weighs $2$ sorting plans. Plan A separates kitchen peels, dry boxes, and foil wrappers at its back prep room before pickup. Plan B loads mixed sacks onto a van for sorting at a municipal transfer station. Which plan fits segregation at source?

**Solution:**

1. Check place and timing: segregation at source separates waste where it is generated before collection.
2. Compare Plan A and Plan B: Plan A sorts at the bakery before pickup, whereas Plan B sorts away from origin after pickup.

Answer: Plan A, sorting in the bakery's prep room before pickup, meets segregation at source.

**Check:** Verify both origin location and pre-collection timing under RA $9003$.

### #2: Sorting Four Items From a Clinic

A clinic finds grass clippings, dry bond paper accepted locally, soiled candy wrappers rejected by recyclers, and empty spray-paint cans. Assign each item under RA $9003$.

**Solution:**

1. Grass clippings break down into compost, and dry accepted bond paper is recyclable.
2. Soiled rejected wrappers are non-recyclable waste, while spray-paint cans are special waste.

Answer: Grass is compostable, bond paper is recyclable, soiled wrappers are non-recyclable, and spray cans are special waste.

**Check:** Match all $4$ Section $22$ categories without putting hazardous cans in recyclables.

### #3: Remixed Bins and Broken Tablets

A barangay collects $4$ sorted household bins, then dumps food scraps, paper, and wrappers into $1$ truck bed to save trips. Workers also drop broken electronic tablets into the metal-can bin because tablets have aluminum cases. Identify the $2$ errors.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether dumping sorted household bins into $1$ truck bed keeps segregation intact.
2. Check whether broken electronic tablets belong in ordinary metal recyclables or special waste.

:::try
Finish the last step: name both errors under RA $9003$ segregation and category rules.
:::answer
The answer is **Dumping sorted bins into $1$ truck bed breaks post-pickup segregation, and broken tablets belong in special waste**. Separated streams must stay apart after pickup, and discarded electronics stay in special waste despite holding metal.

**Check:** Keep streams separate after pickup and route electronics to special waste.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Origin timing: separate waste at its source before pickup instead of at a landfill. {#sort-at-origin-before-collection}
- After pickup: keep separated streams apart during transport instead of remixing sorted bins. {#keep-separated-streams-apart}
- Special waste: keep batteries and electronics out of ordinary metal bins even when they hold metal. {#special-waste-stays-separate}
:::
