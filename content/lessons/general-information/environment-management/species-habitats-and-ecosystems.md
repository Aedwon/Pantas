## Core Concept

### Species, Habitats, and Ecosystems

Ecology separates $3$ connected terms that describe life in nature:

- A **species** is a group of living things of the same kind, such as Philippine eagles or bangus.
- A **habitat** is the place where a living thing gets food, water, shelter, and space, including breeding sites for young.
- An **ecosystem** includes all living things in a place together with nonliving parts like soil, water, and air interacting.

Changing $1$ nonliving part, such as water clarity or temperature, changes which plants and animals can survive there.

Trace how these $3$ terms fit together in a mangrove estuary:

1. Mud crabs and herons are distinct animal species.
2. Mudflats and mangrove roots supply their food, hiding cover, and nesting space (habitat).
3. Crabs, herons, mangroves, brackish water, mud, and tides interact as $1$ estuary ecosystem.

Result: Species name the living kind, habitat names its living place, and ecosystem covers the whole interacting system.

:::pair #species-versus-habitat
Right: A habitat is the place giving food, water, shelter, and space, while a species is a kind of organism.
Wrong: Defining a habitat as a group of living things of the same kind.
A species is the group of organisms, whereas the habitat is the environment supporting them.
:::

:::check
Biologists record frogs, reeds, stream gravel, dissolved oxygen, and water temperature in a mountain creek to see how they affect one another. Are they studying a species alone or an ecosystem?
:::answer
The answer is **An ecosystem**. Combining living frogs and reeds with nonliving gravel, oxygen, and temperature describes an interacting ecosystem.
:::

### How Clearing, Sediment, and Pollution Cause Harm

Wildlife populations can drop even when nobody hunts them. Clearing forests or draining marshes destroys food plants, shelter, and breeding sites, while splitting surviving groups apart.

Plant roots hold soil steady on land and along riverbanks. Removing vegetation lets rain wash loose soil, called **sediment**, into creeks and rivers. Sediment clouds water, blocks sunlight from underwater plants, and covers feeding or breeding gravel.

**Pollution** adds harmful chemicals or waste to air, water, or soil, while overharvesting takes wildlife faster than it can reproduce.

Trace the chain when loggers strip trees from a riverbank above a clear stream:

1. Fewer roots remain to anchor topsoil when heavy monsoon rain falls.
2. Rain washes mud into the stream, turning clear water cloudy brown.
3. Blocked sunlight weakens submerged water plants, and settling mud buries fish spawning beds.

Result: Land clearing reaches the water and harms aquatic life far from the saws.

:::why
Why can wildlife still disappear after hunting stops completely? Animals cannot survive or raise young once clearing removes their food plants, shelter, and connected travel space.
:::

:::pair #hunting-ban-alone-misses-clearing
Right: When clearing removes food and separates groups, protect connected habitat and restore damaged sites.
Wrong: Enforcing only a hunting ban while allowing forest clearing to continue.
Stopping hunting fails to save wildlife if bulldozers remove the forest they need to eat and nest.
:::

:::pair #land-clearing-reaches-water
Right: Stripping riverbank roots sends sediment into water, clouding it and blocking light from plants.
Wrong: Assuming clearing riverbank plants changes only land conditions without affecting river life.
Rain carries loosened soil straight from bare banks into waterways, harming underwater plants and fish.
:::

:::check
Workers scrape away thick shoreline reeds along a shallow lake before the rainy season. How can that shoreline clearing harm fish feeding near underwater grass?
:::answer
The answer is **Soil washes into the lake, clouds the water, blocks light from underwater grass, and buries feeding beds**. Bare banks send sediment straight into aquatic habitats.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Rerouting a Marsh Boardwalk

A town plans a wooden boardwalk beside a marsh where egrets nest in tall reeds. Route A cuts straight through the nesting reeds, while a slightly longer Route B loops around the edge. Both are lawful and safe. Which fits conservation better?

**Solution:**

1. Egrets need undisturbed reed beds as breeding habitat for eggs and chicks.
2. Route B keeps visitor viewing open while keeping foot traffic away from nests.

Answer: Route B better protects the nesting habitat while allowing public visits.

**Check:** Prefer the lawful route that meets access goals without cutting through breeding space.

### #2: When Poaching Stops but Bats Decline

Cave fruit bats enjoy strict protection against hunting, yet colony counts drop each year. Field surveys find orchards and wild fig trees within $2$ kilometers are being cleared for gravel lots. Identify the remaining threat and response.

**Solution:**

1. Enforcing the hunting ban fails to replace lost fruit trees and connected flight paths.
2. Protecting remaining feeding groves and replanting native fruit trees targets the true cause.

Answer: Habitat loss is the remaining threat, requiring protection and restoration of connected feeding habitat.

**Check:** Match the conservation step to the active cause of decline rather than an already solved threat.

### #3: Hillside Grading Above a Coral Bay

Developers scrape grass and shrubs off a steep coastal ridge above a shallow sea-grass bay right before monsoon rains. A foreman claims marine life stays safe since no boats or nets entered the bay. Evaluate his claim.

**Solution:**

1. Trace what happens to loose hillside soil once grass roots are gone and heavy rains arrive.
2. Trace how muddy runoff in the shallow bay affects sunlight, underwater sea grass, and reef fish.

:::try
Finish the last step: evaluate the foreman's claim by tracing the land-to-water impact chain.
:::answer
The answer is **The foreman is wrong**. Bare ridge soil washes into the bay as sediment, clouds the water, blocks sunlight from sea grass, and buries fish feeding spots.

**Check:** Trace eroded soil from bare land into water to catch hidden aquatic harm.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Species versus habitat: define habitat as the living place providing food, water, shelter, and space. {#species-versus-habitat}
- Continuing clearing: protect connected habitat and restore damaged areas rather than relying on hunting bans alone. {#hunting-ban-alone-misses-clearing}
- Shoreline erosion: trace how bare banks send sediment into water and block light underwater. {#land-clearing-reaches-water}
:::
