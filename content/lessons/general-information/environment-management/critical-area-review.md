## Core Concept

### Environmentally Critical Areas Under PD $1586$

Under **PD $1586$ Section $4$**, coverage also depends on site location. An **Environmentally Critical Area (ECA)** is declared because of sensitive site features or hazards.

**Presidential Proclamation $2146$ Section B** lists ECA categories:

- Declared national parks, watershed reserves, wildlife sanctuaries, and habitats of threatened Philippine wildlife.
- Areas with special historical or scientific value and lands traditionally occupied by cultural communities.
- Hazard-prone areas, critical slopes, prime farmland, and **recharge areas of aquifers**. An **aquifer** holds underground water, and a **recharge area** lets surface water enter it.
- Certain water bodies, mangroves, and coral reefs meeting listed conditions, like water tapped for household use.

Never assume every pond or creek shares $1$ status. Officials check the listed conditions for each water feature.

Apply ECA screening to $2$ sites:

1. A warehouse sits over an aquifer recharge area listed in Section B($9$).
2. A shed sits beside an artificial ditch without listed ECA features.

Result: The aquifer recharge site triggers ECA screening, while the plain ditch does not match a listed ECA.

:::pair #water-bodies-need-listed-conditions
Right: Check each water feature against the listed Proclamation $2146$ Section B rules.
Wrong: Giving every water body the same ECA classification as an aquifer recharge area.
Water bodies, mangroves, and reefs count as ECAs only when they meet listed conditions.
:::

:::check
A resort sits beside a mangrove stand and coral reef. Does Proclamation $2146$ limit location-based coverage only to national parks?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section B also covers mangroves, coral reefs, watershed reserves, aquifer recharge areas, and other listed sites.
:::

### Project Type Versus Site Location

**ECP** and **ECA** test $2$ independent things:

- **ECP** asks what type and scale of project is proposed.
- **ECA** asks where the project will sit.

A project does not need to be both an ECP and an ECA to need a check. A major dam can be an ECP outside any ECA. A small non-ECP building inside a watershed reserve or wildlife sanctuary still needs a location-based check. Neither project name nor non-ECP status settles review alone.

:::why
Why check site location separately from project size? Even a small facility can pollute drinking water or harm rare species if placed inside a critical watershed or sanctuary.
:::

Screen $2$ proposals:

1. Proposal A is a small non-ECP storage shed built on steep critical slopes under Section B.
2. Proposal B is a major power plant on dry pasture outside any ECA.

Result: Proposal A needs a location check for critical slopes, while Proposal B triggers ECP review by type.

:::pair #non-ecp-still-needs-site-check
Right: Check the site for ECA status even after finding a facility is not an ECP.
Wrong: Assuming only project type matters once a facility is found not to be an ECP.
Location inside a critical area like a watershed reserve still requires a coverage check.
:::

:::pair #ecp-and-eca-are-independent
Right: Treat ECP project type and ECA location as $2$ independent coverage tests.
Wrong: Requiring a site to be both an ECP and an ECA before checking coverage.
Either ECP type or ECA location can trigger screening on its own.
:::

:::check
A builder plans a small lodge inside a wildlife sanctuary and says no screening applies because small lodges are not ECPs. Sound claim?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A wildlife sanctuary is a listed ECA, so the site still needs a coverage check.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Screening a Small Dock Beside a Reef

A fishing co-op plans a small wooden dock that is not an ECP. Maps place the dock directly beside a living coral reef meeting Proclamation $2146$ conditions. Does non-ECP status end review?

**Solution:**

1. Check project type: the small wooden dock is not an ECP.
2. Check site location: coral reefs meeting listed conditions are ECAs under Proclamation $2146$ Section B.

Answer: No. Because the site touches a listed coral-reef ECA, it still requires a location-based coverage check.

**Check:** Test site location against Section B ECA categories even after ruling out ECP status.

### #2: Town Annex on Traditional Cultural Land

A municipality plans a modest town hall annex on land traditionally occupied by a cultural community. The contractor says EIS coverage depends only on whether the building is heavy industry. How should reviewers reply?

**Solution:**

1. Check Proclamation $2146$ Section B: areas traditionally occupied by cultural communities are listed ECAs.
2. Separate project type from location: sensitive site status triggers coverage screening even for non-industrial buildings.

Answer: Project type alone does not settle coverage because traditional cultural land triggers location-based ECA screening.

**Check:** Verify Section B site status alongside project type instead of checking heavy industry alone.

### #3: Mountain Clinic on Critical Slopes

A province plans a modest clinic, which is not an ECP, on steep critical slopes next to a cultural community's traditional land. The engineer says coverage needs both ECP and ECA status together. He adds that every nearby pond counts as an aquifer recharge area. Evaluate both claims.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether critical slopes and cultural lands trigger ECA checks when the clinic is not an ECP.
2. Check whether coverage requires both ECP and ECA together, and whether every pond shares $1$ class.

:::try
Finish the last step: evaluate the combined ECP-ECA claim and the claim about every nearby pond.
:::answer
The answer is **Both claims are wrong because ECA status triggers a check without ECP status, and water features need listed conditions**. Critical slopes and traditional cultural lands are ECAs alone, while ordinary ponds differ from aquifer recharge areas.

**Check:** Keep ECP and ECA independent and verify specific Section B conditions.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Non-ECP sites: run an ECA location check even when a facility is not an ECP. {#non-ecp-still-needs-site-check}
- Independent tests: trigger checks from either ECP type or ECA location without needing both. {#ecp-and-eca-are-independent}
- Water classifications: verify Section B conditions instead of treating every water body alike. {#water-bodies-need-listed-conditions}
:::
