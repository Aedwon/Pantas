## Core Concept

### Proactive Risk Reduction and NDRRMC

**Republic Act (RA) $10121$** guides Philippine **disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM)**. It shifts focus from reacting after damage to **proactive risk reduction**.

The **NDRRMC** (National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council) coordinates DRRM nationally across $4$ areas: prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and rehabilitation and recovery.

A **hazard** is a dangerous event like a typhoon, flood, or earthquake. A **disaster** causes serious disruption beyond what a community can handle alone.

Compare $2$ towns facing river flood risk before storm season:

1. Town A waits for floodwater before buying boats or planning shelters.
2. Town B maps flood zones, strengthens riverbanks, and stocks gear early.

Result: Town B uses RA $10121$ proactive risk reduction, while Town A stays reactive.

:::why
Why does RA $10121$ stress work before a disaster? Cutting risk and preparing early saves more lives and homes than reacting only after damage hits.
:::

:::pair #proactive-not-reactive
Right: RA $10121$ uses proactive risk reduction across all $4$ areas before a hazard strikes.
Wrong: Waiting for damage before starting all disaster planning.
Reacting after harm skips prevention, mitigation, and readiness that limit losses early.
:::

:::check
A coastal barangay strengthens seawalls and trains volunteers before typhoon season. When a typhoon passes, no homes flood and nobody is hurt. Did the hazard cause a disaster?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A hazard becomes a disaster only when it causes serious disruption beyond local coping capacity.
:::

### Prevention and Mitigation

**Prevention** avoids disaster risk before harm happens. **Mitigation** lessens harm when a hazard cannot be fully avoided.

**Exposure** means people or property sit in a hazard's path. **Vulnerability** means weak walls or poor drainage that make harm more likely.

Safer land-use rules lower exposure by keeping buildings out of danger zones. Stronger building standards and flood works lower vulnerability even when storms still occur.

Test both on a hillside town before rainy season:

1. Officials ban new clinics on steep landslide slopes to lower exposure.
2. Engineers build retaining walls beside roads to lower vulnerability.

Result: Both belong to prevention and mitigation because they cut expected harm early.

:::pair #mitigation-works-with-remaining-hazard
Right: Mitigation lowers expected harm even when the natural hazard can still occur.
Wrong: Claiming mitigation succeeds only when a hazard can no longer occur at all.
A dike or stronger roof cuts damage without stopping typhoons or earthquakes.
:::

:::check
An island town builds concrete seawalls that cut wave damage in half, though strong typhoons still splash over them. Does the seawall count as mitigation?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Mitigation lowers expected damage even when the hazard still occurs.
:::

### Disaster Preparedness

**Preparedness** builds the plans, knowledge, supplies, and skills needed to act safely during emergencies. Drills, early warnings, food stockpiles, and volunteer training all build readiness.

Practicing an evacuation beforehand is preparedness, while evacuating families during rising floodwater is response.

Apply this rule before storm season:

1. Leaders store canned food, water, and flashlights in a safe warehouse.
2. Volunteers practice guiding families from low streets to the gym.

Result: Stockpiling supplies and running drills belong to preparedness because they ready people to act.

:::pair #pre-disaster-purpose-split
Right: Separate pre-disaster work by main purpose: flood works cut harm, while drills build readiness.
Wrong: Calling flood-control construction preparedness simply because workers finish it before typhoon season.
Early timing does not make harm-cutting construction preparedness.
:::

:::check
Before monsoon rains, a city tests river sirens and assigns radio channels so wardens know how to alert families. Which DRRM area fits best?
:::answer
The answer is **Disaster preparedness**. Testing warnings and alert plans readies officials and residents to act.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Lowering Exposure on a Riverbank

A town bans new warehouses in a low valley and strengthens a bridge against fast currents. Which DRRM area do both actions serve?

**Solution:**

1. Banning warehouses keeps property out of the flood zone, lowering exposure.
2. Strengthening the bridge lowers structural vulnerability against water.
3. Both cut expected damage before a flood hits.

Answer: Both serve disaster prevention and mitigation.

**Check:** Confirm each action cuts exposure or vulnerability beforehand.

### #2: Storm Drains and Rooftop Sirens

A barangay clears clogged storm drains to lower flooding and installs sirens to warn families when water rises. Classify each action.

**Solution:**

1. Clearing drains lowers expected flood harm, which is prevention and mitigation.
2. Installing sirens readies residents to act quickly, which is preparedness.

Answer: Clearing drains is prevention and mitigation, and installing sirens is preparedness.

**Check:** Separate cutting physical damage from alerting people to act.

### #3: Bolted Shelves and Rescue Boats

Before typhoon season, a school bolts steel bookcases to walls and stores rescue boats at town hall. Classify both actions by main purpose.

**Solution:**

1. Check what bolting bookcases to walls achieves during a hazard.
2. Check what storing rescue boats ahead of time achieves for responders.

:::try
Finish the last step: classify both pre-disaster actions under the proper DRRM areas.
:::answer
The answer is **Bolting bookcases is prevention and mitigation, and storing boats is preparedness**. Anchoring lowers physical harm, while stockpiling gear readies responders.

**Check:** Match physical harm reduction to mitigation and advance gear to preparedness.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- No early plan: start risk reduction and readiness before damage strikes. {#proactive-not-reactive}
- Hazard remains: count projects as mitigation when they cut harm even if hazards recur. {#mitigation-works-with-remaining-hazard}
- Pre-disaster split: separate harm-cutting works from readiness drills by main purpose. {#pre-disaster-purpose-split}
:::
