## Core Concept

### Wildlife and Critical Habitats Under RA $9147$

**RA $9147$** is the **Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act**. Under Section $2$, it protects wildlife species and their **habitats**, which are the places where species live.

Under Section $25$, RA $9147$ allows DENR to designate **critical habitats** outside existing protected areas where threatened wildlife species are found. Scientific evidence guides critical-habitat selection, and protection stops harmful use or habitat damage there.

Apply Section $25$ to $2$ threatened-bird sites:

1. Biologists document nesting threatened hornbills in municipal woodland outside any national park.
2. DENR uses that scientific evidence to designate the woodland as a critical habitat under RA $9147$.

Result: Threatened species gain habitat protection even outside formal protected-area boundaries.

:::pair #protection-continues-outside-nipas
Right: Use RA $9147$ Section $25$ to protect critical habitats of threatened wildlife outside NIPAS areas.
Wrong: Claiming NIPAS laws prohibit wildlife protection outside designated protected areas.
Protected-area boundaries never erase wildlife protection or critical-habitat tools outside parks.
:::

:::check
Field surveys find an endangered turtle nesting beach along a town coast not listed in any national park law. Which statute allows critical-habitat protection there?
:::answer
The answer is **RA $9147$**. Section $25$ of the Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act covers critical habitats outside existing protected areas.
:::

### Protected Areas Under NIPAS

**RA $7586$** originally established the **National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS)** to conserve natural sites over the long term. **RA $11038$**, the Expanded NIPAS Act, later amended and expanded that protected-area system.

NIPAS sites use management plans with $3$ main tools:

- **Activity zones:** parts inside a protected area with specific rules for allowed use.
- **Buffer zones:** strips outside and beside a protected area managed to shield it from outside harm.
- **Habitat rehabilitation:** repairing damaged vegetation, soil, or water inside the area.

RA $9147$ and the NIPAS laws address different subjects and work together. Wildlife law protects species and critical habitats, while NIPAS establishes and manages designated protected areas.

:::why
Why use both RA $9147$ and NIPAS instead of $1$ law alone? Protected areas guard whole landscapes, while RA $9147$ shields threatened species wherever they live across the country.
:::

Compare $2$ conservation actions:

1. Park managers divide a NIPAS marine park into no-take reef zones and guided dive zones, with a coastal buffer zone outside.
2. Wildlife officers charge poachers caught trapping endangered lizards under RA $9147$.

Result: NIPAS governs protected-area zoning and management, while RA $9147$ governs wildlife protection.

:::pair #ra-eleven-oh-thirty-eight-expanded-nipas
Right: Identify RA $7586$ as the original NIPAS law and RA $11038$ as the law that expanded and amended it.
Wrong: Naming original RA $7586$ as the later law that expanded and amended NIPAS.
RA $7586$ created the system first, and RA $11038$ later expanded and amended it.
:::

:::pair #wildlife-law-distinct-from-nipas
Right: Distinguish RA $9147$ wildlife conservation from NIPAS protected-area creation and management.
Wrong: Treating RA $9147$ and NIPAS laws as identical statutes requiring no separate analysis.
Each statute uses different legal tools, even though both can protect the same ecosystem.
:::

:::check
A $2018$ park briefing cites a law that added protected areas nationwide and updated NIPAS rules originally set in $1992$. Does that briefing describe RA $7586$ or RA $11038$?
:::answer
The answer is **RA $11038$**. RA $7586$ originally established NIPAS, while RA $11038$ (the Expanded NIPAS Act) later amended and expanded it.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Protecting Owls Outside a Park

Researchers confirm a breeding population of threatened owls in watershed trees $15$ kilometers outside the nearest NIPAS park. Which statute lets officials protect that nesting site as a critical habitat?

**Solution:**

1. The site lies outside existing NIPAS protected areas and holds a threatened wildlife species.
2. Section $25$ of the wildlife statute authorizes critical-habitat designation based on scientific data.

Answer: RA $9147$, the Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act, Section $25$ applies.

**Check:** Match critical habitats outside parks for threatened species to RA $9147$.

### #2: Zoning and Buffer Planning

A protected mountain park sets strict no-logging zones on high peaks and marks lower trails for guided hiking. It also restricts spraying on farms bordering the park fence. Which legal system governs this plan?

**Solution:**

1. Internal activity zones and external border buffer zones are protected-area management tools.
2. The national protected-area system was created by RA $7586$ and expanded by RA $11038$.

Answer: NIPAS under RA $7586$, as amended and expanded by RA $11038$, governs this plan.

**Check:** Link internal activity zones and external buffer zones to NIPAS management plans.

### #3: Merging Two Conservation Statutes

A local planner drafts an ordinance claiming RA $7586$ expanded NIPAS in $2018$. The draft also claims RA $9147$ and NIPAS are identical, barring critical habitats outside NIPAS parks. Identify the $2$ legal errors.

**Solution:**

1. Check which statute amended and expanded RA $7586$'s original NIPAS framework.
2. Check whether RA $9147$ critical habitats are limited to NIPAS parks or can sit outside them.

:::try
Finish the last step: correct the NIPAS expansion law and distinguish RA $9147$ critical habitats from NIPAS.
:::answer
The answer is **RA $11038$ expanded NIPAS, and RA $9147$ provides critical-habitat protection outside NIPAS parks**. Wildlife law and NIPAS are distinct tools that complement each other rather than banning protection outside parks.

**Check:** Pair RA $11038$ with NIPAS expansion and RA $9147$ Section $25$ with outside-NIPAS critical habitats.
:::
