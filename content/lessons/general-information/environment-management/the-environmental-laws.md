## Core Concept

Environmental laws cover different duties. Match the action to the law, then check its conditions. A project may have duties under several laws.

### Solid Waste: RA $9003$

Republic Act (RA) $9003$ is the **Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of $2000$**. It requires **segregation at source**, separating waste where it is made. It also prohibits open dumps and open burning of solid waste.

**Waste diversion** keeps waste away from final disposal through recovery and similar actions. Reuse keeps an item in use without changing its basic form. Recycling recovers material for useful use, often through processing.

Composting turns suitable food scraps and leaves into a soil-like product through controlled breakdown. These actions reduce waste sent for disposal. Sorting alone does not prove that any waste was diverted.

### Air Pollution: RA $8749$

RA $8749$ is the **Philippine Clean Air Act of $1999$**. It controls air pollution. Its incineration ban covers burning community, medical, and hazardous waste when the process gives off poisonous and toxic fumes.

The Act states exceptions to this particular ban. They include *siga*, traditional small-scale community burning for sanitation, and traditional burning for farming, culture, health, and food preparation. Crematoria, places where human remains are cremated, are also listed.

An exception under this provision does not cancel other laws. RA $9003$ separately bans open burning of solid waste. Always check which rule the question asks about.

The Act also required existing biomedical-waste incinerators to be phased out within $3$ years of its taking effect. During that transition, only pathological and infectious wastes could be burned under close monitoring. These include body tissues and waste that can spread infection.

That transition was not lasting permission to burn all medical waste. The law calls for safe non-burn treatment. Equipment must meet applicable pollution rules, even if its maker calls it modern.

### Water Pollution: RA $9275$

RA $9275$ is the **Philippine Clean Water Act of $2004$**. It uses water-quality standards, management areas, permits, and monitoring to control pollution.

A **watershed** is land that drains toward a shared outlet. A **river basin** is the land drained by a river and its smaller streams. DENR coordinates with the National Water Resources Board to designate Water Quality Management Areas using such natural boundaries.

Areas with similar water conditions, pollution concerns, or development problems can be grouped. A governing board coordinates policies and checks the area's action plan. This helps places sharing a water system work together.

**Wastewater** is used water that may carry waste. **Effluent** is a discharge flowing from a source, such as a factory. Effluent standards limit the amount, rate, or concentration of pollutants that may be released.

Facilities releasing regulated wastewater need a **discharge permit**. It sets allowed discharge quantity and quality, monitoring duties, and the schedule for meeting requirements. There is no single pollutant limit for every discharge.

A facility must check the standards for its discharge, submit required information, and follow testing and reporting duties. A permit does not allow unlimited pollution.

### Climate Policy: RA $9729$

RA $9729$, the **Climate Change Act of $2009$**, created the Climate Change Commission. The Commission guides and coordinates climate policy. Climate change means lasting changes in climate patterns, not one unusual day.

Original Section $11$ requires a **Framework Strategy on Climate Change**. Section $8$ of RA $10174$ amended it to require a **National Strategic Framework on Climate Change**. Use the name that fits the version asked about.

The framework guides climate planning, research, public information, monitoring, and climate financing. It sets priorities based on climate risks and needs. It helps agencies plan connected work.

**Adaptation** means adjusting to climate effects to reduce harm. **Mitigation**, in climate policy, means reducing human-caused greenhouse gas releases or increasing their removal. Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere.

### Disaster Risk: RA $10121$

RA $10121$ is the **Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of $2010$**. It covers prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and rehabilitation and recovery.

Prevention and mitigation avoid risk or lessen harm from hazards. Preparedness builds readiness through plans and practice. Response meets urgent needs, while rehabilitation and recovery restore and improve community life.

The law renamed the National Disaster Coordinating Council as the **National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC)**. Its work includes earthquake risk as well as climate-related hazards. Disaster mitigation therefore has a wider meaning than cutting greenhouse gases.

### Project Review: PD $1586$

Presidential Decree (PD) $1586$ established the **Environmental Impact Statement System**, or EIS System. It provides environmental review for covered projects before they begin. The review considers likely harm and measures to avoid or reduce it.

A **project proponent** is the person, company, or agency that wants to carry out a project. A covered project must secure an **Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC)** before it starts. This document sets environmental conditions the proponent must follow.

An **Environmentally Critical Project (ECP)** is covered because of its type and scale. Proclamation $2146$ lists specified heavy industries, major mining and quarrying, and major infrastructure projects. The infrastructure list includes major dams, power plants, reclamation projects, roads, and bridges.

Reclamation creates land by filling an area covered by water. Words such as “major” affect coverage. A small path is not automatically a major road.

An **Environmentally Critical Area (ECA)** is identified by features or risks of the place. Listed examples include declared national parks, watershed reserves, wildlife sanctuaries, and habitats of threatened Philippine wildlife. A threatened species faces a risk of disappearing.

The list also covers areas with special historical or scientific value and places traditionally occupied by cultural communities. Other categories include hazard-prone places, critical slopes, prime farmland, and aquifer recharge areas. An aquifer holds and carries underground water; a recharge area is where water enters it.

Certain water bodies, mangrove areas, and coral reefs qualify under listed conditions. A water body tapped for household use is one listed case. Do not assume every pond has the same classification.

ECP asks what kind of project is proposed. ECA asks where it will be located. Officials must apply current coverage and screening rules; neither a project name nor location alone settles every review requirement.

### Wildlife and Protected Areas

RA $9147$ is the **Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act**. It protects wildlife and habitats, the places where species live. It provides for critical habitats outside existing protected areas where threatened species are found.

Scientific evidence guides critical-habitat selection. Protection addresses harmful use or damage that threatens the species there.

RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$, provides the **National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS)**. It supports long-term conservation through protected-area management. Plans can set activity zones and guide habitat repair.

An activity zone has specific rules for use. A buffer zone lies outside and beside a protected area and helps protect it. Wildlife law and NIPAS are different tools that can work together.

### Governing Agencies

The **Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR)** leads national work on the environment and natural resources. Its duties cover conservation, resource use, licensing, and environmental regulation. Regional offices carry out programs and laws in their regions.

The **Environmental Management Bureau (EMB)** belongs to DENR. It handles environmental management and pollution-control work, including environmental-impact review. DENR's work also covers forests, land, and mineral resources.

Local governments share environmental duties. Under RA $9275$, DENR gradually transfers some water-quality duties to local governments or governing boards once it finds they are ready and technically able. This does not automatically give every barangay all national powers.

## Examples

### #1: Matching Solid-Waste Duties

A municipality requires source segregation and expands composting. Which law most directly covers these actions?

**Solution:**

RA $9003$, the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of $2000$, covers these duties.

### #2: Identifying the Council's Law

Which law renamed the National Disaster Coordinating Council as the NDRRMC?

**Solution:**

RA $10121$ renamed the council and strengthened the disaster-risk framework.

### #3: Identifying EMB's Parent Department

Which department includes the Environmental Management Bureau?

**Solution:**

EMB belongs to DENR, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources.

### #4: Matching the Air Rule

Which law contains the toxic-fume incineration prohibition for community, medical, and hazardous waste?

**Solution:**

The prohibition appears in RA $8749$, the Philippine Clean Air Act of $1999$.

### #5: Matching a Water Permit

A factory needs a permit stating its allowed wastewater quantity, quality, and monitoring duties. Which law provides this system?

**Solution:**

RA $9275$, the Philippine Clean Water Act of $2004$, provides the discharge-permit system.

### #6: Matching Climate Policy

Which law created the Climate Change Commission and originally required a Framework Strategy on Climate Change?

**Solution:**

RA $9729$, the Climate Change Act of $2009$, created the Commission and required that strategy.

### #7: Protecting a Critical Habitat

Scientific evidence identifies a threatened species at a site outside existing protected areas. Which law provides for critical-habitat protection there?

**Solution:**

RA $9147$ provides this wildlife-protection tool.

### #8: Managing a Protected Area

Officials prepare activity zones, buffer-zone measures, and habitat repairs for a protected area. Which legal system best matches this work?

**Solution:**

The match is NIPAS under RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$.

### #9: Sorting Without Diversion

An office sorts its waste, but all of it is later mixed and sent for disposal. Does this prove waste diversion?

**Solution:**

1. Sorting where waste is made shows source segregation.
2. All the waste still reached disposal.
3. The facts show segregation but no diversion.

### #10: Checking Project Type

A proposed major dam is outside listed ECAs. Can its project type still bring it under the EIS System?

**Solution:**

1. Major dams are listed ECPs.
2. ECP coverage concerns project type, so location outside an ECA does not remove that basis for review.

### #11: Checking Project Location

A project is not an ECP, but its site is a declared wildlife sanctuary. Does that settle that no review is needed?

**Solution:**

1. A wildlife sanctuary is a listed ECA.
2. Officials must also check location-based coverage and screening rules.
3. Absence of an ECP classification alone does not settle the review requirement.

### #12: Checking Two Separate Duties

An EIS-covered factory will release regulated wastewater. Its owner says the ECC replaces its discharge permit. Is the claim correct?

**Solution:**

1. The ECC follows project review under the system established by PD $1586$.
2. A discharge permit regulates wastewater under RA $9275$.
3. The claim is incorrect. The required discharge permit remains a separate duty.

### #13: Coordinating River Protection

Several towns share a river basin and water-pollution problems. Which Clean Water Act tool supports joint planning and monitoring?

**Solution:**

1. A Water Quality Management Area can group places with shared water concerns.
2. Its governing board coordinates policies and checks progress on the area's action plan.

### #14: Applying a Burning Exception

A claim says traditional burning listed as an exception in RA $8749$ is allowed under every environmental law. Is the claim sound?

**Solution:**

1. The exception applies to a particular provision of RA $8749$.
2. Other rules still apply, including RA $9003$ where it prohibits open burning of solid waste.
3. The exception does not establish permission under every law.

### #15: Comparing Disaster Actions

A town practices evacuation before typhoon season and later rescues residents during a flood. Which DRRM area does each action show?

**Solution:**

1. Practice builds readiness, so it shows preparedness.
2. Rescue meets urgent needs, so it shows response.
3. Both belong to the framework under RA $10121$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Match the duty before choosing the number. Solid waste points to RA $9003$, air pollution to RA $8749$, and wastewater permits to RA $9275$.

Keep the bodies separate: the Climate Change Commission belongs with RA $9729$, and the NDRRMC with RA $10121$. ECP, ECA, and ECC belong with project review under PD $1586$.

Wildlife and critical habitats point to RA $9147$. NIPAS points to RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$. Always read the conditions after making the first match.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Saying the Council Was Created From Nothing

> RA $10121$ renamed an existing council.

The NDCC became known as the NDRRMC. The law also made wider changes to disaster-risk management.

### #2: Ignoring Other Burning Rules

> An exception in one provision does not cancel another law.

Read the waste type, toxic-fume condition, and statutory exceptions. The old medical-waste transition period was not permanent permission.

### #3: Mixing Up Framework Names

> Check whether the question asks about the original or amended law.

Original RA $9729$ uses Framework Strategy on Climate Change. RA $10174$ amended the provision to use National Strategic Framework on Climate Change.

### #4: Treating an ECC as Every Permit

> Project clearance does not replace all other permits.

An ECC and a wastewater discharge permit serve different duties. A covered project must meet both requirements when both apply.

### #5: Confusing ECP With ECA

> Project type and location are different coverage tests.

A major dam can be an ECP outside an ECA. A project in an ECA still needs a coverage check even if it is not an ECP.

### #6: Using One Wastewater Limit for Everything

> Check the standards for the particular discharge.

A permit sets conditions and monitoring duties. It does not allow any amount of any pollutant.

### #7: Treating Critical Habitat as a NIPAS Category

> Wildlife law and protected-area law use different tools.

RA $9147$ provides for critical habitats outside existing protected areas. NIPAS governs the protected-area system under RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$.
