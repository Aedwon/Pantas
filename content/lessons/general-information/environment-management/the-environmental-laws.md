## Core Concept

Environmental laws cover different problems and duties. Read what is being regulated before choosing a law or agency. A project may need to follow several laws.

### Solid Waste: RA $9003$

Republic Act (RA) $9003$ is the **Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of $2000$**. **Segregation at source** means separating waste where it is produced, such as at home or in an office. The law also prohibits open dumps and open burning of solid waste.

**Waste diversion** means keeping waste away from disposal facilities through reuse, recycling, composting, and similar actions. Composting turns suitable organic waste, such as food scraps and leaves, into a soil material. Recycling processes discarded materials into useful materials or products.

Sorting and diversion are different. Sorting separates materials so they can be handled properly. Diversion reduces the waste that goes to final disposal.

### Air Pollution: RA $8749$

The **Philippine Clean Air Act of $1999$** controls air pollution. Its incineration ban covers burning municipal, biomedical, and hazardous waste when the process gives off poisonous and toxic fumes. Municipal waste includes ordinary community waste, while biomedical waste comes from medical activities.

The law states exceptions to this particular ban. These include traditional small-scale community sanitation burning called *siga*. It also lists traditional burning for farming, culture, health, and food preparation, plus crematoria, where human remains are cremated.

These exceptions do not remove duties under other laws. RA $9003$ separately prohibits open burning of solid waste. Check which rule a question asks about.

RA $8749$ called for existing biomedical-waste incinerators to be phased out within $3$ years of its taking effect. During that period, burning was limited to pathological and infectious wastes under close DENR monitoring. This was a temporary rule, not continuing permission for all medical-waste burning.

DENR means the Department of Environment and Natural Resources. The Act calls for safe, environmentally sound non-burn methods of waste treatment. Any treatment must meet the pollution rules that apply; calling equipment “modern” does not prove that its emissions are lawful.

### Water Pollution: RA $9275$

The **Philippine Clean Water Act of $2004$** manages water pollution. Its tools include water-quality standards, Water Quality Management Areas, discharge permits, and monitoring. They link pollution control with the condition of the receiving water.

A **watershed** is land where water drains toward a shared outlet. A **river basin** is the land drained by a river and its smaller streams. DENR uses these and other water-resource boundaries to identify Water Quality Management Areas.

Areas may be grouped when they share water conditions, pollution concerns, or development problems. Each management area has a governing board with government and other sector representatives. The board coordinates policies and checks progress on the area's action plan.

**Wastewater** is used water that may contain wastes. **Effluent** is wastewater flowing out of a source, such as a factory or treatment plant. **Effluent standards** set limits on pollutants in that discharge.

Facilities releasing regulated wastewater must secure a discharge permit. It states the allowed amount and quality of discharge, the schedule for meeting requirements, and monitoring duties. There is no single pollutant limit for every kind of discharge.

A facility must identify which standards apply and provide the required information. It must meet permit conditions and carry out testing and reporting. A permit allows discharge within stated limits, not unlimited pollution.

### Climate Policy: RA $9729$

The **Climate Change Act of $2009$** created the Climate Change Commission. The Commission guides and coordinates government climate policy. Climate change means long-term changes in climate patterns, not one unusual day.

Section $11$ of the original RA $9729$ required a **Framework Strategy on Climate Change**. Section $8$ of RA $10174$ amended that provision. The amended text calls for a **National Strategic Framework on Climate Change**.

The framework guides planning, research, public information, and checks on climate action. It uses evidence about risks to set priorities. These include reducing climate harm and reducing human-caused greenhouse gas releases.

Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere. Adjusting to climate effects is called **adaptation**. Reducing human-caused gas releases or increasing their removal is called **mitigation**.

### Disaster Risk: RA $10121$

The **Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of $2010$** sets the country's disaster-risk framework. It covers prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and rehabilitation and recovery. Here, mitigation means lessening harm from hazards, not only cutting greenhouse gases.

**Preparedness** includes plans and practice before a disaster. **Response** addresses urgent needs when one occurs. Recovery helps restore services and living conditions afterward.

The law renamed the existing National Disaster Coordinating Council as the **National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC)**. Disaster-risk work also covers hazards such as earthquakes. Its scope is wider than climate policy.

### Environmental Review: PD $1586$

Presidential Decree (PD) $1586$ established the **Philippine Environmental Impact Statement System**, or EIS System. It requires environmental review of covered projects before they begin. The review checks likely effects and ways to prevent or reduce harm.

A **project proponent** is the person, company, or agency that wants to carry out a project. A covered project must secure an **Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC)** through the required review. This decision document records environmental clearance and conditions the proponent must follow.

Coverage depends on official project and location rules. Not every small activity needs the same review. The proponent must check the coverage and screening rules instead of deciding from the project name alone.

### Environmentally Critical Projects and Areas

An **Environmentally Critical Project (ECP)** is identified by project type and scale. Proclamation $2146$ lists specified heavy industries and major mining and quarrying projects. Its list also includes major dams, power plants, reclamation projects, roads, and bridges.

**Reclamation** creates usable land by filling an area covered by water. Words such as “major” matter when checking coverage. A small path is not automatically a major road.

An **Environmentally Critical Area (ECA)** is identified by a location's special features or risks. Proclamation $2146$ includes national parks, watershed reserves, wildlife sanctuaries, and habitats of threatened Philippine wildlife. It also lists places with special historical, cultural, or scientific value and places traditionally occupied by cultural communities.

Other listed areas face hazards such as floods, typhoons, or earthquakes. Areas with critical slopes have steep land that needs special care. Prime agricultural land is land especially suited for farming.

An **aquifer** is an underground layer that holds and carries water. An aquifer recharge area is where water enters that layer. Recharge areas are also listed as ECAs.

The proclamation includes certain water bodies, mangrove areas, and coral reefs under stated conditions. For example, use as a drinking-water source is a listed water-body condition. Do not assume every pond has the same legal classification.

An ECP can be covered because of its type even outside an ECA. A project that is not an ECP can still need review because of its location, subject to coverage rules. ECP asks “what project?” while ECA asks “what place?”

### Wildlife and Protected Areas

RA $9147$, the **Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act**, protects wildlife and habitats. A **habitat** is where a plant or animal gets what it needs to live. A **threatened species** faces a risk of disappearing.

The law provides for **critical habitats** outside existing protected areas where threatened species are found. Scientific evidence guides site selection. Protection addresses harmful use or damage that threatens the species there.

RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$, provides the **National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS)**. Protected areas receive long-term management for conservation. Plans can set activity zones, care for nearby buffer zones, protect habitats, and repair damaged habitats.

An activity zone is a part of an area with specific rules for use. A buffer zone is outside and beside a protected area and helps protect it from harm. RA $9147$ and NIPAS provide different legal tools that can support conservation together.

### Governing Agencies

The **Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR)** is the main national department for the environment and natural resources. Its duties include conserving resources, controlling pollution, and setting and enforcing environmental standards. Regional offices carry out laws and programs in their regions.

The **Environmental Management Bureau (EMB)** is part of DENR. It handles major pollution-control and environmental-impact work, including the EIS System. DENR's wider work covers natural resources beyond pollution control.

Local governments also have duties under laws such as RA $9003$ and RA $9275$. Under RA $9275$, DENR must gradually transfer some water-quality duties to local governments or governing boards once DENR finds they are ready and have the technical skills to carry out those duties. National duties do not automatically pass to every barangay.

## Examples

### #1: Matching Solid-Waste Duties

A municipality requires households to sort waste where it is produced. It also expands recycling and composting. Which law most directly covers these actions?

**Solution:**

1. The actions involve source segregation and waste diversion.
2. They fall under RA $9003$, the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of $2000$.

### #2: Identifying the Disaster-Risk Law

Which law renamed the National Disaster Coordinating Council as the NDRRMC and set the disaster-risk reduction and management framework?

**Solution:**

RA $10121$, the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of $2010$, made that change.

### #3: Identifying EMB's Parent Department

Which national department includes the Environmental Management Bureau?

**Solution:**

EMB is part of DENR, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources.

### #4: Identifying the Air-Pollution Rule

A waste-burning process emits poisonous and toxic fumes. Which law contains the conditional incineration ban for municipal, biomedical, and hazardous wastes?

**Solution:**

1. The case concerns air pollution and the defined incineration ban.
2. The rule appears in RA $8749$, the Philippine Clean Air Act of $1999$.

### #5: Matching Water Regulation

A factory must obtain a document stating how much regulated wastewater it may release, its allowed quality, and monitoring duties. Which law provides this permit system?

**Solution:**

1. The document is a wastewater discharge permit.
2. RA $9275$, the Philippine Clean Water Act of $2004$, provides this system.

### #6: Matching Climate Policy

Which law created the Climate Change Commission and originally required a Framework Strategy on Climate Change?

**Solution:**

RA $9729$, the Climate Change Act of $2009$, created the Commission and required that strategy.

### #7: Applying Wildlife Protection

A threatened species lives in an important site outside an existing NIPAS protected area. Scientific evidence shows that human activity threatens its survival. Which law provides for critical-habitat protection there?

**Solution:**

1. The site needs habitat protection for threatened wildlife outside an existing protected area.
2. RA $9147$ provides the critical-habitat tool for this case.

### #8: Matching Protected-Area Management

Officials use a long-term plan to zone activities, manage buffer zones, and repair habitats in a protected area. Which legal system best matches these duties?

**Solution:**

1. The duties concern the formal protected-area system and its plans.
2. The match is NIPAS under RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$.

### #9: Distinguishing Sorting from Diversion

An office sorts paper, food scraps, and other waste into separate bins. All of it is later mixed and sent to the same disposal facility. Does sorting alone show that waste was diverted?

**Solution:**

1. Separating waste at the office is segregation at source.
2. Diversion keeps waste from final disposal through reuse, recycling, composting, or similar actions.
3. The facts show sorting but no diversion. All the waste still went to disposal.

### #10: Recognizing an ECP

A proposed major dam is outside all listed ECAs. Why can it still fall under the EIS System?

**Solution:**

1. ECA coverage concerns location.
2. Major dams are listed ECP types under Proclamation $2146$.
3. The project can be covered because of its type even outside an ECA.

### #11: Recognizing an ECA

A proposed project is not an ECP, but its site is a declared wildlife sanctuary. Does the absence of an ECP classification alone settle whether environmental review is needed?

**Solution:**

1. A wildlife sanctuary is a listed ECA.
2. Project type is not the only basis for coverage.
3. Officials must check location-based coverage and screening rules before deciding the review requirement.

### #12: Distinguishing an ECC from a Discharge Permit

A factory project is covered by the EIS System and will release regulated wastewater. Its owner claims that an ECC removes the need for a discharge permit. Is the claim correct?

**Solution:**

1. The ECC follows environmental review under the EIS System established by PD $1586$.
2. A discharge permit controls wastewater amount and quality under RA $9275$.
3. The documents serve different duties. An ECC does not replace the required discharge permit.

### #13: Coordinating River Protection

Several towns drain into the same river basin and share water-pollution problems. Which Clean Water Act tool can support coordinated planning and monitoring?

**Solution:**

1. The problem crosses town boundaries within a shared water system.
2. A Water Quality Management Area can group places with shared water conditions and concerns.
3. Its governing board coordinates policies and checks the area's action plan.

### #14: Applying an Exception Carefully

A statement says that traditional burning listed as an exception in RA $8749$ is automatically allowed under every environmental law. Is that conclusion sound?

**Solution:**

1. The exception concerns a particular incineration ban in RA $8749$.
2. Other rules still apply, including RA $9003$'s open-burning prohibition where applicable.
3. The exception does not establish that the activity is lawful under every law.

### #15: Distinguishing Preparedness from Response

A town practices evacuation before typhoon season. During a later flood, it sends rescue teams to trapped residents. Which disaster-risk phase does each action show?

**Solution:**

1. The practice builds readiness before a disaster. It is preparedness.
2. Rescue meets urgent needs during the flood. It is response.
3. Both fit the disaster-risk framework under RA $10121$.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

Match the main duty first. Sorting and diversion point to RA $9003$, air pollution to RA $8749$, and wastewater permits to RA $9275$. Then check the conditions in the question.

Keep policy bodies separate. The Climate Change Commission points to RA $9729$. The NDRRMC points to RA $10121$.

For project review, ECP concerns project type and ECA concerns location. ECC is the decision document after the required review. These belong with the EIS System under PD $1586$.

Wildlife and critical habitats point to RA $9147$. The NIPAS protected-area system points to RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Saying the NDRRMC Was Created from Nothing

> RA $10121$ renamed an existing council.

The National Disaster Coordinating Council became known as the NDRRMC. The law also made wider changes to disaster-risk management.

### #2: Treating One Burning Rule as Every Burning Rule

> Read the waste type, toxic-fume condition, and stated exceptions.

RA $8749$ contains a specific conditional ban. Its exceptions do not cancel restrictions under other laws.

### #3: Mixing Up Climate Framework Names

> Check whether the question asks about the original or amended law.

RA $9729$ originally used Framework Strategy on Climate Change. RA $10174$ amended Section $11$ to use National Strategic Framework on Climate Change.

### #4: Treating an ECC as Every Permit

> An ECC does not end the project's environmental duties.

A covered project must follow its clearance conditions and other laws. A required wastewater discharge permit remains a separate duty.

### #5: Confusing ECP and ECA

> Project type and location are different coverage tests.

A major dam may be an ECP outside an ECA. A project in an ECA needs a coverage check even if it is not an ECP.

### #6: Using One Wastewater Limit for Everything

> Use the standards that apply to the particular discharge.

Check permit limits and monitoring duties. A permit does not allow any amount of any pollutant.

### #7: Treating Critical Habitat as a NIPAS Category

> Wildlife protection and protected-area management use different legal tools.

RA $9147$ provides for critical habitats outside existing protected areas. NIPAS is the protected-area system under RA $7586$, as amended by RA $11038$.
