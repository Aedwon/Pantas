## Core Concept

Republic Act $10121$ supports a broad approach to **disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM)**. DRRM is not only about helping people after a disaster. It also includes actions that reduce risk before a disaster happens.

The National DRRM Plan groups work into four connected areas. When a question gives an action, look at the action's main purpose.

### Disaster Prevention and Mitigation

**Disaster prevention and mitigation** means avoiding disaster risk when possible and reducing the harmful effects of hazards. It focuses on lowering exposure and vulnerability before a disaster.

Examples include safer land-use rules, stronger building standards, flood-control works, and other measures that lessen possible damage. These actions do not guarantee that a hazard will disappear. Their goal is to reduce the chance or size of harm.

### Disaster Preparedness

**Disaster preparedness** means getting people and systems ready before an emergency. It is based on knowing the risks and planning what to do if a hazard threatens a community.

Preparedness includes training, drills, emergency plans, stockpiling supplies, public information, and warning or forecasting systems. The key idea is readiness.

### Disaster Response

**Disaster response** means actions taken during or immediately after a disaster to protect life and meet urgent needs. Search and rescue, evacuation, emergency shelter, food, water, and medical help are response actions.

Response is about the immediate emergency. Long-term rebuilding belongs to rehabilitation and recovery.

### Disaster Rehabilitation and Recovery

**Disaster rehabilitation and recovery** means restoring and improving the lives, livelihoods, facilities, and services of affected communities after the immediate emergency. Recovery should also reduce future disaster risk when rebuilding.

This can include repairing roads, restoring water service, helping livelihoods restart, and rebuilding damaged facilities more safely. The goal is not only to return to normal, but to recover in a safer way.

### How the Four Areas Work Together

The four areas are connected. Prevention and mitigation reduce risk, preparedness builds readiness, response deals with urgent needs, and rehabilitation and recovery restore communities afterward.

A single program can support more than one area. In an exam question, choose the area that best matches the action's main purpose.

## Examples

### #1: Reducing Risk Before a Disaster

A city strengthens building rules so new public buildings can better withstand earthquakes. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The action happens before a disaster.
2. Its main purpose is to reduce possible damage from a hazard.
3. Therefore, it is **disaster prevention and mitigation**.

### #2: Practicing Before an Emergency

A school holds an earthquake drill and teaches students where to evacuate. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The drill does not remove the earthquake hazard.
2. It prepares people to act correctly if an earthquake happens.
3. Therefore, it is **disaster preparedness**.

### #3: Meeting Urgent Needs

After a typhoon, rescue teams move stranded families to an evacuation center and provide emergency food and water. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The actions happen during the immediate emergency.
2. Their purpose is to protect life and meet urgent needs.
3. Therefore, they are part of **disaster response**.

### #4: Rebuilding After the Emergency

A damaged health center is rebuilt after a disaster using safer construction standards. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The immediate emergency has already passed.
2. The action restores a damaged facility and makes it safer for future hazards.
3. Therefore, it is **disaster rehabilitation and recovery**.

### #5: DRRM Is More Than Response

A local government maps risk areas, conducts evacuation drills, operates rescue teams during disasters, and repairs damaged public facilities afterward. Which statement best describes this approach?

**Solution:**

1. Risk reduction and drills happen before a disaster.
2. Rescue happens during the emergency, while repairs happen afterward.
3. The program therefore covers several DRRM areas instead of focusing only on post-disaster response.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Prevention and Mitigation vs. Preparedness

> Reducing possible damage is not the same as practicing what to do.

A safer building standard mainly reduces possible harm, so it fits prevention and mitigation. An evacuation drill mainly builds readiness, so it fits preparedness.

### #2: Response vs. Recovery

> Urgent relief and long-term rebuilding belong to different areas.

Rescue, evacuation, and emergency aid are response actions. Restoring livelihoods, roads, and facilities after the emergency belongs to rehabilitation and recovery.

### #3: DRRM Starts Before Disaster

> Do not treat DRRM as work that begins only after damage occurs.

Modern DRRM includes risk reduction and preparedness before a disaster, response during the emergency, and recovery afterward.
