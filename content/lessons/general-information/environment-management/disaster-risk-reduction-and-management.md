## Core Concept

Republic Act $10121$ supports **disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM)**. This means organized work to reduce disaster harm and help communities cope. It includes work before, during, and after emergencies.

A **hazard** is something that can cause harm, such as a flood or earthquake. A **disaster** seriously disrupts a community and causes losses beyond what it can handle with its own resources. A hazard does not always lead to a disaster.

### Disaster Prevention and Mitigation

**Prevention** aims to avoid disaster risk. **Mitigation** aims to lessen harm when risk cannot be fully avoided. Safer land-use rules, stronger buildings, and flood-control works can serve these aims.

**Exposure** means people, buildings, or other things are in a place where a hazard may strike. **Vulnerability** means conditions that make them more likely to suffer harm. A weak house on a floodplain can have both high exposure and high vulnerability.

Keeping new homes out of a dangerous flood zone can reduce exposure. Strengthening a building can reduce its vulnerability. Neither action stops rain or earthquakes from occurring.

### Disaster Preparedness

**Preparedness** means building the knowledge, plans, and skills needed to act during an emergency. It gets people and systems ready to protect lives.

Examples include evacuation drills, training rescue teams, preparing emergency supplies, and planning how warnings will reach families. The main purpose is readiness. Practicing an evacuation is different from carrying one out during a real threat.

### Disaster Response and Early Recovery

**Response** means action during or immediately after a disaster to save lives and meet urgent needs. Rescue, emergency medical care, temporary shelter, food, and drinking water fit here.

Early recovery helps restore basic services and ways to earn a living while emergency work continues. The updated national plan groups response with early recovery. This does not mean all rebuilding is emergency response.

### Disaster Rehabilitation and Recovery

**Rehabilitation and recovery** means restoring and improving damaged facilities, livelihoods, and living conditions. A livelihood is a person's way of earning a living.

Rebuilding a damaged health center and helping businesses restart can support recovery. Rebuilding should also reduce future risk. Restoring the same unsafe structure leaves people open to harm again.

### Choosing the Best Area

The areas can overlap. Look at what an action mainly does: reduce risk, build readiness, meet urgent needs, or restore community life.

Timing helps, but timing alone is not enough. Stronger building rules and evacuation drills both happen before disasters. Their main purposes differ.

## Examples

### #1: Reducing Possible Damage

A city requires new public buildings to withstand earthquake shaking better. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The rule makes buildings less likely to suffer damage.
2. Its main purpose is to reduce harm from a hazard.
3. The area is **disaster prevention and mitigation**.

### #2: Practicing an Evacuation

A school holds an earthquake drill and practices moving students to a safe assembly area. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. The school is practicing before a real emergency.
2. The drill builds knowledge and readiness.
3. The area is **disaster preparedness**.

### #3: Meeting Urgent Needs

After a typhoon, rescue teams move stranded families to emergency shelter and provide food and water. Which DRRM area is best shown?

**Solution:**

1. Families need immediate protection and basic supplies.
2. The teams are saving lives and meeting urgent needs.
3. These actions are **disaster response**.

### #4: Rebuilding More Safely

After the emergency has passed, a damaged health center is rebuilt using safer construction standards. Which DRRM area best describes the rebuilding?

**Solution:**

1. The project restores a damaged community service.
2. Safer construction also reduces future risk.
3. The main task is **disaster rehabilitation and recovery**. It also supports mitigation.

### #5: Comparing Two Actions Before a Flood

A town keeps new housing away from a high-risk flood zone. It also trains volunteers to manage evacuation centers. Do both actions belong mainly to preparedness?

**Solution:**

1. Keeping homes away from danger reduces exposure. It mainly shows prevention and mitigation.
2. Training volunteers builds readiness. It mainly shows preparedness.
3. Both happen before an emergency, but they serve different main purposes.

### #6: A Program Beyond Relief

A mayor proposes spending the entire disaster program on relief goods after floods. Another proposal includes safer land use, drills, rescue, and repairs to damaged services. Which proposal better reflects the broad DRRM approach?

**Solution:**

1. Relief goods meet urgent needs through response.
2. The second proposal also reduces risk, builds readiness, and supports recovery.
3. The second proposal covers the broader DRRM approach under RA $10121$.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Treating All Work Before Disaster as Preparedness

> Ask what the action mainly achieves.

Stronger building rules mainly reduce possible damage. Drills mainly prepare people to act. Both can happen before a disaster.

### #2: Drawing a Sharp Line Between Response and Recovery

> Recovery work can begin while emergency aid continues.

Urgent rescue and emergency food are response actions. Restoring basic services can begin early, while lasting rebuilding supports rehabilitation and recovery. Use the action's main purpose to choose the best area.

### #3: Starting DRRM Only After Damage

> Disaster-risk work includes action before an emergency.

A relief-only program leaves out risk reduction and preparedness. DRRM also includes recovery that helps communities face future hazards more safely.
