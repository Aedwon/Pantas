## Core Concept

### Adaptation: Adjusting to Climate Harm

**Adaptation** means adjusting to actual or expected climate effects to reduce harm or make use of helpful changes. It helps people, buildings, and farms cope with heat, drought, rising seas, or stronger storms.

Common adaptation steps include raising floors above floodwater, storing water for dry months, planting drought-tolerant crops, and adding shade trees along hot streets. Adaptation reduces climate harm without necessarily cutting greenhouse gases.

Test how adaptation works on an island school facing longer dry seasons:

1. The school builds covered rainwater cisterns before the dry months arrive.
2. Students use the stored water for drinking and handwashing when wells run low.

Result: Rainwater storage is adaptation because it cuts expected harm from dry-season shortages.

:::why
Why does storing rainwater count as adaptation rather than mitigation? Water storage helps people cope with drier weather, whereas mitigation targets greenhouse gas releases or carbon storage.
:::

:::pair #water-storage-is-adaptation
Right: Adaptation reduces harm from expected climate effects, such as storing water for dry periods.
Wrong: Calling water storage mitigation on the claim that every water project removes greenhouse gases.
Water cisterns prepare residents for shortages without removing greenhouse gases from the air.
:::

:::check
A coastal barangay clinic builds concrete flood gates around its backup generator room before storm season. Is that adaptation or mitigation?
:::answer
The answer is **Adaptation**. Flood gates protect the clinic's power from expected storm surges and flooding.
:::

### Mitigation and Sorting Mixed Actions

**Mitigation** means reducing human-caused greenhouse gas releases or increasing their removal from the air. **Carbon** is an element found in living things and in carbon dioxide, a major heat-trapping gas.

Saving electricity, switching to cleaner energy, and protecting forests that lock away carbon all support mitigation. A single site can run adaptation and mitigation projects side by side, and still suffer social or economic effects.

Always classify each item by its **main stated goal**:

1. **Action reducing climate harm:** adaptation.
2. **Action cutting emissions or locking carbon:** mitigation.
3. **Harm to health, homes, or schooling:** social effect.
4. **Harm to earnings, output, or repair costs:** economic effect.

Sort a town hall upgrade that pairs solar panels with an elevated emergency exit:

1. Solar panels lower fossil-fuel power use and cut greenhouse gas releases (mitigation).
2. The elevated exit keeps staff safe during coastal floods (adaptation).

Result: One building hosts both mitigation and adaptation because each feature has a different stated goal.

:::pair #forest-carbon-is-mitigation
Right: Protecting a forest specifically to keep its stored carbon out of the air serves mitigation.
Wrong: Labeling a forest carbon project as adaptation on the belief every forest project targets floods.
Read the stated purpose: keeping carbon stored limits greenhouse gas releases, which is mitigation.
:::

:::pair #separate-action-from-effect
Right: Classify each action by its purpose and each effect by whether it hits welfare or finances.
Wrong: Giving both actions and both effects the same label simply because they involve one public building.
Sharing a building never merges emission cuts with flood protection or repair bills with missed classes.
:::

:::check
A town bakery replaces smoky diesel ovens with efficient electric ovens powered by rooftop solar panels to cut emissions. What is the main climate goal?
:::answer
The answer is **Mitigation**. Reducing fossil-fuel electricity and emissions lowers greenhouse gases released into the air.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Cool Roofs and LED Streetlights

A municipality coats market roofs with reflective white paint to lower indoor heat for vendors. It also switches $400$ old streetlights to LED bulbs to cut power-plant fuel emissions. Classify both actions.

**Solution:**

1. Reflective white paint helps vendors cope with extreme heat, so its main purpose is adaptation.
2. LED streetlights reduce electricity demand and emission releases, so their main purpose is mitigation.

Answer: Cool roofs support adaptation, and LED streetlights support mitigation.

**Check:** Ask whether each step shields people from climate harm or reduces greenhouse gases.

### #2: Mangrove Belt Protection

A coastal town protects an old mangrove forest specifically to lock carbon in its deep mud and roots. Beside the shore, it builds raised timber walkways so pupils can walk safely at high tide. Classify each action.

**Solution:**

1. Protecting mangroves to hold stored carbon targets greenhouse gases, which is mitigation.
2. Building raised walkways prevents tidal disruption for pupils, which is adaptation.

Answer: Protecting mangrove carbon is mitigation, and raising walkways is adaptation.

**Check:** Match stored-carbon goals to mitigation and safe-access structures to adaptation.

### #3: Ferry Terminal Upgrade

A port elevates its ticket counters above storm-surge height and runs battery-powered shuttles to cut diesel emissions. Later, king tides cancel trips for $3$ days, forcing commuters to miss shifts and causing ₱$80{,}000$ in dock repairs. Classify both actions and both effects.

**Solution:**

1. Identify the main climate goal of elevating ticket counters versus running electric shuttles.
2. Identify whether missed commuter shifts and dock repair bills are social or economic effects.

:::try
Finish the last step: label both terminal actions and both tide effects accurately.
:::answer
The answer is **Elevated counters are adaptation, electric shuttles are mitigation, missed shifts are social, and repair bills are economic**. Check each stated purpose and impact separately even at one port.

**Check:** Keep harm-reduction, emission-cutting, welfare disruption, and money costs in $4$ distinct buckets.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Water storage: treat dry-season water storage as adaptation to shortage, not carbon removal. {#water-storage-is-adaptation}
- Stated forest goal: classify forest protection aimed at keeping carbon stored as mitigation. {#forest-carbon-is-mitigation}
- Mixed site actions: separate adaptation, mitigation, social impacts, and economic costs at one building. {#separate-action-from-effect}
:::
