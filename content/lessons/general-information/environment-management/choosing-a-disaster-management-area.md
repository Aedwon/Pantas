## Core Concept

### Purpose Over Timing

The $4$ DRRM areas under RA $10121$ are prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and rehabilitation and recovery. To classify an action, look at what it mainly achieves:

- **Reduce hazard risk or exposure** before or after harm: prevention and mitigation.
- **Build readiness** through plans, drills, warnings, and stockpiles: preparedness.
- **Save lives and meet urgent needs** during or right after impact: response.
- **Restore and improve services and livelihoods** over the long term: rehabilitation and recovery.

Timing helps, yet timing alone often misleads. Both stronger building codes and evacuation drills finish before a storm, yet their purposes differ. Likewise, both emergency water deliveries and permanent pipe repairs happen after a storm, yet one meets urgent needs while the other restores services.

Test timing versus purpose on $2$ pre-storm actions in a mountain town:

1. Workers bolt school roofs down to cut wind damage, which reduces risk.
2. Teachers lead students along marked paths to the town gym, which builds readiness.

Result: Both finish before the storm, yet roof bracing is mitigation and the drill is preparedness.

:::pair #rescue-never-replaces-prevention
Right: A complete DRRM program funds pre-disaster risk reduction and readiness alongside rescue and rebuilding.
Wrong: Treating rescue and rebuilding as complete DRRM work that replaces prevention and readiness.
Skipping pre-disaster work leaves communities exposed to damage that safer rules and readiness could prevent.
:::

:::pair #all-four-phases-stay-needed
Right: Proactive risk reduction adds prevention and readiness while keeping emergency rescue active when needed.
Wrong: Removing rescue operations on the belief that proactive risk reduction is the only phase.
RA $10121$ stresses work before harm while keeping all $4$ DRRM areas active.
:::

:::check
In March, a barangay repairs cracked floodgates along its creek and conducts first-aid training for youth volunteers. Do both actions belong to the same DRRM area because both finish before May storms?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Repairing floodgates reduces physical flood harm (mitigation), while first-aid training builds readiness (preparedness).
:::

### Handling Overlapping DRRM Areas

DRRM areas connect and can overlap inside one project. Early recovery can start while emergency response continues. Safer rebuilding during recovery also cuts future hazard risk.

Because areas connect, reject any reporting rule that forces every program into one isolated box or bans risk reduction after a disaster. When a question asks for the single best area, choose the action's **main purpose**.

Apply this rule to a coastal town replacing a storm-damaged school:

1. Check the main task: rebuilding destroyed classrooms restores an essential public service.
2. Check the added benefit: raising the new floor above storm-surge height cuts future flood risk.

Result: The project mainly serves rehabilitation and recovery while also supporting mitigation.

:::why
Why can one recovery project still support risk reduction? Rebuilding damaged services after a disaster is the main purpose, and building them stronger keeps the next storm from causing the same loss.
:::

:::pair #recovery-can-reduce-future-risk
Right: Programs can support several DRRM areas, such as safer rebuilding that restores services and cuts future risk.
Wrong: Requiring recovery to restore the exact conditions that existed before damage.
Copying old weak designs invites repeat disasters instead of helping communities build back safer.
:::

:::check
A town council drafts a rule saying funds for rebuilding a washed-out bridge cannot pay to raise its deck above flood level. Does that rule fit RA $10121$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Recovery under RA $10121$ includes improvements that restore services while reducing future disaster risk.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Reviewing a Relief-Only Budget

A city council puts its entire disaster budget into relief packs and post-storm rebuilding. A planner proposes splitting funds across land-use mapping, warning drills, rescue teams, and safer rebuilding. Which plan follows RA $10121$?

**Solution:**

1. Relief packs cover response, and rebuilding covers recovery, leaving pre-disaster risk reduction and readiness empty.
2. The planner's budget covers prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery together.

Answer: The planner's budget follows RA $10121$ because it covers pre-disaster and post-disaster areas.

**Check:** Confirm all $4$ areas receive support rather than rescue and recovery alone.

### #2: Testing a Pre-Storm Rule

A clerk labels every March project before storm season as mitigation. The town's March list includes building a hillside retaining wall and printing neighborhood evacuation maps. Test the clerk's rule.

**Solution:**

1. The retaining wall lowers landslide harm, so it fits prevention and mitigation.
2. Printing evacuation maps readies residents to act, so it fits preparedness.

Answer: The clerk's rule fails because pre-storm actions can have different main purposes.

**Check:** Compare what each pre-disaster task achieves rather than relying on the calendar.

### #3: Checking an Exclusive-Label Rule

An inspector rejects a town's recovery grant for a storm-damaged clinic because the blueprint adds hurricane shutters to cut future wind risk. He says recovery projects cannot include risk-reducing upgrades. Evaluate his claim.

**Solution:**

1. Check the project's main purpose in replacing a storm-damaged clinic.
2. Test whether adding hurricane shutters changes that recovery purpose or conflicts with RA $10121$.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the inspector's claim under the DRRM framework.
:::answer
The answer is **Reject the inspector's claim**. Restoring the damaged clinic is mainly recovery, and adding shutters validly reduces future risk within recovery.

**Check:** Confirm the main task is restoring a damaged service and remember DRRM areas can overlap.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Relief-only plans: include pre-disaster prevention and readiness alongside rescue and rebuilding. {#rescue-never-replaces-prevention}
- Proactive emphasis: keep emergency rescue active alongside prevention, mitigation, and readiness. {#all-four-phases-stay-needed}
- Safer recovery: allow post-disaster rebuilding to upgrade structures and cut future risk. {#recovery-can-reduce-future-risk}
:::
