## Core Concept

### Proportionality in Attacks

Even when an object is a verified military objective, **proportionality** sets a separate limit (Additional Protocol I, Articles $51(5)(b)$ and $57(2)(a)(iii)$). Planners must compare expected **incidental harm** to civilians and civilian objects against the **concrete and direct military advantage** anticipated.

Incidental harm means civilian deaths, injuries, and property damage that are not the attack's aim. If expected incidental harm is **excessive** compared with that military gain, the attack is prohibited.

A small military advantage can never justify massive incidental civilian harm. Directly targeting protected civilians remains banned regardless of gain.

:::why
Why add proportionality after confirming a lawful military target? Target status clears only the first rule; IHL also forbids sacrificing large numbers of civilians for minor tactical gains.
:::

Evaluate an air strike on a $1$-soldier sniper nest atop a crowded $200$-bed hospital using a $500$-kilogram bomb:

1. Check target status: the sniper nest is a military objective, yet civilians are not intended targets.
2. Check proportionality: killing dozens of patients is excessive compared with removing $1$ sniper.

Result: Proportionality prohibits the heavy bomb strike despite the verified military target.

:::pair #target-status-not-enough
Right: Run a separate proportionality check even after confirming a verified military objective.
Wrong: Treating verified military-target status as removing the need to weigh civilian harm.
Target verification never excuses an attack whose expected civilian harm is excessive.
:::

:::pair #unintended-harm-still-counts
Right: Count all expected incidental civilian harm under proportionality even when civilians are not targeted.
Wrong: Treating unintended civilian harm as unable to make an attack unlawful under proportionality.
Proportionality specifically governs unintended collateral harm beside military targets.
:::

:::check
A verified $2$-truck enemy fuel convoy parks beside a busy rural bus terminal. Bombing it will delay fuel briefly while killing dozens of travelers. May the strike go ahead?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Expected incidental civilian harm is excessive compared with the minor military gain, so proportionality bars the strike.
:::

### Attack Precautions and Cancellation

Under Additional Protocol I, Article $57$, planners must take constant **precautions** to avoid or minimize civilian harm. They must verify targets, choose safer weapons or timing, and issue effective advance warnings when circumstances allow.

Under Article $57(2)(b)$, an attack **must be canceled or suspended** if new facts show the target is unlawful or expected civilian harm is excessive.

Issuing an advance warning never legalizes an unlawful target or a disproportionate strike. Target status never cures excessive civilian harm either.

Check how precautions work when pilots spot school buses near an enemy bridge after leaflets warned residents:

1. Check new facts: crowded buses raise expected civilian harm to an excessive level.
2. Check warning effect: dropping leaflets earlier does not allow proceeding with excessive harm.

Result: Pilots must cancel or suspend the bridge strike immediately.

:::pair #warning-never-cures-unlawful-attack
Right: Cancel or suspend an attack when expected harm becomes excessive or the target fails, even after warning.
Wrong: Treating an advance warning as satisfying every precaution and permitting an unlawful attack.
A warning helps spare civilians, yet never overrides distinction or proportionality limits.
:::

:::check
After broadcasting a $2$-hour evacuation radio alert, drones show $80$ trapped families sheltering beside a minor enemy repair shed. May artillery fire because warning was given?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Broadcasting a warning never excuses excessive civilian harm, so planners must cancel or suspend the strike.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Heavy Strike on a Small Radio Relay

A verified military radio relay stands beside an apartment house holding $120$ residents. Destroying the relay delays enemy signals by $1$ hour while likely collapsing the apartment house. Check whether the strike is lawful.

**Solution:**

1. Check target status: the radio relay is a military objective.
2. Check proportionality: collapsing a $120$-resident building is excessive compared with a $1$-hour signal delay.

Answer: No, proportionality prohibits the planned strike.

**Check:** Match verified target status plus excessive incidental harm to a proportionality bar.

### #2: Choosing Safer Timing at a Munitions Plant

Planners can strike a verified munitions plant at noon with $300$ civilian workers inside, or at midnight when empty. Both yield equal gain. Which timing do precautions require?

**Solution:**

1. Check safety options: midnight gives equal military gain with far less civilian danger.
2. Check Article $57$: planners must pick practical timing and weapons that lower civilian harm.

Answer: Planners must strike at midnight and keep verifying conditions before firing.

**Check:** Pick practical timing or weapon choices that lower civilian harm without losing military gain.

### #3: Stalled Buses Spotted After Warning Sirens

Commanders verify an enemy supply bridge and sound warning sirens ahead of an air strike. Drones then spot $4$ stalled civilian buses packed across the bridge, making civilian harm excessive. An officer says the sirens and valid target allow firing. Assess that view.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether warning sirens and a verified target override excessive civilian harm.
2. Check what Article $57(2)(b)$ requires when new facts show excessive expected harm.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the officer's argument and state what planners must do.
:::answer
The answer is **The officer is wrong, and planners must cancel or suspend the strike**. Neither a valid military target nor warning sirens permit an attack once new information shows excessive civilian harm.

**Check:** Remember all $3$ attack checks: lawful target, proportionate incidental harm, and cancellation when new facts show excess.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Target is verified military: still run a separate proportionality check on expected civilian harm. {#target-status-not-enough}
- Civilians are not the aim: still count expected incidental harm under proportionality. {#unintended-harm-still-counts}
- Advance warning was issued: still cancel or stop an attack if harm is excessive or target fails. {#warning-never-cures-unlawful-attack}
:::
