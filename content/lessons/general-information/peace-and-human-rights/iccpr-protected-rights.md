## Core Concept

### Rights That Cannot Be Suspended

**Non-derogable rights** cannot be suspended under Article $4(2)$, even in a proclaimed emergency. A **contract** creates legal duties, like repaying debt.

**Servitude** means living and working under another's forced control. **Retroactive criminal punishment** applies later laws to punish earlier lawful acts or raise earlier penalties.

**Conscience** means beliefs about right and wrong. Article $4(2)$ shields $7$ core protections:

- Right to life (Article $6$, barring arbitrary killing).
- Ban on torture or cruel treatment, plus medical experiments without free consent (Article $7$).
- Bans on slavery and servitude (Article $8(1)$ and $(2)$).
- Protection from prison merely for inability to meet a contract duty (Article $11$).
- Ban on retroactive criminal punishment (Article $15$).
- Recognition as a person before the law (Article $16$).
- Freedom of thought, conscience, and religion (Article $18$).

ICCPR Article $4$ never authorizes suspending IHL duties either.

:::why
Why shield these $7$ rules? Torture, slavery, retroactive crimes, and erasing legal personhood violate basic dignity in any crisis.
:::

Test an emergency decree holding farm families in lifelong servitude under estate owners and jailing renters for unpaid debt:

1. Check Article $4(2)$: servitude and prison solely for unpaid contract duties are both non-derogable.
2. Check proclamation: an emergency decree never lifts Article $4(2)$ protections.

Result: Both measures break the ICCPR despite the decree.

:::pair #non-derogable-never-suspended
Right: Keep every Article $4(2)$ protection active at all times, even during a proclaimed emergency.
Wrong: Treating an emergency declaration as suspending all ICCPR duties, including non-derogable ones.
Only derogable duties may pause under Article $4$; Article $4(2)$ rules stay intact.
:::

:::check
During a proclaimed war emergency, a town jails residents solely for unpaid bank loans. Does Article $4$ allow that?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Protection from prison merely for inability to meet a contract duty is non-derogable under Article $4(2)$.
:::

### Non-Derogable Status Versus Permitted Limits

Non-derogable status forbids suspending a right under Article $4$, not ordinary treaty limits.

Under Article $18(3)$, outward religious practice may face narrow law-based limits needed for public safety, order, health, morals, or others' rights. Still, Article $4$ never allows suspending freedom of belief or forcing belief changes.

Compare $2$ wildfire rules touching religion:

1. Banning open candles in dry parks by safety law while belief stays free: allowed under Article $18(3)$.
2. Notifying the UN and ordering prisoners to renounce faith: forbidden under Article $4(2)$.

Result: Safety limits on outward practice differ from forcing inner belief.

:::pair #outward-limits-not-forced-belief
Right: Distinguish narrow lawful limits on outward religious practice from Article $4$ derogation.
Wrong: Treating non-derogable status as barring every treaty limit on outward religious practice.
Article $18(3)$ permits necessary legal limits on outward practice without suspending inner belief.
:::

:::pair #belief-cannot-be-forced
Right: Protect inner freedom of thought and belief unconditionally without forced changes of belief.
Wrong: Treating a permitted limit on outward practice as allowing forced changes of belief.
Lawful safety limits on outward practice never permit compelling anyone to abandon inner conscience or faith.
:::

:::check
During an epidemic, a city caps chapel crowds by health law while respecting belief. Does that break Article $18$'s non-derogable rule?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Necessary legal health limits on outward gatherings differ from suspending or forcing belief.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Retroactive Penalty and Legal Personhood

During a proclaimed emergency, a cabinet raises prison terms for acts done $2$ years earlier and strips detainees of legal personhood. Does Article $4$ allow either step?

**Solution:**

1. Check retroactive penalties: Article $15$ bars raising earlier penalties and is non-derogable.
2. Check legal recognition: Article $16$ protects personhood before the law and is non-derogable.

Answer: No, Article $4$ cannot authorize either measure.

**Check:** Match retroactive criminal punishment and legal personhood to Article $4(2)$.

### #2: Midnight Noise Rule for Outdoor Speakers

A town enforces a midnight noise law limiting outdoor speakers at rallies and worship vigils, leaving inner faith untouched. A resident claims Article $18$'s non-derogable status forbids any public-order rule touching worship. Assess that claim.

**Solution:**

1. Check distinction: non-derogation bars emergency suspension under Article $4(2)$, not ordinary Article $18(3)$ limits.
2. Check facts: a narrow public-order law protects neighborhood peace without touching inner belief.

Answer: The resident is wrong because Article $18(3)$ permits necessary legal limits on outward practice.

**Check:** Separate emergency suspension of belief from narrow law-based public-order limits on outward practice.

### #3: Coerced Experiments and Contract Debt in Crisis

After proclaiming an emergency with UN notice, officials propose $3$ measures. They want unconsented medical trials on detainees, forced religious conversions, and prison solely for unpaid contracts. Assess all $3$ proposals under Article $4(2)$.

**Solution:**

1. Check medical trials and belief coercion against Articles $7$ and $18$ under Article $4(2)$.
2. Check prison solely for unpaid contract duties against Article $11$ under Article $4(2)$.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on all $3$ proposals and explain why UN notice does not save them.
:::answer
The answer is **All $3$ proposals are prohibited**. Free consent for medical experiments, freedom of belief, and protection from contract-debt prison are non-derogable under Article $4(2)$, regardless of UN notice.

**Check:** Confirm Articles $7$, $11$, and $18$ allow zero derogation even with proclamation and notice.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Emergency is declared: keep all Article $4(2)$ non-derogable protections fully in force. {#non-derogable-never-suspended}
- Right is non-derogable: still recognize narrow lawful Article $18(3)$ limits on outward practice. {#outward-limits-not-forced-belief}
- Outward limits apply: never treat them as permitting forced changes of belief. {#belief-cannot-be-forced}
:::
