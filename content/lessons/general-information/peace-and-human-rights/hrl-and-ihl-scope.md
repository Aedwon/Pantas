## Core Concept

### Human Rights Law and State Jurisdiction

**Human Rights Law (HRL)** protects people's life, freedom, and dignity. It applies in peace and during armed conflict.

HRL mainly binds governments toward people within their **jurisdiction**, meaning their territory, authority, or control. Duties can reach people controlled outside home borders, depending on the right and facts.

War never turns off human rights. Some treaty duties allow narrow emergency suspension, while core rights cannot be suspended.

Test HRL reach when naval officers hold rescued mariners on a coast guard ship:

1. Check timing: HRL applies in peace and armed conflict.
2. Check control: holding mariners aboard a state ship creates jurisdiction.

Result: The state owes HRL duties toward those mariners outside its land borders.

:::pair #hrl-continues-in-war
Right: Apply HRL in peace and armed conflict to people under state jurisdiction.
Wrong: Treating HRL as ending once armed conflict begins.
Armed conflict adds humanitarian rules, while state human rights duties stay active.
:::

:::check
During an alert, a state unit holds travelers inside an overseas terminal it runs and claims HRL stops outside land borders. Is that valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. State control over the terminal brings travelers under its jurisdiction in peace and conflict.
:::

### International Humanitarian Law Scope

**International Humanitarian Law (IHL)** is the law of armed conflict. It limits conflict harm by protecting non-participants and restricting **means** (weapons) and **methods** (fighting ways).

IHL governs only armed conflict. Political disputes, recessions, or tense elections alone never trigger IHL.

IHL limits how fighting occurs without judging whether starting a war is right. Even a claimed just cause never lifts humanitarian limits.

:::why
Why does IHL skip judging which side started a war? If rules depended on who felt justified, every side would claim exemption and leave civilians unprotected.
:::

Test IHL scope during a dockworkers' strike and tariff clash between two neighbors:

1. Check for armed conflict: neither side uses armed force.
2. Check governing law: domestic law and HRL govern the dispute.

Result: IHL does not apply without armed conflict.

:::check
During a recession, rival parties hold loud street marches with no armed conflict. Does IHL govern those marches?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Economic hardship and political marches without armed conflict stay outside IHL's scope.
:::

### Bound Parties and Protected People

IHL binds **all parties** to an armed conflict, covering both state armies and organized non-state armed groups.

An enemy's abuse or a just-cause claim never excuses breaking IHL. Every party owes the same conduct duties.

Bound parties and protected people are distinct categories. Conflict parties obey IHL duties, while non-fighting civilians and out-of-combat fighters receive protection.

Apply both roles in a contested valley:

1. Identify the bound party: an organized armed faction fighting troops must obey IHL.
2. Identify protected people: unarmed farmers staying out of fighting are protected civilians.

Result: The faction owes conflict duties, and farmers hold attack protection.

:::pair #ihl-binds-non-state-parties
Right: Bind every conflict party, including organized non-state armed groups, to obey IHL.
Wrong: Treating non-state armed groups as exempt because they lack government status.
Common Article $3$ binds each conflict party regardless of official state recognition.
:::

:::pair #bound-party-versus-protected-civilian
Right: Separate bound conflict parties from non-fighting civilians who hold IHL protection.
Wrong: Treating non-state armed groups as protected civilians exempt from conflict duties.
Standing outside state forces never turns an active armed group into a protected civilian.
:::

:::check
A rebel leader claims his unit owes zero IHL duties because state troops broke a truce yesterday. Does that excuse hold?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. An enemy's breach never excuses another conflict party from obeying IHL.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Overseas Detention Camp

During armed conflict, state soldiers run a camp across the border. Guards deny water, claiming HRL ends during war and outside home borders. Is that claim valid?

**Solution:**

1. Check timing: HRL continues during armed conflict alongside IHL.
2. Check jurisdiction: running the camp puts detainees under state control.

Answer: No, HRL duties stay active in conflict and reach people under state control.

**Check:** Match wartime continuity and state control over detainees to HRL jurisdiction.

### #2: Retaliation Against Neutral Medics

During fighting, an armed group attacks neutral medics, saying its cause is just and state forces violated rules earlier. May the group ignore IHL?

**Solution:**

1. Check party status: organized armed groups fighting in conflict are bound by IHL.
2. Check excuses: neither a just cause nor an enemy's abuse suspends IHL duties.

Answer: No, the group stays bound to respect protected medics.

**Check:** Verify all conflict parties remain bound without reciprocity or just-cause exceptions.

### #3: Separating Duties and Protections in a Town

In an armed conflict, an organized militia fights troops around a town. Its leader claims non-state status makes his fighters protected civilians with zero IHL duties, while townspeople lose all HRL rights. Assess both claims.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether non-state armed groups bear IHL duties or hold civilian immunity.
2. Check whether armed conflict erases HRL duties toward townspeople.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the militia's duties and the townspeople's HRL protection.
:::answer
The answer is **Both claims are wrong**. The militia is a bound conflict party, not protected civilians, and townspeople keep HRL protection during conflict.

**Check:** Keep bound conflict parties separate from protected civilians, and keep HRL active in conflict.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Armed conflict begins: keep applying HRL toward people under state jurisdiction. {#hrl-continues-in-war}
- Non-state group fights: bind every conflict party to obey IHL regardless of cause. {#ihl-binds-non-state-parties}
- Non-state label appears: separate bound fighting parties from protected non-fighting civilians. {#bound-party-versus-protected-civilian}
:::
