## Core Concept

### International Armed Conflict (IAC)

An **international armed conflict (IAC)** begins whenever two or more states use armed force against each other. Under Common Article $2$ of the Geneva Conventions, no formal declaration of war is required.

Foreign occupation of another state's territory also triggers IAC rules. That rule holds even when local troops and residents offer zero armed resistance.

:::why
Why does IHL rely on facts instead of a war declaration? States often fight or occupy territory without declaring war, so tying rules to facts stops governments from dodging duties.
:::

Test both IAC rules when State A sends troops to take a border port in State B:

1. Check the parties: two states are involved, even with no war declaration.
2. Check occupation: taking control of State B's port triggers IAC rules without any shots fired.

Result: State A's unresisted port takeover creates an IAC governed by IHL.

:::pair #declaration-not-needed-for-iac
Right: Trigger IAC rules from interstate armed force or occupation even without a war declaration.
Wrong: Requiring a formal declaration of war before IAC rules apply to state forces.
Common Article $2$ looks at actual force or occupation between states, not diplomatic speeches.
:::

:::pair #unresisted-occupation-is-still-iac
Right: Classify foreign occupation of another state's territory without armed resistance as an IAC.
Wrong: Requiring armed resistance before foreign occupation can fall under IAC rules.
Unresisted foreign control still puts territory and residents under Geneva occupation protections.
:::

:::check
Country X stations troops across Country Y's northern valley. Country Y orders police not to fight, and neither capital declares war. Does an IAC exist?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Foreign occupation of another state's territory triggers IAC rules even without resistance or a war declaration.
:::

### Non-International Armed Conflict (NIAC) and Unrest

A **non-international armed conflict (NIAC)** involves sufficiently intense fighting between government forces and organized armed groups, or between such groups. Under Common Article $3$ and $2024$ ICRC guidance, two tests govern.

First, check **organization**. An organized group has leaders directing fighters, discipline, and ability to plan operations.

Second, check **intensity**. Scale, length, and frequency of clashes show intensity, though no single clash count settles every case.

Riots, brief disturbances, isolated violence, or tense political events fail these tests. Facts govern classification, never official labels alone, and HRL still binds the state.

Compare $2$ events inside one country:

1. For $5$ months, a commanded armed front mounts weekly raids on army outposts: both organization and intensity hold.
2. After a rally, an unorganized crowd smashes windows for $2$ hours and disperses: both tests fail.

Result: Only the $5$-month coordinated fighting is a NIAC, while HRL governs policing the $2$-hour disturbance.

:::pair #tension-or-riots-not-niac
Right: Require both intense fighting and organized armed groups before classifying unrest as a NIAC.
Wrong: Treating tense political campaigns as automatically falling under armed-conflict rules.
Political tensions and brief riots never trigger IHL when organization and intensity are missing.
:::

:::check
After a disputed tax vote, angry commuters block a bus terminal for $3$ hours with no armed commanders or continuing clashes. A mayor calls it a NIAC. Is it?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. A brief unorganized disturbance lacks both organization and intensity, so HRL still governs policing.
:::

## Examples

### #1: River Clashes and Unresisted Lighthouse Takeover

State P and State Q gunboats exchange cannon fire across a border river without declaring war. Nearby, State P troops occupy State Q's lighthouse island whose unarmed crew yields immediately. Classify both events.

**Solution:**

1. Check river fighting: armed force between two states creates an IAC without a war declaration.
2. Check island takeover: foreign occupation of another state's territory triggers IAC rules even without resistance.

Answer: Both events fall under IAC rules.

**Check:** Match interstate armed force and unresisted foreign occupation to IAC under Common Article $2$.

### #2: Coordinated Clashes Versus Isolated Unrest

For $6$ months, a disciplined armed faction under regional commanders launches weekly attacks on provincial depots. In the capital, an unorganized street crowd burns $2$ vans during one night. Which event is a NIAC?

**Solution:**

1. Check the faction: regional command and weekly coordinated attacks satisfy organization and intensity.
2. Check the capital crowd: one night without command structure or continuing clashes fails both tests.

Answer: The $6$-month faction campaign is a NIAC; the single-night crowd incident is not.

**Check:** Test internal clashes for both group organization and sustained intensity.

### #3: Harbor Gate Strike and Emergency Label

Dockworkers block a harbor gate for $4$ hours over unpaid wages. During one brief scuffle, $3$ guards suffer bruises before the workers go home. A port director calls the strike a NIAC and says HRL no longer applies. Rule on the classification and governing law.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether the dockworkers show armed command organization and sustained intense fighting.
2. Check which legal framework continues to govern state policing when no armed conflict exists.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the NIAC label and identify which law governs the state response.
:::answer
The answer is **No NIAC exists, and HRL governs the state response**. Brief unorganized labor unrest fails organization and intensity tests, and labels never create armed conflict.

**Check:** Verify both NIAC conditions by facts alone, and keep HRL active when IHL does not apply.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- No war declaration: still classify interstate force or occupation as an IAC. {#declaration-not-needed-for-iac}
- No local resistance: still treat foreign occupation of another state's territory as an IAC. {#unresisted-occupation-is-still-iac}
- Riot or political tension: require organization and intensity before calling internal unrest a NIAC. {#tension-or-riots-not-niac}
:::
