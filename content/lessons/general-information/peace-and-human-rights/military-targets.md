## Core Concept

### Principle of Distinction

Under Additional Protocol I, Articles $48$, $51(2)$, and $52(1)$, **distinction** requires parties to separate **military objectives** from protected **civilians** and **civilian objects**. Civilian objects include homes, schools, clinics, and water plants.

Attacks may target only military objectives. Directly attacking protected civilians or civilian objects is always prohibited.

Pressuring, frightening, or weakening enemy morale never turns civilians or homes into lawful targets. Large expected military gain cannot override distinction either.

Test an air strike plan targeting an empty town market square to panic enemy leaders into peace talks:

1. Check object status: a neighborhood market is a civilian object, not a military target.
2. Check purpose: creating fear or political pressure never overrides distinction.

Result: The strike violates distinction and is strictly forbidden.

:::pair #distinction-forbids-pressure-attacks
Right: Bar direct attacks on civilians and civilian objects even if intended to pressure the opponent.
Wrong: Allowing direct civilian targeting whenever it pressures or weakens the opposing side.
Fear and enemy pressure never turn protected civilians or homes into lawful military targets.
:::

:::check
Commanders propose bombing an empty civic bakery and grain storehouse solely to lower enemy civilian morale. Does that goal allow the strike?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Distinction prohibits directly attacking civilian objects to pressure or frighten the opponent.
:::

### Two-Part Test for a Military Objective

Under Additional Protocol I, Article $52(2)$, an object qualifies as a **military objective** only when it meets **both** conditions in the current circumstances:

1. By **nature, location, purpose, or use**, it makes an **effective contribution to military action**.
2. Its total or partial destruction, capture, or neutralization offers a **definite military advantage** in the circumstances ruling at the time.

Both conditions must hold together. A loose military connection without definite advantage right now fails the test, and vice versa.

:::why
Why require both effective contribution and definite military advantage at the time? A one-part test would let forces bomb bridges or factories over vague guesses instead of real combat value.
:::

Compare $2$ bridges during an armed conflict:

1. A steel bridge carrying active enemy tank convoys to the front: contributes effectively, and dropping it halts the advance, so both conditions hold.
2. An unused stone footbridge $50$ kilometers behind quiet lines whose loss gives zero combat benefit now: Condition $2$ fails.

Result: Only the active tank bridge is a military objective under Article $52(2)$.

:::pair #both-objective-conditions-required
Right: Require both effective contribution to military action and definite military advantage in the circumstances.
Wrong: Treating effective military contribution alone as satisfying both military-objective conditions.
An object failing either Article $52(2)$ condition stays a protected civilian object.
:::

:::check
A rail spur once carried army fuel. Now scouts confirm enemy forces left that sector permanently, so cutting the spur offers zero tactical gain today. Is it a military objective?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Without a definite military advantage in the current circumstances, the second condition fails.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Shelling Apartments for Pressure

An army shells residential blocks containing no troops, weapons, or supply stores, aiming to frighten residents into demanding surrender. May the shelling proceed?

**Solution:**

1. Check status: residential blocks with no military use are protected civilian objects.
2. Check distinction: frightening civilians or pressuring enemy leaders never creates military target status.

Answer: No, distinction prohibits directly attacking civilian apartments.

**Check:** Verify that enemy pressure never turns protected civilian objects into lawful targets.

### #2: Dual-Use Power Substation

A regional power substation supplies electricity to an active air-defense radar station. Disabling the substation immediately blinds the radar during an ongoing operation. Test whether the substation is a military objective.

**Solution:**

1. Check Condition $1$: powering an active radar makes an effective contribution to military action by use.
2. Check Condition $2$: blinding the radar now yields a definite military advantage.

Answer: Yes, both Article $52(2)$ conditions are satisfied (proportionality and precautions still apply).

**Check:** Confirm both effective contribution and definite current military advantage before classifying an object.

### #3: Inspecting a Former Army Warehouse

A port warehouse stored army boots $6$ months ago and still has faded military stencils on its roof. Today it holds only town flood-relief food, and destroying it yields zero military gain, though an officer wants to bomb it to rattle dockworkers. Rule on its target status.

**Solution:**

1. Check both Article $52(2)$ conditions against the warehouse's current use and tactical value today.
2. Check whether past army paint or unsettling dockworkers satisfies distinction rules.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on whether the warehouse is a military objective and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **No, it is not a military objective**. It neither effectively contributes to military action today nor offers a definite military advantage, and rattling dockworkers violates distinction.

**Check:** Test current circumstances for both Article $52(2)$ requirements rather than past markings or pressure aims.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Strike aims to pressure enemy: never attack protected civilians or civilian objects directly. {#distinction-forbids-pressure-attacks}
- Object has a military link: still check both effective contribution and definite military advantage now. {#both-objective-conditions-required}
:::
