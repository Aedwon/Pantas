## Core Concept

The Civil Service Examination's General Information scope includes Peace and Human Rights Issues and Concepts. One useful distinction is between International Human Rights Law (HRL) and International Humanitarian Law (IHL), two distinct but complementary bodies of law.

### Scope of Application

- **Human Rights Law (HRL):** Applies at all times, during both peacetime and armed conflict. In a qualifying emergency, states may derogate from some human-rights obligations under the applicable legal rules, but certain fundamental rights remain non-derogable.
- **International Humanitarian Law (IHL):** Applies specifically in situations of **armed conflict**. Some IHL obligations also require implementation in peacetime, such as adopting legislation and providing training, but the body of law governs armed conflict.

### Bound Entities

- **Human Rights Law:** Primarily places legal duties and obligations on states in relation to persons within their jurisdiction.
- **International Humanitarian Law:** Binds **all parties** to an armed conflict, including state armed forces and non-state armed groups.

### Primary Purpose of IHL

International Humanitarian Law seeks to limit the effects of armed conflict by protecting persons who are not or are no longer participating in hostilities and by restricting the means and methods of warfare.

## Examples

### #1: Applicability During Armed Conflict

During an armed conflict, a non-state armed group claims that international law does not apply to it because it is not a state government. Does International Humanitarian Law bind non-state armed groups that are parties to an armed conflict?

**Solution:**
Examine who is bound by International Humanitarian Law (IHL).

1. IHL applies in armed conflict and binds all parties to the conflict.
2. Those parties can include both state armed forces and non-state armed groups.
3. Therefore, IHL binds a non-state armed group that is a party to the armed conflict.

### #2: Peacetime Applicability

A government agency is updating its routine peacetime administrative procedures. Between IHL and human rights law, which body of law continues to apply in peacetime?

**Solution:**
Compare the general temporal scope of the two bodies of law.

1. IHL applies specifically in situations of armed conflict, aside from obligations that must be implemented in peacetime.
2. Human rights law applies at all times, including peacetime.
3. Therefore, of these two bodies of law, human rights law continues to apply to routine peacetime state conduct.

### #3: Distinguishing Bound Parties and Temporal Scope

An exam question asks for a key difference between Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law regarding when they apply and who they bind. Which statement is correct?

**Solution:**
Compare the general temporal and personal scope of both bodies of law.

1. Human Rights Law applies at all times and primarily places obligations on states.
2. International Humanitarian Law applies in armed conflict and binds all parties to the conflict.
3. Therefore, IHL is specifically the law of armed conflict binding its parties, while human rights law applies in peace and in war and primarily governs state obligations toward individuals.
