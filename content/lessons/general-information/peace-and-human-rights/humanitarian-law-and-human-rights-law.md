## Core Concept

Understanding the distinction between International Human Rights Law (HRL) and International Humanitarian Law (IHL) is a central concept in Civil Service examinations.

### Scope of Application

- **Human Rights Law (HRL):** Applies at all times, during both peacetime and situations of armed conflict.
- **International Humanitarian Law (IHL):** Applies specifically during situations of **armed conflict** (law of armed conflict).

### Bound Entities

- **Human Rights Law:** Primarily places legal duties and obligations upon state authorities regarding their treatment of people.
- **International Humanitarian Law:** Binds **all parties** to an armed conflict, including both state armed forces and non-state armed groups.

### Primary Purpose of IHL

International Humanitarian Law seeks to limit the effects of armed conflict by protecting persons who are not or are no longer participating in hostilities, and by restricting the means and methods of warfare.

## Examples

### #1: Applicability During Armed Conflict

During an armed conflict, a non-state armed group claims that international law does not apply to them because they are not a state government. Does International Humanitarian Law bind non-state armed groups in a conflict?

**Solution:**
Examine the scope of bound entities under International Humanitarian Law (IHL).

1. Human rights law primarily binds state authorities.
2. International Humanitarian Law applies in armed conflict and binds all parties to the conflict.
3. All parties include both state armed forces and non-state armed groups.
4. Therefore, IHL binds the non-state armed group.

### #2: Peacetime Applicability

A government agency is updating its peacetime administrative procedures. Does International Humanitarian Law apply to routine peacetime administration?

**Solution:**
Determine the trigger for the application of IHL.

1. International Humanitarian Law applies specifically during situations of armed conflict.
2. Human rights law applies at all times, including peacetime.
3. Because there is no armed conflict, IHL does not apply to routine peacetime administration.
4. Therefore, only human rights law governs peacetime state conduct.

### #3: Distinguishing Bound Parties and Temporal Scope

An exam question asks for the key difference between Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law regarding when they apply and who they bind. Which statement is correct?

**Solution:**
Compare the temporal scope and bound entities of both bodies of law.

1. Human Rights Law applies at all times and primarily binds the state.
2. International Humanitarian Law applies during armed conflict and binds all parties to the conflict.
3. Therefore, IHL is specifically the law of armed conflict binding all participating parties, while HRL binds the state at all times.
