## Core Concept

### Weapons Indiscriminate by Nature

Under IHL, **means of warfare** are weapons, and **methods of warfare** are ways parties fight. Both face strict legal limits.

IHL bans weapons that are **indiscriminate by nature** (Additional Protocol I, Article $51(4)(b)$ and $(c)$; Customary Rule $71$). Such weapons cannot be aimed at a specific military target, or their effects cannot stay within IHL limits.

Because they strike targets and protected people alike, good intentions or expected military gain cannot make them lawful.

Test an unguided firebomb balloon released over a city holding $1$ army depot:

1. Check control: wind drift prevents aiming at the depot or confining fire.
2. Check intent: aiming in mind for the depot cannot cure an indiscriminate weapon.

Result: The drifting firebomb balloon is indiscriminate by nature and prohibited.

:::pair #intent-never-cures-indiscriminate-weapon
Right: Ban any weapon that cannot be aimed at a specific target or have effects confined.
Wrong: Treating a commander's intended target as removing a weapon's indiscriminate character.
Weapon design and uncontrollable effects govern legality, never good intentions.
:::

:::check
A battery fires unguided rockets scattering explosives randomly across a $5$-kilometer town to hit one radar van. Are those rockets lawful?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Weapons that cannot aim at a specific target or confine effects within IHL limits are indiscriminate by nature.
:::

### Superfluous Injury or Unnecessary Suffering

Under Additional Protocol I, Article $35(2)$, weapons must not cause **superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering**. That means harm beyond what a lawful military purpose can justify.

This ban protects **fighters too**, alongside civilians. Aiming accurately at opposing soldiers never excuses banned suffering.

:::why
Why limit weapons used solely against enemy fighters? IHL allows disabling opposing forces, never inflicting cruel agony or untreatable wounds beyond military need.
:::

Test sniper bullets packed with glass splinters meant to hide on medical X-rays:

1. Check accuracy: a sniper aims strictly at enemy soldiers.
2. Check injury limit: hidden glass blocks care and adds needless suffering beyond disabling fighters.

Result: The round violates the ban on superfluous injury despite pinpoint aim.

:::pair #accuracy-never-excuses-cruel-weapons
Right: Enforce the ban on superfluous injury even when weapons aim accurately at fighters.
Wrong: Allowing any injury whenever a weapon can be aimed precisely.
Aiming precision never lifts the ban on unnecessary suffering.
:::

:::check
An army builds a precision dart gun aimed solely at soldiers, coating darts to block clotting permanently. Does accuracy make it lawful?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Accurate weapons aimed at fighters still violate IHL if they cause unnecessary suffering.
:::

### Poison Gas and Specific Weapon Bans

Specific treaties prohibit whole classes of weapons. The $1925$ **Geneva Gas Protocol** (and Philippine RA $9851$) bans using **poison gas** in war.

Outright treaty weapon bans allow zero military-gain exceptions. Neither commander approval nor tactical gain makes poison gas legal.

Apply this ban when officers propose chlorine gas to clear a mountain bunker:

1. Check treaty rule: the Geneva Gas Protocol flatly prohibits poison gas in war.
2. Check claimed gain: faster bunker capture gives zero exception.

Result: Using chlorine gas stays prohibited despite expected military gain.

:::pair #military-gain-never-waives-gas-ban
Right: Enforce the absolute treaty ban on poison gas without any military-gain exception.
Wrong: Treating definite military gain as creating an exception to the poison-gas ban.
Specific weapon bans stand independently and cannot be waived for battlefield gain.
:::

:::check
A commander orders mustard gas used solely against an isolated enemy trench far from civilians to save troops. Is that lawful?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The treaty ban on poison gas applies without exception for isolation or military gain.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Drifting Sea Mines

Naval forces release unanchored mines drifting freely across sea lanes used by warships and cargo ships. The unit intends to hit only warships. Is this lawful?

**Solution:**

1. Check control: drifting mines cannot be directed at a specific target or kept within IHL limits.
2. Check intent: intending to hit only warships cannot fix an indiscriminate weapon.

Answer: No, drifting mines are indiscriminate by nature and prohibited.

**Check:** Match uncontrollable weapon drift to the ban on indiscriminate weapons.

### #2: Pinpoint Blinding Laser

Engineers mount a precision laser on rifles to blind opposing soldiers permanently. It never misses and harms zero civilians. Which IHL rule blocks it?

**Solution:**

1. Check accuracy: hitting only opposing fighters never satisfies every weapons limit.
2. Check injury ban: permanent blinding inflicts superfluous injury beyond lawful military need.

Answer: The ban on superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering blocks even accurate weapons aimed at fighters.

**Check:** Unnecessary-suffering rules protect fighters too, regardless of accuracy.

### #3: Nerve Gas for an Underground Bunker

A planner proposes pumping nerve gas into an underground enemy bunker $6$ kilometers from any village. He argues the gas stays inside, hits only enemy troops, and avoids a costly assault. Rule on the proposal.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether isolation and targeting only enemy troops affect treaty weapon bans.
2. Check whether saving lives or quick victory creates an exception for poison gas.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the nerve-gas proposal and state the controlling rule.
:::answer
The answer is **The proposal is prohibited because the poison-gas ban still applies**. The Geneva Gas Protocol bars poison gas in war, and neither military gain nor hitting only enemy troops creates an exception.

**Check:** Test specific weapon bans separately from target location and military advantage.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Weapon cannot aim or confine harm: never let good intent excuse an indiscriminate weapon. {#intent-never-cures-indiscriminate-weapon}
- Weapon aims accurately at fighters: still apply the ban on unnecessary suffering. {#accuracy-never-excuses-cruel-weapons}
- Expected military gain is clear: still enforce the absolute treaty ban on poison gas. {#military-gain-never-waives-gas-ban}
:::
