## Core Concept

### Article III and Domestic Guarantees

Article III of the $1987$ Constitution is the **Bill of Rights**. It holds $22$ sections inside one article limiting government power. **Civil and political rights** protect liberty, fairness, and public participation.

International treaties bind states, while Article III supplies domestic guarantees. Both operate side by side, so domestic law never cancels treaty duties.

Locate two liberties inside the Bill of Rights:

1. Peaceful speech sits in Section $4$ of Article III.
2. Protection from forced confessions sits in Section $12$ of that same article.

Result: Both rights are distinct numbered sections inside Article III.

:::pair #article-three-sections-not-articles
Right: Count Article III as one article holding $22$ numbered sections.
Wrong: Calling the $22$ divisions clauses inside one section of Article III.
Each numbered division from Section $1$ to Section $22$ is its own section.
:::

:::check
Does adopting Article III in the Constitution wipe out the state's international human rights duties?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Domestic constitutional guarantees and international state duties coexist.
:::

### Section $1$: Due Process and Equal Protection

Section $1$ holds two core guarantees. **Due process** requires fair laws and fair steps, like notice and a hearing, before government takes life, liberty, or property.

**Equal protection** bars unequal treatment without a valid reason. A real difference tied to the law's purpose justifies treating groups differently.

Compare two city actions under Section $1$:

1. Canceling a stall permit without notice or hearing lacks fair steps, failing due process.
2. Requiring stricter brakes on cargo trucks than bicycles serves road safety, meeting equal protection.

Result: Match missing fair steps to due process and unfair group differences to equal protection.

:::why
Why pair due process and equal protection in Section $1$? Fair steps stop officials from taking rights in secret, while equal rules stop laws from picking unfair favorites.
:::

:::pair #due-process-versus-equal-protection
Right: Match missing notice or hearing to due process, and unfair grouping to equal protection.
Wrong: Calling a lack of fair procedures an equal protection issue.
Ask whether the defect is unfair procedure or unfair group distinction.
:::

:::check
A town doubles market fees only for stall owners born in October. Which Section $1$ guarantee fails?
:::answer
The answer is **Equal protection**. Birth month has no valid link to market fees.
:::

### Sections $4$ and $5$: Expression and Religion

Section $4$ protects speech, the press, peaceful assembly, and petitions to fix wrongs. Peaceful criticism of officials stays protected, while threats of violence do not.

Section $5$ protects religion. Government may not set up an official church, block free worship, or require a religious test for civil or political rights.

Test a town ban on a peaceful fare protest and a church rule for board voters:

1. Check the protest ban: peaceful criticism of fares is protected under Section $4$.
2. Check the voter rule: demanding church membership imposes an invalid religious test under Section $5$.

Result: The protest ban breaks Section $4$, and the voter rule breaks Section $5$.

:::pair #religious-test-barred-by-section-five
Right: Place the ban on religious tests for civil or political rights under Section $5$.
Wrong: Placing a ban on religious tests under Section $4$ on speech and assembly.
Section $4$ protects expression, while Section $5$ specifically forbids religious tests.
:::

:::check
Neighbors distribute flyers criticizing a city bridge budget with no threats. May police seize those flyers?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $4$ protects peaceful criticism of government policy.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Locating Domestic Liberties

A note says Article XIII holds the Bill of Rights and its $22$ clauses inside one section. Correct both errors.

**Solution:**

1. Check the article: Article III holds the Bill of Rights, while Article XIII creates the CHR.
2. Check the divisions: the $22$ numbered parts inside Article III are separate sections.

Answer: Article III holds the Bill of Rights and contains $22$ sections.

**Check:** Count Section $1$ through Section $22$ inside Article III.

### #2: Peaceful Protest Against a Mayor

Residents march peacefully to criticize a mayor's tax plan and deliver a petition. Officials stop them because signs criticize the plan. Is that lawful?

**Solution:**

1. Check the conduct: the march and petition are peaceful with no violence threats.
2. Apply Section $4$: criticism of officials keeps speech, assembly, and petition protection.

Answer: No, Section $4$ protects peaceful criticism and petitioning.

**Check:** Threats of violence lose protection, while peaceful policy criticism stays protected.

### #3: Stall Removal and Worship Ban

A city tears down a licensed kiosk without notice or hearing. Separately, a clerk bars an applicant from a local board poll because she belongs to no church. Check both acts.

**Solution:**

1. Check the kiosk removal: identify which Section $1$ rule governs missing notice and hearing.
2. Check the poll rule: identify which Article III section bars religious conditions on political rights.

:::try
Finish the last step: name the guarantee violated by each act and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **Due process in Section $1$ and religious freedom in Section $5$**. Taking property without fair steps breaks due process, and demanding church membership sets a religious test.

**Check:** Fair steps match Section $1$ due process, while religious conditions match Section $5$.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Counting Article III parts: identify Article III as $22$ sections inside one article. {#article-three-sections-not-articles}
- Missing notice or hearing: assign procedural defects to due process, not equal protection. {#due-process-versus-equal-protection}
- Religious condition on rights: match the religious-test ban to Section $5$, not Section $4$. {#religious-test-barred-by-section-five}
:::
