## Core Concept

### Inherent Rights and Nondiscrimination

**Human rights** belong to every person simply by being human. They are **inherent** at birth and never require government permission.

**Universality** means rights cover everyone. **Nondiscrimination** forbids denying rights based on race, sex, language, religion, nationality, or residence. Listed traits are examples, never a closed list.

Critics, unpopular people, and accused persons still hold rights. Rights are never a reward for approved behavior.

Test this rule when a city library bars workers born in another province:

1. Check the basis: every person holds rights through human worth.
2. Check the trait: birthplace cannot strip basic rights.

Result: The library policy violates universality and nondiscrimination.

:::pair #inherent-means-human-worth
Right: Treat human rights as inherent to every person from human worth alone.
Wrong: Treating human rights as granted or renewed only by government permits or local rules.
Laws enforce rights, but being human is their foundation.
:::

:::check
A town clerk says newcomers must live there $3$ years before holding human rights. Is that valid?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Human rights are inherent regardless of residence length.
:::

### Government Duties in Peace and Conflict

Human rights law mainly places legal duties on the state and its offices. Those duties continue in both peace and armed conflict.

When a state breaks a duty, the victim's rights still exist. Failure to protect never erases a right.

Some rights allow lawful limits. Continuous duty does not mean every claim wins the same result.

Apply this rule when border soldiers hold travelers during a clash without food:

1. Identify the duty bearer: state forces remain bound by human rights duties.
2. Check timing: armed conflict never erases the human rights framework.

Result: State duties continue during conflict, and mistreatment violates them without erasing the travelers' rights.

:::why
Why does human rights law bind the state before private actors? Governments hold police, court, and prison power over everyone, so legal limits must restrain public power first.
:::

:::pair #duties-continue-in-conflict
Right: Apply the human rights framework in both peace and armed conflict.
Wrong: Treating human rights law as ending automatically once armed conflict starts.
Conflict adds humanitarian rules, yet state human rights duties remain active.
:::

:::check
During a security alert, an office claims human rights law stops until calm returns. Is that true?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Human rights duties continue during emergencies, though narrow lawful limits may apply.
:::

### The $1948$ UDHR and Community Duties

The United Nations adopted the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)** in $1948$ after World War II. The **Holocaust** was Nazi persecution and mass murder, including murdering Jews because they were Jews.

That history showed the danger of using state power to deny equal human worth. The UDHR preamble links equal dignity with freedom, justice, and peace. Note that the UDHR is an international declaration, while Article III is Philippine constitutional law.

People owe duties to their community. Still, no one may use a right or community duty to destroy another person's rights.

Test a speaker claiming free speech lets him organize threats against neighbors:

1. Check community duties: the UDHR expects respect for others' rights.
2. Check equal worth: speech freedom never allows destroying another person's rights.

Result: Threats lie outside protected rights under the UDHR.

:::pair #duties-respect-equal-worth
Right: Require community duties and rights claims to respect everyone's equal worth.
Wrong: Letting a majority use community duties to strip basic rights from a minority.
Community duties protect shared life without erasing any person's inherent worth.
:::

:::check
Does the $1948$ UDHR protect rights only for members of approved civic clubs?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. The UDHR rejects favored-group rules and protects everyone equally.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Reading the $1948$ Historical Lesson

A museum links the $1948$ UDHR preamble to World War II and the Holocaust. Why did that history shape the text?

**Solution:**

1. Wartime persecution denied equal human worth to whole groups, including Jews.
2. Linking dignity to freedom and peace rejects rights reserved for favored groups.

Answer: That history supports protecting every person equally from group abuse.

**Check:** Match $1948$ UDHR adoption after World War II to its rejection of favored-group privileges.

### #2: State Misconduct and Existing Rights

A provincial office blocks clean water to a village during a border dispute. Did villagers lose their rights?

**Solution:**

1. Identify the duty bearer: the provincial office is a bound state organ.
2. Separate breach from existence: violating a duty never erases inherent rights.

Answer: No, the office breached its duty while villagers kept their rights.

**Check:** A violation shows state misconduct, never the absence of rights.

### #3: Community Peace Against Critics

A council claims town peace lets residents strip basic rights from critics opposing a local tax. Check whether the UDHR allows that rule.

**Solution:**

1. Check community duties: test whether community duties permit erasing basic rights.
2. Check equal worth: test how nondiscrimination treats critics and unpopular views.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on whether the council's plan fits the UDHR and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **No, the UDHR bars stripping critics' rights**. Community duties must respect equal human worth, and criticism never erases rights.

**Check:** Community duties cannot destroy others' rights, and rights never depend on approval.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Rights look like local permits: treat human rights as inherent from birth. {#inherent-means-human-worth}
- Armed conflict starts: keep applying the human rights framework alongside conflict rules. {#duties-continue-in-conflict}
- Majority cites community duties: protect equal human worth for everyone. {#duties-respect-equal-worth}
:::
