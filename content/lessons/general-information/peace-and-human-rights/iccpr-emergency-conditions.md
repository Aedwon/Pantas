## Core Concept

### Qualifying Emergency and Official Proclamation

The **International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)** is a major human rights treaty. Under Article $4$, a state may temporarily pause some treaty duties through **derogation**.

Two gateway rules must hold first. The emergency must **threaten the life of the nation**, and the government must **officially proclaim** it.

An ordinary protest or routine political dispute never threatens the life of the nation. Neither severe danger without proclamation nor a proclamation over a routine rally unlocks Article $4$.

Test both gateway rules in $2$ situations:

1. A huge storm wrecks regional dams, yet ministers pause assembly rights silently without an official proclamation: proclamation is missing.
2. A mayor issues an official emergency decree solely to halt a peaceful wage march: the threat threshold fails.

Result: Neither situation permits derogation under Article $4(1)$.

:::pair #proclamation-always-required
Right: Require both a nation-threatening emergency and an official government proclamation before derogating.
Wrong: Assuming a grave danger alone removes the need for an official proclamation.
Article $4(1)$ requires an official proclamation so emergency powers stay public and accountable.
:::

:::pair #proclamation-cannot-cover-protests
Right: Limit derogation to emergencies threatening the life of the nation, never ordinary protests.
Wrong: Treating an official proclamation alone as permitting derogation during an ordinary protest.
A government announcement cannot turn routine public protests into a nation-threatening emergency.
:::

:::check
Severe floods cut national power for weeks, and officials quietly pause a derogable treaty duty without any official proclamation. Does Article $4$ allow that pause?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Even a nation-threatening emergency requires an official government proclamation before derogation.
:::

### Strict Limits, Nondiscrimination, and UN Notice

Even after a proclaimed qualifying emergency, every derogation measure must follow strict limits. First, the measure must be **strictly required** by the emergency, going no further and lasting no longer than needed.

Second, the state must follow all its **other international law duties**. Third, measures must never **discriminate solely** by race, color, sex, language, religion, or social origin.

Fourth, Article $4(3)$ requires immediate notice to other ICCPR states through the **United Nations Secretary-General**, naming affected duties and reasons. Another notice is required when suspension ends.

Finally, an official announcement never allows suspending non-derogable rights, such as the torture ban.

:::why
Why require both strict necessity and notice through the UN Secretary-General? Strict limits restrain power at home, while UN notice lets treaty states check what duties paused and when they resume.
:::

Check a state's $7$-day curfew during a proclaimed nationwide earthquake crisis:

1. Check scope and bias: the curfew covers only damaged districts for $7$ days and applies equally across all religions and languages.
2. Check notice and duties: the state notifies treaty states through the UN Secretary-General on Day $1$ and sends closing notice on Day $7$.

Result: The curfew satisfies Article $4$'s limits, nondiscrimination rule, and UN notice duties.

:::check
During a proclaimed qualifying emergency, a state limits travel strictly for $5$ days and notifies the UN Secretary-General immediately. Yet it restricts only speakers of one minority language. Does that satisfy Article $4$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article $4(1)$ forbids measures that discriminate solely by language or other listed traits.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Unproclaimed Cyberattack Detentions

A severe cyberattack shuts down national rail signals and port fuel lines across the country. Officials enforce emergency detentions without any official proclamation or UN notice, claiming the danger skips formalities. Is Article $4$ available?

**Solution:**

1. Check proclamation: Article $4(1)$ requires an official proclamation alongside a nation-threatening emergency.
2. Check notice: international notification through the UN Secretary-General is also missing.

Answer: No, Article $4$ derogation is unavailable without official proclamation and required notice.

**Check:** Verify both a qualifying threat and an official proclamation before testing other Article $4$ steps.

### #2: Proclamation Over a Street Festival

City leaders issue a formal emergency proclamation solely to shut down a noisy annual street festival and restrict public movement. They cite the proclamation as valid grounds for Article $4$ derogation. Does Article $4$ apply?

**Solution:**

1. Check emergency threshold: a noisy street festival does not threaten the life of the nation.
2. Check proclamation effect: announcing an emergency cannot turn an ordinary event into a qualifying threat.

Answer: No, an official proclamation alone cannot unlock Article $4$ without a nation-threatening crisis.

**Check:** Test the actual threat level first, not the proclamation label alone.

### #3: Checking a Proclaimed Pandemic Measure

During a proclaimed emergency threatening the life of the nation, a state pauses a derogable duty. The measure lasts $6$ months when $3$ weeks suffice, applies solely to one religious minority, and skips UN end-of-suspension notice. Identify the Article $4$ defects.

**Solution:**

1. Check length and nondiscrimination rules against the $6$-month duration and religious selection.
2. Check Article $4(3)$ notice duties regarding the start and end of suspension.

:::try
Finish the last step: list every Article $4$ defect in the state's measure and explain what must change.
:::answer
The answer is **The measure breaks necessity duration, nondiscrimination, and UN closing notice**. It must last no longer than strictly needed, drop sole selection by religion, and send closing notice via the UN Secretary-General.

**Check:** Check all Article $4$ filters: qualifying proclaimed threat, strict need and length, other international duties, nondiscrimination, and UN start and end notices.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Grave danger strikes: still issue an official proclamation before invoking Article $4$ derogation. {#proclamation-always-required}
- Official decree appears: confirm a threat to the nation's life rather than an ordinary protest. {#proclamation-cannot-cover-protests}
:::
