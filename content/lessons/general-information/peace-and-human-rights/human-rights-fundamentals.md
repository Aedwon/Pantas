## Core Concept

Human rights are inherent rights possessed by all human beings, without discrimination based on nationality, place of residence, sex, national or ethnic origin, color, religion, language, or any other status.

Human rights law primarily binds the state and its organs in their treatment of individuals at all times, during both peacetime and conflict.

### Domestic Constitutional Framework

- **Bill of Rights (Article III):** Serves as the primary domestic constitutional protection of fundamental civil and political liberties in the Philippines.
- **Commission on Human Rights (CHR):** An independent national human rights institution created under **Article XIII (Social Justice and Human Rights)** of the 1987 Constitution.

## Examples

### #1: Constitutional Basis of the Commission on Human Rights

A student is asked which article of the 1987 Constitution creates the Commission on Human Rights. Is it Article III, Article IX, or Article XIII?

**Solution:**
Match the institution to its constitutional source.

1. Article III is the Bill of Rights.
2. Article IX covers the Constitutional Commissions (CSC, COMELEC, COA).
3. Article XIII is titled Social Justice and Human Rights, which creates the Commission on Human Rights.
4. Therefore, the Commission on Human Rights is created under Article XIII.

### #2: Scope of Human Rights Law Application

Does human rights law apply only during peacetime, or does it bind the state at all times?

**Solution:**
Recall the core application rules of human rights law.

1. Human rights law governs the state's obligations to individuals.
2. It applies continuously at all times, including during peacetime and times of conflict.
3. Therefore, human rights law binds the state at all times.

### #3: Primary Domestic Guarantee of Rights

Which article of the Philippine Constitution serves as the primary domestic guarantee of individual civil and political rights?

**Solution:**
Identify the primary domestic constitutional bill of rights.

1. Article III of the 1987 Constitution is the Bill of Rights.
2. It contains 22 sections establishing fundamental civil and political liberties.
3. Therefore, Article III is the primary domestic guarantee of individual rights.
