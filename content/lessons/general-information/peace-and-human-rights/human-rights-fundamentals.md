## Core Concept

### What Human Rights Mean

**Human rights** are basic rights that belong to every person because each person has human dignity. They do not depend on nationality, sex, race, religion, language, or another status.

This is called **universality**. It means the same basic human rights belong to everyone without unfair discrimination.

Human rights law mainly places duties on governments and their offices. These duties continue in peace and during armed conflict.

Some rights can have lawful limits in special situations. This does not mean a government may remove rights whenever it wants. Any limit must follow the law and the rules that apply to that right.

### Rights and Duties in the Community

Having rights also means respecting the rights of other people. A person cannot use one right as an excuse to destroy another person's rights.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights says that everyone has duties to the community. It also allows lawful limits when they are needed to respect the rights of others or meet proper needs of a democratic society.

### The Philippine Bill of Rights

Article III of the $1987$ Constitution is the **Bill of Rights**. It has $22$ sections that protect important civil and political rights.

**Civil and political rights** protect personal freedom, fair treatment by government, and participation in public life. Examples include due process and equal protection, protection from unreasonable searches, freedom of speech and religion, and rights of people under investigation or accused of a crime.

**Due process** means the government must follow fair legal rules before taking a person's life, liberty, or property. **Equal protection** means the law must not treat people differently without a valid reason.

The Bill of Rights mainly limits government power. It does not mean every freedom has exactly the same scope in every situation. Some sections state conditions, and other lawful limits may apply.

### The Commission on Human Rights

The **Commission on Human Rights (CHR)** is an independent constitutional office created under Article XIII of the $1987$ Constitution. Independent means it is not placed under a department that may control its findings.

The CHR may investigate human rights violations involving civil and political rights. It may start an investigation on its own or act after a complaint by any party.

A person or organization may report a possible violation to the CHR, including through its public assistance channels or official online complaint system. The CHR first evaluates the report, then it may investigate the facts.

An investigation can include interviews and the review of documents, physical evidence, or digital evidence. The CHR process may lead to a case resolution, referral, legal assistance, counseling, or recommendations for protection and other proper measures.

The CHR investigates and helps protect rights. It does not replace a court that decides criminal guilt or other court cases.

### Historical Context

Modern human-rights rules were shaped by serious abuses during World War II. After the war, the United Nations adopted the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)** in $1948$ as a common standard of rights for all people.

The UDHR is an international declaration. The Philippine Bill of Rights is part of the Philippine Constitution. They are different sources, even though both protect human dignity and freedom.

## Examples

### #1: Universality and Nondiscrimination

A government office gives a public service to citizens of one religion but refuses the same service to otherwise qualified citizens of another religion. Which human-rights idea is most directly involved?

**Solution:**

1. Human rights belong to people without unfair discrimination based on religion or another status.
2. The government is treating otherwise qualified people differently because of religion.
3. The case therefore involves universality and nondiscrimination.

### #2: Due Process Protection

A government agency takes a person's property without giving the person the legal procedure required by law. Which Bill of Rights idea is most directly involved?

**Solution:**

1. Due process requires the government to follow fair legal rules before taking life, liberty, or property.
2. The agency skipped the required legal procedure before taking the property.
3. The protection most directly involved is due process under Article III.

### #3: Filing a CHR Complaint

A person reports that a government officer violated a civil right and sends the facts and available supporting information to the CHR. What may the CHR do next within its constitutional role?

**Solution:**

1. The CHR may investigate human rights violations involving civil and political rights.
2. It may act on a complaint by any party or begin an investigation on its own.
3. The CHR may evaluate the report, investigate the facts, and provide or recommend proper assistance or protection.
4. The CHR investigation does not itself replace a court proceeding.

### #4: Article III or Article XIII

Which part of the $1987$ Constitution should you identify for each item: the Bill of Rights and the creation of the Commission on Human Rights?

**Solution:**

1. Article III contains the Bill of Rights and its $22$ sections.
2. Article XIII creates the Commission on Human Rights and states its constitutional powers and functions.
3. Therefore, match Article III with the Bill of Rights and Article XIII with the CHR.

### #5: Historical Human-Rights Source

A question asks which international declaration was adopted by the United Nations in $1948$ as a common standard of human rights after World War II. What is the answer?

**Solution:**

1. The source is an international declaration, not the Philippine Constitution.
2. The United Nations adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in $1948$.
3. The answer is the UDHR.

### #6: Rights and Community Duties

A person claims that freedom means he may ignore the rights of everyone else. Is that a correct human-rights principle?

**Solution:**

1. Human rights protect each person, but other people have rights too.
2. The UDHR recognizes duties to the community and lawful limits for protecting the rights of others and proper community needs.
3. The claim is incorrect because one person's freedom is not permission to destroy another person's rights.

## Speed Tricks & Shortcuts

When a question asks for the constitutional source, separate the two articles first. **Article III** points to the Bill of Rights. **Article XIII** points to the Commission on Human Rights.

For an international source, look for the **UDHR** and the year $1948$. Do not confuse it with the Philippine Bill of Rights.

## Traps to Watch For

### #1: Article III and Article XIII Are Not the Same

> Do not place the CHR inside Article III.

Article III is the Bill of Rights. Article XIII creates the CHR and gives it constitutional powers and functions.

### #2: Universal Does Not Mean Unlimited

> A right belonging to everyone does not mean every right has no lawful limit.

Universality answers who has human rights. The scope and limits of a particular right are a different question and must follow the law that governs that right.

### #3: CHR Investigation Is Not a Court Judgment

> Do not treat an investigation by the CHR as a criminal conviction.

The CHR investigates human rights violations and can provide or recommend assistance and protective measures. Courts decide criminal guilt and other matters that require a judicial judgment.

### #4: UDHR and the Philippine Constitution Are Different Sources

> Do not answer UDHR when a question asks for the Philippine Bill of Rights.

The UDHR is an international declaration adopted in $1948$. The Philippine Bill of Rights is Article III of the $1987$ Constitution.
