## Core Concept

### Article XIII Source and Independence

Article XIII, Section $17(1)$ of the $1987$ Constitution creates the **Commission on Human Rights (CHR)** as an **independent office**. It is a permanent national body, not a temporary panel or private club.

Keep articles distinct. Article III holds Bill of Rights rules, Article IX creates CSC, COMELEC, and COA, and Article XIII creates CHR. Independence lets CHR probe government acts without executive control.

Check two constitutional mix-ups:

1. Seeing "Commission" in CHR and picking Article IX: wrong, Article XIII creates CHR.
2. Mixing Bill of Rights with CHR: Article III sets rules, while Article XIII creates the office.

Result: Trace CHR to Article XIII, separate from Article III and Article IX.

:::pair #chr-belongs-to-article-thirteen
Right: Trace CHR's creation to Article XIII, Section $17(1)$, despite the word "Commission."
Wrong: Placing CHR under Article IX simply because its title uses commission.
Check the constitutional source instead of guessing from an office's title.
:::

:::check
A barangay flyer claims Article XIII lists the $22$ Bill of Rights sections and Article III created the independent CHR. Fix both article numbers.
:::answer
The answer is **Swap them: Article III lists the Bill of Rights, and Article XIII created the CHR**. Rules sit in Article III, while the office sits in Article XIII.
:::

### Investigation, Protection, and Referral

Under Article XIII, Section $18$, CHR investigates **civil and political rights** violations on its own or on complaint by **any party**. It also visits jails and detention facilities.

CHR gives preventive measures, protective steps, and **legal aid** to needy victims. Still, CHR is **not** a court and never convicts or sentences anyone. When prosecution is needed, CHR sends a **referral** to the Department of Justice or proper prosecutor.

Track a CHR abuse probe at a lockup:

1. CHR visits the lockup and gathers records on its own or by complaint.
2. CHR gives legal aid and protection.
3. CHR refers criminal findings to prosecutors, leaving verdicts to courts.

Result: CHR investigates, protects, and refers without acting as a criminal judge.

:::why
Why let CHR investigate and refer instead of sentencing? Separating inquiry from trial lets CHR inspect abuses fast while leaving criminal verdicts to courts.
:::

:::pair #chr-investigates-never-sentences
Right: Limit CHR actions to investigating, protecting victims, giving legal aid, and referring cases.
Wrong: Giving CHR a court's power to convict or impose prison sentences.
CHR findings support referrals and aid, while only courts decide guilt and punishment.
:::

:::check
After investigating an unlawful arrest, CHR finds proof of a crime. Should CHR order prison or refer the case?
:::answer
The answer is **Refer the case to prosecutors**. CHR investigates and refers matters, while courts try guilt.
:::

### Reporting Known Facts and Evidence

Any victim, witness, relative, or group may report a violation at a CHR office or assistance channel. State known facts: what happened, where, when, who was involved, and help sought.

Save photos, messages, medical records, and witness contacts. Never invent missing facts, and never wait for every record before seeking help. Reporting opens an inquiry without guaranteeing a fixed result.

Apply this when a neighbor sees officers beat a vendor:

1. Save clinic bills, phone videos, and witness contacts now.
2. Report observed facts without guessing badge numbers, and ask staff what else is needed.

Result: Prompt reporting with true facts and saved records lets CHR act quickly.

:::pair #report-known-facts-without-delay
Right: Report known facts and saved records now, and ask staff what else is needed.
Wrong: Waiting until every possible record is collected before asking CHR for help.
Any party may report now with available facts instead of delaying help.
:::

:::check
A volunteer has clinic receipts for a bruised detainee, without the officer's full name. Should she guess a likely name?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Report only known facts, save the receipts, and ask staff what else they need.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Matching Articles and Institutions

A note calls CHR a temporary unit inside the justice department under Article IX. Name the errors.

**Solution:**

1. Check status: Article XIII makes CHR a permanent, independent national office.
2. Check article: Article XIII creates CHR, not Article IX.

Answer: CHR is a permanent, independent national body under Article XIII.

**Check:** Separate Article XIII (CHR) from Article III (Bill of Rights) and Article IX (three commissions).

### #2: Jail Visit and Legal Aid

During a prison visit, CHR finds an indigent inmate who suffered beatings and lacks counsel. What fits CHR's powers?

**Solution:**

1. Check powers: CHR may investigate, inspect the jail, and give legal aid and protection.
2. Check prosecution: CHR may refer criminal findings to prosecutors without sentencing anyone.

Answer: CHR may investigate, give legal aid and protection, and refer criminal matters.

**Check:** Inquiry, protection, legal aid, and referral are CHR powers; sentencing belongs to courts.

### #3: Teacher Witnessing Custody Abuse

A teacher sees police beat an arrested student outside a precinct. She saves clinic bills and two witness contacts, yet plans to wait $6$ months for every police log first. She also expects CHR to jail the officers directly. Address both plans.

**Solution:**

1. Check timing: test waiting $6$ months for every log against CHR reporting rules.
2. Check CHR power: test expecting CHR imprisonment against CHR's inquiry and referral role.

:::try
Finish the last step: explain what the teacher should do now and what CHR can order.
:::answer
The answer is **Report known facts now with clinic bills and witness contacts**. CHR can investigate, give aid, and refer the case, while only courts impose jail sentences.

**Check:** Report known facts promptly with saved evidence, and separate CHR referral from court sentencing.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Office is named a commission: trace CHR to Article XIII, Section $17(1)$, not Article IX. {#chr-belongs-to-article-thirteen}
- Inquiry finds criminal abuse: refer the case to prosecutors instead of sentencing. {#chr-investigates-never-sentences}
- Records seem incomplete: report known facts and saved evidence now instead of waiting. {#report-known-facts-without-delay}
:::
