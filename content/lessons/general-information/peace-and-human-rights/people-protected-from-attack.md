## Core Concept

### Civilians and Direct Participation

**Civilians** are people outside armed forces and fighting forces covered by IHL. They must never be directly attacked.

Under Additional Protocol I, Article $51(3)$, a civilian loses attack protection only **for such time** as they directly participate in fighting. Protection returns once fighting stops.

Weapons and attack limits still govern any force used during participation.

Test a civilian driver across $2$ moments:

1. Hauling rockets directly to an active firing line: protection pauses.
2. Farming unarmed $2$ days later: protection applies again.

Result: Loss of protection lasts only during direct participation.

:::pair #direct-participation-is-temporary
Right: Remove civilian protection only while direct participation continues.
Wrong: Treating direct participation as permanently ending civilian protection forever after.
Once direct participation stops, protection against direct attack returns.
:::

:::check
A mechanic joins an ambush, drops his rifle, and bakes bread unarmed $3$ days later. May forces target him there?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Protection from direct attack is lost only during direct participation, not permanently afterward.
:::

### Persons Hors de Combat

A person **hors de combat** is out of the fight. Three cases qualify: held by the opponent, clearly surrendering, or incapacitated by wounds or illness.

Protection requires zero hostile acts and zero escape attempts. Fighting again ends surrender protection, though force limits still apply.

:::why
Why protect fighters who surrender or are wounded? Once a fighter is out of combat, attacking them serves no military need and causes needless death.
:::

Check an enemy rifleman dropping his weapon:

1. Standing unarmed with raised hands: clearly surrendering and protected.
2. Grabbing a fallen pistol to shoot guards: hostile act ends surrender protection.

Result: Surrender protects him only while he avoids hostile acts and escape.

:::pair #hors-de-combat-excludes-active-attack
Right: Grant hors de combat protection only to captured, surrendering, or incapacitated fighters without hostile acts.
Wrong: Treating an uninjured fighter directing an ongoing attack as out of combat.
Directing an active assault keeps an uninjured fighter outside hors de combat protection.
:::

:::check
An uninjured enemy spotter hides unarmed behind a wall, radioing mortar coordinates onto troops. Is he hors de combat?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Directing an ongoing attack is active participation, not being out of combat.
:::

### Humane Treatment and Care in Custody

Detainees in custody must receive humane treatment respecting basic needs and dignity. Torture and execution without trial are forbidden.

Under Common Article $3$, wounded and sick people must receive needed care without unfair discrimination. Opposing-side status never justifies denying care.

Apply custody rules at a field clinic:

1. Captured enemy fighters are protected from abuse.
2. Wounded prisoners receive care by medical need, not side.

Result: Custody requires humane care, zero torture, and fair treatment.

:::pair #enemy-side-still-gets-care
Right: Give humane treatment and needed care to wounded prisoners regardless of side.
Wrong: Withholding medical care from a wounded prisoner because they belong to the opposing side.
Opposing-side status never removes humane treatment and medical care duties.
:::

:::check
After a battle, medics refuse bandages to a wounded prisoner because he fought for the enemy. Does that follow IHL?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Wounded people in custody must receive needed medical care without unfair discrimination.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Part-Time Fighter Back at Work

A civilian guides mortar fire with a laser targeter during a clash. One week later, he repairs a town library roof unarmed. May troops target him on the roof?

**Solution:**

1. Check timing: guiding mortars was direct participation at that moment.
2. Check current status: $1$ week later, he is unarmed and out of fighting.

Answer: No, civilian protection returned once direct participation stopped.

**Check:** Limit loss of protection strictly to the time of direct participation.

### #2: Escape Attempt From a Holding Yard

Captured enemy soldiers sit unarmed inside a guarded holding yard. One detainee breaks through the fence at night and sprints toward nearby enemy lines. Does his captured status still keep him hors de combat during the escape?

**Solution:**

1. Check custody status: capture initially placed him hors de combat and protected from attack.
2. Check escape rule: attempting to escape breaks the conditions required for hors de combat protection.

Answer: No, an escape attempt suspends hors de combat protection, though force limits still apply.

**Check:** Confirm hors de combat requires zero hostile acts and zero escape attempts.

### #3: Wounded Enemy Officer in a Clinic

Troops capture a wounded enemy commander after he drops his rifle. A guard proposes withholding pain medicine and beating him for patrol maps. Rule on his status and treatment.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether capture and wounds qualify him as hors de combat in custody.
2. Check how IHL treats withholding care and beatings against opposing prisoners.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the commander's status and both guard actions.
:::answer
The answer is **He is protected, and both actions are forbidden**. Capture and wounds place him hors de combat in custody, requiring humane care, zero torture, and needed medicine regardless of side.

**Check:** Prior combat role never strips a wounded prisoner of humane care or torture protection.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Civilian joins fighting: remove attack protection only while direct participation continues. {#direct-participation-is-temporary}
- Fighter is uninjured and active: never treat directing or firing in an assault as hors de combat. {#hors-de-combat-excludes-active-attack}
- Prisoner is from the enemy side: still provide humane treatment and needed medical care. {#enemy-side-still-gets-care}
:::
