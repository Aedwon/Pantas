## Core Concept

### Concurrent Application of HRL and IHL

HRL and IHL are **complementary**, meaning they protect people together. During armed conflict, IHL gives specific rules for fighting while HRL keeps restraining unlawful state action.

IHL never erases HRL duties across the board. Both frameworks govern detainees held in custody.

A fighter in custody is never a battlefield target merely because war continues nearby. Both HRL and IHL forbid torture and require humane care.

Test concurrent rules when guards hold a captured fighter $20$ kilometers behind the front:

1. Check HRL: state human rights duties stay active during war.
2. Check IHL custody rules: detainees are out of combat and protected from abuse.

Result: Both HRL and IHL shield the detainee from torture and mistreatment.

:::pair #ihl-never-replaces-hrl
Right: Apply HRL alongside IHL during armed conflict rather than treating war as ending human rights.
Wrong: Treating IHL as automatically replacing all HRL duties once armed conflict starts.
IHL provides combat rules, while HRL keeps protecting people from unlawful state conduct.
:::

:::check
At a wartime prison camp, a captain says IHL suspends HRL so guards may beat detainees. Is that right?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. HRL and IHL apply together in custody, and both forbid torture and abuse.
:::

### Assessing Battlefield Right-to-Life Claims

**Assess** means judge a claim and give reasons for that judgment. Under HRL, the right to life forbids **arbitrary killing**.

A killing can be arbitrary even when domestic law allows it. The test checks fairness, proper legal steps, and whether force was needed, reasonable, and not excessive.

During fighting, IHL rules on target status, weapons, distinction, proportionality, and precautions help decide if taking life breaks the right to life. Attacking an active opposing combatant in an IAC is not automatically unlawful, though attack limits still bind.

:::why
Why use IHL attack rules for battlefield right-to-life claims? IHL was built for active combat, so its targeting and weapon limits show when combat force is non-arbitrary.
:::

Evaluate a strike against an active opposing artillery post during an IAC:

1. Check target status: the post is an active opposing combatant target, not a detainee or civilian.
2. Check attack limits: troops use lawful weapons and follow proportionality and precautions.

Result: The strike is not automatically unlawful because target status and IHL limits hold.

:::pair #battlefield-deaths-follow-ihl-rules
Right: Check target status and IHL attack rules when assessing a battlefield right-to-life claim.
Wrong: Treating every battlefield death as an automatic right-to-life violation regardless of target status.
Active opposing combatants may be lawfully targeted when IHL weapon and attack limits hold.
:::

:::pair #domestic-approval-never-suffices
Right: Test lethal force against IHL and HRL standards even when national law approves it.
Wrong: Accepting national authorization alone as proof that a battlefield killing is lawful.
A domestic decree never excuses an arbitrary killing or a breach of IHL attack rules.
:::

:::check
During an IAC, a domestic statute authorizes firing on anyone near a rail bridge without checking target status. Does that statute satisfy the right to life?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Domestic permission never replaces checking target status and IHL attack limits against arbitrary killing.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Denying Custody Safeguards During War

During a conflict, guards hold a local truck driver far from fighting. They deny him clean water and block any court review of his detention. An officer argues IHL erased HRL once war started. Is that valid?

**Solution:**

1. Check continuing HRL: human rights duties keep restraining unlawful state custody during armed conflict.
2. Check IHL custody rules: IHL also requires humane conditions and basic dignity for detainees.

Answer: No, HRL and IHL work together to protect detainees in state custody.

**Check:** Confirm that armed conflict adds IHL rules without erasing HRL safeguards for detainees.

### #2: Strike on an Active Tank Crew

During an IAC, artillery shells an active opposing tank crew firing from a ridge. It uses lawful shells and confirms no civilians are nearby. One lawyer says the right to life bans every combat killing, while another says war ends HRL. Assess both views.

**Solution:**

1. Check HRL status: the right to life continues in war and bars arbitrary killing.
2. Check battlefield test: IHL attack rules help decide whether combat force is arbitrary.
3. Apply to facts: the active tank crew is not automatically immune, yet attack limits still bind.

Answer: Both lawyers are wrong because HRL continues while IHL rules govern active combat strikes.

**Check:** Pair continuing HRL with target status and IHL attack rules in combat assessments.

### #3: Checking a Domestic Checkpoint Order

A national decree authorizes troops in an IAC to fire on any vehicle approaching a coastal checkpoint without checking target status. A commander says national approval settles HRL compliance, while a critic says HRL bars all wartime force. Assess both claims.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether domestic approval alone prevents a killing from being arbitrary under HRL and IHL.
2. Check whether HRL forbids every battlefield strike regardless of target status and IHL rules.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the commander's claim and the critic's claim with reasons.
:::answer
The answer is **Both claims fail**. National decrees never replace checking target status and IHL attack rules, and HRL does not ban lawful attacks on active combatants.

**Check:** War never ends HRL, domestic decrees never waive arbitrary-killing checks, and active combat follows IHL rules.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- War begins: apply HRL alongside IHL rather than dropping human rights duties. {#ihl-never-replaces-hrl}
- Battlefield claim arises: check target status and IHL attack rules instead of calling every death unlawful. {#battlefield-deaths-follow-ihl-rules}
- Domestic law allows force: still test whether the killing is arbitrary under HRL and IHL. {#domestic-approval-never-suffices}
:::
