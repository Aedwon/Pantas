## Core Concept

### Peacetime IHL Duties and Training

Although IHL governs armed conflict, several implementation duties require action during peacetime. Under Article $127$ of the Third Geneva Convention, states must teach the Convention's rules in **peace and war**, including military instruction.

Authorities responsible for prisoners of war must possess the Convention text and receive special instruction. Under Articles $129$ and $130$, states must also pass laws punishing **grave breaches**, such as torturing a protected prisoner.

Advance preparation builds compliance before fighting starts. Teaching treaty rules or passing grave-breach statutes never turns ordinary peace into an armed conflict.

:::why
Why require IHL training and laws during peace instead of waiting for war? Once combat starts, urgency leaves no time to draft criminal laws or instruct prison authorities properly.
:::

Check a peaceful nation updating military manuals with Third Geneva Convention rules and passing grave-breach laws:

1. Check treaty duties: Article $127$ requires peacetime teaching and military instruction, and Article $129$ requires grave-breach laws.
2. Check conflict status: meeting preparation duties never turns peace into armed conflict.

Result: The state fulfills binding peacetime IHL preparation duties without creating an armed conflict.

:::pair #peacetime-duties-require-preparation
Right: Carry out IHL teaching, military instruction, and grave-breach legislation during peacetime.
Wrong: Assuming IHL requires zero preparation, training, or lawmaking before fighting starts.
Advance preparation builds compliance before conflict while HRL keeps governing peacetime life.
:::

:::check
While completely at peace, a parliament enacts criminal penalties for grave breaches against protected prisoners and funds officer training. Is that consistent with IHL?
:::answer
The answer is **Yes**. Peacetime legislation and training satisfy Third Geneva Convention Articles $127$ and $129$ without creating an armed conflict.
:::

### Practical Community Peacebuilding Steps

While IHL limits harm in conflict, peaceful dispute resolution seeks to settle disagreements early and prevent violence. Any community agreement must still respect basic human rights, since ICCPR Article $5(1)$ bars using agreements to destroy others' rights.

As practical advice rather than a treaty procedure, community groups can follow $4$ clear steps:

1. Hear each side and define the issue clearly.
2. Ask what each side needs rather than stopping at rigid demands.
3. Discuss lawful options that respect basic human rights.
4. Set clear steps and a way to check compliance.

Holding peaceful community talks never proves that an armed conflict legally exists. Armed conflict requires the separate IAC or NIAC tests.

Test these practical steps when youth athletes and senior walkers both want a barangay hall at $6$ AM:

1. Hear needs: athletes need court space for drills, while seniors need quiet, safe walking space.
2. Set clear terms: assign alternating mornings on a posted board with a weekly barangay check-in.

Result: Clear steps and compliance checks settle needs lawfully without ignoring rights.

:::pair #clear-terms-and-rights-in-peace-talks
Right: Agree on clear steps and compliance checks that respect both sides' needs and basic rights.
Wrong: Leaving agreement terms unclear so each group follows its own separate understanding.
Vague promises undermine compliance instead of giving shared community agreements clear steps and checks.
:::

:::check
Two neighborhood clubs dispute park gate hours. After hearing needs and lawful options, one organizer leaves closing rules unwritten so each club guesses, and claims holding talks proves a NIAC. Identify the $2$ errors.
:::answer
The answer is **Terms lack clear steps and checks, and community talks never prove a NIAC**. Practical agreements need clear terms and compliance checks, while NIAC status needs intense fighting and organized armed groups.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Prison-Guard Training During Peace

A peaceful state prints the Third Geneva Convention for prison officers and trains soldiers on detainee rules. A commentator claims IHL forbids any action during peace. Assess that claim.

**Solution:**

1. Check Article $127$: states must disseminate the Convention in peace and war and instruct authorities handling prisoners.
2. Check Article $129$: enacting grave-breach laws also requires advance peacetime preparation.

Answer: The commentator is wrong because IHL requires advance peacetime teaching and legislation.

**Check:** Separate IHL's armed-conflict trigger from its peacetime preparation duties under Articles $127$ and $129$.

### #2: Fixing a Vague Generator Note

Two committees write a vague memo promising to share a clinic generator "fairly," with no hours or review plan. Soon they argue over fuel and runtime. What is missing from their draft?

**Solution:**

1. Check clarity: "fairly" leaves each committee guessing who runs the generator and when.
2. Check compliance: the draft omits written operating hours, fuel duties, and a review check-in.

Answer: The draft needs clear agreed schedule steps and a way to check compliance while respecting rights.

**Check:** Replace vague promises with specific steps, compliance checks, and respect for basic rights.

### #3: Sunday Cleanup Dispute at the Town Market

Shop owners and street vendors argue over Sunday market cleanup. Both sides heard each other's needs and discussed lawful ways to share cleaning duties. One leader proposes letting the larger shop group dictate all future rules with no review plan, while another claims holding these talks establishes a NIAC. Assess both proposals.

**Solution:**

1. Check whether letting the stronger group impose all future rules fits practical community agreement steps.
2. Check whether holding peaceful community talks satisfies the legal tests for a NIAC.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the one-sided control proposal and the NIAC claim.
:::answer
The answer is **Both proposals fail**. Practical agreements need clear steps and compliance checks respecting both groups' needs and rights, and peaceful talks never establish an armed conflict.

**Check:** Require clear mutually checked steps in community talks, and keep legal NIAC tests separate from peaceful mediation.
:::
