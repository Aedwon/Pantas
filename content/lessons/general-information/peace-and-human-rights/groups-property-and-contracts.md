## Core Concept

### Sections $8$ and $9$: Lawful Groups and Just Compensation

Article III, Section $8$ protects the right of public and private workers to form unions, associations, or groups for **lawful purposes**. Officials cannot ban peaceful, legal groups.

Section $9$ bars taking private property for **public use** without **just compensation**, meaning fair payment. Taking property also requires due process under Section $1$.

Check two city actions against these rules:

1. Dissolving a lawful neighborhood mutual-aid club breaks Section $8$ association rights.
2. Taking a farmer's strip for a public canal without fair pay breaks Section $9$.

Result: Separate group bans under Section $8$ from unpaid public takings under Section $9$.

:::pair #separate-association-from-property
Right: Match bans on forming lawful unions or groups to Section $8$ association rights.
Wrong: Treating a ban on forming a lawful group as a Section $9$ compensation issue.
Ask whether officials blocked organizing a group or took private property for public use.
:::

:::check
Public-hospital nurses form a lawful study and welfare club. Which Article III section protects their right to organize?
:::answer
The answer is **Section $8$**. It protects forming unions and groups in public and private sectors for lawful purposes.
:::

### Section $10$: Contracts and Public Welfare

Article III, Section $10$ bars passing any law weakening duties under existing **contracts**. A **contract** is an agreement creating binding legal duties.

This rule is not absolute. A valid law enacted for public health, safety, morals, or general welfare may still affect existing contract terms.

Compare two laws affecting signed warehouse leases:

1. Requiring fire sprinklers in warehouses protects public safety, remaining valid under police power.
2. Canceling rent solely to reward a political ally lacks public welfare, violating Section $10$.

Result: Section $10$ blocks arbitrary contract impairment while allowing genuine public-welfare laws.

:::why
Why may a public-safety law alter older contract terms? Private agreements cannot stop the state from passing valid health and safety rules for everyone.
:::

:::check
A city law bans lead paint in rental units, even inside older leases. Does Section $10$ strike down that health law?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Valid laws protecting public health and safety may affect existing contract terms.
:::

### Section $11$: Free Access to Courts

Article III, Section $11$ guarantees free access to courts and quasi-judicial bodies, plus adequate legal help. No one may lose access **by reason of poverty**.

This right stands alone. A person never needs a contract or group membership to seek court help.

Apply Section $11$ when an indigent widow cannot pay filing fees for a boundary claim:

1. Check the barrier: refusal rests on poverty.
2. Apply Section $11$: poverty cannot block court access or legal aid.

Result: The court must grant indigent access rather than turning her away.

:::pair #poverty-never-bars-court-access
Right: Protect court access and legal aid regardless of poverty, with no contract prerequisite.
Wrong: Requiring an existing contract before poverty protection applies to court access.
Section $11$ protects any poor litigant directly, without needing any prior contract.
:::

:::check
A labor board turns away an unpaid helper's wage claim because he cannot afford a lawyer. Does Section $11$ allow that refusal?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Poverty must not block access to quasi-judicial bodies or legal aid.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Taking Land Without Process or Payment

A city builds a public ditch across a family's lot. It skips legal notice and refuses fair market pay. Which protections apply?

**Solution:**

1. Check missing notice: skipping fair steps breaks due process under Section $1$.
2. Check unpaid taking: taking land for public use without just compensation breaks Section $9$.

Answer: Both Section $1$ due process and Section $9$ just compensation are violated.

**Check:** Match missing fair steps to Section $1$ and unpaid taking to Section $9$.

### #2: Health Rule Versus Lawful Group Ban

A statute requires sanitation upgrades in older food stalls for public health. Separately, an office bans staff from forming a legal savings cooperative. Rule on both.

**Solution:**

1. Check the upgrade law: a valid public-health rule affecting leases works alongside Section $10$.
2. Check the group ban: barring a lawful staff club violates Section $8$ association rights.

Answer: The health rule is valid, while banning the cooperative violates Section $8$.

**Check:** Public health can affect contracts under Section $10$, while Section $8$ protects lawful groups.

### #3: Club Ban and Filing-Fee Refusal

A clinic director bars technicians from joining a lawful mutual-aid club. A local tribunal also refuses an indigent tenant's complaint solely for unpaid filing fees. Rule on both refusals.

**Solution:**

1. Check the club ban: test whether Section $8$ or Section $9$ governs forming a lawful group.
2. Check the fee refusal: test whether Section $11$ lets a tribunal require fees or extra conditions from poor litigants.

:::try
Finish the last step: name the rule broken by each refusal and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **Section $8$ for the club ban and Section $11$ for the fee refusal**. Section $8$ protects lawful groups, and Section $11$ bars blocking court access by poverty.

**Check:** Separate association rights (Section $8$) from poverty-free tribunal access (Section $11$).
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Ban stops a lawful group: cite Section $8$ association, not Section $9$ property pay. {#separate-association-from-property}
- Poor litigant seeks court help: apply Section $11$ directly without any contract prerequisite. {#poverty-never-bars-court-access}
:::
