## Core Concept

### Sections $18$ and $19$: Political Beliefs, Forced Labor, and Humane Treatment

Article III, Section $18$ bars detaining anyone solely for **political beliefs and aspirations**. It bans **involuntary servitude** (forced labor) except as punishment after lawful criminal conviction. Separately, Article II, Section $4$ allows laws requiring military or civil defense service.

Section $19$ bans excessive fines and **cruel, degrading, or inhuman punishment**. It protects all detainees and prisoners from abuse and subhuman conditions, even after conviction.

Check two jail abuses:

1. Forcing unconvicted detainees to pave a warden's driveway breaks Section $18$.
2. Beating sentenced inmates or denying medical care breaks Section $19$.

Result: Lawful imprisonment never removes humane treatment protections.

:::pair #prisoners-keep-abuse-protection
Right: Protect all detainees and prisoners from cruel or degrading treatment under Section $19$.
Wrong: Treating a lawful conviction as permitting every punishment chosen by prison staff.
Section $19$ binds jail staff even after a valid court sentence.
:::

:::check
An Article II, Section $4$ statute calls citizens to civil defense during invasion. Does Section $18$ strike it down?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Article II, Section $4$ allows laws requiring military or civil service to defend the State.
:::

### Section $20$: Debt, Poll Taxes, and Separate Crimes

Article III, Section $20$ bars imprisonment for **debt** or nonpayment of a **poll tax** (a per-person tax). Honest inability to repay a loan cannot bring jail time.

Still, Section $20$ never excuses a separately proved crime involving money. Proven criminal fraud differs from unpaid debt.

Compare two money cases:

1. A tailor misses rent after a flood with no fraud: civil debt, barring jail.
2. A broker runs a proved scam using fake receipts: separate crime, allowing prison.

Result: Separate unpaid debt or poll tax from a proved crime.

:::why
Why bar jail for debt yet allow jail for fraud? Failing to repay a loan is a civil default, while fraud involves proved criminal deceit.
:::

:::pair #separate-crime-differs-from-debt
Right: Separate unpaid debt or poll tax from a separately proved crime involving money.
Wrong: Treating every crime involving money as protected civil debt under Section $20$.
Money alone never turns proved fraud into debt; check for a proved crime.
:::

:::check
A worker loses work and misses his municipal poll tax, with no separate crime. May a court jail him for that tax?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Section $20$ bars imprisonment for debt or unpaid poll tax.
:::

### Section $22$: Ex Post Facto Laws and Bills of Attainder

Article III, Section $22$ bans two statutes. An **ex post facto law** is a later criminal law punishing an earlier lawful act or raising punishment for an earlier crime.

A **bill of attainder** is a statute inflicting punishment on named or identifiable people **without a judicial trial**.

Check two $2026$ statutes:

1. Punishing $2024$ peaceful vending retroactively is an ex post facto law.
2. Declaring three named organizers guilty and seizing pensions without trial is a bill of attainder.

Result: Match backward criminal rules to ex post facto bans, and non-trial legislative punishment to bills of attainder.

:::pair #ex-post-facto-vs-attainder
Right: Label a statute punishing people without a court trial as a bill of attainder.
Wrong: Calling legislative punishment without trial an ex post facto law solely because Congress passed it.
Check whether the flaw is backward criminal punishment or punishment without trial.
:::

:::check
A $2026$ law raises prison terms from $2$ to $4$ years for $2024$ thefts. Which Section $22$ ban applies?
:::answer
The answer is **The ban on ex post facto laws**. Later laws cannot raise punishment for earlier crimes.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Matching Detention and Labor Rules

Match four acts: jailing a blogger for political views; forcing unconvicted detainees to farm private land; a ₱$5{,}000{,}000$ littering fine; denying poor court access.

**Solution:**

1. Political detention and unconvicted forced labor break Section $18$.
2. The ₱$5{,}000{,}000$ littering fine breaks Section $19$'s excessive-fines ban.
3. Denying court access for poverty breaks Section $11$.

Answer: Sections $18$, $19$, and $11$ cover those acts.

**Check:** Match political detention and labor to Section $18$, excessive fines to Section $19$, and poverty barriers to Section $11$.

### #2: Business Loss Versus Criminal Fraud

A baker borrows ₱$50{,}000$ for flour, suffers floods, and misses payment. A clerk is convicted of proved estafa for stealing payroll cash with fake signatures. May either be jailed?

**Solution:**

1. Check the baker: missing an ordinary loan payment is unpaid debt.
2. Check the clerk: estafa is a separately proved crime.

Answer: Section $20$ shields the baker from jail, while the clerk may be jailed for estafa.

**Check:** Check whether a separate crime was proved beyond inability to repay.

### #3: Two Statutes in One Session

A council passes Ordinance A punishing last year's lawful overnight parking. It also passes Ordinance B naming four vendors and barring permits without trial. Rule on both.

**Solution:**

1. Check Ordinance A: test whether it punishes an earlier lawful act retroactively.
2. Check Ordinance B: test whether punishing named people without trial fits ex post facto or bill of attainder.

:::try
Finish the last step: classify Ordinance A and Ordinance B under Section $22$ and explain why.
:::answer
The answer is **Ordinance A is an ex post facto law, and Ordinance B is a bill of attainder**. Ordinance A acts retroactively, while Ordinance B inflicts legislative punishment without trial.

**Check:** Match retroactive criminal rules to ex post facto laws and non-trial penalties to bills of attainder.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Convicted prisoner faces abuse: keep Section $19$ protection against cruel treatment active. {#prisoners-keep-abuse-protection}
- Fraud case involves money: separate unpaid debt under Section $20$ from proved crime. {#separate-crime-differs-from-debt}
- Law punishes without trial: classify it as a bill of attainder under Section $22$. {#ex-post-facto-vs-attainder}
:::
