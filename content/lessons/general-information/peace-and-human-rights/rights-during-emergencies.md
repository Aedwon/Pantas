## Core Concept

### Ordinary Limits Versus Emergency Measures

A right may allow narrow limits under its own rules. For example, a lawful public-health travel limit under Article III, Section $6$ never suspends the Bill of Rights.

The **International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)** is a human rights treaty. Article $4$ allows suspending certain treaty duties only when five conditions hold:

1. An emergency **threatens the life of the nation**.
2. The emergency is **officially proclaimed**.
3. Measures stay strictly required in scope and time.
4. Measures follow other international law duties.
5. Measures never discriminate solely by race, color, sex, language, religion, or social origin.

Test a city statute closing flooded bridges during a typhoon:

1. Check the rule: closing unsafe bridges is an ordinary safety limit.
2. Check scope: speech and fair trial stay fully active.

Result: An ordinary limit leaves other rights intact.

:::pair #ordinary-limit-keeps-other-rights
Right: Keep other rights active when one right has a valid, law-based limit.
Wrong: Treating one lawful travel limit as suspending every other protection.
Check each right's own rules instead of treating one limit as a blanket suspension.
:::

:::check
Officials proclaim an emergency during a crisis and bar only one minority religion from leaving town. Does that follow Article $4$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. ICCPR Article $4$ forbids measures discriminating solely on religion or listed traits.
:::

### Article $4(3)$: Immediate International Notice

During a qualifying emergency, states cannot pause ICCPR duties secretly. Article $4(3)$ requires two notices via the **United Nations Secretary-General**:

1. **Immediately notify** treaty states of suspended duties and reasons.
2. **Send a closing notice** when suspension ends.

Local broadcasts or domestic memos alone fail Article $4(3)$.

Check a state's notices after proclaiming a qualifying emergency on May $1$:

1. On May $1$, it notifies treaty states via the UN Secretary-General, naming affected duties and reasons.
2. On May $20$, when calm returns, it sends notice ending the suspension.

Result: Both Article $4(3)$ notice duties are met.

:::why
Why require immediate notice via the UN Secretary-General? Public international notice lets treaty states check that emergency powers stay narrow and temporary.
:::

:::pair #notify-immediately-via-un-chief
Right: Notify treaty states immediately via the UN Secretary-General, and notify when suspension ends.
Wrong: Waiting until the emergency ends before sending any notice to treaty states.
Article $4(3)$ requires immediate start notice plus closing notice.
:::

:::check
After proclaiming a qualifying emergency on television, a cabinet tells only its justice ministry. Does that meet Article $4(3)$?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Notice must go immediately to treaty states via the UN Secretary-General.
:::

### Non-Derogable Rights: Life and Freedom from Torture

Article $4(2)$ names protections that **can never be suspended**, even during a qualifying emergency with UN notice. These include the **right to life** (Article $6$) and the **ban on torture** (Article $7$).

An emergency never permits **arbitrary killing**. That covers unlawful killing, skipping fair legal steps, and unneeded or excessive force, even if domestic law allows it.

Test an emergency decree letting police beat suspects for answers:

1. Check the act: beatings violate the Article $7$ torture ban.
2. Check Article $4(2)$: the torture ban allows zero suspension.

Result: The decree breaks the ICCPR despite the proclamation.

:::pair #torture-ban-never-suspended
Right: Enforce the torture ban at all times, even during a qualifying emergency.
Wrong: Treating an official emergency proclamation as allowing suspension of torture bans.
Article $4(2)$ bars suspending Article $7$ torture protections under any condition.
:::

:::check
During a proclaimed emergency, a local law shields officers who shoot unarmed fleeing shoplifters. Does that law stop the killing from being arbitrary?
:::answer
The answer is **No**. Unneeded or excessive lethal force remains arbitrary even if domestic law allows it.
:::

## Examples

### #1: Flood Curfew Versus ICCPR Emergency

A town passes a statutory $10$-hour flood curfew for safety. The mayor claims it suspends religious freedom. Is he right?

**Solution:**

1. Check the curfew: it is an ordinary safety limit under its own rule.
2. Check scope: one lawful travel limit never cancels religious freedom.

Answer: No, ordinary limits never suspend the Bill of Rights.

**Check:** Separate narrow statutory limits from ICCPR emergency rules and blanket suspensions.

### #2: Missing Closing Notice Under Article $4(3)$

A state proclaims a qualifying emergency and notifies treaty states immediately via the UN Secretary-General. Months later, it ends measures quietly with no second notice. Did it finish Article $4(3)$?

**Solution:**

1. Check start notice: immediate UN notice met the first rule.
2. Check end notice: Article $4(3)$ requires reporting when suspension ends.

Answer: No, the state missed the closing notice.

**Check:** Verify both start notice and closing notice via the UN Secretary-General.

### #3: Declared Emergency With Harsh Interrogation

After a severe attack threatens the nation, a state proclaims an emergency and sends immediate UN notice. Police beat detainees and shoot unarmed protesters posing no lethal threat. Rule on both practices under the ICCPR.

**Solution:**

1. Check non-derogable rules: test whether proclamation and UN notice allow suspending Article $7$ torture bans.
2. Check shootings: test shooting unarmed protesters against Article $6$ and arbitrary-killing rules.

:::try
Finish the last step: rule on the beatings and the shooting of unarmed protesters.
:::answer
The answer is **Both break non-derogable ICCPR rules**. Neither torture nor arbitrary killing with unneeded force can ever be suspended under Article $4(2)$, despite proclamation and UN notice.

**Check:** Confirm Article $4(2)$ shields Article $6$ life and Article $7$ freedom from torture without exception.
:::

## Traps to Watch For

:::checklist
- Lawful travel limit applies: keep other rights active unless separately limited. {#ordinary-limit-keeps-other-rights}
- State suspends ICCPR duty: send immediate start and closing notices via the UN Secretary-General. {#notify-immediately-via-un-chief}
- Emergency is declared and notified: keep the torture ban and right to life active. {#torture-ban-never-suspended}
:::
