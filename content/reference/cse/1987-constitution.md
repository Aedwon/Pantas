## Article III, the Bill of Rights

**It has 22 sections.** That number is examinable on its own.

**Sections 1 to 11** establish civil and political rights: due process and equal protection,
protection against unreasonable searches and seizures, privacy of communication, freedom of
speech, expression, the press, assembly and petition, free exercise of religion, liberty of
abode and travel, the right to information on matters of public concern, the right to form
associations and unions, non-impairment of contracts, and free access to the courts and to
adequate legal assistance.

**Sections 12 to 22** protect the accused: rights on custodial investigation, bail, the rights
of the accused at trial including presumption of innocence and speedy trial, the privilege of
the writ of habeas corpus, speedy disposition of cases, the right against self-incrimination,
freedom from political detention and involuntary servitude, prohibition of excessive fines and
cruel, degrading or inhuman punishment, no imprisonment for debt or non-payment of a poll tax,
protection against double jeopardy, and the prohibition of ex post facto laws and bills of
attainder.

**Do not write a question that turns on which numbered section carries a right** unless the
number has been verified against the text. Section numbering is the single easiest thing to get
wrong here.

## Article IX-B, the Civil Service Commission

**This is the most directly relevant article to the exam a student is sitting**, and it is
frequently examined.

- The civil service is administered by the **Civil Service Commission**, composed of a
  **Chairman and two Commissioners**.
- They must be **natural-born citizens**, **at least 35 years old** at the time of appointment,
  with **proven capacity for public administration**, and **must not have been candidates for
  any elective position in the election immediately preceding** their appointment.
- They are **appointed by the President with the consent of the Commission on Appointments**,
  for a term of **seven years, without reappointment**.

## Article XI, Accountability of Public Officers

**The impeachable officers, and this list is closed:**

1. The President
2. The Vice-President
3. The Members of the Supreme Court
4. The Members of the Constitutional Commissions
5. The Ombudsman

**The grounds for impeachment, and this list is closed:**

1. Culpable violation of the Constitution
2. Treason
3. Bribery
4. Graft and corruption
5. Other high crimes
6. Betrayal of public trust

**Both lists are examined as sets**, most often as "which of these is not an impeachable
officer" or "which is not a ground". Do not add to either list, and do not drop from it.

## Rules for anyone writing questions from this file

1. **If a fact is not in this file, fetch the article before using it.** Do not fill a gap from
   memory: the Constitution is the single easiest document to misquote confidently.
2. **Never invent a section number.**
3. **Do not write items about who currently holds an office**, or about counts that change.
4. **Where a list is marked closed above, treat it as closed.**
