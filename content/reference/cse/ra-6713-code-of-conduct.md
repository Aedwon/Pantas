# RA 6713: Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees

**Reference file. Every General Information question on the code of conduct is written against
this and against nothing else.**

**Approved 20 February 1989. Took effect 25 March 1989.**

**Sources.** Office of the Ombudsman's published text of RA 6713, and the published
Implementing Rules. Both are linked at the end. **Where this file and a source disagree, the
source wins and this file is wrong: fix it here rather than working around it.**

## The eight norms of conduct, Section 4

**Their names are examinable and their order is the order of the statute.** Do not paraphrase a
name.

| | Norm | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Commitment to public interest** | Uphold the public interest over and above personal interest. Use government resources and the powers of the office efficiently, effectively, honestly and economically, particularly to avoid wastage of public funds and revenues |
| b | **Professionalism** | Perform and discharge duties with the highest degree of excellence, professionalism, intelligence and skill. Enter public service with utmost devotion and dedication to duty |
| c | **Justness and sincerity** | Act with justness and sincerity, without discrimination, and respect the rights of others |
| d | **Political neutrality** | Provide service to everyone without unfair discrimination, regardless of party affiliation or preference |
| e | **Responsiveness to the public** | Extend prompt, courteous and adequate service, and communicate clearly |
| f | **Nationalism and patriotism** | Maintain loyalty to the Republic and to the Filipino people |
| g | **Commitment to democracy** | Uphold the Constitution, and put loyalty to country above loyalty to persons or party |
| h | **Simple living** | Lead modest lives appropriate to their position and income. Not indulge in extravagant or ostentatious display of wealth in any form |

**A very common exam item asks for the count, the order, or which of four options is not one of
the eight.** All three are answerable from the table above and from nothing else.

## Duties, and the deadlines that are examinable

| Duty | The number |
|---|---|
| Act on letters and requests | **within 15 working days** of receipt |
| Acknowledge a written request | **within 15 working days** |
| Signatures on written actions | **not more than three initials or signatures** |
| Annual performance report to superiors | **within 45 working days** of year end |
| Public documents | accessible and readily available for inspection during office hours, except those held confidential |

**Failure to act within 15 days is itself a ground for administrative disciplinary action.**

## Prohibited acts, and the conflict-of-interest rules

Grounds for administrative disciplinary action include:

- **Financial or material interest**, direct or indirect, in any transaction requiring the
  approval of the office.
- **Outside employment** owning, controlling, managing or accepting employment in a private
  enterprise regulated or supervised by the office, unless authorized by law.
- **Private practice** that conflicts with official functions. **Prohibited for one year after
  separation** in relation to any matter before the former office.
- **Recommending persons** to positions in private enterprises that have a regular or pending
  transaction with the office.
- **Disclosing or misusing confidential or classified information** for private interest or
  undue advantage, or to the prejudice of the public.
- **Soliciting or accepting gifts**, gratuities, favors, entertainment, loans or anything of
  monetary value in connection with official duties. **A thing of monetary value is one which
  is evidently or manifestly excessive by its very nature.** Impropriety is judged by value,
  the kinship between giver and receiver, and the motivation.

**Recognised exceptions to the gift rule**, and these are examinable as a set: unsolicited gifts
of nominal or insignificant value not given in anticipation of a favor; gifts from family on
celebrations with no pecuniary expectation; nominal donations from persons with no pending
transaction; humanitarian or altruistic private donations; government to government donations;
foreign government souvenirs of nominal value; scholarships, fellowships and medical treatment;
and travel grants entirely outside the Philippines where appropriate.

## Statements of Assets, Liabilities and Net Worth

**Who files.** Every public official and employee, **including the spouse and unmarried children
under eighteen still living in their households**, files a sworn statement, together with a
disclosure of business interests and financial connections.

**When, and these three dates are heavily examined:**

| Occasion | Deadline |
|---|---|
| On assumption of office | **within 30 days**, reckoned from the first day of service |
| Annually | **on or before 30 April**, reckoned as of the end of the preceding year |
| On separation from service | **within 30 days**, reckoned from the last day of service |

**Relatives in government** are disclosed **up to the fourth civil degree**, on the same three
occasions.

**Where filed, by office.** National officials and constitutional officials with the Office of
the Ombudsman. Members of Congress with the Secretary of the Senate or of the House. Justices
with the Clerk of the Supreme Court, judges with the Court Administrator. Cabinet members,
undersecretaries, assistant secretaries, foreign service officers and heads of GOCCs with the
Office of the President. Regional and local officials, and state university employees, with the
Deputy Ombudsman of their region. **Everyone else with the Civil Service Commission.**

**Public access.** Statements become available for public inspection **after ten working days**,
may be copied on payment of a reasonable reproduction fee, and remain available for **ten
years**, after which they are destroyed unless needed in an investigation.

**Divestment.** A conflict must be resolved by **resigning a private position within 30 days**
or **divesting shareholdings within 60 days**.

## Penalties, Section 11

- Violations generally: a **fine not exceeding six months' salary**, **suspension not exceeding
  one year**, or **removal**, depending on the gravity of the offence.
- Violations of **Sections 7, 8 or 9**: **imprisonment of up to five years**, a **fine of up to
  P5,000**, or both, and possible **disqualification from public office**.
- **Misuse of a filed statement**: penalties up to **P25,000**.
- **Private individuals** who take part as co-principals, accomplices or accessories face the
  same penalties and are tried jointly.
- **Where another law imposes a heavier penalty, the heavier one applies.**
- **An administrative violation is sufficient cause for removal on its own**, whether or not
  there is a criminal prosecution.

## Rules for anyone writing questions from this file

1. **Every question must be answerable from this file.** If it is not, the question is out of
   scope or this file is incomplete, and both are worth reporting rather than guessing.
2. **Never invent a number.** Every deadline, threshold and penalty a question uses must appear
   above.
3. **Do not test the wording of a norm's description**, only its name, its count, its order and
   what it plainly requires. The statute's phrasing varies between printings.
4. **Do not write items about who currently holds any office.** They rot.
