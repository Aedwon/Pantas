# Peace and Human Rights: issues and concepts

**Reference file for the third General Information area.**

**This file is deliberately incomplete and says so.** It records what is settled and names what
must be fetched. **Nothing in this area may be written from memory**, because it is the area
where a confident wrong answer is most likely and most damaging.

---

## What the exam actually names

The Civil Service Commission's published scope names this area as **"Peace and Human Rights
Issues and Concepts"**. It does not enumerate sub-areas, so **the scope has to be inferred from
what is conventionally examined**, and that inference is recorded here rather than hidden:

1. **Human rights fundamentals.** What a right is, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights,
   and the domestic institutions that protect rights.
2. **International humanitarian law.** The distinction between human rights law and the law of
   armed conflict, and the basic protections in the latter.
3. **The Philippine peace process.** Its institutions and its major agreements.

**Because the sub-areas are inferred rather than published, questions here must stay on the
uncontroversial core.** Do not write items that depend on a contested reading.

---

## Settled, and safe to write from

**Domestic institutions.**

- The **Commission on Human Rights** is created by **Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution**,
  which is titled Social Justice and Human Rights.
- **Article III, the Bill of Rights**, is the primary domestic guarantee. It is covered in
  `1987-constitution.md` in this directory and questions should draw on that file rather than
  restating it here.

**The distinction that is most often examined.**

- **Human rights law** binds a state in its treatment of people at all times.
- **International humanitarian law** applies in **armed conflict** and binds all parties to
  that conflict, not only states.

**These two being different bodies of law, applying in different circumstances, is the single
most examinable concept in this area.**

## What must be fetched before anything is written about it

**Do not write from memory on any of these.** Fetch from the sources below.

- The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights**: its adoption date, its article count, and what
  the first articles say.
- The **Geneva Conventions** and their basic protections.
- The **Bangsamoro Organic Law** and the agreements preceding it. **Dates, party names and the
  sequence of agreements are exactly the kind of fact that is misremembered**, and this is
  recent political history where an error is both likely and visible.
- **RA 9851**, the Philippine Act on Crimes Against International Humanitarian Law, Genocide and
  Other Crimes Against Humanity.
- **RA 11188** on the special protection of children in situations of armed conflict.

---

## Rules for anyone writing questions from this file

1. **If a fact is not in the settled section above, fetch it first.** This area has the widest
   gap between what feels known and what is actually verifiable.
2. **Never write an item about an ongoing conflict, a current negotiation, or any living
   person's role in one.** It rots, and it is not neutral.
3. **Never invent a date, an article count or a treaty name.**
4. **Prefer concepts over events.** "What distinguishes humanitarian law from human rights law"
   is durable. "Which agreement was signed in which year" is both fragile and less useful.
